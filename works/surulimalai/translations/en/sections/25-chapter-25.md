## 25

They say love is a rainbow, and that its play of colours cannot be fully discovered. Some call love an unfailing river of life. Others explain that love is glass that breaks at a touch. Tiruvalluvar affectionately scolded it as a sickness that buds in the morning, grows all day, and blooms in the evening.

There are people in the world who say love is an ocean with no shore. There are others who call it a filthy pond into which one must sink the moment one sets foot.

No one has ever explained love clearly.

If one must say with absolute clarity what love is, then in the end one has to say:

"It is confusion."<!-- verified source join: scan 181→182 -->

Aravazhi, who had only just escaped from one such confusion, now fell into an even greater one.

Until then he had calculated that Karagam-dancer Kannan would stand against his love and Theechatti Singaram would stand in support of it.

Now it seemed that Singaram too had become an enemy.

By the villagers' decision, one of the two men would subdue the bull and win. Ponmani would belong to one of them.

What, then, would become of Aravazhi?

He could find no answer.

Ponmani's position too had become difficult. Yet she patiently kept the pain burning inside her from showing.

Karpuram's heart, meanwhile, was blazing in flames. In the end Kannan had deceived her, she lamented.

Two or three times inside the house the possession came upon her and subsided on its own. She was ashamed even to go outside. Her brother Singaram did not come home either. Even if he did, she could not bring herself to face him. Her whole face had swollen from crying.

Now all her prayers were for Singaram to win in subduing the bull. If he won, Kannan would lose Ponmani. In the end he would have no choice but to come running back to Karpuram. Then she could teach him the proper lessons and make him her slave.

It was only because such a hope kept surging within her that she was able to remain alive.

Surulimalai took great trouble to feed his friend Siva rich, strengthening food.

<!-- source boundary: scan 182 → scan 183 -->

He did everything he could to make the bull even stronger.

Singaram and Kannan too began preparing their bodies to face it.

Meanwhile the sixteenth-day memorial observance for Ponnaiya arrived. Pooncholai had to conduct the day economically.

She performed the rites due to her husband according to the means available in her hands.

Panchayat Paramasivam gathered only the important people of the village and went to meet Pooncholai.

Pooncholai, wearing the appearance of widowhood and tears in her eyes, at first could not understand why the village notables had come.

They explained to her the state of mind of both Singaram and Kannan.

"Both of them are competing to marry Ponmani. If this is allowed to go on, suddenly even Ponmani's life—or what the source calls her chastity—could be endangered. That is why the villagers have come together and, according to Surulimalai's condition, arranged a bull-taming contest between the two," the panchayat elder explained.

While they were speaking, Ponmani ran in and began shouting that she did not wish to marry anyone and that she did not want marriage at all at that time.

Her lament carried no weight against the villagers' advice.

In the end Pooncholai too did not wish to oppose the village. She left the matter to their decision.

The date of the contest was fixed there itself: the final Sunday of that month.

<!-- source boundary: scan 183 → scan 184 -->

The news spread throughout the village. It reached the neighbouring villages too.

Everywhere there was excitement.

"What is this? We've heard stories from olden times—a man subdued an elephant, subdued a horse, and married the woman. Now we're going to see such a thing happen before our own eyes!"

People spoke in amazement.

Singaram came and stood before Aravazhi, who had been stumbling about without peace.

"What, Aravazhi! The time I've been waiting for is going to come very soon!" he said.

"What—the day of the contest?" Aravazhi asked.

"Not only the contest! Kannan will lose. The moment he loses, I'll mark him with black and red spots, parade him through the village on a donkey, and fulfil the promise I gave my father!"

"Fulfil the promise—is that all? Won't you get Ponmani too?" Aravazhi asked, hiding his pain.

"I have to tell you why my father gave me such a command, don't I? That's why I came here!" Singaram said through clenched teeth.

So fierce was his anger toward Kannan's family.

"I'll tell you. Listen!" he began speaking rapidly.

Aravazhi had neither the time nor the mind to hear Singaram's story.

"I have a little work now," he said, and slowly slipped away from him.

<!-- source boundary: scan 184 → scan 185 -->

Having escaped Singaram, Aravazhi found Surulimalai coming toward him.

Surulimalai too began talking about the bull contest. Aravazhi had no mind to listen.

Surulimalai's great worry was that no harm whatsoever should come to his bull during the contest. He was deeply regretting within himself that in a moment of anger he had suddenly proposed such a competition at all.

He had actually come to tell Aravazhi how he and the temple bull Siva had become friends.

Aravazhi paid no attention and left him.

Without hesitation, Aravazhi went straight to Ponmani's house.

Only Pooncholai was there.

From speaking with her he learned that Ponmani had gone to the market.

Since her father's death, Ponmani had not met him even once.

She had heard about the quarrels between Singaram and Kannan and the bull contest. She had worried about how deeply Aravazhi must have been hurt and wondered how she could face him.

Aravazhi had thought in much the same way and had not wanted to meet her either.

But as the day of the contest drew nearer, he began to feel he had to see Ponmani.

He set out toward the market.

The market had ended and people were returning along the road.

Dusk was settling.

Aravazhi kept walking, waiting for Ponmani to appear.

She came toward him after selling her pots, carrying only an empty basket.

They met.

Neither spoke.

Tears poured like waterfalls from both their eyes.

<!-- source boundary: scan 185 → scan 186 -->

Ponmani walked toward home without saying anything.

Aravazhi walked beside her.

When they reached a ruined well at the edge of the village, Ponmani stopped.

He stopped too.

She let out a long sigh and looked directly at him.

Until then she had said nothing. Now she spoke only a few words.

"Nothing has been lost completely. Your Ponmani belongs to no one but you. If these words ever prove false, don't look for me anywhere. You'll find me only inside this well!"

Without waiting even a second to hear his reply, she hurried away.

Aravazhi remained where he was, watching her go.

His own condition seemed strange even to him.

As he stood motionless, someone struck him hard on the back.

Startled, he turned.

The panchayat elder's mad son Chiranjeevi grinned and wrapped his arms around him.

"Oh-ho! So *this* is what's going on between you two? Just watch—I'm going right now and telling my appa!" he shouted.

More pain for Aravazhi.

The madman ran from the place at full speed.
