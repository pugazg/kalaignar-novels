## 3

Maina's shout startled Singaram a little, but instead of trying to run outside he summoned a fresh courage and stood where he was.

Maina stared at him too. "Don't you know who I am? Take the lamp and look at my face!" Singaram said calmly. A man who had entered her house and, even after being caught, still spoke with such composure and boldness seemed a puzzle to her.

She took the dimly burning lamp from the wall-niche and held it up before his face. Her hand began to tremble without her realizing it. Suddenly her eyes dropped toward the floor. Her heart pounded. Even the shock of finding some unknown man—a murderer or robber perhaps—suddenly inside the house at night had not made her tremble like this. Singaram understood her state. He grew confident that his sudden intrusion would not lead to any danger.

Softly he said, "Maina!"

"How do you know my name?" She did not actually ask the question like that. She merely looked at him once, her eyes filled with astonishment.

<!-- source boundary: scan 29 → scan 30 -->

"Are you angry with me?" he asked, moving closer. She moved away from him toward the wall. The kitten, ignorant of the mysteries of the human species, sat there looking from one to the other with shining eyes. Singaram came still closer. Tears began to well from Maina's eyes.

"Why are you crying?" Singaram asked, taking hold of her hand. She began to sob convulsively. That crying gave Singaram still more opportunity to touch her and offer comfort.

"Don't cry! Don't cry!... Sit here."

With his arm around her shoulder, Singaram made her sit down. She sat with her head bowed. As he ran his hand through her black hair, curled in wave after wave, a feeling surged through him as though electricity were shooting through his fingers and making him lose consciousness. But even in that state he did not wish to turn into a beast.

Anyone who noticed Singaram's appearance—his speech, walk, dress and manner—and the smell of liquor coming from his mouth might have found it hard to believe that he was sitting beside a beautiful woman with ordinary human decency. Yet among both men and women it is common enough to find people of handsome appearance, high status and wealth, and an outwardly gentle look who are virtuous only in daylight. Just as no one can deny that, neither can anyone deny that Singaram, who looked like a tiger, was by nature free of baseness.

She did not stop crying. Singaram decided that somehow he had to make her speak.

<!-- source boundary: scan 30 → scan 31 -->

"I want a little water!"

Maina rose, drew water from the earthen pot into a vessel, and brought it to him. Even as she handed it over, her hands trembled.

Singaram put the vessel to his lips and drank. "Thoo! Thoo!... Appa! Appa!" he cried, spitting all the water onto the floor and hurriedly wiping his mouth with a cloth.

Maina immediately understood what had happened. In her agitation she had mistaken the vessel that held salt for a drinking cup and brought water in it. A mistake born of panic.

"Forgive me! Do you want some other water to rinse your mouth?" she asked with a small smile—and with a sorrowful look that at once hid that smile. Singaram's heart longed to embrace her tightly. But the thali shining at her neck stood across his path like a high fence and stopped his rush.

"I don't want water. All I want now is the reason you're crying!" Singaram answered in a trembling voice, looking at her face.

After all that time, an unrelated question arose—a question she herself knew was unnecessary.

"Why did you come here?"

"When I was dancing with the fire-pot, why did you look at me like that? That's why I came."

"Please go away!"

"Why, Maina—why are you asking me to go?"

"I'll hold your feet and beg you. Please go away!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 31 → scan 32 -->

"I won't go. I have to know about you. Don't think I came here only to satisfy my desire! At first I came because I was eager to see you. Now, after seeing you, I need to know who you are and why you're crying and melting away like this. Maina—I need to know!"

"What are you going to do by learning about me?"

"Do you think I'm so powerless that I can't help you?"

"Aiyo—why did the two of us ever meet? Oh God—was it not enough that you reduced my life to a broken little wall? Now must this calamity come too?"

"Maina! Has your life become a broken wall?... Then I'll stand guard over that wall like a donkey. Don't worry—tell me everything!"

"I'm a married woman!"

"I know."

"You know? How?"

"I saw the thali around your neck already!"

"Ah—you saw the thali?"

"Why? Is your thali Chandramathi's thali, visible only to her husband and to no one else?"

A tiny smile flashed and vanished on lips wet with tears.

"Even after seeing the thali, you touched me?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 32 → scan 33 -->

"After tying a thali, is it all right for *you* to look at me as though you're taking my life, but not all right for *me* to touch you?"

"I forgot myself and did wrong. Forgive me. Please go away. Save me!"

"I won't trouble you in any way—I swear on God! Look, I'll sit over here. Tell me what happened. Where is your husband now?"

"He's alive."

"I can see that much—you still wear turmeric and kumkum! What village is he in?"

"Perur, near Coimbatore."

"Then why did you leave him and come here like this?"

"God wrote it on my head that way. That's why I came."

"Was there some quarrel between you and your husband?"

"Aiyo—he's a man of gold! Why would there be a quarrel between him and me?"

"You yourself say he's a golden husband—then why did things come to this? Oh! Was it that you and your mother-in-law couldn't get along? Adaadadaa!... The torment these mothers-in-law put daughters-in-law through... terrible! They forget that once they too were daughters-in-law! Was your mother-in-law like that too, Maina?"

"Aiyo, I don't even have a mother-in-law!"

"Some sister-in-law, perhaps...?"

"None of that either!"

"Then what's the trouble? Did your husband leave this Kannagi and go off to some Madhavi's house..."

<!-- verified source join: scan 33→34 -->

"Nothing like that... He's not that sort of man at all!"

"Then what *is* the reason? You have to tell me!"

"Aiyo—stop that talk there... I've cried enough thinking about it again and again. Don't make me tell the story once more so the two of us can sit here and cry together!"

Singaram burned to understand the mystery of Maina's sorrowful story somehow. With no other way out, she began to tell it, sobbing from time to time.

"I was born in Singanallur. My father was a textile-mill worker. I was his only daughter. With the wages he earned at the mill, my father and I managed to fill our stomachs. I had crossed sixteen. Men's eyes had begun to fall on me. My father was suffering from tuberculosis, and he became consumed by one desire—to see me married somehow before he closed his eyes.

"I too came to the decision that I should marry. Appa looked for grooms in all sorts of places. I didn't like any of them. At last I chose a groom I liked myself and told Appa. He did not say anything against my wish. He nodded and agreed.

"I haven't told you where and how I chose my husband. One day the annual celebration of the textile workers' union was held with great pomp. They had arranged a play as part of the festivities: *Alli Arjunan*!<!-- verified source join: scan 34→35 --> The man who played Arjuna was extraordinary. The moment he came onto the stage there was a glow—thunderous applause! Beautiful hair, thick and curling like coiled wire, covering the ears and falling to the neck! Teeth like pearls taken out and set in a row! A bright red complexion! A neat moustache! A majestic gaze! In the story he was supposed to capture Alli—but I felt that he had captured *me* completely.

"The play ended. I came home. I couldn't sleep all night. Nothing but *Alli Arjunan* dreams! The next day, without the slightest shame, I told Appa decisively that I would marry only that Arjuna. Appa made inquiries about the actor. His name was Rajapart Peethambara Bhagavathar!

"'Daughter, I'll go meet Peethambaram. Until the marriage is settled, let this remain secret. If by chance the Bhagavathar refuses, people in the town will mock us!' Appa said, and left.

"Meanwhile I found out my Arjuna's address, enclosed a photograph of myself, and hurriedly wrote asking him to accept me somehow. After seeing my picture, he told Appa that he agreed and even fixed a date for the marriage.

"The wedding was held simply in the groom's village, Perur. Even in his wedding dress my husband appeared in Rajapart costume like Arjuna and plunged me into an even deeper enchantment.

"The 'first night' was arranged on the very day of the wedding. Filled with immeasurable happiness that I was about to meet alone the chosen deity I myself had selected in life, I stepped into the bedroom. My whole body fluttered with some strange feeling. He stood before the full-length mirror admiring his own beauty. As soon as I entered he took my hand, brought me close, and made me sit on the cot. Unable to bear my shyness, I sat there imagining that I myself was Queen Alli. We ate fruit. We drank milk. He blew out the lamp with his mouth.

<!-- verified source join: scan 35→36 -->

"The clock in the hall struck five. I woke. Light had spread through the room. He was sleeping soundly. Something on the small three-legged stool beside the cot glittered. I looked carefully. Two rows of teeth! I was stunned. I stared at him. They were the teeth from his mouth!

"'Aiyo! Is my husband a toothless old man?' I sat there staring at his face in anger, yet trying to summon a little hope. He turned in his sleep. The curled hair hanging beside his ear shifted slightly. The ear gleamed. I looked. I lit the lamp and examined it. The beginning of a terrible disease! That was what he had been hiding beneath that beautiful hair. I moved the hair aside with my hand. What horror—the whole thing came away in my hand. A completely bald head!

"He turned and woke. By then I had put the lamp down and pretended to be asleep. In great haste he set his head in order, took the teeth from the stool and fitted them into his mouth, then stood before the full-length mirror and began his adornment.

<!-- source boundary: scan 36 → scan 37 -->

"I couldn't remain lying there. I got up as though I had only just awakened. Calling me affectionately, 'Mainu,' that Arjuna came near me! He gave me a morning kiss on the cheek. I accepted it, held back both my tears and my anger, and left the room.

"The moment I came out, my ears heard the sound of lamentation...

"'Aiyaiyyo!'

"I ran to ask what had happened. In the passage I saw my father, who at dawn had closed his eyes for the last time......"
