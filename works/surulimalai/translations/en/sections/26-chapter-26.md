## 26

Pooncholai started when Ponmani came home rubbing her eyes. For five or six days she had known that her daughter was wasting away with worry, yet she had asked her nothing.

Now her curiosity grew. What could have happened to make the girl return from the market in tears?

<!-- source boundary: scan 186 → scan 187 -->

"What is this, amma? You're coming home so upset. Why—what happened at the market?"

Ponmani could not answer Pooncholai's question. She remained silent. Tears kept pouring from her eyes despite herself.

"Why? Did you break the pots on the way? Let them go if they're gone. What use is there in crying over that?"

Pooncholai herself invented a reason and offered consolation for it.

As though to show that this was not the cause, Ponmani placed the money from the pot sales in her mother's hand and sobbed.

"Tell me what happened, amma. What's the matter?"

"What more needs to happen, amma? All of you together are going to drop a stone on my head!"

She clung to her mother and began crying in choking sobs.

Pooncholai's eyes too filled.

How could she console her daughter?

"Why, amma? Two men are competing. Whichever of the two wins will marry you and keep you affectionately. Why should you worry about that?"

"I don't like either of them, amma. I'm not prepared to live with either one. If the winner insists, he can tie the thali only around my corpse!"

"What kind of trouble is this, amma? My heart has already been struck by thunder, and now you say all sorts of things and make the confusion worse, kanne! Whom do you *want* to marry? Tell me that!"

"If I tell you what I want, will you make it happen exactly that way?"

<!-- source boundary: scan 187 → scan 188 -->

"Just tell me your wish!"

"The teacher who has come to our village—he and I have decided that we will marry, amma!"

Having said it, Ponmani fell at Pooncholai's feet and began to cry.

"Aiyyo! Is that so, my thangam? What am I to do with this trouble? That Aravazhi is a fine boy. He suits your beauty too. But the village won't agree to a marriage between you and that young man! If we go against the village's decision, they'll cast us aside as a family with no one to support us, Ponmani!"

Pooncholai wailed.

Ponmani sobbed.

The mother suffered, not knowing any way to remove her daughter's anguish.

The daughter lay curled up, reproaching herself for increasing her mother's pain.

Just then Surulimalai rushed inside.

There was a briskness in his movements that neither of them had ever seen in him before. His speech too was full of urgency.

"Why are you worrying, amma? I've found a way to end both your worry and akka's!"

The two looked at him anxiously.

"There aren't two people in the contest now. There are three!"

"Who's the third?"

"Aravazhi!"

"Who—*him*?"

Ponmani was jolted. Terror seized her at the thought of whether he could possibly subdue such a savage bull.

<!-- source boundary: scan 188 → scan 189 -->

"Why are you worrying, akka? You can marry Aravazhi. That's all, isn't it?" Surulimalai said, and left the house.

The news Surulimalai brought shocked Ponmani deeply.

She said she wanted no food and lay fasting on the floor, crying until her eyes were exhausted.

●

Through his mad son, Panchayat Paramasivam heard about the affection between Aravazhi and Ponmani and became still angrier.

Kannan, who was nearby, increased that anger.

They had already sworn to get rid of Aravazhi somehow. Then news reached them that Aravazhi too was going to take part in the contest.

"He's finished!" Kannan said.

Panchayat Paramasivam had tried in vain to make peace between Singaram and Kannan. Now he had been driven into the uncomfortable position of having to let the two clash and sit watching the spectacle.

Aravazhi's joining the contest eased some of that discomfort and even pleased him, because he did not agree with Aravazhi's ideas. Irritation had arisen between them at their very first meeting.

He believed the temple bull Siva would somehow give Aravazhi a thorough lesson.

The contest day approached, keeping everyone's eagerness alive.

●

It was the last Sunday of the month.

A huge crowd gathered at the Mariamman temple ground in Sirur to watch.

People from the village and outside it camped at the ground with bundles of packed rice.<!-- verified source join: scan 189→190 -->

Panchayat Paramasivam and several other important men sat in the principal place and took responsibility for supervising the contest.

Before it began, a small doubt arose.

What if all three men subdued the bull?

The question had been asked jokingly by Chiranjeevi, though he was mad. But it became a serious problem.

Each man would have to fight the bull separately. If all three subdued it, they decided, another contest would then be arranged among the three, and Ponmani would be given as the prize to whoever won that.

At the appointed time Surulimalai arrived at the ground with the bull.

Kannan, Singaram and Aravazhi entered one after another.

From among the crowd of women, Karpuram's eyes shot toward Kannan standing proudly in the arena and cursed him.

The lot fell for Singaram to enter the contest first.

Singaram prepared for battle.

His chest swelled. His eyes widened.

The smell of the arrack he had drunk rather heavily pierced the noses of the people in the crowd.

He believed victory would certainly be his.

A voice rose inside his heart:

"This contest is not to obtain Ponmani! Not at all!

"If she comes to me as the prize, I will regard her as my younger sister. The one who must have her is Aravazhi.

"First, I must win! Second, Kannan must lose! Third, I will stop Aravazhi from even entering<!-- verified source join: scan 190→191 --> the contest! I will place Ponmani in his hands.

"All I want is to fulfil my father's command. I want to mark that Kannan with black and red spots and parade him on a donkey!

"The right time has come to take revenge on the family that pushed my father into a pit of humiliation!

"They say Kannan's father tried to violate my mother's chastity. My father tried to stop him and save my mother, and that man struck my father with a slipper. My father told me this before he gave up his life!

"'Not only you—your own son will be struck with a slipper by my son! Your grandson will be struck with a slipper by my grandson!' that outcaste wretch swore too!

"Aiyyo! The father never fell into my hands. Now his son has!"

Fire flashed from Singaram's eyes. He slapped his shoulder and approached the bull.

Ponmani too arrived hurriedly. Her body trembled.

Surulimalai untied the bull and gave its back one slap.

Singaram and the bull fought for a long time.

Dust rose across the ground.

Singaram's whole body was wounded.

No one could decide whether victory would go to him or the bull.

Little by little his physical strength diminished. In contrast, the strength of his will increased.

Determined to win somehow, he sprang at the bull in one great leap.

The bull moved aside with great agility and, with its powerful horns, tore Singaram's thigh and knocked him down.

Singaram could not rise.

He fell face down in the dirt.

Surulimalai ran and caught the bull.

Several healers surrounded Singaram, lifted him and hurried him away for treatment.

<!-- source boundary: scan 191 → scan 192 -->

"Siva needs a little rest!" Surulimalai said, leading the bull away.

Fifteen minutes later he returned to the ground with Siva.

Now Kannan stood ready to fight.

The lot had fallen to him.

Kannan's first charge was so violent that the bull was startled and fell.

But it rose at once and charged Kannan.

Everyone watching the contest decided victory would surely be Kannan's.

Karpuram's heart became fire.

Kannan fought the bull far more fiercely than Theechatti Singaram had.

Watching the course of the fight, Aravazhi turned toward where Ponmani stood.

Her eyes were closed and her mind seemed to be wandering somewhere far away.

Surulimalai, who had been looking alternately at his sister and Aravazhi, went to Ponmani.

"Akka! Don't worry. Kannan will lose!" he said.

"If he loses, what is that to me? Aravazhi has to win!" She did not say it aloud. She only thought it.

By then a great cry rose from the crowd.

At the feet of the temple bull Siva, Kannan lay in a flood of blood.

The bull's horns had tasted the side of his abdomen.

"His life is not in danger!" the healers reassured everyone as they hurriedly lifted him and carried him away.

Ponmani looked at Aravazhi.

She ran to him, fell at his feet and cried:

"Forget me. You don't need this contest!"
