## 27

When she heard that her brother had lost—and Kannan had lost too—Karpuram's mind became complete confusion.

Now anxiety seized her over whether Kannan would survive.

Though she longed to know the result of the contest, she did not remain there. She ran to see Kannan.

Kannan lay like a corpse without opening his eyes. The doctors rushed about displaying all their skill.

Singaram was not in a dangerous condition, but he was crying out because he could not bear the pain.

Karpuram stood there looking from one man to the other and weeping.

She was afraid to go near her brother. She feared what he might say to her.

While she suffered in this difficult state, the roar from the Mariamman ground reached her ears more loudly.

Singaram, tormented by pain, asked himself aloud, "What? Has Aravazhi entered the contest?"

Just as he thought, Aravazhi had prepared to fight the bull.

Ponmani lay at his feet pleading, "Forget me and live!"

An elder who saw this said, "Why have a contest now? Both the men who competed have already lost. Let Ponmani marry the man she herself wants!"

Panchayat Paramasivam immediately rejected this.

"The common wager was that only the man who wins the contest may obtain Ponmani. We must not change it!" he argued.

Aravazhi accepted that argument.

Ponmani could not.

Determined somehow to save Aravazhi's life, she stood directly before him and opposed him boldly.

Aravazhi looked at her.

"Ponmani! Don't make me a coward!" he shouted.

By then Surulimalai had led the bull away so that it could walk about and rest a little.

Ponmani went to him and whispered urgently into his ear.

"Thambi! Run away somewhere with the bull! Save him!"

"Akka! Don't be afraid. Everything will happen according to your wish," Surulimalai replied, and went away with the bull.

He and Siva reached the neem tree.

Surulimalai stood looking only at the bull's face.

Tears flowed from his eyes like a river.

Leaving Siva standing there, he went to the fence of a nearby garden. A kind of green-leaved plant was growing there, and he plucked some of its leaves.

Why was he plucking them?

Those leaves were a poisonous herb—powerful enough to kill cattle.

Why did he need them?

He returned to Siva with a handful of leaves.

He patted Siva on the back.

The bull licked him.

Surulimalai stared at it.

Then he held the leaves out to its mouth.

The bull hesi<!-- verified source join: scan 194→195 -->tated.

But Surulimalai did not give up. With some insistence he pushed the leaves into its mouth.

The bull stopped resisting and swallowed them.

Surulimalai sobbed in choking bursts and wrapped his arms around its neck.

The bull understood nothing. It stood without moving.

"Kanne, Siva!" Surulimalai said softly.

Then he cried out loudly.

Once, long before, he had saved Siva from great danger. That was when Siva had become his friend.

Siva had entered a field, damaged the crop, and then become badly trapped in the mud. Seven or eight rough men had surrounded the bull and beaten it relentlessly.

Surulimalai had run there, driven them away and rescued the bull.

From then on Siva and Surulimalai had become inseparable.

Surulimalai, who had once saved Siva from violent men, had now decided to kill it.

Why?

For his akka.

To save Aravazhi's life.

He could not even cry properly now.

He led Siva back to the ground and brought it before Aravazhi.

The drums sounded fiercely.

Aravazhi prepared to face the bull.

Ponmani fainted and fell.

The bull charged Aravazhi in one great rush. He managed to evade it.

The struggle went on for a long time.

Aravazhi fought the bull in a way that astonished the gathered crowd.

Four or five times the bull charged him with such force that people feared it would tear open his stomach and crush him entirely. Each time it missed and struck its head against the ground.

It seemed Surulimalai's drug had begun to work.

Aravazhi too sensed the bull's weakness and drove it harder. He made it run. He made it charge. He made it strike the ground.

Whenever it lunged at Aravazhi, the bull staggered, spun, fell and rose again.

After a long struggle, Aravazhi caught its horns, climbed onto its back and sat there.

A single explosion of joy!

Cheering!

The drums thundered triumphantly.

Aravazhi had won.

Ponmani opened her eyes and looked.

Aravazhi had won!

The bull carrying him collapsed.

Everyone ran toward the animal.

The bull was dead.

Surulimalai's dearest friend had stilled all its movements and lay on the ground as a corpse.

Ponmani ran toward Aravazhi.

Aravazhi searched for Surulimalai.

He was not there.

Taking Ponmani with him, Aravazhi ran toward the neem tree to look for the boy. He wanted to ask Surulimalai's forgiveness for Siva's death.

The sight they found beneath the neem tree!

Aiyyo!

Surulimalai's flute lay on the ground.

Ponmani looked upward.

Surulimalai hung dead from a branch of the tree.

For his akka's good life, the younger brother had lost his friend; for the loss of that friend, he had lost his own life.

What remained after that?

Ponmani and Pooncholai wept and lamented.

<!-- source boundary: scan 196 → scan 197 -->

Beside Ponnaiya's grave, a grave for Surulimalai.

Beside Surulimalai's grave, a grave for the temple bull Siva.

Kannan struggled for his life.

Singaram slowly recovered.

As though the deceitful thoughts inside him had flowed out together with the blood from his torn abdomen, good words began to emerge from Kannan's mouth.

He fell at Singaram's feet and asked forgiveness for his wrongs.

Singaram forgave him.

Kannan confessed that he himself had written the letter blaming Singaram and tied it into the sari-end of Maina's hanging body.

With the burdens in his heart unloaded, peace of mind itself became medicine and saved his life.

Then he fell at Karpuram's feet and asked her forgiveness.

Singaram, who had sworn to conduct a donkey procession, instead joyfully conducted the wedding procession of **Karpuram and Kannan**.

Ponmani, having lost both her father and her younger brother, found consolation in her mother's love and in seeing Aravazhi's face, and gathered the courage to live.

Panchayat Paramasivam arrived ready to bless everyone.
