## 10

For so many years the two families had fed their feud, saying it was a sin even to set eyes on one another's faces. Pooncholai-amma was pleased that, through this incident at least, they had come into some kind of relationship. But she could not bear Singaram's suffering. Her eyes filled without her realizing it. And her husband Ponnaiya, regarding all of this as trouble they had gone out and bought for themselves, sat with an irritable face. That caused her great discomfort.

Karpuram sat with a hand on her cheek, sunk in deep thought. Her unending worry was how to free herself from the complicated problem she herself had created.

Ponnaiya kept coughing "lok, lok," letting out between the coughs long sighs that echoed both his distress and his irritation.

Singaram lay there growling, unable to bear either his anger or the pain in his body.

Ponmani, who had hidden near the kitchen while Kannan's ghost-driving puja was going on, slowly put her head out afterward. Karpuram sat watching her.

Looking at Karpuram, Pooncholai said, "Why are you sitting there eaten up with worry, amma? That poor fellow's whole body has swollen up like tamarind fruit... Go inside, grind some turmeric paste and bring it. We can put it on the swellings!" Karpuram rose and, taking Ponmani, who had come toward her, along for help, went into the kitchen.

<!-- source boundary: scan 78 → scan 79 -->

Ponmani herself picked out the turmeric and began grinding it. Karpuram sat beside her, waiting for a chance to start a conversation. Ponmani finished grinding and lifted the grinding stone to scrape the turmeric from the slab; it slipped onto her hand and caused a small injury. Karpuram hurriedly caught her hand, poured water over it and tended to it. "Aiyyo, poor thing! How much trouble you're going through because of us!" she lamented.

"What's so difficult about this? Isn't it only natural in the world for one person to help another?" Ponmani said with a smile.

"Leave that aside—when that Kannan came inside, you went off and hid somewhere. Why, Ponmani?" Karpuram asked eagerly. Ponmani gave no answer.

Karpuram tried to draw one out of her. "Shy?" she asked. No answer.

"Angry about something?" she asked. No answer.

"Oh-ho! Has some marriage talk or other started?" The moment she asked that, Ponmani snapped, "Here! Don't talk about all that. Talk about something else!" and went to warm the turmeric at the stove.

"Ada-da-da! Even *I* don't have this much nervous hurry when I'm doing things for my own anna! But look at you—you're all agitation!" Karpuram teased, hurriedly studying Ponmani's face for a reaction.

"What is this? Why are you talking like this—dragging men's names into matters between us?" Ponmani scolded.<!-- verified source join: scan 79→80 --> "Fine, fine! Can a woman live without a man? Whether for good or bad, didn't God write it on our heads from the beginning—one woman for one man, one man for one woman—and send us down?" Karpuram launched into this philosophical inquiry.

Ponmani did not answer. She watched to see whether the turmeric was heating.

"What, Ponmani? Don't you like what I'm saying? You've gone completely silent! Marriage talk sounds unpleasant to a young woman only when an old granny and a young girl are talking about it. Both of us are young women. Why can't we open our hearts and talk?" Karpuram said.

"Opening our hearts can wait. First let's get the turmeric paste ready!" Ponmani replied indifferently, keeping her attention on the task.

"Hm... I understand, I understand! I understand, amma, I understand! 'I won't answer with my eyes or my mouth; just watch, I'll show my heart through what I do!' That's what your behaviour looks like!" Karpuram said, laughing mockingly. Unable to understand what was really in Karpuram's mind, Ponmani twisted her face and asked, "What are you saying now? Say it so I can understand a little!"

"Why should I say it? You'll find out as things go on," Karpuram replied.

"Let that be. Who was that woman who hanged herself? She seems to have put the blame on your anna. What happened? Tell me properly!" Ponmani asked.

Karpuram said, "Hm...! That's the way to ask! My queen, ask like that! I knew all along you'd ask. You've been sitting here all this time wondering how to bring yourself to ask<!-- verified source join: scan 80→81 -->—and now you've asked! Never mind. Don't worry about it! Somehow that Saturn has gone away!" Her voice trembled slightly. She also feared that Ponmani might answer more sharply than she expected.

"Is there any connection at all between the question I'm asking and the answer you're giving?" Ponmani said, rising.

"Wait, wait." Karpuram caught her hand and seated her again. "There's plenty of connection! The rival you were going to have has disappeared!"

"A rival? For me?" Ponmani asked in surprise.

"Yes. The woman who hanged herself was mad to marry my anna, it seems! Anna told her, 'Forget me. Somewhere else a girl has been born for me,' it seems!"

"What? The girl was only just born? Then how old will your anna be when he gets married?"

"Oh-ho! So you're mischievous too? The girl was born long ago! A face carved like a statue! Beauty, character, family—everything is a perfect match for my anna!"

"Is that so? Where is this wonderful match?"

"Don't worry! No other woman will come as a rival to you. It is you yourself!"

The moment those words reached her ears, Ponmani sprang to her feet. She took the turmeric in her hand to her mother, said, "Here, amma! Put this on him," and went off elsewhere.

<!-- source boundary: scan 81 → scan 82 -->

Coming out of the kitchen and not seeing Ponmani in the hall, Karpuram grew a little uneasy and went over to her brother. She and Pooncholai together began applying the turmeric paste to his swellings. Singaram could not bear even the touch of a hand on his body. Somehow they finished applying it, and Pooncholai-amma let out a long sigh. Karpuram washed her hands thoroughly, cast her eyes all around and searched for Ponmani. She was nowhere to be found.

Then Karpuram took leave of Pooncholai, saying she would go home, see to the fodder for the cattle and return. Her mind was in great confusion. Would anything happen the way she had imagined? Or would everything turn upside down? A battle raged in her mind. What a great burden she had lifted with her own hands and set upon her head—poor woman!

Kannan had to forget Ponmani. Ponmani had to marry her brother. As a result, Kannan had to become hers. She racked her brains over this complicated arrangement. Without her realizing it, a single hope arose within her. With the tiny courage that hope gave her, she walked toward home. Lost in dreams of the future, she failed to notice Kannan standing under a poovarasu tree, watching her.

Thinking it best that she not notice him yet, he followed behind. As Karpuram entered her house, she happened to turn toward the street. She saw Kannan standing there and was stunned with surprise. She felt that the treasure she had been searching for had come looking for her at her very doorstep. She was filled with delight and could not speak.

<!-- source boundary: scan 82 → scan 83 -->

Her cheeks reddened; her head bowed of its own accord. Stammering, she said, "Come in!" and went inside.

He entered the house too. In a flutter she took out a silk mat and spread it in the hall. Not knowing where to put her hands or feet, she searched here and there, found the betel tray and placed it before him.

"What, Karpuram—how is Singaram now?" Kannan began.

"After you've seen him, could he fail to get better?" she answered in a trembling voice.

"What a ghost that is! A remarkable ghost! Where did it catch him from?... A funny ghost!"

"Don't you know? Somehow, you're the one who must cure anna completely. I'm depending only on you!"

"Is that so? I'm depending only on you too, Karpuram!"

"All I ask is that you don't leave a woman who trusts you stranded in midstream!" Karpuram's voice thickened as she said it.

"I never betray anyone's trust!"

"You are a truly noble man! Otherwise, would you forget such a great feud and come running the moment I called?"

"Karpuram! Why did I come running? For what did I forget the feud? You couldn't have understood that!"

"I haven't failed to understand entirely. What troubles me is that I never knew all these days that such a thought was in your heart!"

<!-- source boundary: scan 83 → scan 84 -->

"Karpuram!"

"Can camphor burn without fire?..."

After that Karpuram said nothing. She sat motionless. Kannan looked at her as though he would devour her with his eyes. Then he himself went and bolted the door.

When he returned after bolting it, Karpuram suddenly fell at his feet. "Don't forget me! My life and everything I possess have taken refuge with you," she begged, weeping. He raised her and let her lean against his shoulder, comforting her. With his warm lips he caught the pearls of tears rolling down her beautiful cheeks.

Karpuram surrendered herself to him. The flood of delight that surged in her heart could not be described. "God, my dream has come true!" she blessed God. She had not expected the plan she had devised with such difficulty to succeed so quickly—and so completely.

For a long while they floated in a sea of pleasure, finding a special exhilaration in silence. Only after a long time did they remember that a world existed outside them. "You won't forget me, will you?" she asked, her lips barely moving.

"I certainly won't forget you. But there's one thing! You have to do me a favour, Karpuram," Kannan said.

"What favour? Tell me... I'll give even my life for you," she said.

"Truly?" he asked, holding her close.

"I swear on you!"

"Somehow, you must make Pooncholai's daughter Ponmani agree to me. Only you can do that!" Kannan said, stroking her hair.

<!-- source boundary: scan 84 → scan 85 -->

"Shu!"

Karpuram screamed. What might happen if she suddenly gave herself over to emotion?
