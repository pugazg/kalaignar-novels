# English Translation Plan — சுருளிமலை / Surulimalai

## Authority hierarchy

1. `works/surulimalai/pages/` — canonical Tamil, controlling authority.
2. `works/surulimalai/sections/` — PASSED continuous Tamil reading layer.
3. `works/surulimalai/translations/en/` — derived English only.

English never silently corrects, regularizes or modernizes Tamil.

## Translation objective

Produce readable English while preserving:

- speaker agency and knowledge state;
- rhetorical questions, repetition, humour, irony and melodrama;
- colloquial/rustic dialogue differences;
- paragraph and scene structure;
- source-visible chapter structure;
- documented source oddities and contradictions;
- culturally specific names/terms where forced English would add meaning not present in the source.

## Source-structure rules

- Introduction remains separate.
- Source-visible numbered chapters are **1–5, 8–27**.
- Chapters **6 and 7 are not invented**.
- Mixed chapter-boundary scans follow the PASSED Tamil `sections/` split.
- Scan197 `[முற்றிற்று.]` must be represented in the final English chapter.
- Scan198 wrapper/publisher matter is not part of narrative translation.
- Provenance comments may be retained where they make verified physical joins reversible.

## Source-oddity rules

- Introduction describes Surulimalai as **not yet quite eleven**; Chapter 1 calls him a **fifteen-year-old**. Preserve both; do not reconcile the contradiction.
- The archaic Silappatikaram quotation in Chapter 1 is retained in Tamil in E1 because the project source alone does not securely support a modern English gloss. The surrounding prose is translated.
- Source-specific names, invocations, proverbs and performance terms are handled by the locked glossary rather than silently normalized.
- Chapter 5's assault, chastity, sin, possession and suicide rhetoric is translated as source rhetoric without endorsement, euphemistic repair, or added modern commentary.
- The source's pejorative `வாழாவெட்டி` is retained as *vazhavetty* with a minimal contextual gloss rather than silently neutralized.
- E3 keeps `ஷு!`, `கிருட்டிணப் பறவை`, Surulimalai's scan97 chant, and `மஞ்ச கல்யாணி` source-facing where the canonical Tamil does not justify a stronger English normalization.
- Chapter 14's Payasa Muni / Katteri material preserves both reported explanations rather than editorially choosing one.
- E3 preserves the source distinction between reported possession / village folklore and Aravazhi's in-story rationalist explanation; no external adjudication is added inside translation prose.
- The temple-bull sequence preserves both the immediate narrated event (Surulimalai's flute calms the bull) and Aravazhi's later source explanation that the bull was already accustomed to the flute.
- E4 preserves the one-off canonical `சுருமலை` at scan116 as **Surumalai**, the `ஊதல் / ஊடல்` wordplay, Singaram's father's revenge command as character speech, and the petition-signature explanation without silent normalization.
- E5 preserves chapter23 widowhood / auspiciousness rhetoric as source-era framing, chapter22's `வாடை தூக்க வணங்கிய தாழை!` in source Tamil, the bull-contest agency, the unspecified poisonous-leaf narrative without external identification, and the final `[முற்றிற்று.]` marker.
