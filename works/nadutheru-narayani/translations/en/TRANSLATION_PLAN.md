# English Translation Plan — நடுத்தெரு நாராயணி

## Objective

Create a readable but source-bound English translation of **கலைஞர் மு. கருணாநிதியின் `நடுத்தெரு நாராயணி`** from the audited 1978 first-edition Tamil layer.

Working English title: **Nadutheru Narayani**.

The Tamil archival title remains `நடுத்தெரு நாராயணி`. The working English title is deliberately conservative and transliterative; it does not turn the title into an interpretive paraphrase.

## Translation style

English must be **readable but source-bound**.

- translate every substantive narrative paragraph, dialogue unit, quotation, rhetorical question, pun-bearing exchange and sound effect;
- preserve agency, accusation, irony, satire, humour, emotional force, repetition and polemical intensity;
- do not beautify the prose into modern literary English or flatten its political/social satire;
- do not silently repair unusual Tamil grammar, caste terminology, colloquial speech or source oddities;
- do not add explanatory history, theology or social commentary inside the translated prose;
- where a source expression is genuinely difficult, prefer a conservative rendering and record the decision in `GLOSSARY.md`.

The translation should read naturally in English without becoming an adaptation.

## Caste, community and social terminology

This work contains explicit caste/community terminology and caste-based rhetoric. Translation must preserve that source content without sanitizing, euphemizing, intensifying or endorsing it.

Initial handling:

- `பிராமணன் / பிராமணர்` → **Brahmin**;
- `சூத்திரச்சி` → **Sudra woman** where the source uses the gendered caste form;
- `பறையன்` → **Paraiyan** as a source caste term; do not replace it with a generic social label;
- `ஆதித் திராவிடர்` → **Adi Dravidar**;
- `ஜாதி` → **caste** where that is the source sense;
- `அய்யர்` when used as a community/social address must be handled consistently with the named character Krishnayyar and not erased.

If a term functions simultaneously as identity, insult, satire or social marker, preserve that function in English and document the choice rather than neutralizing it.

## Religious, temple and ritual terminology

Use clear English equivalents where they are genuinely one-to-one; otherwise retain a stable Tamil/Sanskrit-derived form and document it.

Initial policy:

- `தர்மகர்த்தா` → **temple trustee (dharmakarta)** on first significant use; thereafter **temple trustee** unless the source-specific term itself matters;
- `குருக்கள்` → **temple priest / priest** according to syntax;
- `கர்ப்பக்கிரகம்` → **sanctum**;
- `பிரகாரம்` → **prakaram / temple circumambulatory passage**, with the first batch fixing the final consistent form;
- `பிரசாதம்` → **prasadam** unless direct English is clearly preferable in context;
- `விபூதி` → **vibhuti / sacred ash**, consistently after the pilot decision;
- `பூணூல்` → **sacred thread** when the canonical Tamil is `பூணூல்`;
- `நாயனார்` → **Nayanar**, not genericized to “saint” without need.

Mythological and devotional references must remain the narrator/characters' references. Do not convert them into editorial religious explanation.

## Colloquial and dialect speech

Krishnayyar's dialogue contains marked colloquial/Brahmin-Tamil forms such as `நோக்கு`, `நேக்கு`, `நன்னுகத்`, `அவா`, `வச்சிண்டு`, `பண்ணுடி` and related speech.

English policy:

- preserve informality, intimacy, manipulation, pleading and comic/satirical rhythm;
- contractions and colloquial English syntax may be used where natural;
- **do not invent an English ethnic accent or caricature**;
- do not standardize the character's register into formal English;
- repeated address forms (`கண்ணே`, `அடி`, etc.) should retain their interpersonal force without mechanical word-for-word repetition when that would distort English.

Any recurring solution should be locked in `GLOSSARY.md` after the pilot.

## Punctuation, dialogue, rhetoric and sound effects

- use standard English double quotation marks for spoken dialogue;
- preserve speaker changes and paragraph structure;
- preserve rhetorical questions, exclamations, pauses and repetitions when they carry tone;
- minimal punctuation adjustment is allowed for grammatical English, but not to calm or sanitize the source;
- source ellipses/repeated dots should retain a visible pause function;
- source sound effect `டுமீல்! டுமீல்!` is initially locked as **Dumeel! Dumeel!**, preserving the printed onomatopoeia rather than silently replacing it with “Bang! Bang!”;
- page/scan boundaries are not paragraph boundaries unless source structure supports them.

## Source-oddity and wordplay policy

Verified Tamil oddities remain protected. English may make contextual meaning readable, but must not silently replace the underlying Tamil with an expected reading.

Specially protected items include:

- scan81 `பூணால் வலையில்`;
- scan82 wordplay `வைப்பு / ஒய்ப் / வைப்`;
- scan84 `பதி சொல் தட்டாத`;
- scan86 genuine short form `தல தப்பாதடி, தல தப்பாது`;
- scan88 `ஒரு கடாரம் பத்திருக்கு`;
- scan89 `பகற் கொள்ளைக்காரனைப்`;
- scan89 `டுமீல்! டுமீல்!`;
- scan89 `அவர்கள் அய்யரை வீரராக்கிவிட்டார்கள்`.

The `வைப்பு / ஒய்ப் / வைப்` exchange is a mandatory wordplay checkpoint. Do not flatten it into ordinary prose. The batch review must explicitly record how the English preserves the joke/explanation and the relationship to English “wife”.

For `ஒரு கடாரம் பத்திருக்கு`, do not infer or “correct” an intended phrase. Translate only what the verified Tamil supports; if the semantic value remains opaque in English, retain a conservative transliteration/literal handling and document it.
