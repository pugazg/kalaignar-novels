# 12

Dukkaram stood holding his bicycle as Ambalam came close and said coaxingly, “I have an important matter to discuss with you!”

“An important matter—with me? Ada-de! This is the first time in my life I've seen a man like this. What important matter is it, ayya?” Dukkaram asked with a foolish grin.

“Are we going to talk standing in the street? Come, let's go to the shop!”

<!-- source: scan 110 → 111 -->

“Is it something that will take hours to discuss? I have urgent work!”

“Nothing that big! I only thought we could sit in the shop and talk—that's all!”

“All right. Which village are you from?”

“I live in Marungappallam!”

“What—Marungappallam?”

A new excitement overflowed in Dukkaram's heart. He already knew that Marungappallam was the native village of Parimalam, who had captured his heart. Once he heard that Ambalam too came from her village, he built castles in his mind that he could gather plenty of information about her. Perhaps Parimalam herself had finally softened and sent this man as her messenger, he imagined, and became delighted. Then he felt a little afraid: what if this man were Parimalam's father? It would not be surprising if he had come in person to punish Dukkaram for the mischievous way he had behaved toward her. These waves of thought left him silent for a while.

“What, thambi, why are you standing without speaking?” Ambalam asked.

“Nothing—come, let's go to the shop,” Dukkaram said, taking Ambalam with him. Once seated, he eagerly waited to see what sort of words would fall from Ambalam's mouth. He was now pleased that all his friends had already left.

<!-- source join: scan 111 → 112 -->

“Hey, boy! Bring two glasses of tea!”

“No, thambi—I don't have the habit of drinking tea!” said Ambalam.

“Have coffee then—hey, bring coffee!”

“Ayyo—coffee? Uh-huh!”

“All right! Soda!—boy—go!”

“I won't even touch soda!”

“Cash—?”

“Why this unnecessary expense—I don't like those sweet waters—money wasted for nothing!”

“Have badam kheer then—”

“Ada-da—you don't seem willing to let me go—fine—why should I spoil your wish?”

“Go get badam kheer, boy!” Dukkaram said as he opened his purse to take out money. There was only one anna or perhaps two annas in it. Without letting that show, he said, “Oh-ho! Is it a hundred-rupee note? All right—all right, here! Go get change and bring badam kheer!” and tossed the purse closed to the boy. Understanding his master's trick, the boy ran off with the purse. He was happy to have gained a few hours' freedom to wander around town.

<!-- source join: scan 112 → 113 -->

After the boy left, Dukkaram looked at Ambalam and showed on his face how eager he was to learn the matter.

“Do you know anyone in Marungappallam?” Ambalam asked. Dukkaram hesitated to answer immediately, then composed himself and said, “Yes—I do!”

“The person you know—a man or a woman?”

“Why this new question now—who do you think it ought to be?”

“I thought sixteen cubits—”

“No—wrong! These days it's only twelve cubits!” Dukkaram said and laughed.

“You're a mischievous fellow—that's why that girl is lying there smitten!”

Dukkaram lowered his head shyly. Concluding that some sweet message had arrived from Parimalam, he could hardly contain his happiness.

“Did Parimalam send some message?” he blurted out.

“Ada-sakkai! You've said the name yourself, thambi! Hmm—does love have eyes? Whoever it is, without asking who they are or what they are, just speaking away—that's what I've heard is the nature of love. In our days there was no love or brinjal or any of this—only now do we hear about such things!” Ambalam said.

<!-- source join: scan 113 → 114 -->

“What are you saying! Was there no love in your time? Love existed even before your great-grandfather's time! I saw the Valli Thirumanam play only yesterday—Valli and Murugan drank love by the handful—ada, ada! There's a love song in it. Listen!” Overcome with delight, Dukkaram began singing.

He started with the song “Kaayaatha Kaanagathae!” and rolled out musical flourishes: “The deer that has not grazed! The spotted deer that has not joined! My deer that has not grazed!”

“So that's it; he's half-mad with love,” Ambalam thought, while enjoying the song very much.

“Thambi, with one song you've explained that love has been ruling the world for a very long time—turning men mad and women crazy!”

“Only a song? Here, listen to some dialogue!

“O Kuravalli! Do not cut the love you have accepted into pieces! You who sing aalolam in the millet field—this hunter longs to turn you into a mother who sings a lullaby! Moon of love! Star of desire! Sun of pleasure! Sky of affection! Lamp of attachment! Do not betray me! Do not be coy!”

<!-- source: scan 114 → 115 -->

“Enough, thambi, enough! It looks as though you'll conduct an entire kaalakshepam on love!”

“What objection can there be? Here, listen to the kaalakshepam too!—Chandran, having been enchanted by the lotus-face of Tara, forgot that she was his guru's wife and set out to unite with her. In the same way, Tara forgot that Chandran was her husband's disciple and consented to unite with him. Tara's beauty surpassed even Rathi! Chandran's beauty surpassed even Manmathan! While the two were delighting in pleasure, the marks left by the kisses Tara Devi gave are the dark patches still seen on the moon's face today. Those who do not understand the greatness of love call them blemishes. One might say that only to Tara's husband is the blackness on Chandran's face a blemish. Thus Chandran and Tara entered into sinful conduct and became subject to the guru's curse.”

“Bale, thambi—bale! It looks as though you'll write a whole Ramayana about love!”

“I've written that too—here!” Dukkaram opened a box, took out a large notebook and spread it before Ambalam.

“The title of this story is ‘The World of Pleasure, or Two Pairs of Doves.’ It has been a week since I finished writing it. The heroine's name is Parimala Gandhi.”

<!-- source join: scan 115 → 116 -->

“Oh-ho—you used that very name.........”

“Yes—as a memorial!—every incident in this story is nothing but terror! Nothing but love! Nothing but mystery! Nothing but shock! Nothing but happiness! Nothing but infatuation! Nothing but sorrow! Nothing but speed!”

“Nothing but thirst!”

“Hmm—that too!”

“Oh-ho! Here, I'll give it—ada-de, the fellow who went to buy badam kheer is nowhere to be seen—” Ambalam handed him water and Dukkaram opened the notebook again. “The special thing about this Inbalogam story is that even after the two lovers die, their author does not leave them alone. That is, I am the author! What I've done is this: the two dead lovers go to the upper world and live happily!—a villain comes there too—there too there are quarrels and fights—I arranged it like this—and finally Parama Sivan intervenes, sends the villain to hell and brings the two lovers together. That's how the story ends.”

“A good ending, thambi!”

“One of my friends read this story and is dying to translate it into English—I'm hesitating to send it to the press because I can't decide whether to publish it first in Tamil or in English.”

<!-- source: scan 116 → 117 -->

“By God's grace, let it come out in print.”

“There's a passage here—shall I read it to you?”

“We can read it leisurely later, thambi—first let me speak about the reason I came.”

“What matter?—tell me.”

“You—you have to come with me to our village.”

“To Marungappallam?”

“Yes!”

“Why—for what?”

“Your affair with Parimalam—her father has come to know about it!”

“Ayyo!”

“Don't be afraid—that girl has given you her very life—”

“Ah—what doubt is there about that!”

“I've somehow pacified her father and kept him quiet. Once he sees you, he'll say all right. I stand in the relation of maternal uncle to Parimalam—I have great affection for that girl! I can't act against her wishes. If you come to the village, we can arrange the marriage somehow—”

“What did Parimalam say?”

“Wasn't she the one who gave me your whole address and told me to bring you?”

<!-- source: scan 117 → 118 -->

Dukkaram could not hide his happiness. He prepared to leave for Marungappallam with Ambalam.

* * * *

Dukkaram and Ambalam reached the boundary of Marungappallam village.

“The village is very beautiful,” Dukkaram said.

“Why wouldn't the village be beautiful! Isn't there a parrot living here!” Ambalam teased. Dukkaram blushed like a girl. They were walking a little distance from the Siva temple of Marungappallam. Just then Ambalam suddenly had the urgent thought that he should draw some clue from Dukkaram; after all, he was a man driven by desire. So he asked, “Thambi, have you seen that temple over there?” Without any change of expression, Dukkaram replied, “I've seen any number of temples like this.” Ambalam decided that he must not spoil his purpose by cross-examining him any further, and took Dukkaram toward his own house.
