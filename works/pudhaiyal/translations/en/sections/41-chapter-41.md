# 41

It could not be said that the sun had finished its duty and hidden itself in the west to rest from weariness—a part of the revolving world had turned away from the sun's gaze. Darkness came because of that.

Marungappallam was only an atom in that part of the world. In the little village covered by darkness, the movement of people had, as usual, diminished. The lotus tank, lying like a lake opposite the Sivan temple, struggled in vain to display its beauty to anyone. The lamp in the sky had not yet been lit. Even if it were lit, the clouds stood arrayed as though they had taken a vow to hide it. Nothing could be heard except the sound of kendai fish leaping from the lotus tank and falling back into the water, the “songs” of the frogs, and the sound of pigeons making love with a ‘kudugudu’ murmur in the niches of the Sivan temple's short gopuram facing the tank.

A man could be seen walking along the street opposite the temple. That man was none other than old Mayandi himself. He walked rapidly towards the temple. There was a spade in his hand.

He went down the bathing steps of the tank and washed his face, hands and feet. He sat on the platform like a raised veranda opposite the temple and looked all around him. Then, gazing at the dark sky, he sank into thought. A lamp flickering ‘minuk, minuk’ at the temple entrance advertised the confusion and expectant eagerness filling his face.

His attention, deep in thought, was caught by the sudden sound of a cough in the darkness. He looked around. He walked this way and that. No one could be seen. Watching every one of his movements, Velliyambalam and about thirty men were sitting on top of the gopuram. One of them had suddenly coughed—and earned himself a swelling on the head from Velliyambalam's rough hand.

Mayandi walked towards the bank of the tank. Without lifting his face to stare at the gopuram, he looked as though he were looking elsewhere.

Velliyambalam and his men stood motionless, holding their breath like figures on a temple tower. Mayandi returned to the temple entrance and went inside. He stood near the Nandi mandapam opposite the main deity. From there he stared at the Saneeswaran mandapam in the lower corner. He began to sing aloud.

“Between the one who rises and the one who bows—in the shadow of the crow's beak—on the path where thieves enter—even if found, in the Kammalar's eye……!”

He sang the verse with great feeling. He looked once at the crow vehicle on which Saneeswaran sat. By then Ambalam and his men had moved to another side of the gopuram and begun closely watching Mayandi's actions. Mayandi started digging the earth in front of the Nandi mandapam with the spade he had brought.

Ambalam kept watching eagerly. Mayandi kept digging. His mouth continued murmuring the song, “Between the one who rises and the one who bows……” A deep pit had been dug.

Mayandi took a copper plate from the fold of his garment and placed it at the edge of the pit. As though seized by frenzy, he chanted the ‘one who rises, one who bows’ song and began to leap about. Again he raised the spade. He dug the earth furiously. There was a ‘dang’ sound!

“Aha! I found it! I found it!” he cried, dropping the spade and beginning to push the earth aside with his hands. There was a small pot-like vessel. He took it out and turned it upside down.

From the top of the gopuram, Ambalam watched with intense eagerness. Seeing that nothing fell from the vessel, Mayandi examined it with the help of a match. Inside it was a rolled metal plate. He took it out excitedly and read it. Again he peered at the plate by matchlight. “Aha! So that's the matter? I am a lucky man! Victory! Victory!!” he shouted. He filled the pit back with earth and left the temple carrying the vessel, the plates and the spade.

Ambalam's group climbed down from the gopuram. They followed the old man. Mayandi glanced back indirectly to see whether anyone was following him, then walked behind the temple. There stood the image of a deity—a terrifying form—a pavilion built like a raised shrine, and opposite it a mango tree. Mayandi went beneath that mango tree.

“This is where the gold was buried! The clue to seventy crores of pure gold is right here! O hidden golden jewel! O guide who has come to give life to this poor man! The time has come to see you! Joy has entered my life—now my worries are gone!” he cried, dancing as he looked at the earth. Holding in his hand the plate he had taken from inside the temple, he used a match to note various directions. He measured the ground with his feet. He measured ten feet east and ten feet north. With heroic excitement and a victorious smile, he lifted the spade and raised it over the earth. Before the raised spade could strike the ground, Velliyambalam rushed forward and seized Mayandi. Ambalam's men surrounded him. Mayandi stood stunned.

“Aha! What is this? Am I seeing a terrible dream? Where am I now? Am I in the world of dreams? Where else could I be subjected to such enormous, cruel suffering? Ayyo! Will I not wake from this sleep?” Mayandi cried.

“What are you babbling about, old man? Where have you come? What work are you doing?” Ambalam barked.

“What does it matter to anyone where I go? I am a madman! I will go anywhere!”

“What business does a madman have in a temple?”

“Where else would a madman have business?”

“Why does a madman need a spade? What is this plate in your hand?”

“Ayyo, leave me alone—I'll go.”

“Old man! Don't die for nothing! I know very well where you have come and what you are doing. You came to take the treasure—isn't that the truth?”

“What—treasure?”

“Are you pretending to be surprised? Don't get beaten to death—if you tell the truth, you'll live! Otherwise your corpse will float in that lotus tank over there! Be careful!”

“Appada! Such a great punishment? Ambalam, don't do anything like that! I still want to live happily for another thirty or forty years.”

“Then spit out the truth! You came to take the treasure, didn't you?”

“Yes!”

“Give me that plate!”

Mayandi handed the plates in his possession to Ambalam and began speaking with a tremble:

“Ambalam! This is no small amount—there are seventy crores of pure gold! Even I could not manage all of it. Let us take half each. If you give me my life and half the gold, that is enough. I will tell you every secret. There was that fellow Durai. He said he would come as my companion, but I don't know where the man disappeared to—the worthless young fellow! Look, this plate here is the first doorway to finding the treasure! With its help I took this second plate from inside the temple. According to the directions on it, we must now dig this place. The treasure may or may not be here. But that plate will certainly show us the seventy crores of pure gold.”

“Is that so? I'll have them dig right here! Hey—you men, begin!” Ambalam ordered.

With the men's furious speed, the earth there was dug very quickly. Ambalam and Mayandi stood listening for any sound.

There was a great ‘danaar’ clang. The men cleared away the earth and lifted a large box from the pit. An old brass box! Ambalam tried to open it. It would not open. He broke it with an axe. Inside that box was another, smaller box. He opened that too. Inside it was a rolled metal plate. Ambalam unrolled it. Mayandi read it.

“Dig a hundred gaz from here—you will get seventy crores of pure gold!” was written in large letters. In smaller letters were written boundary indications for the place that had to be dug. Lines had been drawn showing direction and measure.

“What shall we do?” Ambalam asked.

“This is not something we can think through and settle before this night is over. Let us try tomorrow,” Mayandi replied.

“What if you run away somewhere?” Ambalam asked suspiciously.

“How can I run? Would I run away leaving all these plates behind? If only Ambalam does not cheat me, that is enough, Lord!” Mayandi sighed.

“All right, let us go home,” they said, and everyone left the place.
