# 33

Velliyambalam cleared his throat and began to speak slowly and deliberately. Cunning plans were forming one after another in his mind, and he was excessively careful in his speech for fear that a wrong word might slip out.

“Parimalam! Listen carefully. The police have obtained strong evidence. The place where they found the severed hand is Durai's house, so there is hardly any way for Durai to get out easily. If Durai and old Mayandi are to be released on bail and then freed from the case itself, Dukkaram must be handed over. I am going to tell the police that I personally saw Dukkaram commit the murder. You too must testify to that. If instead you show sympathy toward Dukkaram, danger will come upon us.”

Parimalam did not know what to answer. She stood silent.

“Ayyo, poor Dukkaram!” she grieved inwardly.

“Silence is a sign of consent. I will go and return,” Ambalam said. He called Raman, who was standing in the street, and warned him, “Go inside and attend to whatever they need. Be careful.” Then he hurried away to meet the police.

Parimalam's mind would not remain steady. She had used Dukkaram's name so that by naming Durai she would not place him in danger with Ambalam. Now she stood bewildered, unable to understand how to rescue Dukkaram from this terrible conspiracy.

It was true that, one day while she was going to school along a street in Pattukkottai, Dukkaram had behaved indecently toward Parimalam. It was no surprise that from that day onward she had become angry with him. Nor could it be denied that Dukkaram's antics had caused that anger to grow day by day.

In that anger, and in order for herself and her lover to escape, she had handed him over. But now he stood at the threshold of death. She had to save him.

To save him, she would have to forget her concern for her beloved. Not merely the concern—she might even be forced into a position where she had to forget her beloved himself. Parimalam's heart became a forest of confusion.

That confusion itself caused sympathy and affection for Dukkaram to begin rising in her. Whenever he grinned foolishly at her, she began to suffer at the thought of the hanging rope suspended above his head.

Even when he bared his teeth and called, “Parimalam!” she no longer snapped at him as she had the previous night. She began answering simply, “Why?”

Seeing Parimalam's softness and yielding manner, Dukkaram once again saw a star of hope. At dawn she had told him in order to deceive him, “Give me two days. I will think about whether I can fulfil your wish.” Now he thought she might finish that period of consideration that very day. He rejoiced inwardly.

Parimalam answered everything he asked without agitation. Dukkaram danced with happiness. She looked at him as one looks at a sacrificial goat standing garlanded before the offering.

Leaving them behind, Velliyambalam set out hurriedly for the Peravurani police station.

Durai and old Mayandi were being held in the lock-up at the Peravurani police station. Velliyambalam reached the station before the police had filed a charge sheet against them. The Peravurani Sub-Inspector already knew him, so he welcomed him courteously and asked, “What brings you here?”

“Would I come without something important?” Ambalam began with that preface.

“I have come to speak urgently with you about a murder case,” he said.

The Sub-Inspector listened with sudden attention. Ambalam behaved as though he knew nothing about Durai and Mayandi being in the lock-up.

“I had gone to the house of a man named Durai at Puduppattinam on some business. At that time a woman came running and rushed into the house. From the way she came screaming, I thought some grave danger was upon her. A young man ran behind her. A shining knife was in his hand. When we saw it, Durai and I came outside.

“‘Ayyo, save me!’ the woman screamed.

“‘Don't make a noise,’ the young man shouted at her. Then he looked at us and threatened, ‘If you come inside, you will lose your lives!’

“We did not know what to do. The woman cried, ‘Dukkaram! Dukkaram! Leave me alone!’ From that we understood that his name was Dukkaram. And when he warned, ‘Parimalam! Don't make a noise!’ we understood that her name was Parimalam.

“At that moment a rough-looking bearded holy man came running there. He looked at us and asked in agitation, ‘Sir! Did a villain chase a woman this way?’

“We pointed inside and said, ‘Yes.’

“‘You sinners! Are you just standing and watching?’ the holy man scolded us and ran inside. We too ran in. He and Dukkaram began fighting. During that moment Parimalam escaped from there and ran outside.”

<!-- source scans 291-292 / printed 287-288: `காப்` + `பாத்துங்க!` is continuous inside the quoted “save me!” across the physical page boundary. -->

The Sub-Inspector sighed. “Yes. That was when we saw her in the street.”

“Listen to the whole thing,” Ambalam said, continuing.

“Even after Parimalam ran away, the fight between the holy man and Dukkaram did not stop. Suddenly the holy man ran toward the backyard. Dukkaram followed him and cut off his arm with a sickle. The arm fell severed to the ground and writhed there.

“Durai was terrified at the sight and ran away from the house somewhere. Such a coward he was. I went forward courageously and flung Dukkaram aside. He raised the knife in his hand. I managed to defend myself. At that moment the holy man whose arm had been cut off began running.

“Dukkaram ran after him through the darkness. I too followed, wanting to see what would happen. I lost the path they had taken. Walking along another route, I saw Parimalam running toward me.

“She told me all the details. Only then did I understand that she was the daughter of Thandavan from our village. She said Dukkaram had given her a drug while she slept at home and carried her away; that he had tried to force her to submit to his wishes; that she had behaved cunningly in order to escape him; and that when the two of them were passing through Puduppattinam a holy man became suspicious and came to rescue her. While Dukkaram and the holy man were quarrelling, she said, she suddenly entered the house.

“Afterward the two of us walked along the riverbank talking. We heard a cry—‘Ayyo! Ayyo!’ We hid and watched. Dukkaram was beating the one-armed holy man with a large stick. I thought of running closer. Parimalam stopped me.

“The battered holy man fell dead.

“‘Get lost, fellow! What is it to you if I bring a woman? You dog!’ Dukkaram shouted. He kicked the holy man and pushed him into a deep pit.

“Parimalam and I saw that too. Then Dukkaram left the place in a hurry. I told Parimalam, ‘You go home. Be careful!’ and followed him. Dawn came. He was coming toward Marungappallam.

“He seemed to be coming to look for her again. I thought, ‘This is the moment,’ and seized him. He struggled. I did not let go. A few men came running to help me. I took him to my house and have locked him there. If you come now, you can arrest that murderer. He is in my house.”

Ambalam finished his account.

The Sub-Inspector found it difficult not to believe these details.

“So that is what happened! I suspected Durai and the man called Mayandi and had them detained!” the Sub-Inspector lamented.

“Ayyo! Them? What a sin!” Ambalam cried, jumping up as though he knew nothing.

“Yes. After finding the hand, we tried to solve the case. Only this afternoon we arrested Durai and Mayandi.”

“Where did you arrest them?”

“At their house.”

“What did they say?”

“They said nothing. They are maintaining silence. I too did not force them to answer anything. They are in the lock-up. We have not even filed the charge sheet yet!”

“Ayyo, then release them! Release them and come with me. I will hand over the real murderer right now.”

“All right! I will release them at once.”

Durai and old Mayandi were released. Velliyambalam addressed Durai as though he had known him before. “What, brother! How did you get caught like this? If I had not come, that would have been the end of it!”

Durai stood without replying.

“What, Mayandi! You too got caught? What a pity!” Velliyambalam said, looking at the old man. Mayandi too stood with his head bowed, absorbed in thought.

Velliyambalam took Durai and Mayandi aside and told Durai, “We will talk about everything later. First, you leave this place! You may have to appear as a witness in this case; that is all. Parimalam has been kept in a safe place. Come to Manora tomorrow night at twelve. I will hand Parimalam over to you there!”

After sending them away, he returned to the Sub-Inspector and said, “We can leave, sir.” The Sub-Inspector took two or three police constables with him and set out with Ambalam.

* * * *

Parimalam sat in anguish at the thought that Dukkaram was about to fall into police hands. Was there no way to save him? A storm began to blow violently through her young heart.

Her beloved in prison! Innocent Dukkaram caught in a spider's web! What way was there to free them both? The questions turned into arrows of pain and pierced her. She wept inwardly, thinking that no woman could ever have faced a stage so full of crisis and trial.

“Some fellow—some helpless fool living a purposeless life! For his sake, should I sacrifice the man who regards me as dearer than life? So many happy dreams—so many plans for the future—must all of them be destroyed?” The question rose. Parimalam wept silently.

“My beloved at least treats life as a mere straw. But this poor fellow treats life as though it were sweet sugarcane! What wrong has he done that deserves death? What do I gain by handing him over? I will get my lover—I will triumph in love! But should I not sacrifice love itself to save an innocent man's life?” Again the question pierced her.

“Even if I hand Dukkaram over and save Durai, who knows what danger Velliyambalam may still be keeping in store for Durai?” Confusion surged within her.

“Perhaps the charge against my beloved may not even be proved without proper witnesses. Even if there is testimony, he may receive only a prison term. After a few years there will still be a way for me to join him. But if Dukkaram is caught, his very life will be cut short!”

Parimalam struggled within herself—should she save the helpless man? Should she sacrifice her love? Or should she betray her lover?

She had to choose one of those three very soon. In the end she arrived at the decision to sacrifice her love.
