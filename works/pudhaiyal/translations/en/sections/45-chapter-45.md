# 45

As though to deepen Parimala's astonishment, Shokasundari suddenly disappeared. In the place where she had stood, Dukkaram now stood in men's clothing. He had cast off the Shokasundari disguise, in keeping with his usual clowning nature, to mock the fortune-teller's astrology and skill; but Parimala was in no state to enjoy any of it.

“Who? Dukkaram?” she blurted out in agitation.

“How do you know my name?” Dukkaram glared at the fortune-teller.

“I know—how could I not know you? Isn't it the trouble that came in your name that caused all this?” Parimala complained to herself.

Unable to understand who stood before him, Dukkaram grew a little frightened and asked suspiciously, “Could you perhaps be from the detective department?”

“No!” Parimala said, and removed her disguise too.

They stood looking at one another with the indestructible affection of brother and sister. Tears streamed from Dukkaram's eyes. Parimala's eyes became waterfalls. Standing at the edge of a silence that could not be described, they conveyed their pain through their eyes. Parimala's lips slowly moved.

“Dukkaram! How did you come here?”

“I escaped from jail.”

“Ayyo—that is a serious crime!”

“Let it be. If the first crime is tried and a judgment is given, I will probably get the death sentence. What punishment can they give for the crime of escaping from jail? I know for certain: no judge, whoever he is, can give one man two death sentences! So I am not worried about that.”

“Why did you have to escape from jail?”

“There's nothing surprising about a man in jail wanting to escape! But Parimala, I did not escape because I wanted to save my life! I wanted to see you once before I died. That is why I ran away from jail. I am going to die anyway. I cannot escape the death sentence! Sister, the suffering I went through because I wanted to see your blessed face before then was no small thing! I crossed so many dangers and escaped.

“That day you suggested setting the house on fire so that I could get out. But I did not listen to you; I rolled your body, precious as gold, on the ground and surrendered to the policemen, didn't I? From that day I was shut in the police station for five or six days. During those days I felt only that I had performed some great sacrifice. That was because I alone was kept there by myself.

“Afterwards they transferred me from there to the big jail. They locked me up in the name of remand. The big jail had all sorts of big shots. Not only Dukkaram, who repairs bicycles—even some proprietors who manufacture bicycles were inside, involved in fraud cases. The fear I had carried in my mind about jail disappeared.

“The one hardship in jail that can torment a human being is loneliness. But that very loneliness gave me happiness. Even if I had remained outside, this same loneliness would have been mine. Once it had been settled that you were not mine, if a millionaire's daughter came, or even a Rathi looking like a Ravi Varma picture, would I think of living with her as my partner? The very goal of my life was Parimala! She became my younger sister. After that, whenever I see any woman, she appears to me only as a sister. That is why the loneliness of jail gave me so much strength.

“They said the entire work of putting together the case registered against me was not yet complete. So the stage of going to court had not yet come. Prisoner friends and warders who asked about my case had already pronounced their own judgment: I would certainly receive either the death sentence or imprisonment for life.

“I argued with one man in jail that there was no eyewitness to my having hacked someone to death!

“He explained to me in a way I could understand what ‘witnesses’ meant. Why waste time on all that story!

“Thoughts about the death sentence slowly began peeping into my heart while I was living in loneliness. During the very period when I was on remand, three or four men were hanged at dawn and their sentences carried out. The cries they made as they were taken to the gallows shook me.

“‘Ayyo!…… In just a little while the world itself will disappear! I won't be able to see anything!’ one man shouted.

“‘Amma—my little sister!…… They're going to kill your brother…… Ayyo, can't I see my sister just once before I die?’

“Another man went on shouting like that as they took him away. That word ‘little sister’ split my heart open. Parimala!…… It was that word that awakened the desire to see you once. Court—case—judgment—hanging—while I kept thinking of all these, an unending desire to see you just once began sprouting in my heart. We are going to die anyway. Let them add punishment for escaping too, I decided. Fear vanished.

“I pretended to have terrible stomach pain and rolled on the floor. That is the one illness doctors cannot discover quickly!

“When I was a little boy at school, I used that same method to fool father and mother and avoid going to school. If I claimed fever, they could touch the body and understand. Even for a headache they would look to see whether a vein was bulging. But false stomach pain cannot be detected quickly. My trick succeeded.

“They took me to the hospital. Treatments were given. There was strong police guard too, all around the bed! For two or three days they kept their eyes open; then they sat by my bed and slept. I decided this was the moment and left. I escaped. I was desperate to see you somehow. I also learned that the police were chasing me. I became Shokasundari.

“I searched for you all over the village. All I learned was that your lover Durai had been trapped here. After deciding that I would not be able to see you at all, I came here wondering whether I could at least do something to help the man you love! And after coming here, I have had darshan of the goddess I wandered everywhere seeking.”

Saying this, Dukkaram took her hands and pressed them to his eyes. Not even an atom of stain showed itself in that moment. Purity had spread out like a climbing vine.

“Dukkaram! To fill the enormous belly of some greedy demon, all of us have been driven to turn our lives into a desert,” Parimala sobbed. But new plans and thoughts moving with the speed of a storm were whirling in her heart. A sense of satisfaction dawned in her that, for the treasure, she too could give her life alongside Durai! How?

If Dukkaram were turned into the kudukuduppai fortune-teller, he would gain his freedom. That unfortunate woman did not know that Ambalam's lawless eyes had fallen on the fortune-teller too! Keeping in mind the sacrifice Dukkaram had made for her, she decided, in return, to send him out in the fortune-teller's disguise. Dukkaram too agreed to put on the disguise. Even if Parimala had said nothing, once he knew that the fortune-teller was Parimala, he himself had thought of taking on that disguise.

He knew that if he became the fortune-teller, another murder case would fall upon him! Though he knew it, he agreed. Why? If Parimala remained as Bhagavathi, the “crime of Menaka's stabbing” would be placed upon her. Dukkaram decided that it was his duty to save her from that. So he did not tell her very much about Ambalam's plan to betray Bhagavathi. Parimala, in turn, did not reveal to him the dreadful conspiracy by which, had he remained Shokasundari, he would have been offered as a sacrifice. If she had revealed it, Dukkaram would not have agreed to remove Parimala's disguise and take on the Kudukuduppandi disguise himself. He would have remained Shokasundari. According to Parimala's plan, Dukkaram became Malayalam Bhagavathi. Parimala remained Parimala. The consolation that she was going to die together with Durai gave her new courage.

Dukkaram did not realize that Ambalam's plan required an unmarried woman to be offered as the sacrifice. Had he realized it, he would never have removed Parimala's Bhagavathi disguise.

Thinking he was helping her, he unknowingly prepared her for something that would cost her life! She too, thinking she was freeing him, somehow and unknowingly became the cause of his being caught in a murder case for the second time.

As though waiting to swallow both of them and belch, Ambalam sat beside Menaka's bed. Menaka's life was leaving her little by little. Ambalam waited for it to leave completely and for the body to grow cold. Menaka, who while dreaming pleasant dreams about the future had talked to herself about a thousand plans, became unable to utter a single word. Speech subsided—the last breath too mingled with the air. Menaka was gone. She who had appeared like a flower in full youth, like a crystal statue, would henceforth be kissed only by the earth!

When Ambalam saw Menaka close her eyes, he cried. Yes, he cried, displaying that he had affection for her. On Ambalam's silken bed lay the corpse he himself had made. As he had sent word through Raman, the police arrived. Ambalam gave them the details of Menaka's murder. The police began searching for where the murderer was now.

At once Ambalam shouted to his men, “Drag here that sinner who killed my darling!” In front of the police he kept singing the lament for Menaka's corpse without missing a note. Realizing that the thugs were opening the door, Parimala hid in a corner of the room. Dukkaram took leave of her and departed. He knew that he was going back to prison!

Parimala, however, believed that by her cleverness she had set him free, and happily bade him farewell. The tears of joy she shed then, he somehow took only for tears of sorrow.

The sub-inspector looked the fortune-teller up and down when he came outside. In his haste, Ambalam did not even realize that the fortune-teller's disguise had changed hands. Handcuffs were placed on Dukkaram.

With the resolve and enthusiasm that he was sacrificing himself for his Parimala for a second time, he followed behind the police.

Parimala, unaware that Dukkaram had been handed over to the police, breathed a deep sigh of joy. Now all her thoughts were about when she and Durai would be killed together.

Ambalam too, after having Menaka's corpse burned to ashes, turned his attention to the arrangements for Durai's wedding. He was impatient: only if the wedding was completed quickly could the sacrifice be made and the treasure obtained without delay. The rage he felt towards Durai over the matter of Menaka had driven him into an urgency to sacrifice him quickly and take his revenge.
