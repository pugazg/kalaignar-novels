# 29

Imagining that the ghost was about to swallow him whole and belch afterward, Dukkaram screamed, “Leave me alone!”

“Not only you. I have caught your beloved Parimalam too. I am going to reduce both of you to ashes now,” the voice threatened.

“Ayyo, my dear Parimalam! Have you been caught too? Ghost! Have mercy. Let my beloved go. The two of us will run away somewhere. We will never come to this place again,” Dukkaram pleaded.

“I will let both of you go. But on one condition,” came the rough voice.

“Whatever the condition, I agree. Hmm, I know what condition you are going to impose. You will say that the two of us must marry and must not carry on a secret love like this. Impose it! Impose it!” Dukkaram said, gathering a little courage.

Before he could finish saying “Impose it,” a stick landed on his back with a loud crack.

<!-- source scans 254-255 / printed 250-251 -->

“Ayyo!” Dukkaram cried, jumping up. A faint hope that he might escape had arisen in him, but he felt it had been destroyed in the womb itself.

“Hey, human insect! Listen to the condition, you fool!”

“I am listening, Swami, I am listening. Tell me!”

“Where is the gold? Tell me at once!”

“What is this, a geography examination? Gold is found in Kolar.”

“Whom are you trying to fool? Don't die for nothing. Tell the truth!”

“Ayyo! Truly, it is Kolar!”

“What?”

Another hard blow landed on Dukkaram's back.

“Amma! What trouble is this! How did I get caught like this? Sir—ghost! Let me go, Swami! If you want, keep Parimalam yourself. Just give me my life. I will run away,” Dukkaram cried, falling to the ground and prostrating himself.

“You cannot deceive me. Tell me properly: the gold is here—I know that. Show me precisely where it is!”

<!-- source scan 255 / printed 251 -->

“Gold or iron—I know nothing!”

“Lies! Lies!! You know very well where the gold has been buried. Tell me! Otherwise I will strip the skin from your back and make sandals from it.”

“Oh-ho-ho! Are you a cobbler ghost? Swami! Swami! If you want sandals, I will buy you a thousand pairs. Don't strip my skin for nothing!”

“I won't stop with the skin. I will break your legs too. I will pluck out your eyes like fruit. I will twist off your head like a palmyra fruit. What do you say? Will you show me where the gold is?”

“What useless trouble is this! What do you mean by gold? Gold used to make bangles—or a woman who wears bangles?”

“Gold! Gold!! It is hidden in this earth. That is what I command you to show me.”

“I understand—I understand. You have invented some excuse to put the blame on me, kill me and eat me! Look here! Don't drag me into trouble like this. I lost my way in some madness of love and came here. Let me go. If you can, give me Parimalam too. If not, at least let me go alone!”

“Very well. Your time has come. I shall have to put you into my stomach—”

<!-- source scan 256 / printed 252 -->

The instant he heard those words, Dukkaram decided that whatever happened, happened, and began to run.

“Where are you running?” someone shouted, and a hand clamped onto his shoulder. He struggled against it and tried to run. A blow crashed onto his head. He endured that too and ran. The rough hand seized his shirt tightly.

Dukkaram stopped motionless, feeling as though he were already dead. Convinced that the ghost stood before him, he squeezed his eyes shut.

“Hey, Dukkaram! Do you know who I am? Look! Open your eyes and look!”

The voice frightened him even more. If death was certain anyway, he thought, he might as well look once at Parimalam, who was in the ghost's hands, before dying. With that resolve, Dukkaram slowly opened his eyes. Squinting through doubt, he peered hard at the figure before him.

The ghost he had imagined was a mountain of flesh, with a belly like a great pot, a mouth like a cave and arms like tree trunks.

But what he saw now was entirely different. He looked carefully. The terrifying figure looming in the darkness was not a ghost at all—it was Velliyambalam. Dukkaram's deep sigh showed that he had reached that conclusion. Yet the terror he felt on seeing Velliyambalam was greater than the terror the word “ghost” had caused him.

<!-- source scans 257-258 / printed 253-254: `வெள்ளியம்` + `பலம்` is continuous across the physical page boundary. -->

Why had Velliyambalam frightened him like this? Why had he shouted at him to show where the gold was? Why had he treated him with such cruelty? Dukkaram wondered.

Only after he understood that some murderer had deceived him by using Parimalam's name did his older memories begin to return—the memories that Parimalam did not love him.

“I wondered even then why this man was working so hard to make love grow between Parimalam and me. Only now do I understand! Ayyo, clarity arrives at the very moment my life is leaving!”

The pitiable young man lamented inwardly. Even Parimalam, had she seen his miserable condition directly, might have felt sorry and wondered why she had unnecessarily used him as a decoy.

“Sir! What is all this? I don't understand anything!” Dukkaram begged, looking at Velliyambalam.

“I told you I was a ghost, didn't I? That is true. I am the ghost guarding the treasure here.”

“Ayyo! Why are you setting off another thunderclap now, Swami? A ghost at night and a man by day?”

“Listen! You cannot act like a madman and make a fool of me. If you value your life, spit out the truth properly!”

<!-- source scan 258 / printed 254 -->

“How can I spit anything out when my stomach is empty? You speak as though all the truth inside me has given me indigestion!”

“Hey, chatterbox! Don't spin stories. Tell me where the gold has been buried without hiding it!”

“Curse my fate! Gold? Or a lump of earth? I understand nothing at all. God! What a trial—what suffering!”

“Look here—don't be afraid! I am asking nicely. Tell me. The two of us will join together and take the treasure. Without anyone knowing, we will keep it safe and become rich. Brother Dukkaram, the truth is with you; the labour is with me. If we knead truth and labour together and work in unity, there will be only two rich men in this whole world. One—you! The next—me! What do you say?”

“Sir—shall I tell you the truth?”

“Tell me, brother, tell me! Aren't you a good boy? Tell me, tell me!”

“Truly, I do not know even one truth, O Supreme One!”

“See? You are still wagging your tail! Hey, arrogant fellow—look what I have in my hand!”

Velliyambalam lifted a sickle used for slicing palmyra fronds. At the sight of it, Dukkaram stood terrified, his eyes nearly bulging out.

<!-- source scan 259 / printed 255 -->

“The treasure secret is with you. If you do not tell me, I will cut off your head right now. Hmm—tell me quickly!”

Dukkaram became certain that there was no way out. A tiny thought arose in his mind: perhaps trickery could be defeated with trickery. Deciding that he would at least try that, he began to act.

“Sir! Don't be angry. If my life is going to go, would I refuse to tell the truth? Here, I will tell you. The treasure secret is indeed with me.”

“Tell me! Tell me! Clever fellow! Tell me now!”

“I cannot tell you that!”

“Why?”

“It has to be given!”

“What do you mean?”

“Yes. That secret is with a holy man. Only he and I know the secret.”

“Hey—hey—are you lying? I heard with these very ears what you told your beloved: ‘The gold is buried under this mango tree.’”

Dukkaram understood nothing, but he managed to keep up the act. He realised that Velliyambalam had overheard someone else's conversation, had somehow dragged him into it by mistake and was now trying to take his life, all for the treasure supposed to be in that place. Once he understood that, deceiving Velliyambalam seemed easy.

“The treasure really is here. The mango-tree part is also correct. But there is one thing. If the treasure is to come out, a magic plate has to be placed there and a mantra recited. If you dig where the treasure lies without that magic plate, that is the end—the person will be done for,” Dukkaram poured out grandly.

“Where is that magic plate, brother?”

“That holy man has it!”

“Where is that holy man?”

“If I tell you, the spell will not work. Come with me. I will show him to you in person.”

“Good. Shall we go right now? Can we get the plate from him immediately?”

“Oh—certainly. We can get it. We can go right now. Come, let us go.”

Listening to Dukkaram, Velliyambalam began walking with him. In the darkness Dukkaram simply went wherever his feet happened to carry him.

<!-- source scans 260-261 / printed 256-257. Scan 261 ends at `விடிந்த`; the sentence continues on scan 262. -->

Dukkaram had devised this trick with one aim alone: make Velliyambalam walk behind him through the whole night, somehow use up the darkness, and once dawn came, escape.

Velliyambalam, not understanding the trick, followed with the thrill of believing that he was going to take the treasure, ignoring even the pain in his legs. Dukkaram walked along the shortcut from Marungappallam toward Puduppattinam; Velliyambalam followed, full of the proud thought that a sacrificial goat was walking before him.

They walked and walked and passed Puduppattinam too. Grumbling that the night seemed determined not to disappear quickly, Dukkaram kept putting one foot before the other.

A river crossed their path. Yes, it was the river between Puduppattinam and Rajamadam. They were walking along the riverbank when they saw a lone figure approaching from the opposite direction. They moved toward it.

It was the figure of a woman.

“It looks like Parimalam,” Dukkaram said.

“I can't see clearly. The shape does look like her,” Velliyambalam replied.
