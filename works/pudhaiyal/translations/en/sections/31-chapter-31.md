# 31

Parimalam recognised the one-armed man. He was not Durai, as she had feared, nor was he the old man. It was Ayyappan.

Ayyappan's chest was soaked in blood. Blood continued to pour from the severed arm. Dukkaram could not stop trembling. He was a warrior of warriors who could faint even at the fake blood a street magician beat a drum and displayed for amusement. What would it be like for him to see a truly horrifying scene like this in some deserted place? His whole body shook.

Yet even through that trembling, a brilliant idea flashed through his mind—an idea for escaping Velliyambalam. Neither he nor Velliyambalam knew who the mutilated man was. Dukkaram decided to make use of that fact and immediately began putting his extraordinary plan into action.

“Ah! Ayyo! You? My dear sir! Has this fate befallen you? I cannot bear it! I cannot bear it!” he wailed, jumping and crying in exaggerated grief.

Velliyambalam stared at him. “Who is this? Do you know him?” he asked eagerly.

“Who? Who is he? Ayyo! The pot has broken just as the butter was beginning to gather! He is the very man we came searching for. I was bringing you to him!” Dukkaram began to weep grandly.

Velliyambalam believed the tragic performance.

Until then he had regarded the wounded man with the indifference one might show some stranger lying hacked up. Suddenly a flood of concern surged through him. In this world there are eminent men who, even while loved ones who are far too young to die struggle for their final breath, boldly demand, “Sign this will!” Velliyambalam was one of those eminent men who wander about after removing their hearts and leaving them elsewhere.

How could he allow Ayyappan to die—without first obtaining the treasure secret?

So he ran to the river, soaked his upper cloth, came back to Ayyappan, squeezed water over his face, gently shook his body and checked whether even a little life remained clinging to him. Such was his concern for the man!

Dukkaram, meanwhile, was thinking that Ayyappan must not accidentally come back to life; if he did, Dukkaram himself would be in danger. To his alarm, Ayyappan slowly opened his eyes.

Because most of the blood in his body had drained through the severed arm, Ayyappan had no strength even to move.

<!-- source scans 272-273 / printed 268-269 -->

“He has opened his eyes. God, if only he opens his mouth a little too and tells the secret, that will be enough!” Velliyambalam prayed.

Ayyappan opened his parched mouth. His dry lips moved. His tongue, without moisture, seemed stuck and unable to move.

Thinking he was suffering from thirst, Velliyambalam squeezed into his mouth the water still held in the wet cloth. Ayyappan eagerly drank the water wrung through that dirty cloth.

His eyes brightened as if to say, “Ah!” He turned only his head and looked around.

“Ayyo! What disaster is about to strike me now?” Dukkaram agonised. Yet he managed himself and sat beside Ayyappan with an appearance of concern.

Ambalam could not contain his impatience.

“Sir! Sir! Where is the treasure secret?” he asked urgently.

Ayyappan stared wildly. Hiccups began in his throat.

“The wretch looks as if he is going to die!” Ambalam panicked. But without abandoning the attempt, he once again fired his question: “Where is the treasure secret?”

<!-- source scans 273-274 / printed 269-270; `மறு` + `படியும்` is continuous as `மறுபடியும்`. -->

“Treasure! Treasure!! Treasure secret!!!” Ayyappan began to babble.

“Yes—the treasure secret!” Velliyambalam insisted. He had surpassed even the man who asks for a light for his beedi while the house is burning.

Ayyappan was rambling at the mouth of death. For a moment consciousness would return and his eyes would open wide. He would try to say something. Then his memory would falter. Back and forth he suffered like this. Ignoring even that death agony, Ambalam burned with impatience to accomplish his purpose.

“Please tell me! Who has the treasure secret?”

Ayyappan opened his eyes and looked around. His gaze fell on Parimalam.

Grinding his teeth, he cried, “Her lover—her paramour—he has it...”

Before he could finish, Velliyambalam shook him by the shoulders. “Who? Dukkaram?”

“No! Durai! Durai! Durai! Her man—the actor Durai!”

“What? Durai? Parimalam's man is Durai? No—surely this Dukkaram here is the one!” Velliyambalam shouted.

<!-- source scan 274 / printed 270 -->

“No. It is Durai! That Durai and old Mayandi... me... me...” Ayyappan muttered something and closed his eyes.

Would Ambalam let him go? He searched for whatever life remained, dug it out, dragged it to Ayyappan's tongue and tried to make him speak.

“Leave all that. Where is the treasure secret now?”

“Here—ask her. She will tell you! That Mayandi—Durai—don't let those two go!”

Ayyappan could speak no more. The hiccups grew worse. Dukkaram stood motionless as though he had forgotten himself and renounced everything in grief. Ayyappan's body writhed like a worm. Twice he opened his mouth. Ambalam waited, hoping new truths might emerge.

Instead Ayyappan released his final breath and shut his mouth and eyes tightly.

Velliyambalam touched him and pursed his lips in disappointment. Dukkaram cried out loudly and began his lament: “Ayyo! What am I to do?”

Parimalam understood nothing. A chill seized her at the thought that, because of Ayyappan's statement, Velliyambalam might expose Durai and old Mayandi.

Velliyambalam's brain began working intensely.

“Parimalam says Dukkaram is her lover. The dead man says her lover is Durai.

“Dukkaram says the treasure secret was with the dead man. The dead man says that secret is with Durai and old Mayandi. Perhaps those two killed him and seized the secret. The secret must now be trapped with Durai and Mayandi. Fine. Then who is Parimalam's lover—Durai or Dukkaram? Perhaps both are her lovers. That must be it! Both her lovers must know where the gold is buried. I am confusing myself for nothing.

“In that case I must keep Parimalam and Dukkaram under my protection for a few days. By doing that I can discover whether Dukkaram and Parimalam really are lovers. And through Parimalam herself I must catch Durai. If that actor Durai and old Mayandi fall into my hands, then without any further confusion my desire will be fulfilled!”

That was how Velliyambalam reasoned. Then, having reached a decision, he looked at Parimalam.

“Parimalam, I understand you now. You have two lovers! One is Durai. The other is Dukkaram!”

“No—no! Only Dukkaram. I don't know Durai at all!” Parimalam replied.

“Leave that aside. We do not need witnesses for it now. You and Dukkaram must obey the order I give you!”

“What order?”

“I am taking both of you to my house without anyone knowing. There I am going to arrange your marriage in secret.”

Dukkaram did not rejoice at hearing this. He feared there was some trick in it.

“Don't be afraid, Dukkaram. I will not trouble you any more. Now that I know the treasure secret is with Durai, why should I make you suffer? Come back with me without fear,” Velliyambalam said.

“If I come to Marungappallam, Appa will see me,” Parimalam said.

“Don't worry about that. We can go to Marungappallam at night. The two of you can stay in my bungalow without any worry. You must agree to this!”

Believing that he would no longer be troubled, Dukkaram agreed.

Parimalam too had no choice but to accept Velliyambalam's proposal. Having already said that Dukkaram was her lover, if she now refused to marry him the truth would come out.

Velliyambalam was certain that if he somehow took Parimalam away with him, he could use her to catch Durai. He also believed that by arranging the marriage of Dukkaram and Parimalam he could discover whether they really were lovers.

<!-- source scans 277-278 / printed 273-274; `விட` + `லாமென்று` is continuous as `விடலாமென்று`. -->

“All right, come. Let us leave,” he said.

Parimalam thought that if Ayyappan's body were hidden, perhaps it might somehow help her beloved and the old man escape. She said to Ambalam, “Why don't we drag this body aside and throw it somewhere out of sight?”

Ambalam raised no objection. He dragged the corpse to a deep hollow nearby and threw it in.

Ayyappan, who had dreamed of digging treasure from the earth and building a fortress reaching to the sky, fell into a pit as a corpse.

Velliyambalam turned to them cheerfully. “Lovers! Set out! Now we know where the treasure secret is. The time has also come for you two to become newlyweds. Come!”

Dukkaram set off in ecstasy at the thought that he was going to marry Parimalam. Parimalam too had to follow them, wondering with dread what further sufferings might still await her.
