# 49

The moment Ambalam realized that Mayandi was nowhere to be seen, he hurried towards the house with his men. He was startled to see flames rising from that direction. Before he could ask, “What is that blaze?” two men came running towards him and cried, “Master! Master!! They've set our house on fire!” Ambalam began running in fury. The thugs ran with him. Raman alone was not especially agitated by the news. Shaking his head, he walked somewhat slowly.

Standing at the end of the street, Velliyambalam looked up at the house. It was burning like a chariot on fire. Tongues of flame seemed to be trying to touch the clouds. Ambalam stood motionless. All the villagers watched the burning house just as they had watched the street play. Only the voice of the bound Thandavan rang out in fury.

“Let it burn—let bridegroom and bride auspiciously burn to ashes! Velliyambalam's house is the proper cremation ground for the two corpses I hacked down! People of Marungappallam! Be ready and come tomorrow morning—milk must be sprinkled in this very cremation ground so that the souls of bridegroom and bride may find peace. If there are any priests, come on the morning of the sixteenth day from today! Dakshina will be given for the karumathi rites! Masons, you listen too—a memorial pavilion must be raised right here for the lovers who are going to be reduced to ash in the fire. Settle your wages with Ambalam even now!…… Hahaha! Hey, Ambalam! You opposed me and arranged my daughter's marriage—were you able to finish it?…… Look there—your house is burning merrily!”

Could Ambalam stand there listening to Thandavan? Those words were fiercer than the fire itself. With all his strength he struck Thandavan across the face. The blow burst Thandavan's bonds apart. He sprang at Ambalam. The two rolled in the street. The thugs surrounded Thandavan and attacked him.

Freeing himself from Thandavan, Ambalam turned furiously on the villagers. He thought they were merely standing there watching instead of coming forward to put out the fire.

“Drive every one of these village fellows away!” he ordered his men. The thugs sprang upon the villagers. Ambalam himself no longer knew what to do or what to say.

“In whose house have you hidden Mayandi? Tell me properly!” he shouted hoarsely.

No one answered him. He was already enraged that Mayandi had deceived him; with the burning house added to it all, he ordered every house searched until old Mayandi was found. The thugs needed no further invitation. They surrounded the houses. Pots and vessels were hurled outside. Roofs were ripped apart. Children were thrown aside. Women were treated degradingly. The whole village was thrown into chaos. Ambalam's men delighted in battering everyone without distinction, old or young.

Unable to resist Ambalam's men, the people fled in every direction. Seeing their chance, the wastrels among the thugs rejoiced in plundering the chastity of unmarried women on whom they had already cast their eyes. Ambalam encouraged his men as though he wanted Marungappallam turned into a field of corpses. His thought was that no one should remain in Marungappallam to speak of the humiliation Thandavan had inflicted on him.

The band of thugs entered Thandavan's house too. Thandavan tried to stop them from entering. But after suffering serious wounds to his head, hands and legs, he had no choice but to run away.

Once Thandavan fled, Anjalai opposed those ruffians—with her tears. They knew no value for tears. They flung Anjalai aside and entered the house to search for Mayandi. They ransacked the entire place, but Mayandi was nowhere to be found. There was a square wooden grain bin. The thugs' axes struck it. The bin split apart. From inside came a woman's cry—“Ah!” Everyone was startled. Durai emerged holding Parimala, who had been wounded by the axe. The thugs stood stunned for a moment. Durai began fighting them. Unable to stand before the sweep of his staff, they ran.

Durai ran after them. The fleeing thugs called for reinforcements. A fierce fight broke out between Durai and them. Breaking free, Durai stopped the villagers who were still running away.

“Cowards! Why are you running? Is Velliyambalam the king of this village? Have you no shame? Let your iron arms rise straight towards that heartless man's face! Let no one be afraid to oppose him! The marriage he arranged for Parimala and me was not arranged with good intentions—he needs the blood of newlyweds to obtain the treasure! That is why he showed so much concern in arranging the marriage. He even prepared a plan to kill us and put the blame on Thandavan. Through old Mayandi's clever schemes, Thandavan's heart was changed and we escaped. And now that scoundrel, holding court like a jungle king, has ordered his men to attack both us, who escaped, and all of you, who have done nothing wrong. Raise your fists against him! Come on—do not earn the name that the people of Marungappallam have no honour! Do not wander about as cowards and fools. I am here to stand with you—and there, my friend Anandan and the others are coming. They are very brave men! Those unmatched warriors rescued us from Ambalam's house. They too will join us. Come on—do not run. Stand and resist!” he cried with heroic fury.

By then Anandan's group had arrived. The army of thugs rushed towards the place where all of them stood. Now, under Durai's leadership, the villagers found the courage to resist the thugs. Marungappallam looked like a battlefield. Ambalam too reached the scene. Seeing Durai alive, he was plunged into astonishment.

“Don't be astonished merely at seeing me, Ambalam! Look there—my Parimala!” Durai said, pointing to her and drawing her into his arms.

Blood was flowing from Parimala's head where the axe had wounded her.

“Why are you stunned, wicked man? Every fortress you built has crumbled to dust! You planned to make Thandavan stand as the murderer, sprinkle our blood and plunder the treasure—that plan has burned to ashes in the fire, sinner! Marriage—street play—nuptial hour—a sacrifice at midnight! Villain, the sacrifice has failed to bear fruit! Love has won! Honesty has triumphed! Old Mayandi's schemes have worn the garland of victory! My friends are warriors who, if told to cut, will bind and bring back instead. Those peaks of duty have accomplished what they set out to do! Treasure—is it treasure you want? It was only so that you might gain some sense that old Mayandi gave you, as treasure, the mantra telling you to turn your own heart into gold! Yet sense has not come to you—why? Treasure is something meant for a man with a heart! At least now understand that it cannot exist inside an empty heart like yours!”

Durai kept speaking. Ambalam signalled to his men. They sprang at him. Another brawl broke out. At that moment old Mayandi came along that way in a rekla cart. To make sure the crowd noticed his arrival, he cleared his throat loudly.

Ambalam saw him. “There! Catch that old man!” he shouted. His men ran towards Mayandi. The old man drove the rekla faster. Ambalam did not realize that Mayandi had done this precisely to draw the thugs away from Durai.

Ambalam and his men ran after the rekla. Ambalam climbed into another rekla and pursued Mayandi's cart with a knife in his hand. Without anyone noticing, Thandavan climbed into Mayandi's moving rekla.

Furious and determined to catch Mayandi somehow, Ambalam drove his own rekla harder. Durai, who had been struggling to drive off the remaining thugs, learned that Ambalam had gone chasing Mayandi. He whispered something into Anandan's ear, embraced Parimala and ran in another direction. There he and Parimala climbed into a rekla that stood ready. Anandan ran up to him and said, “A boat is ready on the shore. Get into it and escape. I am going to meet the police.” Durai nodded and set out with Parimala in the rekla cart.

The rekla carrying Mayandi and Thandavan flew towards Manora. Ambalam drove hard after it. In the same direction, Durai and Parimala too were racing swiftly in their rekla.
