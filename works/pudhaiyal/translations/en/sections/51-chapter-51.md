# 51

They were Mayandi's own words. The letter began, “To dear Durai.”

“Heartfelt greetings to dear Durai. Because of the work in which I am involved, I have begun to fear that the two of us may never be able to meet again. That is why I am writing this letter in the hope that it may somehow reach your hands, or the hands of some other good person.

“If the time comes when you are reading this letter, it will mean that I am already dead. Perhaps you may even have to read it while standing beside my corpse. I began my journey like an ascetic who had come to despise everything. Then you and Parimala crossed my path. Along with the responsibility of protecting the treasure secret came the responsibility of protecting the two of you as well. Even calling it a responsibility does not quite fit. It must be called affection, my son—affection is what it must be called. I cannot see your astonishment or your sharp eyes widening, my boy! I am your father, Kumaravadivu.

“I am that Kumaravadivu who, unable to make a living in his own country, crossed the sea in order to eke out a livelihood. I had told you that Kumaravadivu entered a temple, was bitten by a snake while prying loose the idols, and died. No, my son—no! Kumaravadivu did not die then. After coming out of prison, Kumaravadivu made a living by continuing the work of prying loose idols.

“Not under the name Kumaravadivu, but under the name Mayarandi! It was while doing that work that one day, in the temple at Marungappallam, the treasure secret fell into my hands. I told you I had been bitten by a snake; though it was not truly a snake in form, it came into my life with the nature of a snake. After the treasure secret came to me, my life took a turn. Carrying that secret around as though I had tied a snake inside my garment, I began wandering from place to place. I moved among the people like a doctor, like a holy man. Somehow, I managed to live.

“I am the owner of gold coins worth countless crores. Yet I did not even wish to see them with my own eyes. A kind of weariness had taken hold of me. I have pried loose idols, stolen the coins and stones beneath them, and used that to keep my life going. But I have never even raised my eyes to look at the heaps of gold lying there in crores for me. I have already told you why that is, Durai! Why should I make you remember the old story again?

“If I do make you remember it, is it the gold bars and diamond stones buried beneath the earth that come to mind? Is it not Thangam, precious as gold, my mother, and my beloved Valliammal who lie dimly beneath the soil like treasures that can never be recovered? There is no time to weep over all that, my boy. I have seen you—that is enough. It is as though I found the treasure itself!

“Before meeting you and Parimala, the calculation I had made about that three-letter Tamil word, love, was altogether different. You have read my entire diary and learned about me yourself. My son! If any of those diaries still remain, write this on the last page:

“‘My father thought love was a cremation ground. But he came to the conclusion that, at times, flowering plants can sprout there too.’

“Write it like that, my son! Neither you nor Parimala should think that my opinion of love has changed completely. Just as a rose may grow on a plant full of thorns, there may be a few Durais and a few Parimalas. That is the final point in my calculation. Perhaps this total may someday have to be increased. Would people like me refuse to be happy about that?

“In a land where wild elephants like Velliyambalam roam, it is indeed difficult for fawns like you and Parimala to live in peace. But Durai, you and your beloved endured so many obstacles and stood proudly to defend your kingdom of love. I praise you for that. At the same time, it would be good to reserve half the place you have set aside in your heart for love for service to the people. I lost the strength to enter that work while I was still young. Ceylon squeezed me dry. I wore away my body in the tea country. The later part of my life withered in prison. In the final part I made a small effort. Before I could do much, self-interest intervened—the self-interest of having to save my son and daughter-in-law! That is why I now entrust that work to you.

“So many noble souls of sacrifice leave behind house, threshold, cattle and property and set out singing of the nation, the nation. You too must shine as one among their ranks, Durai! Do not leave Parimala behind. Take her with you! Is there any shortage of work to be done in this country? There are hundreds of Velliyambalams. Every one of them must be taught a lesson.
