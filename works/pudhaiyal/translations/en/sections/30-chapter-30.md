# 30

Parimalam stared eagerly, wondering whether the figures approaching were her beloved and the old man. But when she recognised Dukkaram and Velliyambalam, she trembled.

Dukkaram, on the other hand, could not contain his joy. Forgetting even the great danger in which he himself was trapped in Velliyambalam's hands, he sprang up.

“Parimalam! Is it you? How did you come here, my love?” he shouted.

Parimalam did not have the courage to reject his amorous words. She had not forgotten what had happened at Manora that night: Velliyambalam carrying her there while she slept and frightening her, commanding her to name her lover, glaring at her with fiery eyes, and her own cunning lie that her lover's name was “Dukkaram.” One after another, those memories came back to her.

After telling Velliyambalam, “My lover is Dukkaram,” if she now looked at Dukkaram and snapped, “Pah! Get away from me!” the lie she had told Ambalam would be exposed. So she was forced to look at Dukkaram with affection.

“Where are you coming from at this hour?” she asked him.

“Me? I... I...” he dragged out, grinning so broadly that all his teeth showed.

Velliyambalam's position had now become awkward too. He feared that Parimalam must not learn the secret that he was using Dukkaram only to discover the treasure; if she learned it, the matter might spread throughout the village.

Velliyambalam could not understand why Parimalam had come there at that hour. Wanting to find out, he asked, “Parimalam, how did you come here at this time?”

“Don't you know? That night I came from the house...”

Before she could finish, Velliyambalam loudly cried, “Oh, yes! Yes!” and stopped her. He suddenly feared that she might tell Dukkaram the whole story, so he did not let her speak further.

Would Dukkaram remain quiet?

“Parimalam, why did you send word asking me to come near the Siva temple and then fail to come?” he asked eagerly.

Parimalam did not understand the question. She stared at him.

“What? Why are you staring as if you don't know? Didn't you send word through his servant Raman?”

Parimalam became even more bewildered. Velliyambalam, worried that the situation might go too far, said decisively, “Enough—enough. We can talk about all these stories later. First let us attend to the business that has to be done.”

“Wait! Lovers are meeting after such a long time, and you won't even let us talk!” Dukkaram complained.

<!-- source scan 264 / printed 260 -->

“Finish my business first. After that, talk for years if you want. Who is stopping you?”

“Can't we finish your business in the morning?”

“No, brother. It must be finished now.”

“I won't. What will you do?” Dukkaram said, emboldened by the thought that he now had someone on his side.

Even after all this, he truly believed that Parimalam loved him—poor fellow! When Velliyambalam met him at Pattukkottai, imagining that Parimalam loved him had already been madness. Later, dancing with delight after Ramadas came to Marungappallam and spoke to him had been even greater madness. After concluding that Velliyambalam had deceived him by using Parimalam's name in order to learn the treasure secret, now, the moment he saw her directly, taking her as his own beloved was an incomparable madness.

There had been no connection between them, no kind of conversation, no exchange of love letters, not even a glance of the eyes. Yet even after danger threatening his very life had come in her name, he staggered about in the intoxication of love.

Forgetting the shining knife at Velliyambalam's waist, he opposed him openly—“I won't! Impossible!” and so on.

<!-- source scan 265 / printed 261 -->

Velliyambalam's anger surged. He even thought of killing Dukkaram with a single stab. The thought of the treasure stopped him. He swallowed his anger.

“All right, brother—come. Once we finish our work, we can be at peace afterward.”

Parimalam did not understand what they were talking about. She kept thinking of her beloved and the old man and yearning for them. Her heart burned that a brute and a fool had come between her and them. She wondered how to escape from these two.

“Why don't the two of you go and attend to your work? I will remain right here,” she suggested.

“That is best. You stay here. We will be back at once,” Velliyambalam said, starting to leave.

But Dukkaram did not like that arrangement. The reason was simple: he did not want to die. He decided to tell Parimalam what was happening.

“Parimalam! The work is nothing very great. The treasure secret is with a holy man...” he began.

Velliyambalam sprang forward. “Don't tell her—enough, stop!” he shouted.

“Wait, sir! There is nothing wrong with telling my beloved. We have decided to divide the treasure half and half. My half will be enjoyed by me and my beloved Parimalam, won't it?”

<!-- source scans 266-267 / printed 262-263 -->

“I never said you shouldn't. You and Parimalam may enjoy half the treasure freely. But does that mean you have to tell her the secret?”

“A secret my beloved must not know?”

“I know that your beloved already knows about the treasure. It is because I heard what you told her that matters have come this far!”

Thinking that perhaps some new secret was about to become clear, Parimalam asked, “How would I know about it?”

Velliyambalam immediately said, “Be quiet, girl. I heard with these very ears what you and Dukkaram were saying at the Siva temple. Didn't Dukkaram say, ‘The gold was buried under the mango tree’?” He laughed dismissively.

Everything now became clear to Parimalam. Durai had told her the tragic story of his sister Thangam being buried, and Velliyambalam had overheard it and given the words this disastrous meaning. Even in that grief-laden moment, she laughed inwardly.

“He and I never spoke about anything like that. Perhaps you dreamed it—who knows?”

Only after Parimalam said this did life return to Dukkaram. Otherwise that innocent young man would have tortured himself imagining that Parimalam had spoken to some other man about “Thangam.”

“Enough of all that. Let us attend to what has to be done. Dukkaram! Hmm... move!” Velliyambalam ordered.

“Where?” Parimalam asked.

“That cannot be told to you. We are going to obtain the secret for taking out the gold,” Velliyambalam said.

“You may tell me where the gold is, but not the secret for taking it out?” she asked, pretending to sulk.

“Would I keep it from you, darling? I will tell you right now! That is... there is a holy man...” Dukkaram began.

“Enough—enough! Let us leave!” Velliyambalam said in a rather louder voice.

But what was the point of setting off? There was still time before dawn. Only when daylight came could Dukkaram hope to free himself from Velliyambalam. He yearned for dawn.

Seeing Dukkaram's stubbornness, Velliyambalam drew the knife tucked at his waist.

Dukkaram noticed it and started. Desire for life surged through him.

“Parimalam must come with me,” he imposed as a condition. He hoped that perhaps she might save him somewhere along the way.

“All of us can go. Hmm—you walk first. Parimalam—you come too!” Velliyambalam barked, swollen with greed for gold.

Parimalam had no idea what to do. She worried that after escaping the police, she had fallen into the hands of this treasure seeker. She wilted. What way out was there? None. The river was before them. It was not deep, only broad; even if she fell in, she could not die. With the resolve that whatever came, let it come, she set off with them.

Velliyambalam walked in the confidence that he would take the treasure the next day. Dukkaram walked ahead with the conviction that his life was like a cat perched on a wall—uncertain which way it would fall.

The three of them walked along the riverbank.

“Can't you at least tell me exactly where that place is? I may be able to find it myself,” Velliyambalam asked, his legs aching.

“You can find it only by seeing where he lives. I cannot describe it. I don't know how to describe it,” Dukkaram replied and kept walking.

They trampled the grass and weeds along the riverbank and struggled past the thorns, while Parimalam followed them carrying unending anguish.

“There is no holy man, no lump of earth, no treasure secret with him. It is all lies. I will keep walking like this until dawn.” Dukkaram longed to tell Parimalam the truth, but Velliyambalam walking nearby frightened him.

The weight of darkness began to dissolve. A faint light appeared. It seemed close to the hour when the morning star would rise. Joy began to bloom inside Dukkaram. He expected that within another hour or two he would escape.

As the three walked on, each lost in separate thoughts, a terrifying groan caught all their attention.

“Ah! Ayyo—Amma—Appa! Ayyo—ayyo!” The words sounded in a low register.

All three turned toward the sound. It came from beside a bush. They went closer. A figure writhed beside it. They looked carefully.

One arm was missing. The arm had been cut off, and blood was pouring out. Because the man lay face down with his face pressed into the earth, Parimalam could not immediately tell who he was. She was shaken.

“Lift him!” she cried.

Velliyambalam and Dukkaram together gently turned the man over.
