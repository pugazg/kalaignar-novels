# 13

Dukkaram and Ambalam reached a thatched house beside the canal. Dukkaram looked all around the garden-surrounded house. A thought came to him: perhaps this was Parimalam's home. Ambalam entered first. “Hey, Rama!” he called. A foolish-looking servant boy came rushing in alarm, almost colliding with Ambalam in his haste. A short figure, a mouth that was always grinning, and a head that was always bobbing were the servant Raman's distinctive features. As he stood waiting for orders, Ambalam asked, “What news?”

“What news, ayya......you have to tell me,” Raman grinned.

“You donkey! Are you asking me back? Hey—look here—this ayya is from out of town!”

“You can tell just by looking, master!”

“He has come as a guest to our house—look after him properly! Whatever he needs......”

“All right...” Raman grinned. Ambalam received Dukkaram, seated him on the plank inside, and himself leaned back in the reclining chair there with a yawn.

“What, do you live all alone in this house?” Dukkaram began to ask. Ambalam replied:

<!-- source: scan 119 → 120 -->

“No, no! All our houses are inside the village. This is like a separate bungalow for us—from here it's very convenient for me to watch the fields, open land, gardens and water sources. So I built one here.”

“Their house must be very far away!”

“Oh—the thambi seems to be in a hurry! Their house is a little far......Thambi!—don't rush over that way—because her father is a rather rough man. This love and all that means not even a lump of earth to him!”

“See! How much trouble comes because love has not spread properly through the world? That's why I've written in my book: O God who gave love to the fish in the sea, the deer in the forest, the bee that drinks honey, and the crab in the field! Why did you close your shop before giving it to the human race? What do you think of my idea?”

“Very good, thambi! Did you write this yourself? Or......”

“I wrote it after doing a lot of research. Haven't you heard of inscriptions from ancient times? A man called Socrates came to India and, while he was speaking with Agastya, Agastya said exactly this about love. They carved those words on stone!—and in the Arabic language too!—I searched for it, found it and brought it into our story!”

<!-- source: scan 120 → 121 -->

Raman interrupted Dukkaram's speech.

“Ayya, you've wandered all the way to Agathiyur searching for crabs and beetles—if you'd told me, I could have caught them in our fields and brought them to you!”

“Ayyo—this is a love-crab, appa!”

“So you can't get it in a village, then!”

“Why not! What distinction between village and city can there be for love? Love is a road roller! Love is the egalitarian philosophy that levels high ground and hollows—there is no village-city difference for it!”

“Oh! Is that so?”

“Poor fellow, you don't know any of this. Is it only you? If the whole world is to learn these rare truths, my book has to mount the vehicle of print—”

Dukkaram brooded inwardly in great concern. While Raman and Dukkaram talked, Ambalam, who had been lost in deep thought somewhere, suddenly stood up. “Hey, Rama! Get out!” he said. Raman vanished outside as though he had flown away. Dukkaram understood nothing and stared. Looking at him, Ambalam said:

“Thambi! Wait a little—I'll go tell Parimalam that you have come and return—”

Would Dukkaram say no? Saying “all right, all right,” he rose approvingly. “Raman will be here......I'll be back in a little while,” Ambalam said and left. On the way out he privately called Raman and warned him, “Hey, fellow! Watch him carefully—make sure he doesn't go anywhere—” Raman then returned to keep Dukkaram company.

* * * *

<!-- source join / internal transition: scan 121 → 122 -->

Ambalam walked straight to Parimalam's house. As he went, all sorts of tangled thoughts tormented him. Only one firm decision gave him encouragement: somehow he must learn through Dukkaram where the gold had been buried. If he told Parimalam that her lover had come, what would she think? Would she trust his words and come to his house? Even if she came, how could she help him obtain the secret from Dukkaram? If she did not come, Dukkaram might stop trusting him. How could he make Dukkaram retain a good opinion of him—that he had brought the young man there for Parimalam's sake? Ambalam kept thinking through all this as he walked.

The news he received the moment he entered Parimalam's house flattened all those plans.

Thandavan stood there in a rage. Ambalam asked what had happened and learned that Parimalam, who had been carried away during the night, had still not returned home.

<!-- source: scan 122 → 123 -->

“Who could possibly have carried her away?” Ambalam asked, pretending not to know.

“Who else? It must all be the work of her illicit husband. Afraid that she'd die if she stayed here, the shameless woman ran off with him! If only I'd been one minute earlier that day—I would have cut her into pieces!” Thandavan said, grinding his teeth.

“You've let the matter grow this far—shouldn't you look for Parimalam now?” Ambalam asked.

“Don't imagine I'll make a police report and search—that will never happen with this Thandavan! I'll search myself—I'll find them myself—I'll cut them into pieces myself—the disgrace has already come! What does it matter what happens now?”

Ambalam's brain began to work. If news of Parimalam's disappearance spread through the village, Dukkaram would hear it and run away too. So he spoke craftily to Thandavan.

“Let no one know that Parimalam is missing. We'll tell the village she's in Pattukkottai. We'll search for her secretly. Otherwise the whole village will speak shamefully of us.”

<!-- source: scan 123 → 124 -->

Thandavan nodded at Ambalam's advice, but his fury would not settle. He seethed, feeling that a terrible stain unknown in his entire lineage had now wrapped itself around him. Ambalam returned with only one thought: “Where can Parimalam have gone?” Perhaps she had fallen into the hands of those policemen—but if so, surely the police would have announced it at once. What is this? Nothing but mystery, he asked himself. What answer could he now give Dukkaram? That was another problem.

Dukkaram looked like a fool at first sight. But would he reveal the place where the gold lay so easily? God! Such a great secret had fallen into the hands of this fool! Ambalam fretted. He decided that he had to accomplish his task by keeping Dukkaram happy. If necessary, at the final stage he could achieve it by showing a knife. If Parimalam were in the village, it would be easy to accomplish things pleasantly—she was nowhere to be found. Only if Parimalam were there could he keep Dukkaram for two days. At the end of all this confusion, Ambalam seemed to arrive at a good decision and walked briskly. Without entering the house, he stood at the outer entrance and called, “Rama!” Raman ran out; Dukkaram too came behind him and peeped out. Ambalam said to Dukkaram, “Thambi! The fruit is there!—wait a little—I'll be right back,” and took Raman toward the fields.

<!-- source join: scan 124 → 125 -->

Raman, unable to understand why his master was taking him aside alone for once, followed with his hands folded. Ambalam began to speak.

“Rama! You have to do something!”

“All right!”

“Will you do it cleverly?”

“Why wouldn't I? What happened was, that one day by mistake, when the Ayyar-saami who came to our house was here, instead of asking whether he wanted to drink water, I asked whether he wanted to put down some ‘water’—that's all, isn't it!”

“Hey—leave all that. The man from Pattukkottai who's come here!”

“Yes—my devotee!”

“What?”

“Yes, isn't Dukkaram a devotee of Rama?”

“You donkey!—listen to what I'm telling you! That Dukkaram—he's in love with our Thandavan's daughter Parimalam!”

“All right—let it happen!”

“What do you mean, let it happen?”

“Love!”

“Nothing is happening!”

<!-- source: scan 125 → 126 -->

“Is it running, then?”

“It has run away!—you donkey! That Parimalam has run off somewhere. I brought this fellow here for an important purpose—do you know what?—Parimalam and Dukkaram......”

“Ay-yay-yay! Why do you need this sort of work, master? Let them go however they like—”

“Chi! I'm not going to pair them up! I told him Parimalam was here and brought him along—she isn't here! So what you have to do is.........”

“Tell me. I'll do it!”

“You must behave with Dukkaram as though Parimalam is at home and you're carrying messages from her!”

“All right!”

“Now, in front of him, I'll send you as though you're going to Parimalam's house. Go as if you're going there, return after a little while, and speak as though you're bringing a message from Parimalam—”

“Done!”

“What will you say she told you?”

“Beloved!—Lord of my life!—dear husband!—without seeing you, my eyes swing this way and that like the pendulum of a clock!”

“Hey, hey—sabash!—where did you learn all this?”

“Do you have to study it? Isn't watching the Alli-Arjuna street play enough? And Dukkaram has been talking exactly this sort of thing all this time!”

<!-- source: scan 126 → 127 -->

“All right—all right—come! Tell him Parimalam said that Appa and Amma's orders at home are very strict, so it is difficult to meet him directly!”

“All right—from now on I'll take care of everything.”

Ambalam patted Raman on the back and returned to the house.

Dukkaram stood at the entrance waiting for Ambalam.

“Thambi! Parimalam is at home!—her father's control is very strict. So I couldn't talk much—I only hinted that you had come—if you want to send any message, I've arranged Raman here—send the message through him,” Ambalam said, and went inside.

Dukkaram looked at Raman with affection and a new sense of kinship and moved close to him.

“What, Rama! Will you go and come back?”

“Give the command—this Raman will go as a messenger like Hanuman!” Raman said, kneeling down.

Dukkaram sent a message to his beloved.

“Tell her this, Rama!—tell her that since I was separated from her I have lain without food or sustenance!”

<!-- source: scan 127 → 128 -->

“Uh-huh—I won't tell lies—I myself just served you two pots of rice!”

“Rama!—ask when I can see her—where I can see her—if I can't see her, at least bring me one kiss from her!”

“I'll do it—I'll do it,” Raman said, and ran off.
