# 34

Dukkaram waited as though doing penance for the moment when he would receive a boon from Parimalam. Parimalam, who had spent a long time silently engaged in an inner struggle, approached him with a firm decision.

She did not know that the police were already coming to arrest Dukkaram. But she knew they would certainly come and take him away. So she called softly, “Dukkaram!” There was more sympathy than before in her voice. He mistook that sympathy for the height of love.

They say that when nurses in hospitals help patients, and touch and lift them with sympathy, some patients console themselves by thinking, “Would her hand fall upon me if there were no love?” They even go on to make further attempts.

Dukkaram was in the condition of such a patient. That was why he could not understand the sympathy in Parimalam's voice. He imagined love to be nothing more than a story in which a man and a woman say, “My dear! My jewel!”, embrace each other and laugh merrily. He longed for a ticket that would let him enjoy that story.

Having lost his heart merely at the call “Dukkaram,” he bared all his teeth and asked, “Why, Parimalam?” His eyelids did not move; he stood waiting to see whether her lips would move again.

Parimalam looked at him with the inward satisfaction that she was about to sacrifice her love and save a helpless man.

“Dukkaram! Do you know how much trouble I am going through for your sake?” she began by way of preface.

“I am suffering the same way!” he stammered.

“Within a few hours, your life is going to be in danger!”

He trembled when he heard it.

“What are you saying, Parimalam?” he faltered.

“A terrible case! A murder case! You have been caught in it!”

“What? What?”

“Yes! Velliyambalam is about to come here with the police. He is going to seize you and hand you over to them.”

“Ayyo! I don't understand anything!”

“Listen carefully...... Do you remember the one-armed corpse by the riverbank?”

“Yes—yes!”

“Ambalam has gone to hand you over by saying that you were the cause of that corpse falling there. He is also going to testify that he personally saw you commit the murder. How will your head escape now?”

“It won't! It won't!! Parimalam! Why is he suddenly going to lay such a false charge on me?”

“There is no time to explain all that. There is no need either. Somehow, a murder case has been fabricated against you!”

“Parimalam! If it is a murder case, won't they give the death sentence?”

“No, no—they will seat you in a palanquin and take you in procession!...... Dukkaram! There is only one way for you to escape!”

“What way?”

“Somehow you must run away from here!”

Dukkaram began to think hard. Before his eyes appeared, one after another, shackles—the prison—the gallows.

“Run away—run away,” he kept repeating. Caught between thought and trembling like a fly in a spider's web, he suddenly began to laugh.

“Ayyo, poor fellow; perhaps he has gone mad!” Parimalam thought in shock. Only when he began to speak did she realise he had not gone mad. He had decided that Parimalam was making up an excuse to frighten him and drive him away.

“Oh! Is this how you are trying to frighten me into running away from you? That will not work with me!” he shouted, laughing.

How could she feel? Suppose a man who has fallen into a well is pulled out, and once saved he asks his rescuer, “Oh-ho! Is this your well? I knew there must be a reason—you were afraid a corpse would pollute the well, and that is why you pulled me out, is it?” How would the rescuer feel? Parimalam was in that position.

Somehow, Dukkaram decided that if he did not run away she would eventually yield to him, and therefore that he must remain there. When Parimalam spoke of the murder case and the death sentence, he had truly been afraid. But after she advised him to run away, instead of thinking “How can I escape?” he began asking crookedly, “Why are you telling me to run away?”

“Dukkaram! I am telling you the truth. Your life is going to be in danger—run!” Parimalam pleaded again.

“You come too! Let us both run away!” he invited cunningly.

She could run with him in order to save his life. But to save her beloved, she had to remain there. So she pressed Dukkaram again. “You run first. I will come afterward,” she told him.

Would he listen? He had already decided that Parimalam was plotting against him. How would he run? He showered mocking arrows upon the woman who had come to save him.

“If I die, let me die!” he laughed.

His attitude angered her, but the thought of the death approaching him without his knowledge troubled her. She tormented herself again and again because, in a way, she too had become the cause of it.

Once she became certain that no matter how much she explained he would not believe her, she moved away and sat elsewhere.

“Good thing I did not fall for her words. What would have happened if I had believed her? She would have escaped me!” Dukkaram consoled himself and strutted about the house.

“When Ambalam comes, I must tell him and get my marriage with Parimalam settled. Only after the marriage will Parimalam listen to me. Ambalam already thinks Parimalam and I are lovers. Parimalam cannot tell him the truth that she does not love me. If she does, Ambalam will place her in danger. This is the moment. As soon as Ambalam comes—the marriage!” he told himself.

What did he know? Ambalam was bringing him an invitation to the “mother-in-law's house”!

A knocking was heard at the door. Parimalam sprang up in alarm. Through a small hole in one corner of the wall she looked outside. Ambalam, the Sub-Inspector and constables were standing there.

She understood: Dukkaram's summons had arrived.

She could not rejoice by thinking, “Let him go. I warned him; he would not listen. If he goes, my beloved will certainly be freed!” Could an innocent young man receive such a terrible punishment? She could not bear it.

Dukkaram went to open the door. Before his hand reached the bolt, Parimalam ran over and caught his hand. Even then that fool sighed only at the pleasure caused by the soft touch of her beautiful hand.

“Fool! Look here—see who has come!” Parimalam made him look outside through the crack in the wall.

Dukkaram understood. Parimalam's words must indeed have been true.

“You refused to believe what I told you. What do you say now? A murder case—the shackles are standing outside. Do you understand at least now?” Parimalam asked with both pity and anger.

Dukkaram stood without speaking. He could not speak. Parimalam began pouring out words rapidly to him.

“Pitiful young man! Do not be afraid! Even now everything has not been lost. I myself will save you. Do not open the door! Ambalam will not dare break it down and enter, because he is afraid the police will discover that I am here. Do you know why he has kept me here? You think it is to arrange a marriage for you! Why should Ambalam have such concern to see you and me married? This is a conspiracy staged by using me to catch my lover Durai! Dukkaram! Forgive me! I am the cause of all the trouble that has come upon you! Durai is my beloved! My life! I handed you over only to prevent the danger Ambalam might bring upon him. But now a great danger is waiting for you! You must escape from it!” she said in agitation.

Outside, Ambalam continued knocking at the door.

Raman too kept shouting loudly, “Ayya! Ayya!!”

Dukkaram understood that Parimalam had truly been trying to save him.

“Parimalam,” he said softly. Tears filled his eyes. She too began to cry. A man she had hated—and now she shed tears for him.

“Now both of us are trapped!” he said.

“No! No! I have no intention of letting you be trapped. I have decided to save you.”

“How, Parimalam?”

“Listen to what I say! I am going to set fire to the house now. The house will burn. The village will gather. At that moment you must run out from inside the burning house. In the darkness no one will be able to see you!”

“You?……”

“I must save my beloved!”

“From inside the burning house?”

“No. I too will run outside. But I will not come with you. The villagers and the police will all have gathered. My father and mother will also be there. I will go straight to them and tell the police that I myself committed the murder. I will ask them to release my beloved Durai from prison.”

“Will they believe you?”

“Let them disbelieve me; at least there will be confusion, won't there? Somehow you will escape. That itself will give me satisfaction. After that, I believe I can also free my beloved. Do not ask me how. I know a way to free him! I can make Ambalam the accused in front of the police themselves!”

“What if you too refuse to admit that you are guilty............what then?”

“To free my beloved, there is no other way, Dukkaram! Should I hand you over? Should I betray my lover? Should I sacrifice my love? I considered those three paths. The third seemed the right one!”

“Then……”

“Here—I am setting fire to the house. Get ready to run!” Parimalam said, taking a matchbox in her hand.

Dukkaram simply stood there. The street door was being struck violently. Raman's shouting grew louder.

“Dukkaram! Dukkaram!!” Ambalam shouted.

From inside, Parimalam replied, “Dukkaram is not here!” Ambalam was jolted.

“What is this woman's voice?” the Sub-Inspector asked.

“He is a great actor, the fellow! He speaks like a woman to deceive people,” Ambalam said.

Inside, Parimalam struck a match and placed it on a piece of cloth. She picked up the cloth and carried it toward the roof. Dukkaram, who had been standing motionless, ran over and seized her hand.

“Why?” Parimalam demanded.

“Parimalam! Forgive me! I thought very little of you and behaved disgracefully, treating everything as a game,” Dukkaram wept.

She too wept while holding his hand. Now, in holding each other's hands, neither of them attached any improper meaning to it. They stood close like brother and sister and shed tears.

“All right! It is getting late—I will set fire to the house. Be ready to run!” Parimalam said again and turned with the burning cloth.

Dukkaram pulled her back and gripped her hand tightly. He made the fire in her hand fall to the floor. He hurriedly pushed her into the kitchen room. Parimalam stood bewildered, unable to understand what he was doing.

He bolted the kitchen door from outside, rushed to the street door and opened it. Falling at the feet of the police, he cried loudly, “Sir, I am the guilty one! Release Durai!”

The police put shackles on his hands and took him away.
