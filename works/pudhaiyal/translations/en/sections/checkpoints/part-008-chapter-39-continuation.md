Kudukuduppandi's words gave Ambalam fresh confidence. The man he meant must be Mayandi. It was old Mayandi who was going to try to take the treasure. Ambalam decided within himself that he must wait for that moment and catch him.

The fortune-teller continued:

“Ayya is wondering whether what Saibu says can really be true! Ayya has only two enemies! One is lying in jail! One is going to die today or tomorrow!”

“Who's going to die?”

“Ayya's mind is plain to see! A man whose first letter is ‘ma’. Ayya is going to get rid of that fellow! But Saibu tells Ayya one thing: if that old man is killed, the matter will not succeed! It will not succeed! Bhagavathi says the man in jail must not be killed either!”

“Who's in jail?” Ambalam asked suspiciously.

“By jail I don't mean a real jail! He is lying subdued under Ayya. Bhagavathi says he must not be let go! If he is let go, the matter will be ruined!”

“Kudukuduppandi! You are a great genius, a very clever fellow! You have blurted out exactly everything in my mind. But one thing—you know all kinds of spells, tricks, magic and vashiyam. Use them and do one thing for me!”

“Saibu says if Ayya asks, there is no objection! A torn sari, a torn veshti—a broken copper pot, a cracked and broken vessel, that's enough……”

“All right, all right—I'll give you what you want. The man I'm talking about is confined right here. Work your vashiyam medicine on him and somehow draw a great truth out of him!”

“Saibu will do exactly that. Bhagavathi says there is no need to doubt. A cremation-ground bone—here it is! Ayya, don't worry. This is the wrist bone of a pregnant woman! Bhagavathi says it can melt stone and dry up the sea!”

“All right—then come! Begin the vashiyam! Get the secret out of him! Come, come!”

“Ah, no! Saibu won't come like that. Malayalam Bhagavathi says Saibu must be taken to the place where that sinner is and shut in as though Saibu too were a prisoner. Saibu will lie there like a captive—the vashiyam work will go on. He will spit out the secret.”

“Ah! Sabash! I'll do exactly that!” Ambalam said, and set off inside taking the fortune-teller with him.

While Ambalam and Kudukuduppandi were talking, Menaka thought this was the moment and began showing her face at the window of the room where Durai was locked up.

Durai, however, closed his eyes and lay as though asleep, pretending not to notice her.

“Are you asleep?”

No answer.

“Must be tired after eating!”

Still no answer.

A small stone fell on Durai. He woke and looked. Then he turned and lay facing the other way.

Menaka could no longer bear to stand there. She imagined that it was not Durai in the cage but herself. She thought that perhaps, in the pain he was enduring, the desire to enjoy sweet moments of speaking with her had not arisen in him. The fresh flood held back behind a dam had begun to swell and break through it. The colour of revolt that appeared in Menaka's eyes was proof of that.

“Please come here—I have something to tell you,” Menaka said.

He did not come. Just then she saw Ambalam and Kudukuduppandi approaching and disappeared.

Ambalam opened the room door and practically shoved Kudukuduppandi inside. He too fell in crying, “Ayya! Ayya! Swami, Swami! Let me go, Maharaja!”

Unable to understand the acting of Ambalam and Kudukuduppandi, Durai woke fully.

“Let both fellows lie here!” Ambalam growled. He locked the room and moved away.

Watching all this from a distance, Menaka could not bear her anxiety. Anger arose in her too. She seethed that, just when she had been unable to speak privately with Durai and reveal her love, Ambalam had brought some fortune-teller and put him in there.

Durai stared intently at Kudukuduppandi's face. Perhaps it seemed like a face he had seen somewhere; he kept staring at him. Kudukuduppandi said nothing to Durai and went and sat in a corner of the room. Durai looked at him and asked, “Who are you?” Without answering, he shook the kudukuduppai with a ‘kudugudu’ sound.

“Who are you, man?” Durai asked, a little angrily.

“I am Malayalam Bhagavathi!” said Kudukuduppandi.

Menaka came once more to the window, cast a glance at Durai and moved away. Kudukuduppandi noticed Menaka's coming and going. Durai noticed that he had noticed it.
