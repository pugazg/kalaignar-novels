## Bilingual-review corrections made

The bilingual pass found and corrected the following English-layer issues. **No canonical Tamil text was changed.**

1. **Chapter 29 — caste-marked wording**  
   Tamil `சக்கிலிய பூதமா?` had been flattened to “cobbler ghost.” It is now conservatively retained as **“Chakkili ghost”**, with the caste-marked/insulting source usage documented in `GLOSSARY.md`. This preserves the source term while the surrounding sandal-making joke remains understandable from context.

2. **Chapter 29 — `காட்டிக் கொடுத்தோம்`**  
   The earlier English “used him as a decoy” was too interpretive. It now reads that Parimalam might have wondered why she had **needlessly betrayed him**, matching the source action more closely.

3. **Chapter 29 — source-odd `தவழுகத்`**  
   The English had silently interpreted this unusual canonical form as “by mistake.” That contextual repair was removed. The sentence now keeps the source uncertainty conservatively as Velliyambalam having **somehow dragged him into it**.

4. **Chapter 31 — source anomaly `அவன் மணக்க மாட்டேன்`**  
   The prior English inserted an object and read “refused to marry him.” The revised English avoids repairing the anomalous Tamil underneath the translation: **“if she now said she would not marry, the truth would come out.”**

5. **Chapter 31 — satire/readability**  
   The `வீராதி வீரன்` street-magician sentence was recast into readable English while preserving the sarcasm and fake-blood comparison. `மரணமுகப்பில்` is also rendered directly as **“on the threshold of death.”**

6. **Raja Madam consistency**  
   The same place had appeared as both `Raja Madam` and `Rajamadam` in English. The controlled English now uses **Raja Madam**, while Tamil source spacing/forms remain untouched. The glossary records the decision.

7. **Glossary support**  
   `சக்கிலிய` → **Chakkili**, `நளபாக` → **nalabagam**, and `ராஜா மடம் / ராஜாமடம்` → **Raja Madam** are now explicitly documented.
