## 21

<!-- source: scan 160; printed page 159; post-centered 21 -->

"Naina! Forgive me. I deceived myself into thinking that I alone possessed a heart and that the privilege of claiming ownership over the things of this world had been written as my exclusive right.

"Why should I feel jealousy because, in your heart filled with poetry, you have built a house of love for a beautiful young woman? That such an evil force should wander in my breast, overcoming even me and my friendship—is that not an unforgivable great wrong?

"My dear friend! Beloved companion who never left my side in joy or sorrow! Forgive me. Let no blame or sin fall upon that loving sister Anandi, as though a pure friendship that could not be separated by earth or gold had been divided by a woman. Somewhere hidden in my blood a frenzy grew hot, raised a cyclone, and has now disappeared. It will never show its head again. Forgive me!"

<!-- source continuation: scan 160 ends `நமது தூய நட்பு`; scan 161 begins `ஒரு பெண்ணால்...` -->
<!-- source: scan 161; printed page 160 -->

Azhagappan began to cry. Unable to stop the waterfall flowing from his own eyes, Naina embraced his friend.

"Don't speak like this, Azhagu. What happened has happened. Let us regard all this as a shapeless dream and attend to what must now be done. You ask me for forgiveness—but who am I to forgive you? What great crime have you committed? If you truly regard what you did as a grave wrong, there is only one atonement. If you can fulfil it, I will be very happy."

"What is it, Naina? Tell me. I will certainly perform that atonement."

"My dear friend—you must marry Chintamani."

"What!"

"She is a very good woman. At the urging of that traitor Tiger, Anandi tried to place a stain upon her. We too rushed into a false conclusion. The moment Anandi's conscience began tormenting her over what she had done out of fear of Tiger, she ran to tell us the truth. Then came the car accident—and everything that has happened since."

"Then how was Chintamani arrested?" Azhagu asked.

<!-- source: scan 162; printed page 161 -->

"That is something I too do not know. Perhaps she ran to Bangalore to see us, and some calamity happened on the way. That is only my guess. But when I hear Anandi praise Chintamani, I can only conclude that she is gold of the purest touch.

"Do not think I am trying to turn your mind, Azhagu. Chintamani truly is unstained."

"I am completely confused, Naina. I cannot think about anything."

"Take time to think. There is no need to reach any hasty conclusion about Chintamani now. Begin with this alone: when we decide with a single word that a woman is stained, her entire life may set like the sun. Investigate her carefully and then reach a firm conclusion, my friend."

"All right, Naina. As you say, I will take time and decide. Come—I must ask Anandi's forgiveness."

Naina and Azhagu left their room and went toward Anandi's. Azhagu walked intending to console her and accept her as his younger sister. Naina rejoiced, believing that calm had formed after the storm.

But in Anandi's room they saw a terrible sight that threw earth upon both their expectations.

<!-- source: scan 163; printed page 162 -->

Yes—Anandi lay dead upon the bed. The whole bed was drenched in blood. Blood was still pouring from her head. On the floor beside the bed lay a heavy grinding-stone roller.

"Aiyyo!" Naina screamed. Azhagappan shouted too.

Naina seized Azhagappan.

"Sinner! You did this terrible wrong, didn't you? Because your desire could not be fulfilled, you dared such a vile act! Azhagappa—when did your mind become capable of such cruelty?" he cried.

Azhagappan, stunned, looked at Naina.

"Aiyyo! Do not place the blame on me, Naina. I have no connection at all with this murder. Do not suspect me without reason and load upon me the title of a vile man!" he cried.

Naina was not ready to believe him. He fell upon the corpse of Anandi, that golden painted doll shattered as though the full moon itself had broken into pieces, and lamented, forgetting himself.

"Aiyyo, Anandi! Was I not the cause of the act that brought your life to an end? Was not the love that arose between us, beautiful one, turned into the instrument that destroyed you?

"Azhagappa! I told you that I would sacrifice what cannot be sacrificed, this treasure that cannot be found again. Even then, sinner, why did you destroy her? What could you do? Perhaps you decided that something of no use to you should not exist in the world!

"You ruined my treasure and then acted like a good man before me and deceived me—murderer!"

<!-- source: scan 164; printed page 163 -->

Naina could not control himself. Anandi lay in unending sleep, free of all care. Azhagappan stood shocked and unable to explain his position. He had no connection with the murder, yet Naina was not prepared to believe him. Poor man—what could he do? He hardened his mind to let events take their course.

From the stairs came the continuing sound of heavy boots. The sound made it clear that seven or eight people were approaching.

Naina and Azhagu turned toward it. Seven or eight policemen entered in a rush.

How had they come? Who had informed them?

The question astonished both men. Behind the police came Vembu. Sorrow had taken up residence in his face; his eyes were red from crying.

"Which of these men is Naina Muhammad?" the inspector asked Vembu.

Vembu identified Naina.

Azhagappan immediately went to the inspector. "Why, sir?" he asked anxiously.

"Naina Muhammad is under arrest in connection with this murder," the inspector replied.

At once Azhagappan cried, "No, sir, no! Naina has nothing whatever to do with this murder. I killed Anandi."

Immediately Naina shouted, "What Azhagappan says is false! I killed her!"

<!-- source word join: scan 164 ends `கத்தி`; scan 165 begins `னான்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 165; printed page 164 -->

The two beloved friends had become ready to sacrifice their very lives for one another.

Though Naina firmly believed that Azhagappan had killed Anandi in a frenzy of desire, he did not wish to betray his friend.

Before Naina's mind appeared Azhagappan's aged mother, standing before him with tears in her eyes as though pleading, "Save my son!"

Azhagappan, meanwhile, began insisting that he himself had committed the murder—both in panic that his friend was becoming the sacrifice for someone else's crime and in a despair that made him unwilling to live after his friend had suspected him.

The police were bewildered to see both men competing to argue that each was the culprit.

Vembu said, "Sir, I suspect both these men!"

The equally bewildered inspector ordered the arrest of both Azhagu and Naina. Anandi's body was taken to the Palaiyur hospital for post-mortem examination.

To the very Palaiyur hospital where love had grown between Anandi and Naina, her corpse was now carried for examination. Azhagu and Naina were put in restraints and led away.

* * *

At home, Chintamani sat crying with her head pressed against the feet of her mother, whose body still hung there.

"Amma! I could not take revenge upon that sinner Anandi. Before I could—someone else has already completed the work!" she lamented to herself.
