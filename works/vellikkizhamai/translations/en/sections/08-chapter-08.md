## 8

<!-- source: scan 68; printed page 67; post-centered 8 -->

Tiger kept sighing as though a great burden would be lifted from him once Azhagappan and the others left for Bangalore. Why were they fussing over Anandi with such concern? Would they not hurry up and go away? he muttered.

Then the old mother began speaking in a way that cooled his mind. Her talk was about the journey back to Bangalore. Azhagu, Naina and Tiger had all been waiting for just such an opportunity. Azhagu seized it and began mixing tact into his words.

<!-- source join: scan 68 ends `எதிர்`; scan 69 begins `பார்த்து இருந்தார்கள்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 69; printed page 68 -->

“Mother’s mind is unsettled. If she stays here, she will only grow more confused. Poor Naina too—we dragged him here and disrupted all his work. I want the two of you to get back to Bangalore somehow,” Azhagappan said.

Naina answered at once, agitated: “Nothing has been disrupted for me, man! I can stay here another ten days if necessary.”

“That’s not the point, Naina! Mother must go home in any case. One of the two of us has to accompany her. I was thinking I could stay here for two days and leave once Anandi has fully recovered,” Azhagu managed.

“See, Mother? Listen to your son! Don’t leave him here! It could become a great danger. I know Azhagappan’s mind is not at peace. It looks as though he has some plan to send us away and go off somewhere,” Naina Muhammad whispered poison into the old mother’s ears.

He himself had planned to stay in Palaiyur and win Anandi’s affection. Fear seized him that Azhagappan’s idea might ruin that plan. So he decided to say whatever he could to make Azhagappan return to Bangalore. Taking the old mother aside, he warned her that Azhagappan was pining from disappointment, that humiliation had left him diminished, and that leaving him alone might lay the foundation for some terrible consequence.

The old mother decided Naina was right and stubbornly insisted that Azhagappan return to Bangalore with her. Azhagappan could not disobey his mother. He grieved inwardly that his cherished friend was destroying his dreams of desire. He did not know that Naina’s heart had gone toward Anandi. Nor could Naina possibly know of Azhagappan’s infatuation with Anandi. Each was also ashamed to reveal his own feelings. They thought they could speak once matters had developed a little further.

<!-- source join: scan 69 ends `ஏங்கியிருப்`; scan 70 begins `பதாகவும்` -->
<!-- source: scan 70; printed page 69 -->

Azhagappan and the old mother began attending to their travel arrangements. Tiger did not particularly like even Naina remaining there. What if Anandi told Naina the truth? That fear troubled him.

So, rather pointedly, he said, “Our Anandi needs no one here to keep her company. Go and attend to your own affairs!”

Naina did not answer. He stood looking at Tiger with his eyes opened wide. Without realizing it, Tiger lowered his head and moved away like a mute man.

The old mother came to Anandi. “My child! I’m going back home and will return, dear,” she said.

Unable to speak, Anandi signalled her assent with her eyes.

<!-- source: scan 71; printed page 70 -->

Azhagappan too came near her. “I am going too, and I’ll come back,” he said.

Anandi tried to say something. Azhagu imagined that she wanted to tell him not to go. She looked at him without blinking. Only a faint sound escaped her, almost as if she were asking, “You too?”

Immediately Naina said, “They are the ones leaving. I am staying here for four or five days.”

Anandi’s eyes turned toward Naina as well. A smile flashed and vanished in his heart. Azhagu and Naina each invented all sorts of new meanings for it.

“Anandi has fallen in love with me. That is why she is showing affection toward Naina. In stories of kings and queens, princesses love princes and also show affection to the companions of those princes. Anandi has developed that kind of affection for Naina. This makes it certain that she loves me!”

Thus Azhagu rejoiced blindly. That one smile raised so many fantasies in his mind. When clouds wandering through the sky come together and separate under the pressure of the wind, do we not imagine, “Ah! That looks like an elephant; that looks like a cat”? In the same way Azhagappan created new meanings within himself for every movement of Anandi’s lips.

Naina Muhammad stood intoxicated by that smile. In the end, unable to tear himself away yet having to leave, Azhagappan got into the car with the old mother. Naina Muhammad saw them off and returned to the hospital with Tiger.

Naina dragged a chair to a spot a little away from Anandi’s bed and sat down. Tiger burned to drive him away somehow. Could he tell the doctors that he alone was the person Anandi needed, that Naina was a stranger to her and therefore had no reason to stay? But what if Anandi herself opposed him when he said it? That fear arose too. Tiger stood helpless, not knowing what to do.

Finally he told Naina, “Since you are staying here, look after Anandi. I’ll come in the morning,” and hurried out of the Palaiyur hospital. His face showed that the foundation of a dreadful new plan was being laid in the pit of his heart.

<!-- source join: scan 71 ends `நயினா`; verified continuity carries the name into scan 72 -->
<!-- source: scan 72; printed page 71 -->

The evening sun poured golden rays from the west through the hospital window onto Anandi. In that yellow light, her softly smiling face shone. She opened her eyes slightly and looked around. Naina immediately ran to her side.

“What do you want?” he asked.

Anandi could not speak. Her lips moved.

“Horlicks?” Naina guessed.

Her beautiful eyes moved as though in assent. He began preparing Horlicks in a hurry. Even while making it, pleasurable thoughts filled him. *I myself am going to give her the Horlicks! Ah—what a wonderful opportunity! When I hold the cup near her lips, won’t my eyes meet hers too?*

<!-- source: scan 73; printed page 72 -->

There would be an intimacy in that meeting unlike any of their earlier glances, would there not? The very thought sweetened his heart. Naina rose with the Horlicks.... Alas, the fort of his imagination was shattered into dust. A nurse came quickly, asking, “What, Horlicks?” She took the cup from his hand and turned toward Anandi with it.

The nurse’s intervention made Naina stand like a tree. Without showing his embarrassment and pain, he sat down again where he had been. The nurse attended to Anandi’s needs and left. Naina sat waiting for the next opportunity.

* * *

Sivanesar and Sivakami, who had raised Chintamani praising her as a spotless gem and an unstained moon, trembled and sobbed as though struck by a volcano’s lava.

Sivakami was a woman who lived with “God alone is my support.” Sivanesar firmly believed that not even an atom moved without Him. He himself could not understand how this wrongful movement had happened without God knowing it. But was the “Lord” who calmly watches movements of the earth and shocks of earthquakes, families perish root and branch in storms, and towns and countries be destroyed by the raging sea going to be shaken by the little upheaval in Sivanesar’s family?

Would the “Lord” who watches in amusement while “noble women” who sell chastity, put a price on their bodies and rub themselves against pleasure-seekers for money wander about come running to wipe away the stain placed upon Chintamani? Poor, foolish Sivanesar believed completely that Lord Siva would show a way out of this crisis.

<!-- source join: scan 73 ends `வரவேண்டுமா`; scan 74 begins `என்ன?` -->
<!-- source: scan 74; printed page 73 -->

Sivanesar ought to have been grateful that Lord Siva had not come to test Chintamani! Instead he grieved that the Lord’s grace had still not arrived.

Sivakami fell at her husband’s feet, sobbing. “Come, let us go to the hospital and see Chintamani. We will understand everything,” she said.

“I have placed my trust only in God. He must remove my suffering! God! This Sivanesar lived with honour—could such a disgrace come to me? What harm have I done to anyone?” Sivanesar began to wail aloud.

Sivakami stubbornly took hold of him and set out for the hospital. “Once we see Chintamani everything will become clear. Come without worrying! If this groom is gone, there will be another groom!” she consoled him.

“I do not care if the groom is gone, Sivakami. It is our honour that is gone!” he cried.

“Why should our honour be gone? Just because some fellow babbled something, does gold turn into brass?” Sivakami retorted angrily.

The two entered Anandi’s hospital. No one was at the entrance. Since Anandi had been taken to Palaiyur after the accident, the hospital looked deserted. Even Vembu, who usually received people, was nowhere to be seen. Only one nurse was sleeping in a reclining chair.

Sivakami hurried to the bed where Chintamani had been lying. Chintamani was not there.

She woke the sleeping nurse. “Where is Chintamani?” she demanded.

<!-- source join: scan 74 ends `அங்கே`; scan 75 begins `சிந்தாமணி காணப்படவில்லை.` -->
<!-- source: scan 75; printed page 74; Chapter 8 carryover before centered 9 -->

“Aiyyo, I don’t know! I have been asleep for a very long time,” said that dutiful woman.

They searched the entire hospital. Chintamani could not be found. The other patients too said only that they knew nothing; no one there knew the details.

“Chintamani! Chintamani!!” Sivakami screamed and sobbed. Striking her belly and mouth, she rolled on the hospital floor.

Sivanesar simply sat there in silence. For a long time he did not speak. Then he began to laugh softly.

“What is this?” Sivakami asked in shock.

He burst into loud laughter.
