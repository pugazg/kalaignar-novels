## 5

<!-- source: scan 45; printed page 44; post-centered 5 -->

Seeing Azhagappan coming like a volcano, with Naina following and trying to restrain him, Anandi asked, “What? What happened?”

“What else is there to happen? Everything that had to happen has happened!” Azhagappan shouted. Keeping a hand over his mouth, Naina Muhammad looked at Anandi and asked, “Madam, when did Chintamani give birth?” “Only this morning,” Anandi replied, and looked at Tiger. Without showing outwardly the joy that rose within him, Tiger signalled Anandi with his eyes, and she took him aside. Though he had been pushed into a pit of humiliation, a feeling of relief—that he had been permanently rescued from a terrifying future—rose on one side of Azhagappan’s mind, and he rushed into Sivanesar’s house.

<!-- source join: scan 45 ends `வேறுபக்கம்`; scan 46 begins `அழைத்துக்கொண்டு போனாள்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 46; printed page 45 -->

“What is it, groom? Why such haste?” Sivanesar asked with a smile.

“Oh-ho! You’re even baring your teeth and grinning? Shameless people! Wicked minds who thought you could throw our family honour into a pit and build a tower over it! And you laugh too—you laugh!” Azhagappan spat fire with his eyes. Sivanesar was shocked. Sivakami turned rigid. The old mother came fully awake. Naina Muhammad did his best to calm Azhagappan.

“Naina! What is it, son—what happened?” the old mother asked in agitation.

“Mother! We were about to be ruined! We were about to be cheated! Thank goodness—the disguise has fallen away. We escaped, Mother! We escaped!” Azhagappan cried as though standing on burning coals. “Don’t you understand, Mother? Do you know why they forbade us from seeing the precious daughter born to these demons now? They put all the blame on the day and the stars—do you understand the mystery? They insisted in their letter that ‘the groom must not come to see the bride’s house.’ Now I understand why, Mother!”

Sivanesar began to wonder in shock whether what was happening before his eyes and the fiery words falling on his ears were real. He became motionless.

“What is the groom saying?” he asked Naina slowly. Naina stood silent. Azhagappan again raised his voice in anger.

<!-- source join: scan 46 ends `நயினா மெளனமாக`; scan 47 begins `நின்றான்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 47; printed page 46 -->

“Do not call me ‘groom’! We are people who live with honour above all else! There can never be a marriage alliance between us and people like you who do not care about honour at all!”

“Young man! Restrain your words a little! Why are you exploding without telling us what the matter is? You are not old enough to teach me lessons about honour and disgrace, young man! Speak calmly! Don’t lose your head! People will decide you are mad if they see you behaving like this!”

Sivanesar spoke calmly but firmly. The old mother stopped Azhagappan’s furious display and shouted, “Tell us what the matter is, son!”

“It is not something fit to be spoken, Mother! But I cannot leave it unsaid either! Look at this great man standing here. The girl he promised us—the diamond statue! A pebble! Chintamani! She became the mother of a child only this morning—and this noble man has become a grandfather!” Azhagappan did not stop. Sivanesar cried, “Aiyyo! God!”

“Who has invented this monstrous accusation?” Sivakami began to wail.

Azhagappan moved close to his mother. “Mother! We must not remain here another moment! Leave at once! If anyone even learns that we came to this rubbish heap asking for a bride, they will never respect us again! No one will want an alliance with us! Leave, Mother; leave!”

Sivanesar, like a man possessed, cried, “My daughter has become a mother? Atrocity! Slander! Aiyyo! It will not stand! It will not stand! Someone has plotted to destroy my family! People who were growling that Sivanesar was about to form an alliance with a great household have used this terrible trick! Do not believe it! Do not believe it!! This is a story someone has made up!”

<!-- source join: scan 47 ends `விரும்ப`; scan 48 begins `மாட்டார்கள்!` -->
<!-- source: scan 48; printed page 47 -->

“Ah! What emotional acting! Perfect casting for the role of a ‘father’ in a play or film!” Azhagappan said and laughed loudly.

“Aiyyo—do not listen to village talk!” Sivakami ran forward, clung tightly to the old mother’s feet and cried.

“I am not listening to anyone’s talk, Madam! Naina and I saw it with our own eyes—in Lady Doctor Anandi’s hospital, Chintamani lying there with a child beside her!”

“Who knows whose child that was?” Sivakami wailed.

“The nurse there also told us that Chintamani was exhausted from childbirth, Mother! What we saw with our eyes is not false! What we heard with our ears is not false either! Come away from here at once, Mother!”

“Sivakami! Sivakami! What is this? What treachery is this? Chintamani gave birth? Aiyyo, God!” Sivanesar struck his head. Sivakami fell to the floor, rolled and wept.

“Young man! If you do not want an alliance with this house, simply say you do not like it and go! But do not pour lava from a volcano over us like this! You are the son of a wealthy house; we are poor! Even with a ladder we could never reach your level! This is what our greed for an alliance with people like you deserves—and deserves still more! Chintamani is a girl waiting to be married; she is not merchandise in a shop! A stain and accusation like this will destroy an unmarried girl’s life, young man! Please change this mistaken belief of yours! Chintamani is gold! My daughter is a jasmine flower without spot or blemish! Did we call you all the way from Bangalore to crown her with such a title? No—if you do not want my daughter, do not take her! Go away! Go without speaking evil of her!”

<!-- source join: scan 48 ends `சொல்லிவிட்டுப் போய்`; scan 49 begins `விடுங்கள்!` -->
<!-- source: scan 49; printed page 48 -->

Sivanesar’s heart broke and overflowed. He poured out the fire of emotion that rose in a confused blaze. Azhagappan did not descend even one step from the peak of his anger. He decided that, because their plot had failed, they were now speaking in circles and looking for a way to escape.

“You may say a thousand things, but I cannot believe you! I saw them directly in the hospital—your daughter and your grandchild! You stopped us from going to the hospital, calling it a bad omen, precisely because the truth would come to light, didn’t you? You wrote, ‘The groom need not come; Mother alone is enough,’ because you hoped you could deceive Mother’s dim eyes somehow and tie a woman who had gone astray around my neck! You were caught at the very beginning, and now you are staging Harischandra’s cremation-ground episode to manage the crisis. If we had been deceived by you, wouldn’t a ‘Bharata’ have been enacted in our house every day?”

Azhagappan mocked and jeered, snarling in between. Naina Muhammad, who had remained silent all this time watching the disastrous scene, went up to Sivanesar and said gently, “I want to ask you one thing, and you must not get angry. If what we suspect is wrong, why don’t you prove that your daughter is a chaste, blameless woman? Make an effort to establish it.”

Sivanesar thought for a moment. Azhagappan interrupted. “Hey, Naina! Fool! What is there left to prove? We both saw it with our own eyes—do we need another witness? Come on!”

<!-- source join: scan 49 ends `கேலியும்`; scan 50 begins `கிண்டலும் செய்தான்` -->
<!-- source: scan 50; printed page 49 -->

“Even if we proved it, would you ever agree? You have made all these preparations only so that you could find fault with our girl—the very apple of our eye! Never mind! Chintamani is fortunate indeed! It is a great stroke of fortune that she escaped people as hasty and unjust as you!” Sivanesar replied sharply.

Sivakami could not bear it. Was she not the mother who had carried the girl? Could she tolerate others saying such things about her daughter? She burned to prove somehow that her daughter was spotless.

“Come—let us all go to the hospital again! Let us investigate thoroughly. I will make you understand that my daughter is blameless,” she said, rising firmly and calling the others.

“What—is the arrangement that you will bribe the Lady Doctor somehow, deceive us and somehow tie Chintamani around my neck? No! No!” Azhagappan seized his mother’s hand and led her outside. Naina could reach no conclusion either, and followed them.

<!-- source join: scan 50 ends `தாயாரின் கையைப்`; scan 51 begins `பிடித்து...` -->
<!-- source: scan 51; printed page 50 -->

“Murderers who have destroyed my family from the roots and are going away! Will you prosper? May you turn to dust!” Sivanesar shouted in fury, laying a ‘curse’ on them.

“Is my daughter a bad woman? Oh God! Will these people prosper?” Sivakami beat her breast and sobbed aloud.

Amid the shouting and confusion, Azhagappan seated his mother in the car and himself took the wheel to drive fast. Sivanesar and Sivakami kept cursing them in turns. Unable to bear those curses, the old mother retorted, “Why are you cursing us like this—may you be ruined! You who tried to deceive my son and destroy our standing will die rotting!” Fortunately, neither side’s curses had any power.

Azhagappan started the car at speed. The very way the car set off revealed the speed of his mind. As it shot away from the house, a woman came running in front of it, shouting, “Stop! Stop!” Before the car could be halted, she fell in front of it. The front wheel ran over her. Only her cry—“Ah!”—was heard. The car stopped. Azhagappan, Naina, the driver and the others jumped out and ran to her. The woman lay in a flood of blood.
