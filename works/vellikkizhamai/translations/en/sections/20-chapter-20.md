## 20

<!-- source: scan 154; printed page 153; post-centered 20 -->

Unable to free herself from Azhagappan's grip, Anandi looked at him with great pity and said faintly, "Save me."

Azhagu misunderstood the meaning.

"Wealth lies piled like mountains. Love rises wave after wave. Would I leave you unsaved, my dear? I want to draw you in through my eyes and settle you within my heart," he said in a trembling voice.

"You must think of me as your younger sister."

<!-- source: scan 155; printed page 154 -->

"Do not invent such a relation yourself and throw me among rocks and thorns! I am a man who ran until his legs ached to pluck a fruit. Do not collapse saying, 'I am only an unripe fruit.' A heart that wandered searching for a flower—do not tell it, 'I am a thorn,' and make it wither!"

"You must forget me."

"You are the doctor; I am the patient."

"I am a doctor. I can prescribe medicine for your illness, but can the doctor herself become the medicine?"

"You cannot prescribe the medicine!"

"Why not? Chintamani is there—the finest medicine to cure the illness of your mind."

"A dying man asks for Sanjeevi and you recommend poison!"

"No. Chintamani is not poison. Tiger and I together turned her into poison in your eyes. It was because I submitted to Tiger's plot that we made you believe the unfortunate Chintamani was stained.

"In truth she is spotless. We placed another woman's child beside her and deceived you into believing she had given birth. Afterward my conscience would no longer allow me peace. I ran to tell you. I was struck by your car. Now I am caught in your hands too. Let me go!"

"All lies. You are inventing a story that Chintamani is good merely so you can escape me! Anandi—does my condition not raise even a little pity in you? Why do you not like me? Have you lost trust in me because I was hasty and behaved wrongly toward you?

"Have mercy. I dreamed that you would come into the grove of my heart, burned down by wildfire, and spread fragrance like a garden of flowers; that you would come as a new moon shedding light, as a golden bee in flight, as the breeze from Podhigai, as the music of a flower-cuckoo. Have all those memories simply melted away? Tell me, Anandi! Tell me!!"

<!-- source join: scan 155 ends `சோலை`; scan 156 begins `யில்` -->
<!-- source: scan 156; printed page 155 -->

"What you ask from me is not an object that can simply be given when asked. You are asking for my heart."

"Why? Can you not give it?"

"It has already been exchanged."

"Then?"

"Yes. Your friend took my heart and gave me his."

"Ah! Naina Muhammad has betrayed me?" Azhagu gasped. He released Anandi and turned.

"He has not betrayed you, Azhagu. Your friend is not a man accustomed to betrayal."

With those words Naina entered.

Azhagappan could not contain his anger. He struck Naina Muhammad across the cheek with a loud *Palar!* Naina's cheek immediately reddened; a little blood began running at the edge of his lip.

Naina took the blow and stood quietly.

Azhagu had become like a man possessed. Again he sprang upon Naina like a tiger and began attacking him. Naina endured Azhagu's blows and kicks. Another punch on the cheek. A hard blow to the stomach. A wound on the head

<!-- source: scan 157; printed page 156; literal source begins after physical discontinuity; no supplied punctuation -->

Naina did not strike back at all. At last he sagged and fell to the floor.

Azhagu stood staring at him with frenzy. Naina slowly rose. Blood was running from his head and mouth. With a careless smile he left the room.

Anandi fainted onto the bed. Azhagu stood motionless. His frenzy had still not passed. His hands trembled. He could not even think whether what he had done was right or wrong; his thoughts had sunk into sleep.

After a while he too left the room and went to his own.

Chintamani, hidden nearby, heard only the confused sounds of these events. She did not know who was speaking or what had happened. Because she had followed Anandi earlier, she understood only that there was a woman in the room and that the woman was Anandi.

A little while after the confusion ended, complete silence settled. Chintamani looked around the place where she had hidden. She saw a grinding stone and its heavy roller. Sparks seemed to leap from her eyes.

Her father's death—her mother's corpse—the title of prostitute placed upon her—the accused person's dock—the prison: one after another they moved like pictures across the screen of her mind.

"Revenge for revenge!" her dried lips groaned fiercely.

She lifted the heavy grinding-stone roller with both hands, raised it above her head and began walking terribly toward Anandi's room.

<!-- source join: scan 157 ends `பயங்கரமாக—`; scan 158 begins `ஆனந்தியிருக்கும்` -->
<!-- source: scan 158; printed page 157 -->

Her decision was to drop the stone upon Anandi's head and kill her. She seemed to make a vow within herself: *Upon the head of the woman who made me a prostitute by placing another woman's child beside me, I will drop this black stone.*

Anandi lay unconscious on the bed after seeing the condition of her beloved. Chintamani had come to send her into the world of death without Anandi feeling even a little fear of approaching death.

No fear of committing murder rose within Chintamani. A reckless courage born of daring everything shone on her face. In the dim light she looked terrifying. The young bird was ready to aim and bring the stone down upon Anandi's head.

No one was there to stop her. Conscience alone should have intervened. Instead, conscience itself seemed to urge her toward the killing.

Azhagappan sat stunned in his room; his thoughts still slept so deeply they could not even turn over.

Naina Muhammad staggered downstairs, washed his face and his wounds, applied some medicine himself and sat in a chair, thinking intensely.

He thought of how his one-week relationship with Anandi had created such great hostility in his lifelong beloved friend. Tears that had not come even when the blows hurt most now flooded out and washed his cheeks.

<!-- source join: scan 158 ends `வராத`; scan 159 begins `கண்ணீர்,` -->
<!-- source: scan 159; printed page 158 -->

Naina concluded that the same qualities in Anandi that had captivated him had enslaved his friend too. Who could prevent the love that had arisen in him from arising in Azhagappan? Did not his friend possess a heart like his own?

Aiyyo! He suffered at the thought that he himself had come between Anandi and Azhagappan, obstructing his friend's desire.

A severe spirit of sacrifice arose in Naina's loving heart. He resolved to sacrifice even his love for Anandi and unite her with Azhagappan.

In their school days Naina and Azhagu had always gone about together. Often both would desire the same thing. The object would not come in pairs. Then Azhagappan would give the thing he wanted to Naina and make him happy. Azhagappan could do that because it was only an object.

But what Naina would lose now was life itself. Was this not a tremendous sacrifice?

"Whatever it costs, I will sacrifice my love. I will make Azhagappan and Anandi husband and wife," he resolved and rose from the chair to go upstairs.

As soon as he stood, he fell. His injured places hurt that badly.

Slowly he got up and moved step by step up the stairs. He entered Azhagappan's room.

Seeing him, Azhagappan himself could understand nothing. With a loud sob Azhagu embraced Naina Muhammad.

<!-- source join: scan 159 ends `அழகப்பனுக்கும்`; scan 160 begins `ஒன்றும் புரியவில்லை.` -->
<!-- source: scan 160; printed page 159; Chapter 20 carryover before centered 21 -->

"Azhagappa! My dearest friend! Forgive me. I was the one who did wrong. If only you had given me some sign at the beginning, I would never have gone this far.

"Even now nothing is completely lost. I will sacrifice Anandi. You take her. It is my responsibility to make her agree to this decision," Naina said, tears filling his eyes.

"No, Naina! Do not kill me with those words! Beloved friend! I never imagined that such a break would appear in the pure friendship in which we embraced, rejoiced, played and ran together. I am the one who did wrong. I am the traitor. I am the sinner!"

Azhagappan clung to Naina's feet and lamented.
