## 7

<!-- source: scan 59; printed page 58; post-centered 7 -->

“Chintamani—pure gold? Only this morning I saw her beside the very stain on her name. How has she suddenly come to be painted as pure gold? Perhaps the woman you showed me was not Chintamani at all? Did that nurse mistakenly show us some other woman?” Azhagu asked in agitation.

“No! No! The woman you saw was Chintamani herself! There is no doubt about that—Sivanesar’s cherished daughter herself! And she is indeed pure gold!” Tiger spoke with deliberate emphasis.

<!-- source: scan 60; printed page 59 -->

“What is this confusion, man! The woman I saw was Chintamani; the child beside her was her child. What do you mean by this ‘gold’? Explain!”

“Do not be hasty. This is the time to remember the saying, ‘What the eye sees is false; what the ear hears is false; only thorough inquiry is true.’ The child beside Chintamani was not her child!”

“What? What? What are you saying?” Azhagappan sprang up.

“I am telling the truth. Enemies hostile to Sivanesar’s family tempted Anandi with money and made her commit this terrible sin! Swayed by the words of those murderous villains, Anandi too set out to prove that Chintamani was mud. The enemies succeeded. But only after that success did the struggle begin—Anandi’s struggle within herself! I strengthened that struggle. ‘You have stained the whole world of medicine!’ I accused her. In the end truth won. The evil thought that had suddenly surrounded Anandi’s heart like a spark of fire went out. She ran to you to save dharma! Now I am speaking in her place. Chintamani is pure gold!”

“Don’t believe him, Azhagappan! Don’t believe him! Don’t believe this drama!” Naina Muhammad shouted, stepping in front of Tiger with a terrifying look.

“What story are you spinning, man? Have you come to prove that Chintamani is not mud but a red lotus?

<!-- source: scan 61; printed page 60 -->

“That chapter is over! That grave has been closed! Don’t dig it up again and bring out the rotting corpse!” Naina thundered in a rough voice, bringing his face close to Tiger’s.

“No—believe what I say! It was a crime committed by enemies! That was not Chintamani’s child. It was another child—a child arranged to deceive you! Believe me!” Tiger pleaded.

“I cannot! If you babble anything more, your life will not be yours! Tell the truth! Tell the truth at once!” Naina Muhammad began squeezing Tiger’s neck. Tiger gasped and struggled. Naina’s hands pressed harder and harder.

“Speak! Sivanesar and Sivakami bribed you and sent you here, didn’t they? Speak—spit out the truth!” he growled.

“Let me go! I’ll tell the truth!” Tiger cried as though his life were leaving him.

Naina’s hands released him. Tiger stood there drawing breath.

“Come on! Speak!” Naina growled.

“Here—here...” Tiger trembled in fear.

“Your acting won’t work here. The truth had better come out properly,” Naina said, raising his fist.

<!-- source: scan 62; printed page 61 -->

“Everything I said was false. Let me go and I’ll run away.”

“False? Then why did you begin preaching to me that Chintamani was pure gold?” Azhagu snarled.

“I said she was gold, didn’t I? Gold acquires its brilliance only after it has been through fire! In the same way, this gold fell into the fire called a mistake. The light born from that fire is that child—that is what I came to say!”

“Ah! Ah! Ah! My modern poet! What imagination! What imagination! This is the song bribery makes a man sing!” Naina laughed angrily.

“Not bribery, friends—a heart! My tender heart! A tender heart that thought a family must not be ruined because of us! That is why I am trapped before you, blinking like this!”

“All right. Tell us the details.”

“What can I say! O Lord! Kanchi Ekamba! I can speak only after placing the burden on your head!”

“Putting a burden on him? By the pound? By the ton? Be careful how much you put on him, man—the supports might break!” Azhagappan mocked angrily.

“Fearing fellows like this would load burdens on Him, our God is neither stone nor statue. He remains invisible!” Naina said irritably.

<!-- source: scan 63; printed page 62 -->

“All right! All right! Let him tell us the matter,” Azhagappan said, staring at Tiger’s face.

Tiger began:

“Do you want my sister Anandi and me to be responsible for ruining a family and a woman’s life?”

“We want the truth!”

“Wretched Sivanesar! If you had at least hinted beforehand that the groom’s people might come, things would never have gone this far! You boasted, ‘I’ll take care of it,’ and now you have trapped us in danger. If we had known beforehand that these people were coming, we could at least have hidden the child as a precaution!” Tiger lamented.

“See? The fellow is spitting out the matter!” Naina said.

“Not the matter—poison. I am spitting out the deadly poison that will destroy a family’s future!” Tiger said in a subdued voice.

“Hmm... and then? Continue!” Naina said.

“What more is there? When the two of you came to the hospital, we did not know you were the groom’s people! There was no reason for us even to know you were coming. Nor was there any reason for us to suspect anything about Chintamani. We thought she was a married woman. Only after you stormed out of the hospital did we make inquiries and learn the truth. ‘Aiyyo! A woman’s whole life will be ruined because of us!’ Anandi cried. I tried to console her: ‘What has happened has happened. What can we do now?’ She would not listen.

“‘Chintamani may somehow have gone astray through the mischief of youth. Must her entire future become a dark room for that? Is she not a woman like me? I will save her somehow! I will establish her as a queen of chastity!’ Anandi vowed. She arranged to hide Chintamani’s child and ran to you. God was on your side. Your family deities protected your family honour. The deity decreed that the honour of your lineage must not be destroyed for the future of one woman named Chintamani. So our plan did not succeed. God made Anandi meet with an accident, trapped me in your hands as well and forced the truth out of me!”

Tiger finished with a deep sigh. Inside, joy surged over its banks because he had deceived Azhagappan and Naina so thoroughly. Without showing it, the scoundrel made himself look like a man pouring out pain.

Afraid that Anandi might wake and reveal the truth, he had prepared this plot in advance—and it had succeeded perfectly.

There was another plan: somehow he had to send them off toward Bangalore. He began speaking toward that end.

“Friends! We failed in our attempt. God has won! Never mind. I will look after my sister Anandi. You need not wait. You may leave for home!” he said by way of a preface.

<!-- source: scan 65; printed page 64 -->

At that moment Azhagappan’s driver came running in hurriedly. “Anandi has opened her eyes! The old mother is calling all of you!” he said.

All three rushed inside.

Anandi lay there with her eyes open, filled with a new light. The doctors and the old mother stood beside her.

Tiger ran up. “Anandi! Anandi!!” he called, overflowing with affection. He had reached a moment when he had to act that way.

Anandi looked at him. Only Tiger could understand the meaning of that look. It was a look that shot him and reduced him to ash. The wrongdoer shrivelled like a worm. Anandi turned her gaze from him toward Azhagappan and Naina.

Her eyes became pools of tears. Naina and Azhagu looked at one another as though they understood the meaning of her agitation. In truth, she had come to prove that Chintamani was innocent. They, however, believed that she was compassionate and, unable to bear ruining a woman’s life, had come to lie that “Chintamani is innocent.” Whatever her reason for coming, Azhagu and Naina silently wrote the same verdict in their minds: she possessed an exceptionally good heart.

“Give her Horlicks!” the doctors advised.

Tiger hurriedly prepared the Horlicks and brought it to Anandi’s lips. She refused it. The others thought perhaps she did not need Horlicks. But what she did not need now was Tiger’s service. Why—she did not need Tiger himself! She had become a ‘Tiger’ now.

That spotted deer had acquired the nature of a tiger bent on taking revenge on the sinner Tiger.

“Let her rest. Don’t make her speak,” the doctors said and left. The others sat down on the chairs there.

“My dear! My child!” the old mother cooed affectionately, moving close.

All Anandi could manage was a soft “Hmm.”

“Have a little Horlicks, child! You must be very tired,” the old mother said.

Anandi turned her eyes and looked the old mother up and down. Again her eyes filled with tears. The old mother gave her the Horlicks. Anandi drank all of it. Tiger could no longer bear to remain there. In Azhagappan’s heart, meanwhile, waves of thought began to gather.

“How good she is! How angry she became merely to save some woman who had made a mistake! Forgetting her own condition, unaware of what might come before her, she wanted to give another woman a life. What could equal such a heart? Not merely beautiful—the very form of love! Ah, what depth of attraction there is in her eyes! Those wondrous eyes seem to move as if saying without words, ‘The beauty of hair, the beauty of kovvai-red lips, the beauty of gentle eyes—none of these is true beauty; greater than all of them is the beauty of my heart.’ I can now know with proof that this world, which wants only to see others ruined, has left rose blossoms like Anandi to droop amid clusters of arali flowers! Are not such good women the noble ones born to give life to home and country? Chintamani was the woman who was to come to me! No, no! A gem pressed into dusty earth and spilled away—a scattered gem! Her name alone is Chintamani. This one is Anandi. Her very name glitters with a little modern fashion; so does her profession. But her heart is gold! No—I cannot even call it gold. Tiger has given that comparison a new interpretation too. This is gold untouched by fire! Mullai untouched by a hand! A silver moon untouched by stain! If this queen among women became my companion in life?......”

There was no question mark in Azhagappan’s heart; a sign of desire arose instead. Thought is a wild river, is it not? It can travel wherever and however it wishes. Yet even that journey has an end. A wild river may spread through the forest and disappear there, or it may narrow like a little irrigation channel between fields and merge into the sea. Azhagappan’s river of thought took the second course.

Anandi had captivated him from her sickbed. He resolved somehow to join her loving heart to his own.

He decided that the next chapter of his life was to put an end to the episode of searching for a bride and make Anandi his companion.

<!-- source: scan 68; printed page 67; Chapter 7 carryover before centered 8 -->

He planned to send his mother and Naina Muhammad back home, remain at the Palaiyur hospital to help Anandi, and reveal his purpose while serving her there.

“At the Palaiyur hospital I will get a chance to grow close to Anandi. Then she will understand me! I will understand her! My wish will be fulfilled! My life will be painted in colour,” he imagined. Somehow he had to send Naina and his mother home.

Naina, sitting there, was thinking along exactly the same lines. He had already begun worshipping Anandi in his heart. To put it briefly: Naina was in love with Anandi. He too was thinking that somehow Azhagappan and the old mother should be sent to Bangalore while he remained in Palaiyur to serve her.

Understanding none of this, Tiger sat immersed in thoughts about the future.
