## 12

<!-- source: scan 99; printed page 98; post-centered 12 -->

Hearing the sound of someone approaching, Naina Muhammad hurried away from Anandi's bed and returned to the veranda. Sweat had poured over his whole body and soaked his clothes. He told himself how such matters could turn even manly men into cowards: deciding something about a woman by oneself, wondering whether she would agree, and then beginning the first steps toward that imagined conclusion.

From the veranda he watched to see who was approaching Anandi's room. A nurse opened another door and entered carrying a medicine bottle. Seeing Anandi sleeping soundly, she called to her twice in a low voice. Anandi did not wake.

<!-- source: scan 100; printed page 99 -->

Standing by the doorway, Naina asked, "What do you need, Amma?"

"Nothing. I brought her medicine. She is sleeping well; she should not be disturbed. Please call me as soon as she wakes. I will come and give her the medicine. Here—it will be on this table," the nurse said, setting the bottle on the table before leaving.

Naina Muhammad sat on the veranda and sank into thought. Before his mind's eye appeared a beautiful future filled with delight. He lightly closed his physical eyes and began enjoying that imagined scene.

Two wings sprouted from his shoulders. He began flying through the sky. Amid the clusters of stars—in that garden of heavenly flowers—he swam like a bee. The star-flowers bent down their rich honey and called to him, but he flew on seeking something else. A glittering butterfly spread splendid wings and danced through that garden; Anandi's face was set upon it. He joined her in a dance of delight. Together they left the grove of stars and flew still higher, tearing through the blue roof of the sky and passing beyond it. Stars spilled from the torn roof. The moon swayed from side to side like a luster lamp swinging in a storm. Then, as the lovers of that world of delight flew beyond the very crown of heaven, their wings suddenly fell away. Without the power to fly, they crashed down upon the earth.

<!-- source: scan 101; printed page 100 -->

What Naina saw was not the sort of dream films show, where one wakes in shock and discovers that everything was a dream.

With his outer eyes closed and his inner eyes wide open, Naina Muhammad was searching for answers to questions such as these: If he and Anandi became united in life, how would they run a household? In what town would they live? How well would their family life suit them? Would Anandi perhaps decide that she no longer needed even her work as a doctor and choose to live as an ordinary woman of the household?

At that very time, while he sat on the veranda performing his penance of love, the hand of the dark figure standing by the window pushed aside the curtain and reached into Anandi's room. It searched toward the table where the nurse had left the medicine. In the darkness the bottle was beyond easy reach, but the hand found it and drew it nearer. Once the bottle had been moved to another part of the table, the hand set it down and withdrew toward the window. It returned with a small vial. In tiny letters the vial was marked "poison." It was tilted over the medicine bottle, and a drop fell into the medicine. The medicine had become poison.

The dark figure remained there a little while and then slipped away without a sound. After the poison was added, the liquid in the bottle bubbled up and then settled again.

A short while later Anandi turned in bed. The sound of her groan made Naina spring up from the veranda and rush inside.

"What do you need?" he asked anxiously.

Anandi looked at him without blinking for a little while. He clearly understood that her gaze held surprise, trust and a certain coolness. He could hardly contain the fullness rising in his heart.

<!-- source join: scan 101 ends `ஆனந்தி`; scan 102 begins `புரண்டு படுத்தாள்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 102; printed page 101 -->

"The nurse brought medicine. She did not wake you because you were asleep. She said you should take it when you woke. Shall I give it to you?" he asked.

Why did his throat refuse to obey him and tighten as he spoke? Why did his tongue tremble? A strange feeling—an extraordinarily strange feeling—was tossing him about.

The nurse had told him to call her when Anandi woke. But if he called her, would he not lose the chance to give Anandi the medicine with his own hands? That fear held him back. Anandi nodded that she was willing to take it. Naina's happiness doubled. What did he know of the poison mixed into the medicine?

The poor man had waited so long for this moment, only to be on the verge of saying in effect, "Here—drink this poison."

The bottle on the table seemed ready to raise a great cry within Naina's heart, smash his palace of desire to dust and make him writhe like a worm thrown into fire.

<!-- source join: scan 102 ends `உடைத்தெறிந்து`; scan 103 continues -->
<!-- source: scan 103; printed page 102 -->

Naina took the medicine bottle and came to Anandi's side. The poison that could take a life within half a second of being swallowed had dissolved thoroughly in it.

"Well? Shall I give it to you?" he asked, his voice overflowing with affection.

With her eyes seeming to say, *Aiyyo, why should I put you to unnecessary trouble?*, she nodded once again.

Naina brought the bottle toward her bright red lips. But in the position in which she lay she could not drink. With one hand he gently raised her head a little and brought the medicine to her mouth.

Anandi placed her lips upon the bottle. Naina tilted it to pour the medicine into her mouth. Suddenly she thrust the bottle away, twisted her head from side to side and cried, "Poison! Poison!!"

"What? Poison?" Naina said, smelling the bottle. He could detect nothing. She was a doctor; she had discovered it. Naina's hands trembled like the strings of a veena.

"Poison! Poison!!"

Naina struggled, unable to speak.

"Why are you trembling? Because the matter has been discovered? What benefit does anyone gain by killing me? Why should this be done? I am calling the police at once! Do not run anywhere!" Anandi said, gripping Naina's hand tightly.

The weakness caused by her accident seemed to vanish somewhere. It was true that Naina had longed for Anandi to hold his hand—but in such a terrifying situation her touch felt like a burning brand.

<!-- source join: scan 103 ends `சொல்லு`; scan 104 begins `கிறேன் போலீசை!` -->
<!-- source: scan 104; printed page 103 -->

Still standing, Naina bent his head toward her feet and began sobbing. He did not know what answer he could give. What words could make her believe him?

"You must believe me! I... I... I..." he cried.

"Do you think of me that way, Anandi? What enmity is there between you and me that I would poison you to death? Think about it, Anandi!" he pleaded.

"There is neither enmity nor relationship between us! Someone has sent you here as Yama to me!" Anandi said firmly through her agitation.

"Aiyyo! Don't say that! From the moment I heard of you—from the moment I learned of your generosity—you became an indelible picture in this heart of mine that bows before goodness. One day! A few hours! Within that time I came to think that I had become a slave to your beauty and your affection. We exchanged no words; even our eyes exchanged no language. Yet your enchanting form, lying there in silence, bewitched me. I lay suffering, wondering whether this one-sided love would ever succeed. I waited for a joyful moment when I might serve you with fruit juice, milk, Horlicks, medicine—anything at all. That good moment came. I never dreamed it would become such a poisonous moment, Anandi!" Naina said, opening his heart.

<!-- source join: scan 104 ends `உபசரிக்கிற`; scan 105 begins `அந்த மகிழ்ச்சிகரமான...` -->
<!-- source: scan 105; printed page 104 -->

Anandi's mind filled with questions. *Who is this man? Where did he come from? Why has he stayed at the hospital since morning, taking such care of me? Was he only waiting for a chance to carry out a plot? Now he is crying and telling a story that he fell in love with me. How can I believe him? What confusion! Even if a conspiracy had been planned in advance, how could he have guessed that I would meet with an accident this morning, come to a hospital, and give him a chance to mix poison? Or did someone decide only after this situation arose to use the car accident as a cover and finish me off? If so, someone inside the hospital must be involved.*

Thinking in many such directions, Anandi became confused.

Naina, meanwhile, struggled to erase the mistaken opinion she held of him.

"I know nothing of any crime, Anandi! I came with my friend to see a bride. My friend was heartbroken in the place we came to. Then you entered our lives. The accident made the two of us meet. Your beauty struck awake thoughts that had been hiding somewhere inside me and tied me here so that I could not leave. Forgive me for speaking so openly and boldly without even knowing whether you approve. Would I think of poisoning the very deity of my love?"

<!-- source join: scan 105 ends `உன்`; scan 106 begins `சௌந்தர்யம்!` -->
<!-- source: scan 106; printed page 105 -->

As he spoke, Naina struck his own face and cried aloud.

Confusion and agitation competed within Anandi. She lay motionless for a while, staring at the wall opposite. Naina stood looking only at her, tears flooding his eyes.

"Anandi," he called. Affection, compassion, fear and devotion were all contained in the word.

Anandi turned. She stared at the table where the medicine bottle had stood. There was something there. Yes—the little poison vial. It was still there.

"Take that," Anandi said. There was gentleness in her voice now.

Naina ran over, picked up the vial and handed it to her. She examined it carefully. It was a vial from her own hospital. She recognized its identifying mark.

"How did this come here?" She could not immediately find an answer.

"Did Tiger come here?" she asked Naina.

"He came during the day. He did not come at night," he replied.

"I suspected you wrongly. Please forgive me," she said, tears filling her eyes.

Naina could not express his happiness. Still crying, he pressed her hands against his face without even realizing what he was doing. Anandi withdrew her hand and looked once more at the poison vial. She told him to pour the medicine away exactly as it was. Naina poured it onto the floor. The poison that had been waiting to drink Anandi's life ran across the ground.

<!-- source join: scan 106 ends `அவள் கண்களில்`; scan 107 begins `நீர் கலங்கிற்று.` -->
<!-- source: scan 107; printed page 106; Chapter 12 carryover before centered 13 -->

Turning back to her, Naina asked, "Anandi! Do you believe me now—that I was not the one who mixed the poison?"

"You did not mix poison. They mixed nectar instead!" she said with a smile.

At that instant two gunshots suddenly rang out—*Dumeel! Dumeel!!* A great cry rose through the whole hospital.
