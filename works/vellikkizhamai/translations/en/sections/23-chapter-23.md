## 23

<!-- source: scan 172; printed page 171; post-centered 23 -->

Thevar returned inside in haste and eagerness, only to find Chintamani gone. She had appeared and vanished as though she had come in a dream. Who was she? The longing seized him.

He sent men to search for her. Chintamani escaped without falling within anyone's sight.

There was no route out of that region except the road toward Vedapuram. She thought that if she stayed outside the village itself, wandering only through the outskirts until night came, she could then cross past it unseen.

<!-- source: scan 173; printed page 172 -->

A desire pushed her toward the Vedapuram cremation ground. Her mother's body had to come there. She longed to remain hidden from the villagers and at least witness that sight.

Without room for another inner struggle, she reached the cremation ground. The bushes around it gave her concealment.

Just then the police, the servant Vembu and others were leaving after Anandi's murdered body had been buried. Hidden among the bushes, Chintamani waited anxiously for her mother to arrive.

Had anyone taken responsibility for burying Amma? Or had she become an ownerless corpse? Thinking such thoughts, Chintamani shed tears.

Then she saw four or five people carrying Sivakami's body. Even in that pain she felt a little consolation: at least some people had shown enough compassion to perform her mother's last rites.

Soon fire caught the body. In a little while her mother's form would become ash. Aiyyo—the mother who bore, raised, cherished and praised her was merging with the earth.

Those who had come to the cremation ground left satisfied that their duty was finished. Chintamani ran from the shelter of the bushes toward the place where her mother burned. She watched through tears as her mother became food for the flames.

The cries that rose within her heart—Appappa! Circumstances of pain beyond description.

She left the cremation ground. Aiyyo, how quickly her family had shattered. Her father had gone to the very boundary of devotion. Her mother had lived trusting only the sacred feet of God. Yet God had made both of them suffer so greatly. What wrong had they done to God?

<!-- source continuation: scan 173 ends `அவள் நெஞ்சிலே`; scan 174 begins `எழுந்த குமுறல்கள்!...` -->

Sivanesar would not even cross a doorstep without consulting day and star—such a great Sanatani was he. Yet his house had fallen into such chaos. Even before writing the first letter about the Bangalore groom, he had spent a whole day turning the pages of the panchangam and wrote only after choosing a good lagna.

And Bhagavan had left such a crown among devotees trembling in anguish. His whole family had been turned into a wasteland.

The remaining Chintamani spent the whole day wandering through fields and riverbanks without entering Vedapuram.

While she wandered, another shocking piece of news reached her: Azhagappan and Naina Muhammad had been arrested in connection with Anandi's murder.

She could not bear it. She decided that Azhagappan had to be freed somehow.

Because she had already thought of him as her husband, the urgency rose in her heart. Hearing that he had been arrested for Anandi's murder turned her thoughts back to the events at Balagangadhara Thevar's house. She resolved to trap the real Balagangadhara Thevar and discover who the false Balagangadhara Thevar was.

<!-- source continuation: scan 174 ends `உண்மையான பாலகங்காதரத் தேவரை`; scan 175 begins `சிக்கவைக்கவேண்டுமென்றும்` -->
<!-- source: scan 175; printed page 174 -->

Darkness arrived just as she was ready to begin acting upon that decision. Ignoring the pain in her legs, she walked and walked until she reached the DSP's office.

The DSP listened with great care to everything Chintamani told him. She narrated her whole story. He developed deep sympathy for her.

With Chintamani and a police force, the DSP went directly to Ayyanar.

At Balagangadhara Thevar's house a Bharata kalakshepam was being conducted in great splendour by a Bhagavathar. Thevar sat on an ornate seat listening, and villagers had gathered around him.

At exactly the right moment the police force rushed inside.

Chintamani showed the police the place where Thevar and Idumban had buried the bloodstained clothes and the aruval. When the place was searched, the bloody clothes and weapon were recovered.

Thevar and Idumban were arrested there and then.

But the false Balagangadhara Thevar still had to be identified. Following Chintamani's plan, the police hid in her house.

As he had told Chintamani in Bangalore, the false Balagangadhara Thevar arrived in a hired motorcar. The police surrounded him.

The DSP stripped away the disguise. Balagangadhara disappeared—and Tiger stood there in his place.

Chintamani understood that he had helped her only with the intention of drawing her into his own net.

The police were delighted. The man who had deceived them and escaped had finally been caught. They thanked Chintamani, told her they would meet her again later, placed Tiger in restraints and dragged him away.

<!-- source continuation: scan 175 ends `போலீசார் அவரைச் சூழ்ந்து`; scan 176 begins `கொண்டார்கள்.` -->
<!-- source: scan 176; printed page 175 -->

The next morning Naina Muhammad and Azhagappan were released. Neither understood what had happened.

They asked the servant Vembu for the details.

"I know nothing about this murder," he explained. "I had gone somewhere outside. When I was returning to the hospital, some man ran out as though he knew me and said, 'Hey, Vembu! Naina Muhammad has killed Anandi! I'm going to bring the police!' Then he ran away. He never came back. Before long a police van was passing this way. I stopped it, brought them up and came along. That was all I knew then."

"Why should Balagangadhara Thevar have wanted to kill Anandi?" Azhagappan asked.

Vembu offered a guess. "For some days Thevar had repeatedly begged Anandi to come to his bungalow. Perhaps he did this because Amma refused."

<!-- source: scan 177; printed page 176 -->

When Azhagappan learned that Chintamani had uncovered all these truths and secured their release, an immeasurable respect for her arose in him. Vembu too poured out evidence showing that Chintamani was unstained.

Azhagappan told Naina that they had to go to Chintamani and ask forgiveness. Naina's grief over Anandi's death had not faded. He had become almost mute and merely nodded through tears.

The two left for Chintamani's house.

On the way Azhagappan bought the day's newspaper and turned through it. There was a report that the true culprit in the Bangalore railway opium-smuggling case had been caught and that Chintamani had been proved innocent.

Just as Chintamani had vowed, she had freed herself both from the title of prostitute and from the accusation of opium smuggling. She had proved to the world that she was an unstained lamp of a gem.

Azhagappan ran with Naina to ask forgiveness from that lamp.

Chintamani sat in a chair reading the day's newspaper. Azhagappan rushed in, clung to her feet and cried, "Chintamani! Forgive me!"

She fell at his feet and shed tears.

<!-- source: scan 178; printed page 177 -->

"At least now, do you believe me?" Chintamani asked, her voice trembling.

"I believe you, Chintamani. Forgive me!" Azhagu cried.

"Then... our marriage must take place now—this very moment!" Chintamani said through sobs.

Azhagu removed his ring and placed it on her finger. They looked at each other with smiles spreading across their faces.

Even as Azhagappan watched her, she suddenly collapsed to the floor.

"Chintamani!" he cried, springing forward and lifting her.

She slowly opened her eyes and drew a laboured breath.

"Forgive me. I have taken a deadly poison," she said.

Azhagappan and Naina started in shock.

"One day... Friday... I waited eagerly for your arrival. Today too is Friday. That longing has been fulfilled!" she said, and kissed his face.

After that her eyes never opened again.

Having fulfilled her vow, that spotted peahen became a corpse—on a Friday.

The two friends who had come on a Friday to see the bride's house returned to Bangalore, their hearts softening again and again as they remembered the two women who had earned an indelible place in the ledger of their lives.
