## 19

<!-- source: scan 149; printed page 148; post-centered 19 -->

What other path was left to Sivakami except death? Her daughter was gone. Honour was gone. In the end even the mangalyam was gone. Nothing remained that could give her peace except a length of rope.

Mother and father had ended their journey through life and become corpses, leaving Chintamani a walking corpse.

Chintamani clung to her mother's feet and cried.

"Who is left for me now, Amma?" she lamented.

"Amma! You bore me, raised me, cherished me and sang lullabies calling me 'bronze statue! Pure Tamil!' You carried me upon your hip saying, 'Should my gold's feet even touch the ground?' You bore endless hardship and cared only for my growth, prosperity and health. And at the end you have withered like this—why, Amma?

"What harm did we do to anyone? Why has such cruelty come upon us? Aiyyo! Could I not have been born a son from your womb? If I had been born a man and had strayed from chastity, this world would have forgiven me. It would not have piled blame upon me and shamed me like this. It is because I was born a woman that a nonexistent accusation has been laid upon me and our whole family burned away with wicked words.

"Amma! Whom will I now call 'Amma' with all the longing in my heart? Who is left? You used to tell everyone, 'In my old age I'll go to my daughter's house, sit in peace without even having to fold my stretched legs, and eat there!' Now there is no daughter's house for you. You have set out for the house of the cremation ground!

"What else could you do? When they said your daughter was a prostitute, how could a mother's heart hear it and remain calm without boiling? And not merely a prostitute—a prison bird too! Accused of a degrading trade! How could life remain within you after all that?

"Who is responsible for everything? Who? That wicked Tiger! That unjust Anandi! She is the one who pushed me and my family into a pit of disgrace. Only after I take revenge upon her will my mind find peace!"

<!-- source join: scan 149 ends `புறப்`; scan 150 begins `பட்டுவிட்டாயே!` -->
<!-- source: scan 150; printed page 149 -->

"Murderess who destroyed a family! Woman-shaped demon who never thought even for an instant, 'We are unjustly sacrificing a woman's life!' Taking revenge upon her is my first task. Only after destroying her will there be any other task. She must not remain alive! If she does, who knows how many more women's futures she may become Yama to?

"I am going now. I will return after taking revenge upon Anandi! Chintamani—become a murderess too! What title is left now? Prostitute! Fraud!! Next—murderess!!!

"Amma, I will not kill myself the way you did. Before I die I will do one good deed. For the deed I am going to perform, death itself will be the reward given to me. I need not hide from the world and die as you did. Openly, under proper guard, with the bell ringing, they will take my life upon the gallows!

"If the stories of the Puranas are true and both of you are in Devaloka, I will come there and tell you the joyful news that I have killed Anandi. Only now, Amma, do I feel how good it would be if there really were such a thing as Devaloka!

"Here I go. I will finish my work and return. Anandi! I will take revenge upon you. You cannot destroy my family and live in splendour. You too must come with us!"

<!-- source join: scan 150 ends `தேவ`; scan 151 begins `லோகத்தில்` -->
<!-- source: scan 151; printed page 150 -->

Chintamani shouted in fury, hardly aware of herself. She pressed her mother's feet to her eyes, left the house like one possessed and ran toward the street where Anandi's hospital stood.

If Anandi fell into her hands, Chintamani would tear her apart and kill her before taking another step. Such was the flood of vengeance rising within her.

Meanwhile Naina, who had resolved to watch what changes would unfold, lay sleepless in his room. At one moment the suspicion arose in him that perhaps Anandi and Azhagappan really did have some connection. At another he reassured himself: *Tch, no, impossible.*

Anandi's telling him her past was precisely what had made such doubt possible. Why remain confused by needless suspicion? Why not conduct a test and understand her?

If she was casting a net over Azhagappan too, she would certainly come to his room at night. What if Naina were there when she came, and caught her red-handed?

<!-- source: scan 152; printed page 151 -->

With that question in mind, Naina rose to carry out his test. He left his own room and went to Azhagappan's. Azhagappan too was tossing sleeplessly on his bed.

"There are many mosquitoes here. There is a good mosquito net in the room where I sleep. Why don't you lie down there?" Naina suggested.

At first Azhagappan refused. Yet he too had a desire: to discover whether there was any connection between Anandi and Naina. If she came to Naina's room, he thought, he could speak openly there and set matters right.

After Naina insisted, Azhagappan went and lay under the mosquito net in Naina's room. Naina lay in Azhagu's room, sleepless.

After a while he heard approaching footsteps. He rose and watched closely. A woman seemed to be approaching while hiding herself from place to place. Naina slipped inside the room and waited without making a sound. No one came.

He rose carefully and looked out. No one was visible. He went back inside and kept watch.

Again footsteps cut through the midnight silence. Naina hid himself and fixed his attention on the direction of the sound.

It was Anandi. She was going into the room where Azhagu lay. She believed Naina would be sleeping there.

Most of Naina's suspicion disappeared. If she truly had her eye on Azhagappan, would she not have gone to Azhagappan's usual room—the room Naina himself was now occupying? Instead she was going to the room where Naina normally slept.

Naina lowered his head in shame. Yet an uncontrollable curiosity burst from his heart. He wanted to know what was happening in Azhagappan's room and slowly moved toward it.

<!-- source join: scan 152 ends `வழக்கமாக உறங்கும்`; scan 153 begins `அறைக்கல்லவா போகிறாள்!` -->
<!-- source: scan 153; printed page 152 -->

Inside the mosquito net Azhagappan lay wide awake. A soft flower-like hand touched him. Lost in the pleasure of that touch, he remained motionless.

At the same time Naina Muhammad came to the outer side of the doorway and hid there, sharpening his ears.

"Asleep?" Anandi asked, stroking Azhagappan's cheek.

Perhaps afraid that speaking loudly would reveal his voice, Azhagappan answered faintly, "No."

"Where is he?"

"He's sleeping in that room."

The exchange shook the hidden Naina's heart violently.

"Do you have any suspicion about me?" Anandi asked.

"Why?"

"The way he behaves toward me... is not quite right."

"It is all right."

Azhagappan's tongue would no longer speak. His body trembled. Very soon his actions revealed who he really was.

"Who are you?" Anandi sprang up.

Azhagappan caught her hand and would not release it.

<!-- source: scan 154; printed page 153; Chapter 19 carryover before centered 20 -->

"Please let me go!" she said angrily. The fire in those words delighted Naina.

"Anandi! My life must be reborn through you. I am a man deeply wounded. Save me!" Azhagu pleaded.

Hearing the words from outside, tears filled Naina's eyes. He was overcome with sorrow at the condition of his friend.

Then a small sound arose from the opposite side of the room. Naina turned, but saw no one.

Chintamani had arrived before Anandi. Without falling within the sight of Naina or the others, she had slipped to another side and hidden herself.

Seeing nothing where the sound had come from, Naina turned his attention back toward the room.

Anandi was begging Azhagappan to let go of her hand. He only repeated, "Do not kill me while I am still alive!"
