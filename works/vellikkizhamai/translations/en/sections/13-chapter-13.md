## 13

<!-- source: scan 107; printed page 106; post-centered 13 -->

Naina Muhammad ran outside. All the hospital lights had been switched on. Everyone rushed toward the main entrance. Two men were locked together, rolling on the ground. One was the hospital watchman. The other was Tiger.

Blood was gushing from the watchman's shoulder. Even through the pain he held tightly to Tiger and cried, "Come quickly! Come quickly!"

The watchman pinned Tiger's gun hand down carefully so that the weapon could not open its mouth again. Tiger struggled desperately to fire at those running toward them, drive them back and escape, but the watchman would not let go. By then Naina Muhammad had leapt upon Tiger and snatched away the pistol. Hospital staff surrounded him.

Only now did the watchman begin to feel the full pain of his wound. Blood from his shoulder had soaked his body. He was hurried away for first aid.

<!-- source join: scan 107 ends `தப்பித்துவிட`; scan 108 begins `எவ்வளவோ திமிறினான்...` -->
<!-- source: scan 108; printed page 107 -->

The watchman explained that he had dozed briefly while on guard; then he saw someone climbing over the wall and trying to leave. When he ran toward the man, he was unexpectedly shot. Ignoring the wound, he had run after him and caught him.

The police were informed and arrived within minutes. No matter how much the inspector questioned him, Tiger stubbornly maintained silence. He was arrested and placed in the police vehicle.

When Anandi learned the details from Naina Muhammad, she was stunned. She trembled like someone caught in a terrible earthquake. Though she had escaped two great dangers, an indescribable fear disturbed her heart. The thought terrified her: Tiger had regarded her as lower than an ant that could be crushed underfoot and had engaged in such a bestial act.

"Who is Tiger?"

"Why did he come here?"

"What is the cause of this terrible event?"

<!-- source: scan 109; printed page 108 -->

The questions dug into Naina Muhammad's heart. He asked Anandi about Tiger.

"It is true that Tiger is your brother, isn't it?"

"Did that vile wretch tell you that?"

"Yes!"

"He is not my brother! The sinner meant to deceive you. He is a 'big man' roaming about Vedapuram—and here 'big man' describes the smallness of his mind!"

"Then everything he told me was false?"

"What did he tell you?"

"First he said Chintamani was unstained. Then he told me that you had gone through all this trouble only to hide the terrible wrong she had committed!"

"Which of those two things did you believe?"

"The second."

"Aiyyo! You believed Chintamani was a ruined woman?"

"Yes."

"Wrong! Very wrong! Chintamani is a red-lotus bud. Do not throw her into the mud!"

"Anandi! It is this great heart of yours that has captivated me. Your heart is like the spread tail of a peacock. What wrong is there in my longing for thoughts of me to appear within it like blue crescents?"

"My heart is not holy in the way you imagine! To put it briefly—"

<!-- source join: scan 109 ends `சுருக்கமாகச் சொல்லப்போனால்`; scan 110 begins `நான் ஒரு புழுதிமேடு!` -->
<!-- source: scan 110; printed page 109 -->

"—I am a mound of dust! My heart pounds when I think that this dust-storm became the cause of soiling the sacred life of a virtuous woman."

"Again you are only opening your sacrificial heart before me. I admire your attempt to bring light to Chintamani. But in that light the truth will begin to show clearly. No matter how hard you try, you cannot change the fact that Chintamani is milk spilled upon the earth!"

At Naina's words, tears began flowing from Anandi's eyes in streams.

"Believe what I tell you. Listening to that sinner Tiger, I became the traitor who placed another woman's child beside Chintamani in the maternity hospital and laid this accusation upon her! Chintamani is his uncle's daughter—a distant relative. When he heard that she was to be given to a Bangalore groom, he decided that if she would not be of use to him she should be ruined. He devised this plot to crown her with the label of prostitute.

"At first I refused. He lied that it was being done for Chintamani's own good and for the good of her parents. I was deceived. I did not understand that he was plotting in order to seize Chintamani for himself. From the moment you and your friend came to the hospital, abused Chintamani with whatever words came to your mouths and left, my mind was no longer steady. My conscience spat in my face: 'Are you a woman too?'"

<!-- source: scan 111; printed page 110 -->

"That question echoed whichever way I turned. I ran to tell the truth... and before I could, all this happened."

Anandi finished through sobs. To Naina it was all one great puzzle.

"Then Chintamani is spotless?" he asked with a deep breath, pressing a hand to his heart.

"Certainly! Think a little yourself. What parents would boldly arrange a marriage for a woman ten months pregnant? What foolish father would write to the groom's family inviting them to see a bride in the last stage of pregnancy?"

"We came on our own!"

"Without advance notice?"

"We sent a telegram first."

"Mm... Is that not enough? If there had truly been guilt on their side, would they have kept the girl in the village after receiving the telegram? Would they not have sent her away somewhere?"

"That is precisely why we suspected they had sent her to the hospital!"

"Circumstance and situation have both turned into scheming villains in Chintamani's tragic history. I am ashamed and pained that I must speak so plainly to you. If you and your friend truly cared about a woman's future, what should you have done? When Sivanesar and Sivakami cried and pleaded so much, you should have thought at least a little. You should have sent your mother, or another woman, to examine Chintamani and find out whether she had actually given birth. Her physical condition itself would have revealed the truth. Without any of that, is it just to turn a pitiable young woman into a desert? You tell me!

"Even now everything is not lost. The power to restore beauty to Chintamani's life is in your hands. Change your friend's mind at once. Find a path to protect Chintamani from becoming a broken veena!"

<!-- source join: scan 111 ends `சிறிதா`; scan 112 begins `வது` -->
<!-- source: scan 112; printed page 111 -->

Anandi's words gave Naina Muhammad hope. He felt that through a hasty conclusion he had poisoned the life of a family woman, and he wept.

"All right, Anandi! Chintamani will be Azhagappan's wife. I take responsibility for that. The atonement for my hasty judgment will be the marriage of Azhagappan and Chintamani!"

"Appada! The burden in my heart has lightened. Swear to me—Azhagappan and Chintamani's marriage..."

"I will see that it happens! Anandi, consider that matter settled. Tomorrow we can speak about the matter between us that is still unsettled. You must be tired. Sleep."

"The matter between us? There can be no conclusion to it."

"What are you saying, Anandi?"

"I have understood you. I feel only sympathy for you."

"Why?"

"You are a very good man."

<!-- source: scan 113; printed page 112 -->

"And?"

"Handsome—affection itself—full of good character... I could go on describing you like this."

"I don't understand your preface, Anandi."

"I do not want to deceive you."

"You will not deceive me, will you? That is enough for me."

"You... me..."

"Why hesitate? I... love you. Nothing in the world seems higher to me than you."

"I am a rubbish heap! My life is refuse! Do not love me. Do not give me your affection. I am not worthy of you. For your beauty and your character, so many women would come running after performing penance for you!"

"Anandi! Instead of saying 'No' directly, are you speaking in circles? Praising me while lowering yourself—it sounds like the formal modesty of an eloquent and learned speaker before an assembly!"

"No. I have no such talent for speech. I only hinted at my condition, that is all. You are a young man from an honourable family. I..."

"What are you saying, Anandi?"

Anandi did not speak. She cried. Without realizing it, Naina Muhammad wiped her eyes and said, "Don't cry."

When someone is close enough to offer comfort, do affection and attachment not swell all the more until grief reaches its height? So it was with Anandi. She sobbed uncontrollably.

"I am poison! Untouchable!" she moaned.

<!-- source join: scan 113 ends `அருகே இருக்கும்போது`; scan 114 begins `பாசமும் பற்றும்...` -->
<!-- source: scan 114; printed page 113 -->

"What of it? I am a Muslim. Caste difference means nothing to me," Naina said.

"Neither caste nor religion made me untouchable. I made myself waste matter of this world. Please do not touch my tears with your gentle fingers.

"They are not tears! They are pus draining from the wound in my heart!

"Chintamani is not the stained woman. I am the ruined gem. For a noble man like you even to speak with me is a sin!"

Anandi's grief broke all restraint. Her throat closed despite her effort to control it.

Lines of anxious longing appeared one after another on Naina Muhammad's face.

"You are pure. Do not touch me!" she said, removing his hand. Yet the touch of her soft hand upon his raised in him a sensation that even an *amarazh* could not have produced. A string had been plucked in his hand and sounded within his heart. Naina stood motionless. His lips moved faintly.

"Anandi..."

"Do you know who Anandi is? She is a woman who has lost her chastity! Tiger's kept woman!"

<!-- source: scan 115; printed page 114; Chapter 13 carryover before centered 14 -->

She flung out the words as though possessed, then closed her eyes and fell back.

Her words struck Naina's heart like a hammer blow upon red-hot metal.
