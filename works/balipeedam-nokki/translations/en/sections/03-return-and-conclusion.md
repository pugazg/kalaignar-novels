# Return to the Sacrificial Altar — Conclusion

> **Translator's note:** This English translation follows the audited first-edition Tamil source preserved in this repository. It aims to carry Kalaignar's argument, rhetoric, dialogue, historical-cinematic form and political intensity into clear English without modernising or silently correcting the Tamil. The audited Tamil page records remain authoritative. `ராயசம் வெங்கண்ணா — தஞ்சை சரித்திரக் கதை` remains an internal film-like sequence of `பலிபீடம் நோக்கி`, not a separate work.

The picture is over. Have you learnt the lesson? How many of you are now ready to go towards the sacrificial altar?

Look towards the sacrificial altar. Upon the sacrificial altar...

Look at Cheran's honour lying reduced to dust. Look at the Nayak lineage lying destroyed. Look at Rajaraja Chola's treasure lying scattered. Look at Kamban's garland placed upon the sacrificial sword. Look too at the Kamarajs, the Kalyanasundarams and the Namakkallars standing with folded hands towards that sacrificial altar. Stop them. Sound the warning: danger. If they go beyond even that... you rush towards the sacrificial altar.

<!-- source join: scan 30 ends `கைகூப்புவதை`; scan 31 begins `யும்` -->
<!-- source: scan 30 / printed page 29 → scan 31 / printed page 30 -->

Let the blood you spill upon the Aryan sacrificial altar be an ocean of sweat that surged up because you shook its foundations.

Let the flood of your blood that courses across that sacrificial altar have swelled as you strike and break one of its corners.

Let your head roll upon the sacrificial altar. But not like Kamban's “blindfolded” nature! Let it be severed with eyes that spit fire.

Let your muscles be torn and flung upon the sacrificial altar. But not like Anjaneya's muscles without fury! Let them be severed while the heat of the blood of self-respect has not cooled.

Are you a community meant only to die?... Are you a people who fear... battle?... A sea of blood was your ancestors' swimming pool. Spears and swords were their playthings. The battlefield was the veranda upon which they crawled. The war camp was the cradle in which they lay!... O you who came by that path! O you born to rule! O you who dedicated Muthu and Natarajan to Tamil itself!

Look towards the sacrificial altar... Keep the hammer of rationalism in your hand.

<!-- source: scan 31; printed page: 30 -->

There are the signposts that tempted the people who once ruled the world and dragged them towards the sacrificial altar!...

Temples, agraharams, ornate monasteries, Bharatas and Ramayanas, old almanacs, moksha, hell and heaven—place all of them upon the sacrificial altar. Break them apart, shard by shard! Smash them to pieces, fragment by fragment. Burn them atom by atom. First, let that cruel sword of Hinduism be broken.

It is Hinduism that has pushed you into the *cheri* today. It is Hinduism that has made your women widows. It is Hinduism that has hidden your people. It is Hinduism that treats you like animals.

It is Hinduism that... builds temples and grows fat by devouring your accumulated wealth.

It is Hinduism that, in the name of Manudharma, has issued the prohibitory order: “Do not speak against it! Stand back!”

It is Hinduism that created the Ramayana and, to the point of saying “even if a sandal rules, so be it,” has allowed British imperialism to remain in place.

It is Hinduism that made you Shudras... handed you over into the hands of capitalists..... and made it possible for your blood to be sucked.

<!-- source: scan 32; printed page: 31 -->

Let that cruel sword of religion... be shattered into powder and go to dust under the swing of your hammer. Let the sacrificial altar burst “padaar.. padaar..,” its splinters flying as dust. Let the Aryan signposts that beckon with the eyes, kindle desire... and show the way.... to slit the throat become prey to fire.

Enough of being destroyed... now we shall rule. Enough of falling... now we shall live. Let your work go on—wherever! Let your army set out singing.

Aryan sacrificial altar......

There—the cruel sword of Hinduism! Sword  
We set out to destroy......  
Behold, they are dust! Dust!

✾

## Batch 6 translation notes

- Batch 6 begins exactly after the internal film's `வணக்கம்` end-card, at scan 30's `படம் முடிந்துவிட்டது. பாடம் கற்றுக்கொண்டீர்களா?`; the end-card is not duplicated here.
- The scan 30→31 continuation `கைகூப்புவதை` + `யும்` is translated as one continuous sentence and explicitly documented above.
- `பலிபீடம்` remains **sacrificial altar** and `ஆரியபலிபீடம்` / `ஆரிய பலிபீடம்` remains **Aryan sacrificial altar**, preserving the governing metaphor established in the pilot.
- The pluralized source forms `காமராஜர்கள்`, `கலியாணசுந்தரங்கள்`, and `நாமக்கல்லார்கள்` are represented conservatively as **the Kamarajs, the Kalyanasundarams and the Namakkallars** rather than silently resolving them into externally identified individuals.
- The unusual scan-31 verb forms `வெட்டப்பட்டட்டும்`, `கிழித்தெறியப்பட்டட்டும்`, and `கழிக்கப்பட்டட்டும்` remain untouched in canonical Tamil; English conveys their immediate imperative sense without attempting to reconstruct corrected Tamil forms.
- `பகுத்தறிவுச் சம்மட்டி` is **hammer of rationalism**, retaining the movement-era force of `பகுத்தறிவு`.
- `சேரி` is retained as **_cheri_** rather than flattened to a generic neighbourhood term inside the polemical sentence.
- `மனுதர்மம்` is retained as **Manudharma** and `சூத்திரர்கள்` as **Shudras**.
- The source phrase `செருப்பாண்டாலும் சரி` is translated literally and cautiously as **“even if a sandal rules, so be it”**; no explanatory Ramayana gloss is inserted into the prose.
- `இந்துமதக் கொடுவாள்` is rendered **cruel sword of Hinduism** and the later `மதக்கொடுவாள்` as **cruel sword of religion**, preserving the repeated weapon metaphor.
- The closing sound effect `படார்.. படார்..` is retained as **“padaar.. padaar..”** rather than replaced with an invented English effect.
- The closing verse preserves the source's repeated `வாள்` as **“Sword”** rather than silently smoothing the line.
- The printed floral ornament is retained as `✾`.
