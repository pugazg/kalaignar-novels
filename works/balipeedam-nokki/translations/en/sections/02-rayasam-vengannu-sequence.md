# Rayasam Vengannu

## A Historical Tale of Thanjavur

> **Internal sequence of _Towards the Sacrificial Altar_.** This is the film-like historical sequence introduced by the narrator on scan 7; it is not a separate work.

> **Translator's note:** This English translation follows the audited first-edition Tamil source preserved in this repository. It aims to carry Kalaignar's argument, rhetoric, dialogue, historical-cinematic form and political intensity into clear English without modernising or silently correcting the Tamil. The audited Tamil page records remain authoritative. `ராயசம் வெங்கண்ணு — தஞ்சை சரித்திரக் கதை` is retained as an internal film-like sequence of `பலிபீடம் நோக்கி`, not treated as a separate work.

### Erimalai ‘Release’

**Direction, screenplay arrangement, dialogue:**  
M. Karunanidhi (me)

**Distribution rights**  
Thu. Vi. Narayanan & Co.

Erimalai's fiery breath comes to a stop. Sweetness threads through the background music. The sky-reaching tower of the Pragadeeswaran Temple is shown. Then mansions, towers and the streets of Thanjavur sweep and turn across the screen. The background music stops.

A palanquin in the street!... The lines of sorrow spread across the bearers' faces tell of the pain in their shoulders.

“For the great Vaishnava Kumar Tatacharya...” shouts a man wearing a broad *namam*. A chorus of “Jai!” follows. The street reverberates.

The palanquin keeps moving. It enters the fort gate. The scene changes.

The durbar hall. Ministers and chief officials are seated.

<!-- source: scan 8; printed page: — -->

The durbar begins to cries of, “Long live King Vijayaraghava, warrior among warriors!” An aged king sits on the throne.

“Prince Mannaru Nayak is arriving,” announces a guard.

Brimming with youth and beauty, Mannaru Nayak takes his seat. A “tak-tak” sound is heard. Everyone turns towards the direction of the sound. Footwear appears. Then a foot. Then a *panchakacham*. Then a broad *namam*. Kumar Tatacharya, who came in procession in the palanquin, walks in with stately ease. Vijayaraghava Nayak, Mannaru and everyone else rise and bow. Worship is performed at the guru's feet. The guru speaks.

“Vijayaraghava! Why did you summon me?”

**Vijayaraghava:** Guruswami. Sokkanatha Nayak of Madurai wishes to marry our Mannaru's daughter.

**Guru:** A marriage matter?... Let it take place auspiciously.

**Vijaya:** There is no way for anything auspicious to happen.

**Guru:** Vijayaraghava?... What are you saying?

**Vijaya:** The king of Madurai... comes from a line that served Vijayanagara. To give him my granddaughter would be folly. A great disgrace!... So I refused.

<!-- source: scan 9; printed page: 8 -->

**Guru:** A girl must be given only after knowing the *gotra*... good!

[Mannaru Nayak interrupts.]

“There is no way even for happiness, Swami!... The king of Madurai has announced that he will meet us on the battlefield.”

**Guru:** Is there no way to peace?

**Mannaru:** Peace!... If I give my daughter to him in *kanyadanam*, there will be peace!... Otherwise, peace will sprout only in the flood of blood that rises on the battlefield.

**Guru:** Have you resolved upon war?......

**Mannaru:** Yes! As long as there is strength in these shoulders, there will be struggle. I have resolved to die with a spear in my chest; I have resolved not to lose my honour by giving my daughter to that *mitthanamathan*.

**Guru:** Make the right decision.

[King Vijayaraghava rises.]

“Swami... Madurai is preparing to spring upon Thanjavur with immense strength. Our army is very small. If we are to win the war... we need your blessing. Grant it.”

The king kneels and bows. The hall is utterly silent. Tatacharya begins to speak:

“Vijayaraghava! May you and your clan live! By the divine grace of Tirumal, who abides and sleeps upon the sea—

<!-- source: scan 10; printed page: 9 -->

—may your crown live! May peace prevail in your land! May your fame spread! May your life grow! May your reign lengthen!... Victory in war shall be yours!”

The blessing ends. A “kala-kala” sound is heard—the sight of gold coins being poured at the Acharya's feet! A flash on the screen. Terrifying music again!....... The war drum sounds. A single cloud of dust. Horses gallop. Cries of “Aa! Oo!” rise.

Cannon blasts roar like thunder. Red water runs through the canals.

Warriors with wounds in their chests, spears in their sides and lances driven into their heads fall broken to the ground with a “sada-sada” sound...... A bullet pierces one warrior's eye and passes through towards the back of his head. A pitiful sight!

Another part of the battlefront.

Vijayaraghava, his queen, Mannaru and Mannaru's daughter appear armed. Vijayaraghava asks:

“Where are the women of the inner palace?”

**Mannaru:** They are building a fire so that, if the enemies enter, they can kill themselves.

[Vijayaraghava, eagerly.]

“Mannaru!... Is your child Sengamaladasan safe?”

<!-- source: scan 11; printed page: 10 -->

**Mannaru:** He is with his mother. There is no need to fear.

Before this exchange is over, disaster-like sounds erupt. The fort walls collapse. Stone and earth fly through the air. A single sweep of flame!.......

A spear strikes Mannaru Nayak in the chest. His daughter cuts down the man who threw it and then herself becomes the victim of another spear. Mannaru's terrible death. Sorrow echoes.

The Madurai commander approaches to arrest Vijayaraghava and his queen. Vijayaraghava throws a spear upward and receives it upon his own chest. He falls. The queen likewise kisses death and falls upon the king's chest. From the enemy side, the roar of victory resounds like the sea.

We see the mortally wounded King Vijayaraghava in “close-up.” His lips murmur:

“To behold Srirangam; in Thanjavur, a tower reaching to the sky; every day *santharpanam* for twelve thousand Brahmins; for Mannargudi Rajagopala Swami, a nine-gem armour, a diamond crown, temple enclosures, a flower garden, a tank, the Raghunatha *agraharam*—I performed all these acts of service. And today, seeing enemies enter my country—

<!-- source: scan 12; printed page: 11 -->

—I tremble. Were my charities, my acts of righteousness, my devotion to the gods all in vain? Tatacharya's blessing has dissolved like ash fallen into water!... Had I spent on enlarging my army the treasure I exhausted upon the gods, would this fate have come upon me?......”

Vijayaraghava closes his eyes. Blood pours from his chest in a rush. At the fort gate we see rows of warrior figures encircling it. The scene changes. Inside the palace! A fire-pit lashes about with tongues of flame in search of prey...... One after another, women leap into the fire.

Mannaru Nayak's wife approaches the flames with the child Sengamaladasan. Two waterfalls stream from her eyes. She gives the child in her arms to a companion.

“He is the one entitled to the crown. Keep him safe.”

Even as she says this, she comes near the fire-pit. She looks at the child once more. That is all—there is a “sarr” sound. Yes... the fire sucks away her tears just like that. The companion runs somewhere with the child.

She runs... runs... keeps on running. The scene goes dark.

## Batch 2 translation notes

- The scan-8 title card is reproduced prominently but explicitly labelled as an **internal sequence** of the one work.
- `எரிமலை ‘ரிலீஸ்’`, `டைரக்ஷன்`, `திரைக்கதை அமைப்பு`, `வசனம்`, and the distribution-rights line are retained as film-credit vocabulary rather than rewritten into ordinary prose.
- `மித்தானமத்தனுக்குக்` is a verified but lexically unusual source form. The English therefore retains **_mitthanamathan_** rather than inventing a historical or dictionary correction.
- `முச்சுற்றுப்படுத்திருக்கும்` is also retained in the canonical Tamil as printed. The English gives the cautious contextual reading **“encircling it”**; the exact Tamil form remains documented in the glossary and canonical page record.
- `கன்னிகாதானம்` is retained as **_kanyadanam_** rather than flattened to a generic “marriage.”
- `சந்தர்ப்பணம்` is retained as **_santharpanam_** because the source uses a specific ritual/service term; no expanded ritual definition is inserted into the prose.
- `அக்கிரகாரம்` is retained as **_agraharam_**.
- Cinematic cues such as “close-up,” screen flashes, background music, scene changes, bracketed action directions, and source sound effects remain visible.
- The dying speech across scans 12–13 is kept as one continuous quotation; no artificial closure is inserted at the scan boundary.
