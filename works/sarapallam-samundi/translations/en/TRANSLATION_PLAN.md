# English Translation Plan — சாரப்பள்ளம் சாமுண்டி

## Objective

Create a readable but source-bound English translation of **கலைஞர் மு. கருணாநிதியின் `சாரப்பள்ளம் சாமுண்டி`** from the audited 1978 first-edition Tamil layer.

Working English title: **Sarapallam Samundi**.

The Tamil archival title remains `சாரப்பள்ளம் சாமுண்டி`. The working English title is intentionally conservative and transliterative; it does not reinterpret the title beyond the source.

## Source authority hierarchy

Use this order throughout translation and review:

1. controlling source scan — `TVA_BOK_0064361_அரும்பு.pdf`;
2. canonical verified Tamil records — `../../pages/`;
3. PASSED assembled Tamil continuity layer — `../../sections/01-sarapallam-samundi.md`;
4. `../../FULL_TAMIL_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`, `../../audit.md`, page map and metadata;
5. derived English translation.

The canonical Tamil records remain final textual authority. The assembled layer is continuity support, not permission to repair or reinterpret Tamil. Never translate from OCR, memory, a modern retelling, another edition, or the 1978 `பெரிய இடத்துப் பெண்` witness material.

## Translation style

English must be **readable but source-bound**.

- translate every substantive narrative paragraph, dialogue unit, quotation, rhetorical question, list-like sequence and source note;
- preserve agency, sequence, emotional force, irony, humour, repetition, praise, accusation and rhetorical intensity;
- preserve the work's historical-fiction framing: narrative claims remain narrative claims and must not be converted into editorially asserted historical fact;
- do not modernize the worldview, explain history inside the prose, beautify into contemporary literary English, or simplify repeated rhetoric merely for fluency;
- do not silently repair unusual Tamil grammar or wording through English;
- where a source expression is genuinely difficult, prefer a conservative rendering and document the decision in `GLOSSARY.md`.

The translation should read naturally in English without becoming an adaptation.

## Names and transliteration policy

Use stable, readable Roman forms without scholarly diacritics in ordinary prose unless a distinction later proves necessary.

Initial locked forms:

| Tamil | English form |
|---|---|
| இராசராசன் | **Rajaraja** |
| இராசேந்திரன் | **Rajendra** |
| குந்தவையார் | **Kundavai** |
| கண்டராதித்தன் | **Kandaradittan** |
| பஞ்சவன் மாதேவி | **Panchavan Madevi** |
| இனியன் | **Iniyan** |
| அழகி | **Azhagi** |
| சாமுண்டி | **Samundi** |
| இராசராசேச்சரம் | **Rajarajeswaram** |

Do not silently substitute later/common names for a source name. In particular, `இராசராசேச்சரம்` is not to be replaced in prose by a modern temple name merely for reader familiarity.

Honorific or respectful Tamil morphology need not be mechanically romanized, but its social force must not be erased where it affects address or characterization. Any changed or additional name decision must be recorded in `GLOSSARY.md` and then used consistently.

## Political, religious and caste/social terminology

Preserve the source's political and religious rhetoric at the same force.

- imperial claims, conquest language, dynastic praise and comparisons must not be softened or amplified;
- references to kingship, empire, subordination, war, victory, temple endowment and royal authority must retain their source agency;
- religious language must not be secularized or converted into commentary;
- caste/community/social-status terms, if encountered, must be translated or conservatively transliterated without euphemism, modernization or added judgment;
- the narrator's historical or religious assertions remain the narrator/source's assertions.

No explanatory correction is to be inserted into the translated prose.

## Historical offices, institutions, ritual and cultural terms

Use direct English equivalents only where the meaning is safely one-to-one. Where an office, institution, measure or ritual term carries period-specific meaning that would be flattened by a generic English word, retain a stable transliteration and document it.

Special attention is required for terms such as:

- `மாராயம்`, `பிரமராயன்`, `பேரதிகாரி`, `சேனாதிபதி`;
- `தேவதானச்சிற்றூர்`, temple-service offices and personnel;
- historical measures such as `பலம்`, `கழஞ்சு`, `வேலி`, `கல்`;
- named `தாண்டவ` forms and dance-posture terminology.

Do not silently convert historical measures into modern metric units. Named dance forms should normally retain a stable transliteration with enough English syntax to make their role clear; do not invent iconographic explanations not present in the Tamil.

## Cinematic / visual vocabulary

This work is prose, not a screenplay. Do not invent scene directions, camera language, sound cues or visual narration.

Source illustrations — especially scan 28's large landscape spread — are not translated into prose. Visual or sculptural imagery already expressed in the Tamil text should be translated as prose imagery, not recast as stage/cinematic direction.

## Punctuation and dialogue policy

- use standard English double quotation marks for spoken dialogue;
- use single quotation marks for embedded quotation where needed;
- preserve speaker changes and paragraph structure;
- preserve emphatic repetition and rhetorical punctuation where it carries force, while allowing minimal English punctuation adjustment for grammatical readability;
- do not silently turn source dashes, pauses or repeated exclamation into calmer prose;
- preserve source lists/sequences as lists or rhetorical sequences when structurally meaningful.

Page/scan boundaries are not paragraph boundaries unless the source structure supports them.

## Source-oddity policy

Verified Tamil oddities remain protected. English may make their contextual meaning readable, but it must not silently rewrite the underlying Tamil into what the translator thinks it should have said.

Known protected items include:

- scan 29 / printed 26 controlling reading **`வாளாயிருந்துவிட்டான்`**;
- scan 34 `நிழலில் தானம்மா`, `மன்னர் மன்னு`;
- scan 36 `தொடங்கப் பெறுமைக்கு`;
- scan 41 `நீர்த்திவலை`;
- scan 42 `சிற்பங்களை யல்லவா`, `ஏதோ ஓர் ஏக்கத்தை ஏற்பட்டது`;
- scan 43 `தீண்டாக்குவானேன்`;
- scan 46 `எண்பத்தொரு சிலைகளும்`, `மானழியது`, `கைநெருப்பு`;
- scan 47 `ஒரு சிலருன்`, `நிறைவேற்று வதற்கு`.

If a fluent English rendering necessarily hides a source oddity, the source form and decision must be recorded in `GLOSSARY.md` or the relevant batch review rather than "correcting" canonical Tamil.

## Page traceability

English must remain reversible to the Tamil source.

- preserve source-scan HTML comments at meaningful boundaries;
- retain all physical scans **24–48** in order;
- preserve verified cross-page joins rather than inserting artificial paragraph breaks;
- where a Tamil word is split across scans, the English need not imitate the visual split, but the provenance marker must remain traceable at the corresponding source boundary;
- batch review must record scan coverage and any non-trivial join decision.

The final English section must cover **25 / 25 scans exactly once** with no duplicate or omitted substantive source text.

## Review states and gates

Use the Section 15 workflow:

`planned` → `draft-translated` → `source-checked` → `reviewed` → whole-work `verified`.

For each batch:

1. translate only the authorized audited Tamil scans;
2. compare English back against the canonical Tamil records;
3. check omissions, additions, speaker attribution, agency, recurring names/terms, rhetoric and boundary joins;
4. record terminology/source-oddity decisions in `GLOSSARY.md`;
5. mark the batch `reviewed` only after the source comparison passes;
6. synchronize `README.md` and `PROGRESS.md`.

After all five batches are reviewed, perform Section 16 whole-work bilingual review in `TRANSLATION_REVIEW.md`. Only after that review passes may the English translation be called whole-work **VERIFIED**.

Section 17 release-readiness remains a later, separate gate.
