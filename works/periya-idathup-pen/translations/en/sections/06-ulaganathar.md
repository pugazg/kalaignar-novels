## Ulaganathar

> **Translator's note:** This translation follows the source-audited Tamil preserved in this repository. Ulaganathar's accusations, wounded pride, self-justification and limited knowledge remain his viewpoint. They are not corrected inside this section using Kumudha's preceding testimony or Kannamma's later conclusion. Canonical Tamil remains authoritative and stays under the separate verification freeze.

It was Veeran the cartman! The very man who ate my salt has done this. What use is there in being angry with him? It is all the result of what was done that time. Kannamma. She said she regarded me as a god! Was it she who did this? Adulteress! How long have Veeran and Kannamma been involved? Could it be that she did not like my loving Kumudha? She could have told me directly! What unprecedented wrong have I committed? The hereditary honour of my family, which had lived earning the village's praise, has been stained! Oh God!

Could Kannamma think Veeran is more handsome than I am? What can I do about being ugly? I admit I am not handsome. Can anyone say I have no feelings? Kannamma! I trusted you! Woman-devil! One may even trust a snake's venom; your false words cannot be trusted. If you did not like me, could you not have told me then, you deceitful woman! You acted as though you were full of affection—Bhadrakali! Will God ever show you any good?
