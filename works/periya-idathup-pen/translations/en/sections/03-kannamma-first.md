## Kannamma

> **Translator's note:** This translation follows the source-audited Tamil preserved in this repository. The canonical Tamil page records remain authoritative. The English preserves the work's source-printed character accounts, rhetoric, colloquial language and historical-period vocabulary without silently modernising the Tamil. Yes! I am an adulteress. Is that my fault? If I had been married to the man who suited my heart, would I<!-- source join: scan 19 `என்` + scan 20 `னிலே` → `என்னிலே` --> have become like this? When I was a little girl, I was very good indeed. In the village, the very name Kannamma drew a special affection. I had immense devotion to God. Even if an appointed day slipped, my vows did not. I would eat only after making an offering to Ambikai. My heart was filled with the great ambition of becoming, in the future, a mighty “jewel of devotion.” At all times I would utter only the holy names of the deity. And what is different now? I am still always praising the Lord. Those who have done wrong must ask forgiveness, must they not! For the poisonous, disastrous things I have done, must I not ask God's forgiveness! But there is one thing. Do not let anyone pass over it without noticing! It is precisely because I have the courage that Bhagavan will forgive me that I still have not given up my deeply ingrained ways; why ask me about it! You will say, “An adulteress is preaching Vedanta,” and mock me for uttering words of wisdom. Do you want to know me properly? Here is the answer! Kannamma is an adulteress! Kannamma is a crown-jewel among devotees! Is that enough? If you want still more, here is the explanation. Listen!

My father was a wealthy man. My mother raised me with great tenderness. I too<!-- source boundary: scan 20 `நானும்` → scan 21 `நன்றாகத்தான் வளர்ந்தேன்.` --> grew up well enough. I do not know how it happened; one day I fell under Ulaganathar's gaze. “Do not go where men are,” my mother had often warned me. But the times were otherwise, and I became prey to his eagle-eye. Like an eagle lifting rats in the iron grip of its pincer-like feet, Ulaganathar seized me. How can I describe that burning anguish! Ulaganathar and I were married. Yes, I was bound into a contract called life. Was that a marriage? If blindfolding someone, leaving her in a forest and ordering her to fondle a bear is called “marriage,” then what happened to me was marriage too. If smearing poison on a spear and driving it into the eye is called a “life-contract,” then mine too was a life-contract! Ulaganathar appeared to me as a rhinoceros, not as a husband. He was the ugliness of the world risen into bodily form. What could I do? It is true that he had youth. But he had no pleasant face that could capture my heart! He had the vigour of youth, yes. But did that form have to put even a boar to shame? You will ask whether I should not have endured all this. You will even conduct Puranic discourses saying, “Even if he is grass, he is the husband; even if he is stone, he is the husband.”<!-- source join: scan 21 `காலக்ஷேபங்` + scan 22 `கூட` → `காலக்ஷேபங்கூட` --> If you were in my place, you would not speak so. As for Ulaganathar's ugliness, one word is enough: when he opened his mouth, it was a dry sound like something rising from the stomach of a man about to vomit. Must I call that the voice of a cuckoo? You give the verdict. Do not think I am praising myself. I was a girl from a rich house, with enough beauty; a girl just beginning to roam at the foot of pleasure in the surge of youth, one who had “thought thoughts upon thoughts,” who had built “castles” of unreachable hopes about the future, who had earned the village verdict, “Kannammal? Why, she has become a fine beauty! What a shapely body!” If in the end such a girl had to marry a hideous man, was that just? Even a tiny child would mock it! Her friends would spit “thu!”

Was this the age when Bhuma Devi burned with longing on seeing the Varaha incarnation? It was not even that! You call me an adulteress; was it not your Vaidika world that fashioned that life for me!

I grieved that a crookedness had entered my life; I withered. But I did not show it to Ulaganathar. I kept cursing my mother and father; if only my curses had had<!-- source join: scan 22 `மட்` + scan 23 `டும்` → `மட்டும்` --> power, this whole world would have been destroyed. Dowry goods poured in from my parental home. Beds, earrings—what did they not give! They simply followed custom; that was all! I needed none of the things they poured out in heaps. Those “parent-demons” who would not find me the man who suited my heart—what use was anything they gave me? If only they had given me one man as a companion! Dowry, indeed! Was dowry what I needed!

I looked. I stepped forward to let the cartman Veeran drive away my anguish. I sighed in the pride of victory. I rejoiced as though, after crossing many moats and suffering great hardship, I had captured a foreigner's fort. Along the street of my heart marched an army of thoughts to the thunder of victory drums. My youth, which had lain bent down, crowed, raised its flag and set forth. Are you angry and saying, “You whore! Have you started boasting?” Wait a little. Look here at the differences between Veeran and Ulaganathar!

Ulaganathar had only youth; Veeran had beauty worthy of that youth! Ulaganathar was a round pot-doll; Veeran was handsome in form! Ulaganathar was a madman; Veeran a man of sense! Ulaganathar had wealth, but no charm! Veeran was poor<!-- source boundary: scan 23 phrase continues on scan 24 -->, but what I wanted was a man rich in beauty. Was that not it! Ulaganathar's voice was nothing but a rasp; Veeran's tone carried the sound of a veena! An owl was a fine comparison for Ulaganathar's eyes; Veeran's eyes were spears that stabbed my ribs and raised pain! Ulaganathar's row of teeth was frightening to behold; Veeran's teeth were a string of pearls!

Why say more? Veeran carried me aboard Indra's vimana, to make a pleasure-tour through the city of delight.

Ulaganathar never knew that Veeran and I had a relationship. I behaved so that he would not know. I behaved like his loving wife. I told him I regarded him as God. I pressed his feet to my eyes. I put on the pretence of a loving, chaste wife. Ulaganathar continued to trust me. If I had not played out all this drama, would he have trusted me? He would say his head hurt; I would summon up grief as though the pain were mine, and he would be thoroughly deceived. Let him be deceived! What was it to me!

Did Veeran fall into my net easily? Oh, no! What difficulty! At first he looked at me as though to say, “The lady of the house is calling me?” He was troubled that this was a matter of a great house. How was he to know the saying, “A graceful coiffure, thazhampoo and all; inside it, lice and nits”?<!-- source join: scan 24 `‘ஒய்யா` + scan 25 `ரக்` → `‘ஒய்யாரக்` --> Poor man!

Somehow the thing succeeded. My husband would often go out of town. Was taking the cart to the railway station the only job of the cartman who stood at the gate with folded hands? The work of circling around me was added to it. Ulaganathar was away in town! Veeran and I were in Pleasure-Town! Was he not off in some forest or upland? We had no worry about that—we were in bed!

At first Veeran was afraid to touch me! After touching me, fear that Ayya might see! Suspicion that word might spread! In the end, longing for the time when he would go out of town! In this state our love grew. One month, two months, then many months! Why, two or three years passed. In the midst of this another dreadful thing happened. Veeran and I together committed a killing too. We sent the two-and-a-half-month-old foetus growing in my womb to Yama's realm. Veeran bought the medicine and brought it to me. The fear that the child, if born, might resemble Veeran was the reason for the killing. Now you may say Kannamma is a murderess. I do not care<!-- source join: scan 25 `கவலை` + scan 26 `யில்லை.` → `கவலையில்லை.` -->. As though there were any shame left in my body! My mind had become numb!

A few days after destroying the child, a slackening arose between Veeran and me.

Suddenly Veeran stopped speaking to me. Whenever he saw me, he behaved as though he disliked me. If I asked him anything, he would snap out an answer and move away. Ulaganathar went out of town and returned any number of times. I spent all those periods alone. Many times I sent people to fetch Veeran. I summoned him saying there was urgent news. He would not move for any of it. I stared, not knowing the reason.

Seeing my agitation, my husband asked, “What is it, Kannamma? What troubles you?” “Nothing; my body is not well,” I would say. At once he would begin treating me. I had an illness he could not understand! There was one medicine he could never bring me to cure it. How could he know, poor man! A few months passed in this state.

Ulaganathar, who had been affectionate toward me, suddenly changed too. Always thinking; always disgusted; always irritable!

Even at an ordinary time one needed a thousand eyes to look at my lord. In this<!-- source join: scan 26 `நிலையி` + scan 27 `லேயே` → `நிலையிலேயே` --> state, need I tell you what he looked like! I did not understand why he had changed so completely. I trembled, wondering whether the “secret” between Veeran and me had been exposed. Or perhaps it was some new kind of illness, I thought. I could reach no conclusion. On one side, life without Veeran! On the other, the investigation of Ulaganathar! I stood between the two and suffered. I asked Ulaganathar again and again. He achieved only silence.

It must have been around nine one night. Ulaganathar came home from somewhere and sat on the swing. I sat beside him. I could hardly bear to look at his face. He had raised those vast sat-chit-ananda eyes toward his brows and fastened his thought somewhere far away. I began to act. I fell from the swing. Not truly—to deceive Ulaganathar. Ulaganathar scooped me up and stroked me. He may have thought his touch would remove my pain. How could he know that my heart was crying, “This useless fellow where Veeran ought to be!” We sat on the swing again. The swing moved; Ulaganathar began to speak.

(An old man named Uthandi, a talaiyari, worked on our estate. He had a<!-- source boundary: scan 27 phrase continues on scan 28 --> daughter. Her name was Kumudam. That Kumudam was beautiful. My lord and master had become infatuated with her. That “lord of beauty,” who could not satisfy me, had pounced upon Kumudam. A rich good man? Is it not the philosophy of the rich to fall in love with every woman they see!) He told me openly, “I want to dally with Kumudam.” I expressed my willingness: “Why not go ahead freely?” He said there was one obstacle. “What obstacle?” I asked. He told me. Only then did I understand the secret of why Veeran had forgotten me.

The Kumudha Ulaganathar desired had an Aththan. That man was Veeran, the beloved who had enslaved me. “They say Kumudha and Veeran are in love. They have decided to marry. The marriage too is going to happen soon,” Ulaganathar said. If Ulaganathar had seen the changes that appeared on my face at that moment, he would have been astonished. I felt as though I had died. Controlling my anger, I asked, “How do you know that Veeran and Kumudha are in love?” (My husband said he had seen the two of them talking secretly beside the canal behind the cultivators' street.) Ulaganathar did not stop there. “Kannamma! My<!-- source boundary: scan 28 phrase continues on scan 29 --> darling..... Don't think I am neglecting you.... Somehow, one day.... Kumudha must adorn my bed. You must do this.... Please! I clasp your feet. Won't you take pity on me?” he begged; he wailed; baring his teeth, he pleaded like a beggar. One thought came to me too; and it turned out to be a very fine thought indeed.

I decided that if I separated Kumudha and Veeran, Veeran would come near me again. If I handed Kumudha over to Ulaganathar, he would remain sunk in her. Veeran and I could build another inner chamber of our own, and he would not come forward to notice it. So I gave him the idea of bringing Kumudha to my husband. I had the courage to think that by making Kumudha an adulteress I could draw back the Veeran I had lost! Are you asking, “You traitor! Was it not enough that you were ruined? Must you do that to Kumudha too?” What could I do? What other way was there for me to reach my Veeran? Kumudha could show the strength of her chastity if she wished!

The next day dawned. Following my idea, Ulaganathar sent for the old man Uthandi and told him that “Kumudha should work in the house” and “stay for a few days<!-- source boundary: scan 29 phrase continues on scan 30 --> as company for Kannamma.” The old man smiled and faltered. Ulaganathar specified a monthly wage of twenty rupees for Kumudha. The old man was deceived. He had never seen money, after all—the poor wretch! He sent Kumudha to our house as a maid. I had seen Kumudha two years earlier. But now Kumudha was a young woman come of age. Her graceful walk and the radiance of her face made even me, a woman, desire her.

From the day Kumudha came to the house she behaved with great respect, but her thoughts were somewhere else. She could not hide that. She often went toward the doorway. She would look all around somewhere. Did I not know whom she was looking for! I was ready to sprinkle poison into the innocent love that little girl bore Veeran.

Two days passed after Kumudha came to the house. One night I began a conversation with her. Meanwhile I laughed to myself thinking of the state my husband had been in. Kumudha told me all the stories of her home. I kept weaving my suggestive words into her speech. Beginning here and there, I finally told her of the<!-- source boundary: scan 30 phrase continues on scan 31 --> desire Ulaganathar had for her. Kumudha said nothing. Without speaking, she went and lay down. After a while, all I could hear was the sound of Kumudha sobbing and sobbing. That night ended there.

The next morning, in the backyard garden, Kumudha was talking with her father. I feared the little one might escape. “Kumudha! Kumudha!” I called sharply. She came running. I did not fail to see her wipe her eyes without wanting me to notice. If it was so that I might get Veeran back again, what did it matter what I did to Kumudha? Was I responsible for the blame and sin that came to me? In a world where there is a law that “nothing happens as one intends” and a certainty that “not an atom moves without Him,” was I not an innocent woman! With that courage I mixed poison into Kumudha's good life. My body did not tremble while I did it. It was on the very night after Kumudha had met Uthandi in the garden that I carried out that plot. In your language it may be a “plot.” But that very act was the great revolution that turned my fate into a blaze! What if I killed Kumudha for my own selfish interest?
