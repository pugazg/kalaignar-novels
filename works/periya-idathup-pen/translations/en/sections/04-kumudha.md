## Kumudha

> **Translator's note:** This translation follows the source-audited Tamil preserved in this repository. The canonical Tamil page records remain authoritative. The English preserves the work's source-printed character accounts, rhetoric, colloquial language and historical-period vocabulary without silently modernising the Tamil. They could have killed me if they wished. I would have died happily. Death at six, death at a hundred—death is death. But after desire for life had arisen in me, finding the courage to die no longer seemed an ordinary thing. What Kannamma did turned me into a woman of courage. I became ready to play with death. You will wonder, “Why did Kumudha come to this decision?” First read this account of me.

I am Uthandi's daughter. My mother died when I was still a child. In a way not customary in my community, I was educated up to the fifth standard. It is with that elementary education that I am writing this letter. But I have one sorrow. Even if my Aththan does not read this letter, let it fall into someone's hands and let it establish that I am innocent and without guilt. Will there not be at least one person in the world who will say, “Kumudha was a blameless woman”?

I am an educated woman. Yet, though educated, I am a woman of deeply ingrained ways. You must believe that I am not one of those women whose minds have been turned into monkeys by the destructive works of “civilisation.”

So many maidens in this world ruin their own lives by their own doing. The condition that befell me was not something I sought out for myself.

Veeran is my aunt's son. Aththan was not a man I disliked. He was fit to be my companion in life. I believed I could pass my days happily with him. There was no mistake in what I thought. It was trusting him that turned out to be foolish. Only after my father said he would give me in marriage to Veeran did I begin to associate with him. Until then no improper thoughts had entered my mind. I need not speak of Aththan's charms. If I say that I had become his slave, is that one word not enough? If only to make sure Aththan's desire for me did not change, I would sometimes adorn myself. Did I have diamond ornaments? The jasmine growing in the backyard and the thazhampoo from the garden were my ornaments. My hands knew only glass bangles.

Somehow Aththan and I became one. He truly loved me. Whether he knew what love was or not, I do not know! But he had the power to enslave me. Always<!-- source boundary: scan 33 phrase continues on scan 34 --> I had seated Aththan upon the altar of my heart and worshipped him. No one could even look into that place. It is that same Kumudha who now shamelessly writes this letter. Am I writing a letter? No, I am screaming! Is this a letter? No, a reservoir of tears! Are these letters of the alphabet? No, drops of blood bursting out after breaking open my heart!

That night........ Ayyo......... even thinking of it makes my body tremble! So as not to spoil my father's wish, I went to Ulaganathar's house as a maid. Kannamma tried to make me a sacrificial goat for him. One night she brought up that subject. I do not know why she felt the need to find a woman for her husband. She spoke all manner of coaxing words. She treated me with studied delicacy. At first I did not understand that she was sprinkling turmeric and garlanding the goat that was about to be slaughtered. When I understood the matter, I sobbed. I planned to tell Veeran and escape. I could not find Veeran at all. Appa came. I could not tell him the whole matter. Before I could, the lady of the house, Kannamma, called me; the moment had passed.

It must have been ten that night. Kannammal said Ulaganathar was not in town.

I felt a little relieved. I decided that somehow, in the morning, I would run back to my own house, and I was happy.

Kannamma then gave me an order. It was an ordinary task; that was what ended as the plot. An order had been issued for a child to crawl into the wide-open jaws of a tiger.

“The silver cup is in that room. Bring it, Kumudha!” was all Kannamma said. I entered the room to fetch it. Suddenly the doors closed. I turned around; Ulaganathar stood there. He did not merely stand; he laughed loudly. It sounded as though a demon were shrieking, “You have lost, Kumudha, you have lost.” My state collapsed. The whole world spun round and round. It seemed as if a terrible sound rang through the open sky. Many flashes of lightning! Roars of thunder!

I do not know what happened after that. I opened my eyes and looked around. I realised I was lying on a bed. Ulaganathar lay beside me. He smiled as though he had just gone around the very orbit of the sun; he said all kinds of things. None of it reached my ears. The blood-soaked claws of a demon of lust-darkness had torn my heart apart. The sharpness of<!-- source join: scan 35 `அத` + scan 36 `னுடைய` → `அதனுடைய` --> its back teeth had split me open. I was now a whore! An adulteress! A man had tasted Kumudha's body. That madman had not enslaved my inner self; but could I therefore proclaim myself a pure woman?

In the Ramayana, they say Sita escaped by saying, “Rama Prabhu! Ravana touched only my body; he did not touch my heart.” I did not think of winning a good name from Veeran that way. Should the guileless Veeran touch the Kumudha whom another man had touched? Should I offer Aththan another man's leftovers? He was beside me; I looked. He smiled a smile of victory; I did not rage. He embraced me; I submitted. It was an act of force, a violation! I had no courage at all to resist. You will ask, “What, Kumudha! Could anything happen against your will?” It is true. But it happened—what can I do?

I had become milk mixed with sourness; food mixed with poison. But I did not intend to live as an adulteress! Can you not pity me for that? Can you not forgive me? Read my letter once again. Kannamma deceived me! The “woman of the great house” did this; the lady of a wealthy house plotted it. I had read the story of Nalayini, who carried her husband to a courtesan's house<!-- source boundary: scan 36 phrase continues on scan 37 -->. I never imagined that this Kali-age Nalayini would make me an offering to her own husband.

What life is left now for this adulteress! Kumudha, who had never worn a diamond jewel, ground a diamond itself into powder and ate it. Yes, I crushed the diamond from the lady of the house's nose-stud and swallowed it. Let the nose-stud of the very lady who sent me to her husband's bedchamber send me to Kailasa.

Aththan! Forgive me. Kumudha has become soiled; she has been stained. Forget her!

Human society! Will you not, at least from now on, stop producing Ulaganathars! Will you not stop sending Kannammas into the world? On that plea, I offer my life to you. I give my body to the earth.

Aththan! Aththan!!

Yours,

The one who is about to die,

Kumudha
