# The Woman of the Great House

★

“Kumudha! Is he her very nectar?” Lost in that thought, Ulaganathar sat on the swing. “Why, is all the work finished?” Kannamma asked as she came and sat beside him. The swing moved fast. Yet it did not seem to Ulaganathar to have outdone the agitation within his heart. Kannamma's hands moved over Ulaganathar's body. Even when she pressed his shoulders with her ornamented hands, no light of delight fell upon the curtain of his heart. Ulaganathar's thoughts wandered through the slum at the far end of the village; Kannamma would shine before him as his golden woman. Her gentle touch, the laughter that scattered his thoughts, the luxurious amorous play that carried him to heaven—he would lie enthralled by all of it.

But the sight he had seen that day alone was bitter as neem to him. “My dear ………… what's with you?” Kannamma asked, turning Ulaganathar's face toward her.

At the mere touch of Kannamma's finger, Ulaganathar would normally turn his neck like iron drawn by a magnet. That day he remained stubbornly still, without moving his face. Determined to see it through to the end, Kannamma got down from the swing. The swing, still moving from Ulaganathar's push, knocked Kannammal down. Kannammal fell. The blow was not much. Even so, she poured out pain as though she had slipped from the summit of Everest.

Compassion arose in Ulaganathar. “Kannamma! Kannamma!” he cried as he lifted her. The swing began to move again.

Ulaganathar and Kannamma began their journey through the land of pleasure. Ulaganathar—yes! If a convention were called, they might even confer on him the title “King of the Mandi Shop.” He was that great a merchant. And Kannamma—was her family not close to his? Wealth joined hands with wealth. Money rubbed against money. Four years! Those were the years that had passed since Ulaganathar and Kannamma were married.

Ulaganathar was somewhat thick-skinned! Not because he lacked pride; his body was simply made that way! Kannamma was a rose! They married her to him<!-- source boundary: scan 9 ends without visible terminal punctuation; scan 10 begins directly -->, and the world did not fail to talk about it. Can the chatter of common folk climb the steps of a rich man's house? Kannamma's life had indeed been bitter. But the plan she fashioned for herself lifted her broken life and straightened its hunch. Ulaganathar's cartman, Veeran, was a handsome man. Veeran caught Kannamma's eye. Was that his fault? Or was it the fault of Ulaganathar's withered looks? Veeran did not pursue that inquiry. He licked the honey smeared on his lips; he tasted it; he went looking for the honeycomb. Would a cat that had acquired the taste keep from leaping at the hanging pot? While Kannamma was troubled by the thought that she ought to live in chastity, he said, “Why all this ceremony when we're going to die!” Her attentions to Ulaganathar did not diminish. Kannamma said she was offering him her love. In that drama Ulaganathar's king's part carried grandeur. Yet he was in such a state that he might even wonder whether a man was playing the female part! He had time to taste Kannamma's cheeks! He never thought to test her mind.

He never imagined that a daughter of a wealthy gentleman's house could be small-minded. Veeran alone knew the pangs of her longing!

Ulaganathar had a bed! There were caresses! But all of it was merely what remained to be passed on to Veeran!

With Ulaganathar there was dancing! There was swaying! But the customer for that entertainment was the cartman Veeran!

With Ulaganathar there were lovers' quarrels! Union! Song! Yet even then Kannamma had the ache of not seeing Veeran!

Pleasure was exchanged with Ulaganathar! There was dalliance! Everything was there! But these were rehearsals for the performance with Veeran.

Veeran the cartman was quite a man; solidly built, with a body seasoned by hard work. There was always a distinctive radiance on his face! Even the way he shouted “Hai!” at the cattle sounded like music. When he cast a casual glance, one could forget that he was a labourer. A distinct beauty danced in his smile. A small budding moustache! Movements that always seemed to be in a hurry!

Was this not enough to stir Kannamma's mind? At first Kannamma thought she must live in chastity. But Ulaganathar's grotesque form shattered her resolve. Veeran's bark of “Kan!” made the bullocks quake. To Kannamma, that same sound was a song. The cartman's glare made the maddened cart-bullocks shudder; to Kannamma it looked like a majestic gaze. His whip-strokes gave the bullocks pain. In that sound Kannamma thought his strength lay hidden.<!-- source join: scan 11 `கிடப்ப` + scan 12 `தாகக்` → `கிடப்பதாகக்` --> Kannamma's embrace was bliss to Veeran. But had he not become the master of that life? What had never been his to taste, he tasted; what could not be obtained was obtained; what could not be done was done; he had become the object of the lady of the house's desire! The connection between Veeran and Kannamma turned into an affair of discretion. Like a cat that shows no fear while leaping for the hanging pot but becomes afraid once it reaches it—like a thief who is unafraid while boring through a wall but grows afraid after entering the house—Veeran, once caught in Kannamma's bed, began to tremble for fear of the village! News from Ulaganathar's house spread through the village. Would the village revile them? What if the master learned of it? Those questions put a veil over Veeran's daring.

After that, things came to this: there were bullocks with the cart; there was Veeran the cartman! Whenever he saw Kannamma, he would turn his face away and leave. Unable to understand the reason, Kannamma stared in bewilderment. She could write a letter, she thought! She had the thought, she had the time, she had paper, she had a pen—but Veeran did not have even the scent of an education! Kannamma passed the time with a troubled heart. Until Veeran's estrangement ended, she continued to stage only rehearsals of the pleasure-drama with Ulaganathar.

Then, one day, the Ulaganathar–Kannamma swing episode took place. Only after Kannamma pressed and pressed him with questions did Ulaganathar open his mouth.

An old man named Uthandi worked as a **talaiyari** on Ulaganathar's estate. His daughter was Kumudha. Kumudha was a shortened form of the name Kumudam. Veeran was Uthandi's sister's son—Kumudha's **Aththan**, her cross-cousin and prospective husband. It was on the day Ulaganathar saw Kumudha and Veeran together in intimacy that the conversation on the swing took place.

Ulaganathar lusted after Uthandi's daughter. He put a higher price on the pleasure dripping from Kumudha's kovvai-fruit lips than on the nectar of love Kannamma gave him. For all that, this great soul was not even much to look at. He mistook as the summit of love the affection Kannamma showed him—whether because she thought, “Whatever else, he is the husband I was married to,” or because she feared the village, or desired the property. He concluded that it was enchantment with his beauty. He dreamed that Kannamma regarded him as God.

Like a priest who, saying that an offering is for God, lays out “pongal, pallayam, pavadai” and feeds it to golden beauties who suit his fancy, Kannamma too offered herself as **naivedyam**, a ritual offering, to her husband and gave the pleasure to Veeran. That gullible fellow knew none of it. Poor man! God at least is a stone idol—without eyes! This husband had neither sense nor attention!

<!-- source: scan 13; printed page: 12 -->

Veeran had once held out his tongue for the **prasadam** shown to this god. That was then! But now the same Veeran counted the days until he could put into practice, with his aunt's daughter Kumudha, the lessons the lady of the house had taught him.

Ulaganathar's heart burned and his body grew thin, so much desire had he poured toward Kumudha.

Kumudha was only a girl from a cultivator's household. Even without adornment she was beautiful. Even without “make-up,” her body had a graceful charm. In short, Kumudha was a cool breeze!

Veeran had a right to have her as his. Not merely because he was her Aththan; he was also a sound match for her. Kumudha did not know that Veeran was the lady of the house's favourite. She was a tender young girl, after all! She trusted Veeran. Veeran too dared to accept her as his companion in life.

Into this stood Ulaganathar. A little fox eager to taste Kumudha? “I must somehow have Kumudha”—he spat that thought out to Kannamma. Yes! It was while sitting on the swing that he opened his heart. He took out what he had hidden and held it before her. “Kannamma, my darling.. Don't think I am neglecting you<!-- source join: scan 14 `நினைக்` + scan 15 `காதே!` → `நினைக்காதே!` -->.... Somehow, one day.... Kumudha must adorn my bed.... You must do this.... Please! I'll clasp your feet. Won't you take pity on me?” he pleaded.

What happened after that? Each of them tells that history. Listen!
