# They Came There!

Kuruvichchi came quickly toward Pandaraka Vanniyan, who was walking on the upper terrace of the palace, admiring the beauty of the sun reddening like a vermilion mark on the forehead of the east.

“What, girl, have you only just got up?” Pandarakan asked, as though teasing her for waking late.

“No! I did not sleep properly at all last night. I merely lay there with my eyes closed.”

“So you must have fallen asleep from exhaustion at dawn, didn't you?”

“I did not sleep at dawn. I did not sleep even after daybreak.”

“Lies! Lies! You slept soundly, snoring away, and now you lie to me?”

“So my snoring from below the palace was heard by you while you were sleeping on the upper terrace, was it? Perhaps you removed just your ears and sent them off to investigate?”

Pandarakan laughed. Kuruvichchi Naachchi smiled too.

“Kuruvichchi! I forgot to ask! Some of our spies should have arrived at dawn. Did they come? Do you know?”

“I came running here with the very news those who arrived brought!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 226 → scan 227 -->

“What did they say? What else is supposed to have happened in Kandy after Piyasili's death?”

“They say that while remaining in Colombo, Muthusami proclaimed himself king of Kandy and had the news spread in Kandy!”

“A complete madman!”

“Why say that so quickly? Muthusami must have made such a proclamation believing that he has some backing!”

“What great backing? The military strength of that British-imperial fanatic Lord North is the backing he trusts! With that, can Muthusami sit in Colombo and rule Kandy? Kuruvichchi! Do you know what this is like? It is like a man utterly gone mad standing on the earth and declaring, ‘I am ruling the solar realm!’ A country cannot be conquered by an army alone! If the people of that country come together and stand on the battlefront with their chests held high for their freedom, it is impossible to defeat and enslave them! No just ideal has ever been destroyed forever! The people of Kandy must have clapped and laughed when they heard the proclamation of Muthusami, who lies enslaved to Lord North!”

“Yes! The spies said that too. They say the people of Kandy laughed when Muthusami's proclamation came out! They rose in anger! Young men are writing on the walls that Muthusami has lost his mind! They say even Minister Pilimathala condemned Muthusami's proclamation and addressed a gathering of the public!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 227 → scan 228 -->

“Is that so? That is surprising!”

“The spies say that at the gathering held at the palace entrance, Pilimathala's emotional speech and the oath of royal loyalty taken before him by all the people were very moving!”

“The public, all right! What surprises me is that Pilimathala too condemned Muthusami's proclamation!”

“The spies said one more thing! In some circles in Kandy, Muthusami's proclamation has created confusion!”

“What confusion?”

“Some among the people are saying that Muthusami must have made this proclamation with some definite aim, and that perhaps with the help of British troops he may invade Kandy.”

“Did the spies say anything else?”

“They say the parents of the Madurai princess have brought her to Kandy! Your friend Vikrama Rajasingan has married her in a very simple ceremony!”

“May that Tamil woman at least give peace in life to my friend of golden character! What is it, Kuruvichchi? Your face seems to change! Is it because the man who prays that all his friends should live good lives does not worry about our life?”

By then Kuruvichchi Naachchi had composed herself. She was anxious that nothing mistaken about her should arise in Pandarakan's mind.

<!-- source boundary: scan 228 → scan 229 -->

“My face has not changed at all! Are you imagining things? When have I ever worried about my own life? Your life is my life! The goal of my life! I am one who understands that only if you live will the Tamil people in Lanka live!”

“Do you think I alone have no concern for your life? Our ideal will certainly be fulfilled! Their dream of bringing Kandy under control will fail, their drive to crush Mullaitheevu will fade, and the British will have to run away. After that, there will be no obstacle to the next stage of our love!”

“I have no doubt about the victory of our ideal. Still, I fear that the traitors who move among us and betray us may damage our victory.”

“Betrayal—treachery—these did not arise today or yesterday! They have been practised for centuries, even from the age of the epics! Without wavering at the sight of traitors and betrayers, we must keep moving forward on our journey toward the ideal. Even if the seed of longing for freedom that we sow today does not sprout, flourish and stand as a great tree in our own time, we must keep moving forward in the faith that it will happen at least after us.”

“When we hear that because of Ettappan's vile betrayal the hand of the British Company grows stronger day by day in Tamil Nadu, while the strength and protection of Panchalankurichi decline, we grieve, ‘Alas, Tamil Nadu!’ And we shudder to ask—will the same happen in Eelam too?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 229 → scan 230 -->

“Kuruvichchi! Listen, I will tell you one thing with certainty! It is wrong to think that the person who plants a seed must be the one who eats the fruit of that tree! Even those of us who labour for the future—for that future homeland of freedom—must not desire to live until it is established and only then die! If the sacrifices we make and the work we do today succeed in the future, then even if we are not alive then, we will live in the hearts of the people! However long it may be, a human being is going to live only a hundred years. If he wishes to live beyond that, he must build the foundation for it while he is alive. Since the human race first appeared in this world, countless crores upon crores of people have been born and died. Yet only a few still live in the lines of history! That is why, Kuruvichchi, even if you and I never pass beyond this stage called love into the next stage of sexual union, and instead give our lives for our ideal, we will still be living hundreds of years from now.”

“Even if people say that Pandaraka Vanniyan and Kuruvichchi Naachchi lived and died for the protection and freedom of the country without letting sensual pleasure come near them, that too would be a matter of pride for me!”

said Kuruvichchi. Tears appeared at the corners of her eyes. Pandarakan wiped them away with his fingers and stroked her hair.

While the two were thus drawn into thoughts of their ideal and talking, they heard someone approaching. Pandaraka Vanniyan turned and asked, “Who is it?” A *kudukuduppai* fortune-teller stood there. By then,

<!-- source boundary: scan 230 → scan 231 -->

Kuruvichchi smiled softly and said, “Don't you recognize him? He is one of our spies!”

“Oh! Thanigai? The disguise is very good! I have learned from Kuruvichchi all the information you gave her.”

The spy bowed to Pandarakan and said,

“Two people—a man and a woman—have come from Kandy. They say they are husband and wife. The husband is mute. They want to see you!”

“Is that so? Come, Kuruvichchi, let us go downstairs!”

With Kuruvichchi, Pandarakan descended from the upper terrace of the palace.
