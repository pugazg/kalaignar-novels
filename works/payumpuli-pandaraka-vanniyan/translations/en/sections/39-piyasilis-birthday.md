# Piyasili's Birthday!

After returning from Colombo, Pilimathala did not even go to the Kandy palace to meet the king. He went instead to his ministry and began attending to official business. The expressions on the faces of even the senior Sinhala officials working under him made it clear not only that they had been shocked by Pilimathala's conduct, but that they were also dissatisfied with him. Even so, they did not neglect their duty and carried out the work he assigned them. Whatever hostility Pilimathala might have felt toward Pandaraka Vanniyan, those Sinhala officials did not approve of betraying him to foreigners, nor of bringing down Vikrama Rajasingan in pursuit of the Kandy throne and subjecting Kandy to British domination. For that reason, they thoroughly detested the very fact that Pilimathala had gone to Colombo, spoken with North, and returned. One of the most important among those officials, Vijayathunga, who was also Minister Pilimathala's private secretary, decided that he would gradually make his views known to Pilimathala. But he waited for the proper moment.

For two or three days, Chief Minister Pilimathala's office functioned with an unusual bustle. Reading the many problems that had come to him through petitions,

Pilimathala, perhaps because he did not feel inclined to make a quick decision on any of them, briskly pushed the whole pile aside with his hands and stuffed it into the large wastebasket waiting there.

“What is this? It looks as though you have found an easy solution to every petition!”

Vijayathunga entered the room laughing as he asked.

Pilimathala sat like a statue, staring straight at him without blinking his bulging eyes and stroking his bald head in agitation. Was someone actually mocking him? And was the man mocking him his own private secretary Vijayathunga?

“Forgive me. You should not think, ‘Why is he speaking in such a strange way?’ It is not right to hide what one feels. For your own good and for the good of this kingdom of Kandy, there are some things I must say.”

Vijayathunga now clasped his upper arms with his hands and stood erect with dignity, like a man afraid of no consequence. A moustache like two lines drawn above his upper lip! A broad forehead! Deep, penetrating eyes! A tall, commanding build! He was fit not only to be Pilimathala's secretary, but his bodyguard as well.

Tapping rapidly on his bald head with his fingers like a scholar playing a clay-pot drum, Pilimathala rose arrogantly.

“What is this, Vijayathunga? Have you suddenly become a royal loyalist?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 191 → scan 192 -->

“If I had ever been a traitor to the king, only then could I have suddenly become a royal loyalist. Why invent what does not exist? I have always been loyal to the king! And not merely loyal to the king—I am loyal to this kingdom of Kandy as well! It is you who are engaged in betraying both the king and this region!”

“What? What did you say? How much insolence must you have to speak to me this way!”

“I am going to pour out everything in my heart! If you like it, accept it! If not, punish me however you wish for having spoken beyond my place! But one thing—do not suspect even for a moment that this Vijayathunga knows all our secrets, and that if you punish him he will expose everything. I swear to you: as long as you remain loyal to the kingdom of Kandy, I will reveal none of your secrets!”

Pilimathala was bewildered, feeling as though the man were killing him without killing him, and rubbed his bald head furiously.

“Vijayathunga! Do you have even a little sense of shame or self-respect? A Tamil named Vikrama Rajasingan rules the kingdom of Kandy! Should a Sinhala man like you or me simply stand and watch?”

“When did this new thought arise in you? The man ruling Kandy is indeed a Tamil! But for some reason you have forgotten that he is the son-in-law of the Sinhala lion Pilimathala!”

“Fool! Fool! Do you not even know why I made my daughter Piyasili Vikrama Rajasingan's wife?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 192 → scan 193 -->

“I know! If Muthusami came to the throne, he would directly become the pampered child of the British Company and begin governing the kingdom of Kandy. Your plan was to stop that for the time being, and first use Kannusami to do so. That is why you made him your son-in-law and placed Kandy's crown on his head. Is not your true aim that the crown should soon move onto your own head?”

“Bravo! If you understand my aim, why are you still needling me like this?”

“Because I am your private secretary, I can bury the secrets of your personal life in my mind. But if the consequences of your actions create a terrible situation in which Sinhalas and Tamils clash within the kingdom of Kandy, and the kingdom itself falls under foreign domination, then patriotic and king-loyal Sinhalas like me cannot keep our mouths shut and remain silent.”

“Vijayathunga! You say that Pilimathala, whose private secretary you are, should not receive the good fortune of ruling this country! Ah! What splendid loyalty to your master!”

“What use is it to sell one's eyes and buy a beautiful painting? If, according to the plans and intrigues you have devised, you succeed, the crown will be on your head—but the kingdom will have gone into British hands!”

“Is it so easy to govern the kingdom of Kandy without their help? Not only money, but their army too will always be there for our protection! Vijayathunga, you have not associated much with the British! They are very cultured people! They show us great affection and respect!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 193 → scan 194 -->

“They sprinkle turmeric and garland goats too! For what? To sacrifice them! If you understand that small example, this country will survive—and so will your honour! Please listen to what I am saying. Do not formally invite destruction with betel and areca!”

Realizing that there was no use arguing with Vijayathunga, Pilimathala struggled. He hoped that by yielding a little he could eventually bring him over to his side. So he decided to change direction.

“Vijaya! My trust in you is like the sea! Your attachment and affection for me are like the sky! There can be no difference of opinion about that judgment! You must approach this issue with the awareness that you are a Sinhala man. I will give you time for that. Think carefully. We must speak about this again in detail.”

“We must speak. Not for my sake—for your good and for the good of this country! But one thing: do not try to turn me to your side by showing me a distinction of ‘Sinhala’ and ‘Tamil’! The fact that the Sinhala woman Piyasili married the Tamil Kannusami, and that you yourself presided over that wedding, are not things that can simply be forgotten! Why did the Sinhala feeling that did not arise then suddenly burst through the sky and shower down now like stars?”

“I understand, Vijaya, I understand! You were the one who longed to marry Piyasili! You still have carved in your heart like an inscription the fact that she and I rejected you. Please turn that into writing on water and let it vanish! Where has Piyasili gone now? Is she not confined in this very palace? And is she not under the guard of officers who come under your supervision? If you wish to meet her, would you not know a way?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 194 → scan 195 -->

Vijayathunga wondered whether the man standing before his eyes was really Pilimathala, or some creature that felt neither shame, honour, pride nor self-respect. He felt like spitting “Pah!” right in Pilimathala's face. Paying no attention to Vijayathunga's feelings, Pilimathala smiled.

“Angry, Vijaya? Here is a cool piece of news! Today is Piyasili's birthday! The king has apparently ordered that for this one day she may move freely in the palace! There will be a feast and celebration tonight! You must come!”

“Why should I come? I will not! I cannot!” He turned to leave. At that moment, a flower-like hand, cool as a snow blossom, caught his forearm.

“Why? Should you not come to my birthday?”

As the question flowed like a stream of honey through the slit of her lips, Piyasili stood there, devouring him with her eyes.
