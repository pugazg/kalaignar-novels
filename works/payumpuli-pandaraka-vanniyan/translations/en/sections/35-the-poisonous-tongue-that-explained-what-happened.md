# The Poisonous Tongue That Explained What Happened!

Pandaraka Vanniyan bent close to the face of his beloved Kuruvichchi, who had opened her eyes, and spoke softly. He spoke with tear-filled eyes. He spoke tenderly. He spoke with a heart melting in emotion. He spoke as hope took wing within him that this noble woman, whom he loved as his very life, would now survive.

But without paying attention to what he was saying, she slowly raised her hands, stroked his cheeks with her fingers and asked, “You have not been hurt anywhere, have you?”

Would the loving hearts of women of character worry about themselves? Would not their concern be for the beloved man they had chosen? Stroking with his own fingers the fingers that moved across his cheeks, Pandarakan said, “Nothing has happened to me! Tell me how you are, dear!” Even before those pearls of words could fall from his mouth, pearls of water fell from his eyes and rolled down Kuruvichchi's cheeks.

“In the struggle for our ideal in which we are engaged, the two of us are bound to surrender ourselves to the jaws of death very soon anyway. My only wish is that I should go before you. And even if it should

happen, I want it to happen while trying to save you. If that wish were fulfilled even now, it would be supreme joy to me!”

“Kuruvichchi! Do not speak like that! Why should we die? We will never die! No history has ever portrayed those who gave their lives for a noble ideal as ‘the dead’. They are the ones who live forever! The ones who will live!”

“Somehow, what I thought would happen has happened! If only I had not come to Kandy, by now you... you...!”

Kuruvichchi could not speak. She sobbed uncontrollably.

“Yes—how did you come here? Why did you come?”

“Oh-ho! Are you asking how I could come after you ordered me not to come with you? I obeyed your order: I did not come *with* you; I came separately. You were not wrong to be angry with me! When I myself understand the wrong I committed, how could I be angry with you?

“Going to Kaakkai Vanniyan's palace in order to save my grandfather was indeed a grave wrong. Affection blinded my reason. We had been bound by love alone—not joined by worldly desires, not lying in base longing for bodily union—and could not bear to be apart even for a few naazhigai. If you suddenly wished to reject me and push me away, that was not your fault at all. It was mine! I realized that going to the palace of that Kaakkai Vanniyan, who has made betrayal a mastered art, and speaking with him was a betrayal of our ideal. I realized it—truly, I swear I did!”

Saying this, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar slowly rose despite her pain and tried to grasp Pandarakan's feet. Before she could, Pandarakan stopped her, eased her back onto the bed, patted her gently and calmed her.

“Kuruvichchi! Do not use the word betrayal! In your agitation to save and bring back Commander Sangili Thatha, your reason faltered. I too could simply have rebuked you and ended the matter there. I should not have issued that cruel command, ‘Do not come with me!’ All right—let us forget what happened. You have come here yourself now, after all!”

A smile blossomed on Kuruvichchi's face. She rolled her eyes toward the physician, Kannusami and Pilimathala. Then she took Pandarakan's hand and asked, “Yes—but why have you stayed so many days in this fortress of conspirators?”

Embarrassment spread across Pilimathala's face, though he did not let it show. Stroking his bald head and rolling his bulging, egg-like eyes, he cried toward the sky, “God! God! Is the Kandy palace a fortress of conspirators?”

Startled, Vikrama Rajasingan looked at his friend Pandaraka Vanniyan. Pandarakan immediately said to Kuruvichchi, “What you think is wrong! Do you not know that Kannusami, king of Kandy, is my dearest friend? If it were true that my friend had conspired to kill me, I would rather die. For just as Kattabomman and Vellaiya Thevan were friends in Tamil Nadu, we are friends of equal

<!-- source boundary: scan 175 → scan 176 -->

measure!”

Kuruvichchi answered Pandarakan as though accepting that point.

“I know about the noble friendship between the two of you. But I also know of the conspiracy being carried out to split that friendship, create enmity between you, and place the Kandy kingdom beneath their feet. The moment I learned of it, I disguised myself as one of the warriors of Mullaitheevu, set out for Kandy, and waited to protect you. Just as I intended, I have protected you.”

As she spoke, she pulled down the head of Pandarakan, who was bent toward her face, and planted a kiss on the crown of his head.

By then, the serpent-like nerves of the brain inside Pilimathala's bald head were throbbing rapidly and had drawn the outlines of yet another picture of conspiracy.

“Lord! In this world good people are considered wicked, and wicked people are described as noble! What a great tower of affection has been built upon the baseless lie that there is a deep friendship between Pandaraka Vanniyan and the king of Kandy. If we explain that this is not a tower at all but the lac palace from the Mahabharata, will these lovers believe us? Still, Lord, I shall at least blow the conch I have to blow!”

Thus Pilimathala began a magnificent performance. Pandarakan and the king of Kandy watched closely to see what poison would pour through the gaps between those venomous teeth. Clearing his throat lightly, Pilimathala spoke.

“Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar! You who have returned from the jaws of death may believe me if you wish. If not—

<!-- source boundary: scan 176 → scan 177 -->

“I do not care! You love Pandaraka Vanniyan as your very life. True. But what happened? The king of Kandy married my dear daughter Piyasili. My daughter Piyasili is a queen of chastity who does not so much as raise her eyes to look at other men! But Pandarakan had his eye on her. Without caring that she was his friend's wife, Pandarakan grew close to Piyasili. My daughter too, with a good heart, became close to Pandarakan.

“Meanwhile, my son-in-law, the king of Kandy, did not conduct himself toward Piyasili to the degree that satisfied her. In some torment of youth, my daughter Piyasili fell into Pandarakan's net. The king of Kandy discovered the two of them in an obscene state in a room of the women's quarters. As punishment, the king ordered his own wife Piyasili to kill Pandarakan before his very eyes, with her own hand! It was only to carry out her husband's command that Piyasili threw the dagger at Pandaraka Vanniyan. You intervened and saved him!

“Now tell me—who are the conspirators? Is not this Pandarakan a conspirator, deceiving you, in whom beauty and love are united, and yet not wishing to enjoy his friend's wife Piyasili as well? Is not the king of Kandy a conspirator for ordering his corrupted wife herself to kill Pandaraka Vanniyan, the man who corrupted her?”

Before Pilimathala could finish his furious speech, Vikrama Rajasingan, king of Kandy, suddenly sprang at him, seized Pilimathala's bald head in his rough hands and smashed it repeatedly against a nearby pillar. Pandaraka Vanniyan rushed between them and saved Pilimathala. Clutching his skull, Pilimathala—

<!-- source boundary: scan 177 → scan 178 -->

“Alas—God! The great sinners who dishonoured my chaste daughter Piyasili have split my skull as well!” he cried, collapsing weakly beneath the pillar.
