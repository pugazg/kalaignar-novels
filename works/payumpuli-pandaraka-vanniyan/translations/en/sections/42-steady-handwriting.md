# Steady Handwriting!

“On my birthday your golden hand has been stained with blood, Your Majesty!” said that enchantress Piyasili, trembling with distress as she took the king's hands and pressed them against her bosom.

Pilimathala was overjoyed! An enemy had arisen against him. He praised in his own mind the brilliance of his extraordinary brain: he had used his own daughter to trap that man and had placed the blame and sin upon Vikrama Rajasingan. The king had insulted and abused his daughter directly before him, and had treated Pilimathala himself with utter contempt. His plan was to take revenge for all this while smiling. Only one stage of that plan had now been completed. Many more stages still had to be crossed successfully. Though a great enmity writhed like a snake within his heart, Pilimathala wielded a smile as his weapon and spoke to the king with great feeling.

“Your Majesty! I cannot measure my happiness that this worthless wretch has received the punishment he deserved at your own hand. Today I have understood one truth. Whatever anger you may have toward my daughter, I understand that you cannot bear even the slightest harm to her chastity! Ah!

The sight of you springing up in fury to protect my daughter's honour and chopping off the head of this ungrateful dog—what a sight for the eyes! It is itself proof of Kandy's courage and sense of honour! Leave the task of removing the body of this lust-maddened donkey to me, and I ask that you and Piyasili go to the women's quarters. My daughter's young heart, having been shut away in loneliness and separated from you for so long, and on her birthday of all days, must be yearning to spend time happily with you, must it not? Forget what has happened and give Piyasili a birthday gift as well!”

Even as her father was speaking with the king, Piyasili pulled the king's hand onto her shoulder and began walking, drawing him along with her. At that moment her mischievous eyes seemed to exchange some secret with Pilimathala's equally mischievous eyes. He watched without blinking as the two left that place and moved toward the women's quarters. His next stage of the plan had to succeed—that was why.

Still lost in the emotion of having cut Vijayathunga down, Vikrama Rajasingan followed wherever Piyasili pulled him and reached her room in the women's quarters.

“What is this? Your whole forehead is beaded with sweat! Has your anger at that madman Vijayathunga still not subsided?” she asked, wiping his forehead and then placing a gentle kiss upon it.

Her hands moved to his neck too and stroked it.

“What is this? Your whole body is a sea of sweat! All right! All right! Take off these heavy clothes!” she said,

<!-- source boundary: scan 208 → scan 209 -->

and without waiting for his answer that skilful woman began removing his clothes. As she did, her passion-filled limbs brushed against his body again and again, carrying him away from the killing and back into the women's quarters.

“Piyasili! What is this?” he asked.

“Is it not a birthday?” That trickster winked.

“Does that mean I must be in the state in which I was born?” the king asked with a laugh.

“Oh! Is it not my birthday?” said that flirtatious woman. “Have you forgotten in your intoxication?”

“You are quite a person! You are pointing out that you still have not given me wine!”

The king kept looking at her. She lowered the light of the burning lamp.

“By rights, on my birthday should I not be like you?” she said in a melody of seduction, and became as he was.

Crown and palace, chariots, elephants, horses and infantry, a thousand men ready to run at a command, petty kings lined up to pay tribute—had not the bodily beauty of the dancing queen Menaka shaken even Viswamitra, who had renounced all those things and become a sage performing penance for an ideal?

A beauty like that had taken female form here, rising and falling like a wave. Skilled in all the arts of love, she moved before his eyes like a feast, took the cup of wine in her flower-like hand, reclined across his lap, and fed it to his mouth. When she made him her plunder in this way, what could that poor man do? Especially one whose mind was already somewhat unsteady in such matters! So he clung to her like a monitor lizard, his whole body burning hot,

<!-- source boundary: scan 209 → scan 210 -->

and lay there. Judging that the right moment had come, that empress of daring stopped giving him wine from the cup. Instead, she poured wine into her own mouth and let him draw it from her mouth into his. Doubly intoxicated, the king of Kandy surrendered on that battlefield of the bed and began to plead.

“Great king! You suspected me! Was that fair?”

“Wrong, my golden one, wrong! You are pure gold of the highest measure! In some moment of haste and anger I must have spoken that way. Forget all that! Come closer!”

The moment she realized he had become her slave, she moved slightly away to make him suffer with longing and stood beyond the bed.

In the dim light of the lamp, the beauty spread across her body was beauty itself.

They say the life of a painting gains prominence from the shadow-colour laid alongside it. In the same way, the shadows created by that dim light clinging to every part of her body made him writhe like a worm touched by fire.

Drunk, he leapt from the bed and sprang toward her. She playfully moved aside as though avoiding him, and he fell to the floor. She too fell beside him. The polished stone floor was smooth enough for the two of them to roll upon. Still lying on the floor, she took the wine cup and poured a mouthful into her mouth. But she did not swallow it. Yes—she swirled it in her mouth to show that it was for him! And was he not longing for exactly that? In a world where everything else had been forgotten, there was a little while of caressing on the floor—and his pleading.

“Uh-uh! All that only on the bed!” came her sweetly strict command.
