# Not a Warrior, but a Woman Warrior!

Pandaraka Vanniyan took leave of the king of Kandy, Vikrama Rajasingan, and prepared to depart for Mullaitheevu. Pilimathala's heart was pierced by the worry that his plan had failed. Piyasili, meanwhile, was deeply confused. On one side was the sense of defeat that even she, an enchanting rainbow of beauty, had failed to captivate Pandaraka Vanniyan. On the other was the troubling realization that her husband Vikrama Rajasingan knew all the details about her.

Pilimathala, who had vowed that he would separate the devoted friends and had withered at his failure to fulfil that vow, felt ashamed at the thought of how he could now show his face to the British. In this situation, Vikrama Rajasingan came with his wife Piyasili to the gateway of the Kandy palace to see Pandaraka Vanniyan off. Minister Pilimathala too stood beside the king, attending to the arrangements for Pandarakan's journey.

They behaved as though the distasteful incident of the previous night had never happened. But a different kind of wave was crashing within each heart.

*So I was fated to marry this woman! She thinks sinful acts are the steps to heaven, and has decided

<!-- source boundary: scan 163 → scan 164 -->

that love, desire and affection must all amount only to the cry of bodily union that enslaves men. And such a woman lives among us! Lord! How many glittering snakes like Piyasili have you created in this world in the form of beautiful dolls? She calls me her god, yet waits for the moment when she can strangle my neck! She says she cannot live even a moment apart from me, yet longs for the moment I leave so that she can enjoy herself with many others like him and compare the pleasures! And now this great sinner comes and stands beside me like a spotless fragrant sprig! Can she be forgiven? No! But what am I to do? She has ended up as my queen!*

—This was the lament in the heart of the king of Kandy.


*What use is all my beauty? None of it worked upon this Pandaraka Vanniyan! Artful beauty in my eyes! Twin-mountain beauty below my neck! Beauty of waist, beauty of both thighs—and the beauty of my walk: all of it was water poured upon barren ground before him! I thought he would spring upon me like a tiger upon a deer the moment he came, that in that leap I would receive all the bliss of heaven and give it back to him in return. All those thoughts have turned to dust! The Puranas say that even sages, speaking the language of desire to women with enchanting eyes, bowed their heads and fell into their laps. Yet this man has become a great sage surpassing every sage! A face like the moon in the sky! Lips dripping honey, forever sweet! Had he experienced me once, he would afterward have said, “Enchantress, the whole universe is defeated before you!” Father made the plan, and why did I agree to it? Father wants this land of Kandy. As for me, I wanted the world

<!-- source boundary: scan 164 → scan 165 -->

forgotten, and to lie upon this handsome man's body for even one moment. That was why I agreed to carry out Father's plan. I failed! How many men turn my beauty into poetry with their eyes! How many describe my limbs in every possible way and send me palm-leaf letters of admiration! When I see and read them, I myself feel proud of my beauty. But last night that pride was shattered!*

—This was the anguish in Piyasili's heart.


*I planned to use her to reshape the politics of this whole region! Who else but she could win success for my scheme? I know that there are many young men in Kandy who yearn merely to touch her feet and, unable even to do that, attain ecstasy by caressing her footwear! There are countless nobles who, bewitched by her exploits and forgetting their honour, stand in ranks ready to act before her like dogs awaiting orders. But my ill fortune is that this Pandaraka Vanniyan alone did not surrender to her. Though on one side I regret that my conspiracy failed, my heart cannot help secretly admiring this pure warrior who does not succumb to temptation!*

—This was the struggle in Pilimathala's mind.

*Deceit in the form of a woman! Conspiracy in the form of a minister! Friendship in the form of the king of Kandy! I expected that, at least after marrying my friend, she would become the beloved wife of a man seated on a throne, a chaste woman of character. Instead she wanders as the queen of nights, the dream-enchantress of young men, a woman who has learned the grammar of living exactly as she pleases! And Pilimathala tried to use her in his hunt for political advantage in this land and make me

<!-- source boundary: scan 165 → scan 166 -->

the sacrifice to his plan—the sinner! Thank goodness my friend trusted me! Our friendship survived! This country survived!*

—This was Pandaraka Vanniyan's sighing thought.


The four looked at one another as though nothing were unusual.

“Shall I go?” Pandarakan asked, and the king of Kandy embraced him. Tears in Pandarakan's eyes glittered like diamonds in the morning sunlight. The king of Kandy was in the same state.

“I am leaving,” Pandarakan said, looking at Pilimathala and Piyasili. He might just as well have said it to the palace pillars and departed. Neither of them even moved their head.

Pandarakan mounted his horse and urged it forward. The other horses prepared to follow. As Pandarakan's horse sprang away, Piyasili suddenly let out a demonic scream, raised the dagger she had concealed and hurled it at Pandaraka Vanniyan's ribs.

At that instant, a warrior on the horse beside Pandarakan sprang across in a flash and took the dagger thrust in the chest.

The warrior fell from the horse with the dagger embedded in the body, and the warrior's turban came undone.

Not a warrior? A woman warrior! Who was it?
