# Battle Within Kandy!

When the English army set out from Mahawilla Ganga toward Kandy, Muthusami hurried up with a small force and joined it. General Macdowall patted Muthusami approvingly and ordered him, “You must show us the way to enter Kandy easily and surround the palace without difficulty.”

Muthusami had already proclaimed himself king of Kandy and become a dependent crouching in the shadow of English authority. So he accepted Macdowall’s order with great eagerness, swelling with pride at the thought that he would now enter the city for the first time as its king. Muthusami’s force went ahead as though showing the way to the English army. Seeing not the slightest sign of resistance anywhere along the route, Macdowall looked at Muthusami in surprise.

“Are you wondering that we are advancing without any resistance? It is all this Muthusami’s work! I sent word beforehand to Kannusami and Pilimathalavai: Do not fight! Do not fight and be destroyed!”

“Who is this Kannusami?”

“You do not know? He is the man who rose as my rival—

<!-- source boundary: scan 402 → scan 403 -->

and who now struts about the Kandy palace under the name Vikrama Raja Singan. His own name is Kannusami! From today he will be Kannusami again, just as before!”

“Then perhaps he and Pilimathalavai have fled the city?”

“They will not have fled. They will be preparing to welcome us, bow down and surrender. You must spare their lives, send them to prison in Kolumbu, and declare this Muthusami king of Kandy.”

“There can be no two opinions about our need for a loyal servant like you. First let them surrender. After that, Lord North will attend to the formalities that must follow.”

Talking like this as they rode their horses and led the army through a landscape without opposition, General Macdowall and Muthusami reached the boundary of Kandy city. There was no commotion even at the frontier. Ordinarily a hundred armed soldiers stood guard at the entrance to the city’s boundary fort. But when Macdowall’s army—

<!-- source boundary: scan 403 → scan 404 -->

entered, there was not even a token guard at the frontier. Still on horseback, Macdowall turned toward his soldiers, waved his hand with arrogance and immense delight, and ordered them to follow him forward.

Not only were there no soldiers in the streets of Kandy; there was not even any movement of the city’s people.

Without pause or obstruction, the white army marched grandly into Kandy like floodwater rushing downhill.

A short while after they crossed the boundary, a Kandy warrior rode toward Macdowall’s force, saluted and stopped before them. Thinking they must remain cautious, the English soldiers raised the weapons in their hands. Nothing they expected happened. The Kandy warrior bowed his head and held out a rolled letter. When Muthusami moved to take it, the warrior ignored him and respectfully handed the letter to General Macdowall. The general read it and laughed aloud. “Bravo!” he said. Muthusami stared at the general’s face, waiting to hear what he would say next.

“The Kandy king, Vikrama Raja Singan, has decided to abdicate,” the general declared with grandeur.

“Then the truth already proclaimed—that this Muthusami is the king of Kandy—has been confirmed today.”

“Muthusami! Do not be so hasty. Vikrama Raja Singan has handed his position over to Pilimathalavai—

<!-- source boundary: scan 404 → scan 405 -->

and instructed him to remain completely loyal to us.”

“Then what is to become of me?” Muthusami cried out.

“Your fate is ruin!” came a voice. It was a woman’s voice. Everyone turned toward it. Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar stepped out from behind a great pillar in battle array, a sword in her hand. For one instant there was shock. The next, General Macdowall warned his soldiers. Kuruvichchi showed no fear. Holding her raised sword, she stood before them with her head high. Her eyes flashed toward Muthusami.

“Muthusami! Many thanks! Just as we planned, you have brought the English army inside Kandy city. The forces of Kandy and Pandaraka Vanniyan are ready to surround this army and smash it to pieces. Muthusami, for so cleverly leading Macdowall and his army into Kandy and trapping them, we ought to build you a shrine and worship you!”

At Kuruvichchi’s words, Muthusami began to tremble.

“Me? I acted according to your plan? What nonsense are you babbling, woman? Who are you?”

“Do you not know me? I am Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar! Macdowall, that letter you read—it was not written by the Kandy king Vikrama Raja Singan. I wrote it! Our plan was to let you enter Kandy, surround you and destroy you. This Muthusami gave us great help with it. That is why I thanked him first.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 405 → scan 406 -->

Kuruvichchi deliberately repeated that lie with great skill because she wanted the enemy themselves to finish off a traitor.

While Muthusami kept denying it, the doors of every closed house along the streets of Kandy opened. Warriors of Kandy and soldiers of Pandaraka Vanniyan poured out like swarming insects and began closing in, surrounding the English army so that it could turn in no direction.

Macdowall’s rage at being deceived boiled over. He became like a volcano.

“You played the friend and did a fox’s work, traitor!” he roared at Muthusami.

Before Muthusami could finish saying, “No, my lord,” Macdowall’s sword made his head cease to exist. Muthusami’s head fell one way and his headless body another as both slid from the horse to the ground. Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar’s heart cooled at the thought of the reward the traitor had received at the enemy’s own hand. Agitated by the loss of their leader, the men of Muthusami’s small force rushed at Macdowall. The Kandy warriors and Pandaraka Vanniyan’s soldiers used the fighting that broke out between them and the English soldiers to their own advantage and launched a great attack on the enemy.

Pilimathalavai stared in confusion. How had Kandy’s warriors attacked the English army without his knowledge? From whom had they received their orders? He ran to Vikrama Raja Singan.

Vikrama Raja Singan explained the matter to Pilimathalavai with great composure.

“Because Pandaraka Vanniyan’s army suddenly came to Kandy’s—

<!-- source boundary: scan 406 → scan 407 -->

aid, I myself made this decision,” he said firmly. Pilimathalavai immediately recovered his composure too.

“A good decision! A very good decision! I was worried that I might have to carry the entire burden of this kingdom. Fortunately there has been a sudden turn, and I have escaped it. Long live Pandaraka Vanniyan!” Pilimathalavai said. But the self-control behind those words was filled with poison to the very tips of his hairs.

Allowed to enter Kandy without resistance and then suddenly attacked, the English army was caught and shattered by that royal stratagem. Neither General Macdowall nor Colonel Barbut could withstand the sudden opposition. Even so, they fought savagely to take Kandy city under their control. The streets were heaped with the bodies of soldiers. At one point Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar became the target of Macdowall’s pistol. It was a matter of an eyelid’s blink—Kuruvichchi’s life would have flown away. But then something happened that sent Macdowall crashing down with his horse and fleeing from the place: Pandaraka Vanniyan sprang upon him.

After that came the arrows Kuruvichchi and Pandaraka Vanniyan rained upon the enemy, the spears they hurled, and the feats of valour performed by their soldiers. Unable to match them, the white army began to flee. Pandaraka Vanniyan dragged a deputy commander named Major Davy through the streets of Kandy and ordered that he be imprisoned. The others, thinking only of escape and survival, fled and hid at the Vattapelika ferry landing. At last—

<!-- source boundary: scan 407 → scan 408 -->

Pandaraka Vanniyan and Kuruvichchi Naachchi shattered the English invasion of Kandy through their skill and courageous hearts.

The Kandy king planned to hold the victory celebration in magnificent fashion in the presence of Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi. “Yes! It must certainly be held!” Pilimathalavai agreed, but the poisonous wind of “How can these sinners be destroyed?” kept emerging as his very breath.

Pandaraka Vanniyan’s soldiers surrounded the fort in which Macdowall had fled and hidden.

On hearing that his own warriors might take Macdowall’s life, Pandaraka Vanniyan hurried away in great haste to save him.
