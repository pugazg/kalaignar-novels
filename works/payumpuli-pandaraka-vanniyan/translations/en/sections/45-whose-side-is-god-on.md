# Whose Side Is God On!

Looking at Piyasili, who was disguised as Jeyaseelan, the young woman flicked her horse onward and asked, “Yes! How much farther do we still have to go?”

“Marthani! More important than how far we are going is where we are going! More important still is why we are going! So keep coming with me without tiring.”

“That is exactly what I am doing—and I am going to keep coming!”

“Just coming along is not enough! The two of us must also live as husband and wife! Don't forget that!”

“I haven't forgotten, Piyasili! Still, what frightens me is—what if one day our disguise falls apart?”

“Why say ‘our’ and include yourself? Ask what happens if this Jeyaseelan disguise falls apart! Don't be afraid! Your friend is not so easily fooled. If she were, could she have gone and wound flowers around the very sword of the king of Kandy and come back?”

“Only one quarter of that was your cleverness! Three quarters belongs to your father Pilimathala! Poor Bamini!”

“Hey, Marthani! Do you know something? When my father and I were devising that extraordinary scheme,

<!-- source boundary: scan 219 → scan 220 -->

if you had arrived at that moment instead of Bamini, you would have become the Piyasili burned in the fire! Luckily your life was strong and you escaped!”

“I had heard that palace people have no such thing as a heart. Only after seeing you and your father did I decide it must be true! Hmph! Who knows what date you have fixed for my life?”

“Mad girl! Don't be afraid! Everything happened in the palace of Kandy exactly as we wanted! From now on no danger is going to come to you! I guarantee it!”

“Piyasili! My family has served in the palace of Kandy since my grandmother's time. If that is what you wish, I am ready, in royal loyalty, even to give my life for your good!”

“Don't babble about royal loyalty! We are fighting against the king now. I may be that king's wife. But not only does he interfere with my freedom, it looks as though through his friendship with Pandaraka Vanniyan he will surrender Kandy's very freedom! That is why my father and I have devised all these plans and are labouring to protect our motherland.”

“Piyasili! What do you gain from the king of Kandy believing that you are dead?”

“If I had been lying alive beside him that morning, he would have put me under guard again. He gave me one day's freedom only because of my birthday! And even that was freedom to be only with him!”

“And within that very time you managed to play with Vijayathunga!”

“Even thinking of it now gives me gooseflesh! We decided to finish Vijayathunga off because he was opposing my father!

<!-- source boundary: scan 220 → scan 221 -->

We were afraid that if my father killed him, all the palace soldiers would rise against my father. That is why we arranged for the king himself to do the deed!”

“He was a fool too! If he had any sense, would he have kissed you in front of so many people?”

“Did he kiss me of his own accord? My eyes ordered him to do it! Was he not a bull of a man? So he never expected the sword coming down over his head! He swept me into his arms! Even so, Marthani, when his head was cut off I shuddered! Do you know why? The pleasure he gave me that day was that special! There was no chance to meet him one more day and only afterward finish the matter as my father wished! Even now, when I remember the pleasure he gave me, a certain thrill runs through me, Marthani!”

As Piyasili spoke, she noticed a meaningful smile appearing on her friend Marthani's face.

“Why are you smiling like that?”

“Nothing, Piyasili! I was thinking about palace secrets. A laugh escaped me without my knowing, so I turned it into a smile. That is all!”

“Neither for my selfish interest nor for my father Pilimathala's have we made any secret plan! Everything is for the future of our country! Think for a moment! With enormous military strength the British have brought many parts of Lanka under their control. Only the kingdom of Kandy remains! And the region of Mullaitheevu! Mullaitheevu is not some

<!-- source boundary: scan 221 → scan 222 -->

great kingdom! But Pandaraka Vanniyan, who rules it, is powerful! It is because our kingdom is friendly with him that the British regard us as enemies. I have tried telling the king that the friendship must be severed. My father Pilimathala has tried too. Vikrama Rajasingan trusts Pandaraka Vanniyan like a mountain. All the royal-strategy measures my father and I undertook to break that trust failed to work on the king of Kandy. Yet we must not abandon that attempt—that is why we have gone through all this trouble!”

“If hostility arises between Pandaraka Vanniyan and our king, will the British not step in between and defeat both of them easily?”

“What concern have we for Mullaitheevu or for Pandaraka Vanniyan? Our concern is to save the people of the kingdom of Kandy!”

“How does that work, Piyasili?”

“Must I explain it to you again and again? If this journey of ours succeeds, hostility will flare between Pandaraka Vanniyan and the king of Kandy! With Kandy's support, the British army will defeat Pandarakan and hand Mullaitheevu over to Kandy. Then I, supposedly dead, will suddenly appear and be crowned queen of Mullaitheevu.”

“Can that really be done?”

“Why not, when Lord North is in Colombo? Is it not enough if he and I meet one night? With Pandaraka Vanniyan gone, I will be the queen of Mullaitheevu!”

“When he learns that you are alive—and understands your schemes—will the king of Kandy, Vikrama Rajasingan, simply remain quiet?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 222 → scan 223 -->

“Fool! By then he will not even be there! For the good of the kingdom of Kandy, for the good of the people of Kandy, my father Pilimathala will take responsibility for governing Kandy!”

“Oh my! So many plans...” Marthani said in amazement, placing a hand on her chin. At that very moment the sky suddenly roared terribly! Darkness closed in! Lightning flashed and tore at the eyes! Raindrops began to fall like tamarind fruits beaten loose with a clatter! A great rain! A tremendous gale! Thunder that split the ears!

The horses of Piyasili and Marthani struggled to move. Both women's eyes fell at the same instant upon a pavilion nearby; they drove the horses quickly toward it and entered.

The pavilion was ruined. Only one corner seemed even slightly suitable for the two of them to sit. They tied the horses to its pillars, and, unable to bear the cold, Piyasili and Marthani huddled into that corner, their bodies trembling.

The rain and storm showed no sign of stopping. The pavilion shook with the thunder and frightened them.

“Careful! Your Jeyaseelan disguise is going to wash away in the rain!” said Marthani.

Adjusting beard, moustache and turban, Piyasili shivered violently. “Oh! I cannot bear this cold!”

“A man, shivering like this in the cold?” Marthani teased, though she too, tormented by the cold, wrapped her arms around her knees and tried to endure it.

Nature's fury crossed all bounds. Rainwater entered the inside of the pavilion. Everywhere except the place where the two were

<!-- source boundary: scan 223 → scan 224 -->

sheltering had sunk into hollows from the ruin and debris, looking like pools. The two lay huddled in a slightly raised corner.

“If this pavilion collapses on our heads in this thunder and rain, what will happen to all the plans you and your father made?” Marthani asked, shivering with cold.

“Don't kill me now by talking about all that!” said Piyasili.

Seeing Piyasili suffering badly from the cold, Marthani hugged her tightly. “Is the cold a little less now?”

“Your body is warm like a man's! Hold me properly!” said Piyasili.

In that warmth, the two managed to withstand the force of the cold to some extent.

The rain and storm did not stop until dawn. The storm did not fall silent either. Daybreak and calm arrived almost together.

Without realizing it, the two had dozed off. Hearing the horses neigh, they started awake and realized that dawn had come.

A long sigh from both! Without looking directly at one another, they exchanged sidelong glances and let smiles creep over their faces.

“Even this thunderstorm could do nothing to us. What more proof do we need that God is on our side?” said Piyasili as she mounted her horse.

“I have heard that God is often on the side of wicked people,” Marthani replied with a laugh,

<!-- source boundary: scan 224 → scan 225 -->

and mounted her horse too.

At first the horses walked slowly; soon they began to run fast.

Near a signpost pointing toward the road to Mullaitheevu, Piyasili—no! no! Jeyaseelan—stopped the horse, looked at the sign, and turned onto that road. Marthani's horse followed him along the same way.
