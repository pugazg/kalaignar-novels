He pounced on her and threw her onto the bed! He too lay down beside her. But she twisted away, got up, went near the burning lamp, picked up a writing pen, and began writing something.

“What are you doing?” asked Vikrama Rajasingan, going up to her, embracing her, and looking.

“No, darling! I have drunk so much, haven't I? I am just seeing whether I can sign steadily,” she said, and wrote the name Piyasili five or six times!

“What is so difficult about that? I too can sign my name steadily,” said the king.

“You cannot! You simply cannot! You have drunk even more than I have!” she said, wrapping herself around his back and deepening his intoxication with desire.

He took that reply as a challenge.

“Give me the writing pen! I will put down a crore signatures!” he said.

“You cannot! In the state you are in, you won't sign—you'll only scribble!” said Piyasili.

“Let us see about that too!” he said, snatching the pen from her.

Then Piyasili said, “Wait a moment!” She opened the chest there, took out a blank sheet without his noticing, and held it out to him. Swaying on his feet, he took it, placed it on the stand beside the lamp, and, saying, “Here—look at my signature!” took hold of the pen.

A banner of victorious smiles flying high, Piyasili pointed with her finger and said, “Here! In this place! Right here!” He signed, then asked, “How is it? Have I signed steadily?” and pressed his lips against hers.

“Mmm! You are clever indeed!” cooed that clever enchantress, and hid the sheet away. The king, who had reached the height of intoxication, swept her into his arms—

“Come, my delight! Let us journey through the heavens!” he fondled her. That was what she needed too, and she yielded to every wish of his! She had the delight of seeing her purpose fulfilled; he had the weariness that came from its fulfilment. Now he needed another companion! She was Nithradevi—the goddess of sleep!
