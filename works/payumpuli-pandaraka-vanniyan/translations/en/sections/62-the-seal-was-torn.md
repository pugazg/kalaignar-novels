# The Seal Was Torn!

Before he could finish speaking, the “devotees” who had been near the bushes with their *kavadis* and *karagams* sprang forward, their waists girded and shields in hand, and surrounded the flagstaff. Kakkai Vanniyan was startled by the scene that had transformed before his eyes in the blink of an eye. Realizing in an instant that it would be dangerous if the Mullaitheevu warriors gained the upper hand, he shouted loudly while signalling to his own warriors hidden behind the bushes. The very next second they burst out of the bushes and encircled the Mullaitheevu warriors. The clash of swords! Spears flashing like lightning! Severed heads fell and rolled on the ground! Pierced bodies soaked the earth with blood! Nalla Naachchiyar understood nothing! She had come there to save Pirmanandan's life and surrender herself to him, but suddenly two armies were fighting—one was Mullaitheevu's army, that is, her brother Pandaraka Vanniyan's army—but whose was the other? Before she could find an answer, the man calling himself Pirmanandan—Kakkai Vanniyan—said, “Don't you know? One is Pandarakan's army! The other is Kakkai Vanniyan's army!”

By then a suspicion had arisen in Nalla Naachchiyar's mind. She ran toward Thanigaimala, who was tied to the flagstaff—

<!-- source boundary: scan 304 → scan 305 -->

but Kakkai Vanniyan would not let her go. He pulled her back and stopped her.

“Thanigaimala is a spy from Mullaitheevu. Why have you tied him up? What is happening here? I don't understand any of this!”

Nalla Naachchiyar asked in a loud voice. Preparing himself to continue deceiving her, Kakkai Vanniyan explained, “Oh! Is he from Mullaitheevu? I didn't know! While I was doing penance before Goddess Kannagi, thinking of you, he came inside and made mischief as though to spoil my penance. I told my men to tie him up.”

“But you are a merchant! What have you to do with this battle?”

Amid the thunderous noise of battle, Naachchiyar looked at him and asked this question.

“That is exactly what I don't understand either! I came here to do penance for you and give up my life! But everything here is one great mystery! Why should he disturb my penance? Why should I tell them to tie him up? Why should Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors and Pandaraka Vanniyan's warriors suddenly clash in this place?”

“Let them fight if they want! You made some kind of signal—and Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors came out of the bushes! I don't understand why!”

“That? You didn't understand? Two groups of people were fighting. I thought, why should we remain here? I made the call my horse is trained to recognize, so that it would come and we could both mount it and ride away. By then this place had become a great battlefield. How can my horse come through all this? There is the horse you came on! Come, let us leave on that!” Saying so, he dragged Nalla Naachchi toward her horse.

<!-- source boundary: scan 305 → scan 306 -->

Meanwhile the fighting around the flagstaff and at the temple entrance had reached a furious stage. Unable to withstand the assault of the Mullaitheevu warriors, many of Kakkai Vanniyan's men fell dead. Some fled. Thanigaimala's bonds were cut loose, and he too entered the fight, hacking off the limbs of his enemies as though shaking ripe fruit down from a tree.

With one eye on the tip of his sword at the battlefront and the other intent on finding where Kakkai Vanniyan was taking Nalla Naachchi, he fought fiercely with Kakkai Vanniyan's commander, kicked the commander's lifeless body away with his foot, and sprang onto his own horse, whirling his blood-running sword!

By then Kakkai Vanniyan and Nalla Naachchi had mounted her horse and set off. Only Nalla Naachchi could handle her horse, so Kakkai Vanniyan rode pressed close behind her. To her, he was still Pirmanandan!

Yet a doubt remained. Her lover Pirmanandan was a merchant! Why had he involved himself in this needless affair? Why should he have had Thanigaimala tied to the flagstaff? With that unanswered question in her mind, she drove the horse fast.

“Where are you going, my treasure?”

“Tell me where we should go!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 306 → scan 307 -->

“Let us go to my mansion!”

“How far is it?”

“I'll show you the way! Just keep riding along the route I tell you!” As he called out right and left, directing the path, she made the horse fly.

After they had gone a long distance, both of them heard the sound of a horse rapidly following behind.

Still riding fast with great care, Nalla Naachchiyar looked back, was shocked, and stopped the horse.

“Why? Why did you stop?” Kakkai Vanniyan asked in alarm.

“The man following us is Thanigaimala! He is a spy I know!”

“Whoever he is, what does it matter? No! No! Drive the horse on!” Kakkai Vanniyan shouted.

“Why not stop and ask what he wants? Thanigaimala will not remain angry with you against my wishes. I can even reconcile the two of you.”

“Whatever it is, not halfway! First let us reach my mansion! If he follows us there, we can talk there.” Kakkai Vanniyan was afraid that his Pirmanandan disguise might be exposed. Somehow he had to get Nalla Naachchi inside his fort and defile her body. He wanted to sate his desire on her. Pandaraka Vanniyan must hear that she had come seeking him and had become his wife; he must writhe and hang his head in shame. With such a plan in mind—

<!-- source boundary: scan 307 → scan 308 -->

Kakkai Vanniyan urged her to drive the horse even faster.

Even amid so much confusion, Nalla Naachchiyar could not dislodge him from the place she had already given him in her heart. Her only question was why this great merchant should have any hostility toward Thanigaimala. Even as they sped like an arrow, she asked him, “Would the battle at the Kannagi temple entrance be over by now? Would Kakkai Vanniyan's army have been defeated?”

“Kakkai Vanniyan or Eagle Vanniyan—what does it matter! Any army will be torn to pieces before Pandaraka Vanniyan's forces! Don't be afraid—drive the horse!”

Saying this, he pressed his face close against her and leaned heavily upon her.

The two horses ran at a four-legged gallop through clouds of dust.

Suddenly there was a sound—the sound of Thanigaimala's horse losing its balance and falling when it planted a hoof on a small slippery rock while leaping!

Nalla Naachchiyar noticed it and pulled her horse to a stop.

The horse that had rolled down did not rise. Thanigaimala, blood dripping from him, staggered as he tried to stand.

“Oh, poor man! Turn the horse! We can help him somehow!”

Kakkai Vanniyan said. Nalla Naachchi rejoiced at the thought that compassion and humanity still flourished green even in her lover's hostile heart. She turned the horse and hurried toward the place where Thanigaimala had fallen. Before she—

<!-- source boundary: scan 308 → scan 309 -->

could jump down from the horse, Kakkai Vanniyan had already leapt off. Not only that—he raised his dagger and drove it into Thanigaimala's chest.

Trembling, Nalla Naachchiyar ran to stop him, but failed.

“Thanigaimala! What has happened to you?”

she cried aloud, rushing to support him. Clutching his chest, Thanigaimala said in anguish, “This is a warrior's state, Amma! What I cannot understand is your state—joining hands with Kakkai Vanniyan, Amma?”

“Kakkai Vanniyan? Who? Where?”

Nalla Naachchiyar cried in shock.

“Here—this man! This traitor!”

Thanigaimala pointed at him, but before he could explain anything further, he collapsed.

Seeing Thanigaimala become a corpse before her very eyes, Nalla Naachchi screamed and wept. Kakkai Vanniyan tried to console her.

“He is babbling. Me, Kakkai Vanniyan? Death has clouded his eyes! Don't believe what he said!”

“A man who says, ‘Come, let us help him,’ and then takes his life must be without humanity. That beast must indeed be Kakkai Vanniyan.”

“Standing before you is your beloved Pirmanandan! The husband who married you with Kannagiyamman as witness!”

“Husband? Or scoundrel?”

“The lover who wishes to guard you as the eyelid guards the eye!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 309 → scan 310 -->

“Enough! I have ruined myself by trusting you long enough!” Nalla Naachchiyar removed the ring he had placed on her at the temple and flung it away.

“I am Kakkai Vanniyan! But I am also your husband! I will not let you divorce me before I have tasted you completely!”

he said, gripping her hands and pulling her violently toward him.

“Shame! Let me go!” Nalla Naachchiyar hissed.
