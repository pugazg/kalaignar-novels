# The Pasaik-Kodi Sign!

“May I come in?” Marthani asked, and without waiting for an answer entered carrying a plate of snacks. Thanigaimala did not much enjoy her dancing gait. Instead, the question mark already drawn in his heart grew larger as he stared at her and said, “Oh! Marthani?”

Putting the plate before him, she said with a gentle smile, “I am new to the town! New to this house too! I have made whatever I could. Taste a little and tell me whether it is good!”

The words Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar had spoken before leaving—words that had strengthened the suspicion already in him—kept ringing in Thanigaimala's ears.

“You came without even closing the door!” he said, watching her from the corner of his eye.

“Why should the door be closed just to eat snacks?”

“If it is only snacks we are going to eat, there is no need to close the door!”

There was mischief not only in Thanigai's speech, but in the play of his eyes as well.

“It seems you have come to some hasty conclusion about me! I am not that kind of woman!”

“What have I even said now? You have already carried the conversation such a long way! We are not going to eat

only snacks! There may be any number of political matters to discuss—that is why I thought it would be better if the door were closed.”

“Oh! Is that what you meant? What frightened me was this: my husband is mute as an owl! No child has been born to us! What joy can she possibly have living with him? Perhaps you thought, ‘Why not try a little...’”

“Shame! Shame! What a mad woman you are! Have I forgotten our ancestral saying: ‘Even if he is stone, he is the husband; even if he is grass, he is the man’? Wasn't Nalayini the woman who, in accordance with the wish of her leprosy-stricken husband, carried him all the way to a courtesan's house? You too must surely come in the line of such chaste wives!”

“Certainly—truly, I am exactly like that! I mingle naturally and without blemish with everyone. Seeing that, some people make the wrong calculation about me!”

“Do not imagine that I made any wrong calculation. If you thought so, forget it at once!” he said. Then he tasted a little of the snack and praised it: “Ah! It is very, very tasty.”

Thanigaimala's mind grew confused. *Did she really come to ensnare me? I showed the tiniest sign just to find out—but it does not seem so! She speaks firmly! She talks like a queen of chastity! What is this? I cannot understand a thing!*

Inside, Marthani too felt a little disappointed. Her plan when she entered that room had been not to collapse the instant he touched her, but to show a little coy reserve, make him trust her, and then bring him into her net!

<!-- source boundary: scan 257 → scan 258 -->

Thanigaimala seemed to have taken her for a Kannagi herself! Thrown off by that thought, she blinked about in confusion.

Even so, she did not show it. When he praised the taste of the snack, she smiled and said, “Thank you.” He continued concentrating on eating.

“You aren't angry with me, are you?” she asked Thanigai in a melting voice.

“Why should I be angry? Did I expect something from you and then grow angry because you refused to give it? Don't imagine things on your own!” he said, bringing firmness into his tone.

“I too am sorry that I could not give you what you were thinking of!”

*What devilry is this? It is circling back again from somewhere!* Thanigai muttered inwardly.

“Marthani! I was not thinking of anything from you! Only if I had asked for something could I be angry that you had not given it!”

“Tell me truthfully! When I came in, did you ask me to close the door only so that we could talk politics?”

“What else do you think it was for?”

“You tell me! Why did you ask me to close the door?”

“A woman of good manners should leave this matter here.”

“Then you admit that there is a ‘matter’!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 258 → scan 259 -->

“Marthani! Marthani! What on earth are you making me say now?”

Thanigaimala lifted a hand to strike his own head. Marthani sprang forward and caught it.

Thanigaimala started. Yet he made no attempt to free his hand from her grip.

“I too am talking about a political matter,” she said, taking a snack with her own hand, placing it in his hand, and saying, “Here! Eat!”

Thanigaimala shook his head as though to say, “No, enough.”

“You don't like it if I put it in your hand? Here, I'll put it straight into your mouth,” she said, and with her beautiful fingertips pushed a piece of snack between his rough lips. Though each was deceiving the other, in the very plan they had each devised the heat naturally rose in both their bodies! Roll in fire and the body burns; fall into water and the body chills—is not the net of desire cast by one upon another the same? Even if one wishes to reach the shore, so long as one remains under its sway, one must submit to the climate of its heat and cold.

Along with the piece of snack pressed between his lips, Thanigaimala savoured her fingers too!

“Now shall we talk politics? Shall I close the door?” Marthani asked.

Thanigaimala had now decided that she was indeed a consummate sly temptress. He resolved to go along with her course and break whatever plan she and that mute man had devised.

“As you wish!” he said with a smile. At once she bounded like a spotted deer, went and shut the door

<!-- source boundary: scan 259 → scan 260 -->

and bolted it. But she did not immediately return to him from the doorway. Like a bride who, after being pushed by the neck into the bridal chamber where the groom waits on the first night and after the door has been closed behind her, stands there shyly, she remained looking down at her big toes.

Thanigaimala slowly rose and went toward her. With his rough hand he touched her chin and asked, “Shy?” Her eyes swayed with intoxication. She stood looking only at him. He too stared fixedly at her. The next instant, though he lost himself in Marthani's embrace, he managed to keep from forgetting that he was Pandaraka Vanniyan's bodyguard. She, having embraced him, would not let go! She pushed him down onto the bed there and began devouring his lips. He closed his eyes and held her tightly!

“Shall we talk politics?” she asked in an extremely soft voice, like the trembling string of a yaazh. He only clasped her more tightly.

Then, as though she had suddenly realized she had committed some terrible wrong, as though she had stepped on fire, she tore herself quickly away from him.

“Ayyo! What will happen if my husband finds out?” she trembled violently.

“Nothing will happen! Aren't we only talking politics?” Thanigaimala replied calmly.

“All right! I urgently need a green cloth and a small stick,” said Marthani.

“A green cloth and a stick? What for?” Thanigai asked in surprise.

<!-- source boundary: scan 260 → scan 261 -->

“To make a flag.”

“A flag? What for?”

“To hang at the entrance of this house.”

“A green flag at the entrance to my house? I don't understand!”

“That flag must be at the front of this house—as a sign.”

“What sign?”

“I'll tell you!”
