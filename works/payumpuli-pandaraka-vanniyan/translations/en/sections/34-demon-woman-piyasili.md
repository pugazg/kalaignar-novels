# Demon-Woman Piyasili!

It did not take Pandaraka Vanniyan long to realize that the one who had saved him from the murderous attack of the man-bewitching Piyasili—and had taken the dagger thrust upon herself in order to save him—was his beloved Kuruvichchi Naachchi.

“Kuruvichchi!” he screamed, rushing to her and lifting her into his arms.

Vikrama Rajasingan, agitated and in a terrible fury, struck Piyasili hard across the cheek and ordered, “Take her away and imprison her in the women's quarters!” He did not even heed Pilimathala, who anxiously tried to interrupt and say something.

When the palace guards approached Piyasili to imprison her on the king's orders, she cried out loudly.

“Alas! They have plotted to kill me! Is he a king, ordering me imprisoned without even considering that I am a woman? Father! Tell the king! It is not righteous to punish me when I know no sin! Even the Lord would not accept it! If necessary, I will leave this palace altogether. I will never again set foot across its threshold! I will go somewhere and live as I please! Only do not imprison me here—no, no, never!”

Though Pilimathala stood without speaking as he listened to Piyasili's cries, Vikrama Rajasingan laughed aloud, overflowing with anger.

“Oh-ho! What a clever rogue you are! I understand why you are pleading, ‘I want no palace life—let me fly away as a free bird!’ You have already made many men in this palace your slaves and satisfied your desires. Now you want to sing songs of pleasure as an enchantress of men in the outside world. Enough! Is it not enough that I alone have been caught by your wicked poisonous fang and ruined? More than enough! You need not leave the palace to corrupt others too. You are a smiling spider, after all. Even without your going in search of them, plenty of prey will fall into your web by themselves. Why worry? Never mind. Rot in confinement in the women's quarters!”

Having said this, Vikrama Rajasingan hurried to Pandaraka Vanniyan. He grasped the shoulder of his dearest friend, who was holding the unconscious Kuruvichchi Naachchi in his arms.

“Vairamuthu! I shall summon the physician at once. I have hope that she can be saved. Please, let us go inside the palace,” he pleaded in a trembling voice.

Pandarakan lifted Kuruvichchi and entered the palace with tear-filled eyes.

Piyasili watched with a venomous stare as Pandarakan, Vikrama Rajasingan and Kuruvichchi went inside. Her desire to possess Pandaraka Vanniyan had failed; worse, her murderous plan to take revenge for his rejection of her had failed as well. Maddened by that failure, she tore at her hair and

<!-- source boundary: scan 168 → scan 169 -->

screamed. She looked like a demon leaping into the battlefield of the *Kalingattu Parani* and shrieking. The rogue woman's face reddened like a kalli fruit.

Pilimathala, whose eyes forever spat poison, embraced her.

“Daughter! Be patient! This dancing and prancing of Vikrama Rajasingan will last only a few more days. The time when North will have to help us is bound to come soon. I am waiting for the right moment! And meanwhile you say you will leave this palace and become an ascetic—is that intelligent? No! Not at all!

“That Governor North—he roams the capital of Colombo every night searching for a young woman for his feast! I know he has his eye on you. But I am not prepared to send my daughter to his bed in a cheap barter. I must sit as ruler of the kingdom of Kandy. For that I need North's support. It was to win easily through that support that I prepared and sent you to create enmity between Pandaraka Vanniyan and Kannusami.

“Our first defeat! But not a total defeat. Now go into confinement in the women's quarters as though accepting Vikrama Rajasingan's order. Is that confinement really so terrible? There is a swimming pool filled with wine there too! It is imprisonment only in name; all the prison guards are our Sinhalese men, are they not? You will get whatever you ask for. The king will not come there—that is all!”

As Pilimathala spoke, Piyasili consoled herself with a thought: *So what if the king does not come? Is there any shortage of men there?*

<!-- source boundary: scan 169 → scan 170 -->

“Go, girl, go! This confinement will not bring the whole household crashing down. There is a vow of vengeance forming today in both our hearts. We shall fulfil it. If not today, then very soon, one day we will bring Vikrama Rajasingan down! We will make Pandaraka Vanniyan a slave beneath British feet!”

Clutching his bald head, his round eyes bulging, Pilimathala swore the oath.

“Yes, Father! That is right! For the patriotism you envision to triumph, and for those wicked men—my husband and his friend—to fall, I am ready to make this body an object of embrace not only for North but for however many more British men it takes!” Piyasili crowed, raising one hand high.

“Ah! What resolve! What a diamond heart! I am blessed indeed to have fathered such a woman! Still, your aim was a little off. Had you thrown that dagger accurately, one accursed nuisance would have been gone by now!”

Pilimathala patted his daughter approvingly.

“I came to the palace gate pretending to see Pandaraka Vanniyan off only because I meant to finish him today! Some woman sprang in between and saved him. Who was she?” Piyasili muttered.

“She is said to be the love of his life! The two of them are supposed to be divine lovers, united for an ideal! Do you know her name? Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar!”

“Kuruvichchi Naachchi!” Piyasili repeated mockingly. Placing her hands on the shoulders of the Sinhalese soldiers standing on either side of her, she said, “Soldiers! Take me

<!-- source boundary: scan 170 → scan 171 -->

to prison now!” and drew them along with her toward the women's quarters.

Only the word “Kuruvichchi” kept boring into her heart.

Emergency treatment was being given to Kuruvichchi in one of the palace halls. Pandaraka Vanniyan sat with his heart pounding, looking only at her. Vikrama Rajasingan sat beside him to comfort him. Pilimathala too came and stood there with a long face, as though he had arrived out of concern to share in the sorrow.

With great difficulty but remarkable skill, the palace physician removed the dagger buried deep in Kuruvichchi's chest. Yet stopping the blood that continued to gush out was no easy task. The necessary herbs were placed upon the wound and bandaged. Her breath continued faintly, but Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar was in no condition to open her eyes.

*How did she come here? How did she come dressed as a warrior and pass among the warriors as one of them? Had she somehow sensed beforehand that such a danger would befall me and come precisely to prevent it? Only if she fully recovers can I learn all these details!*

Pandaraka Vanniyan kept his eyes fixed upon that golden statue of a woman who had not hesitated to give her life to save his.

Then came a faint moan from her.

Everyone looked eagerly toward her. The eyes that had remained closed all this time slowly opened.
