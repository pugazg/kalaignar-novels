# Another Madurai?

The warriors of Pandaraka Vanniyan's army, who had surrounded the Vattrappalai Kannagiyamman temple, looked toward their leader in bewilderment because Thanigaimala, whom they had come searching for, was not there. Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi saw drops of blood scattered at the temple entrance and around the temple, and warriors who had suffered terrible wounds and now lay dead. Warriors from both sides lay on that field.

“Kuruvichchi! You come toward Kakkai's fort by that single-foot path over there! I will come to the fort by this path here! Let one division of our warriors go with you and another with me! Whichever of us meets Thanigaimala on the way will take responsibility for protecting him!” Pandarakan said, urging his horse forward.

“Because Pandarakan is filled with affection and feels that Thanigaimala must not die—must not already be dead—he still refuses to believe the news that he has perished. Even if Thanigaimala is alive, as he hopes, or has been killed, as I think, we must in any case go and see that loyal warrior.”

So Kuruvichchi's heart sang like a sorrowful cuckoo—

<!-- source boundary: scan 336 → scan 337 -->

—and she too spurred her horse along the path Pandarakan had pointed out, accompanied by one division of warriors.

Not long after taking his route from Vattrappalai, Pandarakan was able to see Thanigaimala's body. The moment he saw that great warrior lying dead, a shaken Pandaraka Vanniyan leapt down from his horse, ran to him, and, driven by tenderness and a rush of emotion, gathered the body into his arms and wailed. By giving second place to the news concerning his sister Nalla Naachchi and first place to Thanigaimala, the trusted spy of his army, Pandaraka Vanniyan showed himself to be a selfless lion. He kissed the crown of Thanigaimala's head. He removed a slender necklace from his own neck and placed it around Thanigai's.

“Warrior! My loyal friend! The help you gave me in shaping Mullaitheevu into a beautiful land fragrant with freedom, wiping away even the traces of foreign domination, was no small thing! But before your dream could be fulfilled, before a state could arise in which no foreigner could enter this land, you have closed your eyes. What justice is there in this?”

Pandarakan's voice trembled as he asked this. A flood of tears burst through the barriers of his eyelids. He lifted Thanigaimala and handed him to the warriors beside him. “Let this trustworthy gold be laid away as treasure within our palace grounds,” he said. Four or five warriors turned back toward Mullaitheevu with Thanigaimala's body. Until the warriors carrying Thanigai's body disappeared from his sight—

<!-- source boundary: scan 337 → scan 338 -->

—Pandarakan stood staring in that direction without taking his eyes away. Vairamuthu Pandarakan, that warrior-tiger who stood armed and ready to give his own life for the rights of the soil in which he was born and to defend its honour, seethed when he thought that his beloved companion had been unjustly struck down through conspiracy. At the same time he broke down and wept aloud. A warrior is not someone from whose heart all tenderness has been squeezed away. That tender feeling now rose even above his valour.

“If there is a leader, this is what a leader is! He has eyes that weep for the body of every warrior of his who falls! Those are no false tears! They are a great flood that has lost its direction as it pours from a storm-struck heart!”

A warrior standing at a distance said this to his companion.

“Yes! Is that not why we stand in formation with this leader without caring about suffering or trials? Who else would set out saying, ‘We can find out later what happened to my sister—first let us see Thanigaimala’?”

The warrior's companion replied with pride, through a question.

Pandaraka Vanniyan, the tiger who showed his grief in his gait, mounted his horse again. Now the thought of Nalla Naachchi returned to him.

The horses walked slowly for a short distance and then began to race.

Meanwhile, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar was hurrying toward Kakkai Vanniyan's fort with her division. Though her whole mind was fixed on somehow freeing Nalla Naachchiyar from Kakkai Vanniyan's grip—and though she had no doubt that Thanigaimala had been killed—

<!-- source boundary: scan 338 → scan 339 -->

—she left the dead man behind and yearned to save the woman who was still alive.

Nalla Naachchi, after striking Kakkai Vanniyan with the torch, fought back without losing heart even when his warriors surrounded her and launched a fierce attack. Astonished by the power of that woman warrior, Kakkai Vanniyan groaned and grunted as he rose unsteadily, blood pouring from the wound on his head. But Nalla Naachchiyar was not going to let him go. While fending off the warriors around her, she swung the torch hard at Kakkai Vanniyan's leg. Crying out, “Ayyo! Amma!” Kakkai Vanniyan suddenly turned his ears toward an unexpected sound.

It was the sound of horses' hooves. At once a scheme flashed like lightning in his poisonous mind.

“Aha! Bravo! The cavalry I sent for has arrived! Hey, Nalla Naachchi! Submit quietly! Otherwise your life and honour will be taken from you before the eyes of hundreds of warriors!” Kakkai Vanniyan shouted.

Not realizing that the approaching cavalry was being led by Kuruvichchi, Nalla Naachchiyar flared up. She could see no other way to escape the pack of little foxes. She decided to feed Kakkai Vanniyan's fort itself to the flames. She did not take even the time of a blink to carry out her decision.

She had been fighting in the outer corridor of the bedchamber; now she ran inside. Kakkai Vanniyan, still suffering from the blow he had received—

<!-- source boundary: scan 339 → scan 340 -->

—gathered all his strength and, steeling himself with the thought that this was his final attempt, sprang into the bedchamber after her. A mighty blow landed on his head. Though it fell from the woman's hand with a torch, it felt as though a rock had fallen upon his skull. His limbs twitched like the legs and tail of a lizard whose head had been crushed. By then Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors had entered the room and begun to encircle her. The thunderous sound of horses racing toward the fort rang loudly enough to deafen the ears.

Ayyo! Fearing that if that cavalry too arrived her honour would be taken from her, Nalla Naachchiyar set fire to all the silk curtains swaying in the air inside the marble bedchamber. The fire spread rapidly. Tongues of flame filled the room. Some warriors ran. Some fell into the fire itself and suffered there.

The feet of fire walked through Kakkai Vanniyan's entire fort. The hands of flame struck dancing gestures. Nails burst from timbers with crack after crack; beams broke and fell. As though burned silk curtains, cloth, ornaments and costly objects were not enough, the tongues of fire rose and swayed upward as if stretching to seize and devour even the clouds in the sky.

Kuruvichchi, approaching with her warriors, saw the fort burning and was shaken. What had happened to Nalla Naachchiyar? With that fear pounding within her, she drove her horse toward the fort. From inside the burning fort, Nalla Naachchiyar came running wildly, her body aflame, still holding the torch raised high without dropping it.

The moment she saw Kuruvichchi, Nalla Naachchiyar laughed terribly and cried, “I have destroyed—

<!-- source boundary: scan 340 → scan 341 -->

—and finished Kakkai Vanniyan! The traitor is gone! His own fort has become his cremation ground!”

Wondering whether this was Kannagi herself standing after setting Madurai on fire, Kuruvichchi first ran toward Nalla Naachchiyar to rescue her from the ring of flames. Fire wrapped around and scorched Nalla Naachchi like a mountain snake coiling around a fawn. Whatever might happen, Kuruvichchi pulled her down to the ground and rolled her this way and that to put out the flames.

The fire went out, but Nalla Naachchiyar rose and stood swaying, burns all over her body and half her face surrendered to the flames.

Kuruvichchi decided to take her away from there. Holding a raised sword in one hand to keep enemies from approaching, she supported Nalla Naachchi with the other.

But Nalla Naachchi suddenly pulled away from Kuruvichchi and shouted, “Kuruvichchi! Do not touch me!”
