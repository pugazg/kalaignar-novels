# Thanigaimala's Fury!

Pulling close the woman who had moved away, Thanigai embraced her and said, “Sweet one of my desire! What is the green flag for? You said, ‘I'll tell you,’ and now why are you standing silent? Move those lips of yours, gold, lips that would put even sweet syrup to shame!” He had decided to deceive her by travelling along her own path, after all!

“You sound like a great poet! Descriptions are pouring down like rain!” Marthani said, resting her face on Thanigai's shoulder.

“I am neither bard nor poet! The moment I see you, imagination overflows. That is all. Leave that aside! Tell me what the green flag is for!”

“My husband has a younger sister! Her name is Kala! She came with us from Kandy. On the way we sent her off on some other work. When she finishes that task, she will come to Mullaitheevu. We told her that a green flag would be flying at the doorway of the house where my husband and I are staying, so that she could identify the place. She will see it and come here.”

“May I know what work you sent that Kala to do?”

“Why should I hide it? Everything is being done under the orders of the king of Kandy, and all for Pandaraka Vanniyan's good! We have sent Kala to Kaakkai Vanniyan's fort.”

“A woman—and alone! And to Kaakkai Vanniyan's fort at that!”

“You say that now! If you saw Kala in person, you would cry, ‘Ayyo! You sent such a beauty into that traitor's fort?’”

“Is she so beautiful?”

“A thousand times more beautiful than I am! Two eyes would not be enough for you to enjoy looking at her! You would have to borrow a thousand eyes!”

“What is so difficult about that? We can simply borrow them from Indra!”

“What is Indra—a shopkeeper dealing in eyes? How can one borrow a thousand eyes from him?”

“Haven't you heard the story that Indra's whole body is covered in eyes? There was a rishi's wife named Ahalya. A ravishing beauty. Indra developed desire for her. One day he deceived the rishi, sent him away, changed his own form to look like the rishi, and entered the hermitage.”

“What did Ahalya do?”

“What would she do? She was delighted, thinking, ‘After all these days, today our husband has suddenly developed such desire for us!’”

“And then?”

“Nothing but pleasure!”

“Did she never discover the truth until the end?”

“She understood within a little while!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 263 → scan 264 -->

“And once she understood?”

“She began caressing him!”

“What are you saying? Indra comes taking the form of her husband...”

“Not an ordinary Indra! Devendra! Lord of all the devas!”

“All right! Lord of whoever he may be! Are you saying Ahalya did not flare up in anger the moment she realized he was not her husband?”

“Just imagine that the situation in that hermitage was like ours! You are afraid of your husband—and you desire me. In that same state, even after learning that he was Indra, Ahalya lay with him.”

“Did the rishi who had gone out never return?”

“He came back! Like a bear intruding into a Shiva worship!”

“And what did he do?”

“Good question! What would your husband do if he came here now? The sage did exactly that! ‘You sinful Ahalya! Once you knew that the man who came in my form was Indra, instead of grieving over the deception and driving him away, you lay with him. I curse you! Become stone!’ he said. Poor woman, she became stone. Only when Rama's foot touched that stone did she gain release from the curse.”

“What did he do to Indra?”

“What, would he seat him down and praise him—‘Devendra! There is no noble man like you! What skill you showed in deceiving me! My blessings upon you for satisfying the longing of my chaste wife Ahalya!’—and then kiss the crown of his head before sending him away? No! ‘You wicked man! For engaging in such misconduct, may your whole body become eyes!’ he

<!-- source boundary: scan 264 → scan 265 -->

cursed him severely. At once a thousand eyes appeared on Indra's body! I only meant that I could borrow those eyes and then enjoy the beauty of the Kala you described.”

“Stop with enjoying the sight! Kala has a fierce temper! She might say something rash!”

“You too were fierce-tempered at first! Marthani! I have a certain fortune. Whatever the woman, she has to fall into my net.”

“Even Kuruvichchi Naachchi?”

She had barely finished asking the question when—slap!—a blow landed on her cheek. Who knew where such fury had suddenly come from in him!

“I am letting you off with this only because you came here as a guest! You escaped! Otherwise, by now I would have made sure you no longer existed in this world!” Thanigaimala shouted.

“I asked wrongly. Forgive me!”

Marthani clutched his knees.

Thanigaimala regretted having been swept away by emotion and forgetting that he too ought to deal deceitfully with a woman who had come with deceitful intent. Calming himself, he said, “It is all right. Get up. I lost control because you spoke that way about the Naachchiyar whom I regard as a sister though she was not born with me.”

“Look how swollen my cheek is! You must soothe it and make the swelling go down,” she said, bringing her cheek close to his lips. To make her smile, he too gave what she asked for. What loss was it to him? What hardship?

“I asked for a flag!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 265 → scan 266 -->

Marthani twined herself around Thanigaimala like a creeper.

“How can I fetch it unless you let me go?” Thanigai said. Freeing himself from her, he rummaged through a pile of cloth lying there and finally took out a green piece of cloth, tied it to a small stick to make a flag, and placed it in her hand.

“I will tie this at the entrance of the house and then go to my husband.”

Thanigaimala nodded. She gave him one more on the lips, opened the door, and went out.

Scratching his head and rubbing his palm across his forehead, Thanigaimala sank onto the bed.

“They say a woman called Kala is coming! A beauty of extraordinary form—an Amaravathi! They say she is the younger sister of this mute Jeyaseelan! They say they have sent her to Kaakkai Vanniyan's fort! Nothing is clear! We must act with great caution.”

These were Thanigaimala's thoughts. Then a scream came from the upper floor of the house!

“Ayyo! God! I have been ruined!”

It was Marthani's voice! Thanigaimala sprang up and ran out. His feet flew over the stairs leading to the upper floor.
