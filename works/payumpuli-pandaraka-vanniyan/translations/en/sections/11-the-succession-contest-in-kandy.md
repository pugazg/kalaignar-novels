6

# The Succession Contest in Kandy!

Both of them came to the conclusion that the letter must have slipped from his possession at the very place where the fight with the elephant had taken place.

“I already know everything written in the letter. It speaks only of the events that have taken place there and the actions you must take here. The letter itself is not essential. Kattabomman and Vellaiyathevan both told me, ‘Since you are going there in person, explain everything to the king and return.’ I was the one who insisted to Vellaiyathevan that he write the letter and give it to me as a precaution. The reason was that some danger might befall even me on the way at the hands of foreign enemies—in that case, if I were unable to come here myself, I could send the letter through someone else, couldn't I?” said Sundaralingam.

“That letter will not have gone anywhere! If we search with a little effort at the very place where my warriors met you, we will find it. I will send a few warriors there right now,” said Pandaraka Vanniyan. The moment he raised his hand in a signal, two or three warriors ran toward him and stood bowing before him.

“Go quickly to the place where he struck down the elephant! Somewhere there will be a letter bearing our address and the Panchalankurichi

<!-- source boundary: scan 50 → scan 51 -->

seal. It may not be easy to find at first sight. It could have fallen and remained hidden in bushes, pits, tree hollows or somewhere else. Search without worrying about fatigue, find it and bring it back!” the king ordered them.

Then, turning to Sundaralingam, he added, “Friend! Until these warriors return, if you tell me the important matter in the letter, we can discuss it.”

“I'll first tell you the situation there and the recent events,” Sundaralingam began.

Kulasekaram Vairamuthu, proud that a struggle of honour against foreigners was taking place in Tamil land just as it was in the land of Sri Lanka, sat upright and sharpened his attention for the news Sundaralingam was about to tell him.

“Since the British commanders who have been there until now have not been able to subdue Panchalankurichi, the British have sent a man named Jackson there as Collector, a man in whom brutishness and foolishness are mixed in equal measure. Though he is brutish and foolish, he is highly experienced in military training.”

When Sundaralingam said this, Vairamuthu nodded and ground his teeth. “Yes! I too have heard of him—that he is a man without a heart!”

“The British commanders have sworn: this is the year 1798, and before the end of the coming year 1799, they say they will conquer Panchalankurichi!” Sundaralingam said angrily and continued.

<!-- source boundary: scan 51 → scan 52 -->

“As soon as Jackson took charge as Collector, the palaiyakkarar of Ettayapuram and a few other palaiyakkarars—men who sing the praises of whoever comes to power—went to meet Jackson and promised to serve as his lackeys. Since neither we nor our guards of Panchalankurichi possessed that cowardice or fox-like nature, we did not concern ourselves with Jackson. Poor fellow, he must have been seething because we had not gone into his august presence! After waiting and waiting, he finally sent a letter saying Kattabomman must come and see him immediately. But Kattabomman paid no heed to that letter. Then Jackson kept writing letter after letter, so it was finally decided that we would go meet him and see what he had to say. Surrounded by four thousand warriors, Kattabomman and all of us set out for Tirunelveli to meet Jackson. Seeing that so many warriors were coming, Jackson decided to make us wander and left Tirunelveli for Courtallam. We did not give up. We went there too with our forces. Saying that he had urgent work at Sokkampatti, Jackson emptied out of Courtallam and left. Perhaps that *Parangi thurai* was afraid because we were coming with troops; Kattabomman suggested that everyone return to Panchalankurichi and that he alone would go and meet Jackson, but no one accepted the idea. We went to Sokkampatti surrounded by our forces. But Jackson began playing a game of dodging us. From Sokkampatti he kept shifting from place to place—Sivagiri, Tiruvilliputhur, Peraiyur, Pavalipallimadai and Kamuthi—and finally Jackson reached Ramanathapuram!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 52 → scan 53 -->

Even though many advised that we should turn back, Kattabomman stubbornly wanted to follow him, determined that he had to discover what Jackson's scheme really was.

We received word that Jackson was staying at a small palace called Ramalinga Vilasam in Ramanathapuram and that he had stationed thousands of soldiers as guards both inside and outside the palace.

Jackson, weaving a web of conspiracy to trap us inside Ramalinga Vilasam, invited Kattabomman to come there and meet him. Do you know the chief condition in that invitation? Kattabomman was not to come surrounded by his troops; Kattabomman alone was to come with only a few personal guards. None of us frowned on hearing that condition. We firmly believed that British mercenary troops could not match the shoulders of men who had vowed to offer even our corpses as manure for the freedom of the land of our birth. So Jackson's condition was accepted. Only forty of us, including Kattabomman, Thanapathi Pillai, Oomaithurai, Vellaiyathevan and me, set out for Ramalinga Vilasam. Seeing our arrival from the upper floor of Ramalinga Vilasam, Jackson sent men down to say that Kattabomman alone could come upstairs to where he was. Kattabomman agreed even to that and went upstairs; only Oomaithurai and Thanapathi Pillai pushed aside the guards who tried to stop them and went with Kattabomman. As for those who had come,

<!-- source boundary: scan 53 → scan 54 -->

Jackson, who did not know how to receive them with respect, arrogantly believing that his scheme was about to succeed, reeled off accusation after accusation and demanded, ‘What is your answer to this?’”

Pandaraka Vanniyan now interrupted Sundaralingam. His moustache quivering, he asked, “What were these accusations?”

Sundaralingam continued.

“Not paying tax to the East India Company was one offence! Refusing to live in accord with the Ettayapuram people, who were servants of the British, was one offence! Collecting tax without the permission of the whites was one offence! Panchalankurichi occupying some territories belonging to the East India Company was one offence! Failing to come and meet the British officials and seek their protection was one offence! Bringing other palaiyakkarars together against the Company was one offence!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan interrupted again. “In other words, the very same offences they accuse me of here! Never mind—go on!”

“Could Kattabomman remain patient after hearing Jackson's accusations? Seeing before his own eyes the spectacle of outsiders who had come in now driving out those who belonged to the place, Kattabomman became a volcano—a blazing flame—a storm wind! But Jackson immediately rang the bell to set his conspiracy in motion. British soldiers who had been hiding as arranged beforehand sprang upon Kattabomman. Undaunted, Veerapandiya Kattabomman swung his sword and fought them. A man named Lieutenant Clarke, deputy commander in Jackson's force, was cut to pieces by Kattabomman's sword.

<!-- source boundary: scan 54 → scan 55 -->

“As soon as we learned that chaos had broken out inside Ramalinga Vilasam, we ordered the Panchalankurichi troops massed outside to come in. Bullets from the British force were raining down upon our men. Fighting them with swords, spears and staffs, we broke our way out. Seeing the skill and courage Vellaiyathevan showed in that battle, Kattabomman bestowed on him the very title ‘Bahadur’; I was filled with joy at the honour my friend received. In his own happiness, Vellaiyathevan swept me into an embrace and lifted me up. But there was one thing! In that sudden attack, Thanapathi Pillai alone, who was with us, was caught by them. Later they released him and sent him away.”

When Sundaralingam finished saying this, Pandaraka Vanniyan's face lit up. He felt proud, thinking of the spirit of sacrifice and valour with which their people were resisting foreign domination in Tamil land.

“Your Majesty! Everything I have told you so far concerns the events that have taken place there. What I am going to tell you now is the request that Bahadur Vellaiyathevan has made on behalf of Veerapandiya Kattabomman in the letter sent to you.”

“I am a man who would regard what my friends send not as a request, but as a loving command. Tell me!”

“We have received news that the king of Kandy in Sri Lanka, Rajadhi Raja Singa, died suddenly a few days ago. We have also received some information about the situation that has arisen in Kandy because of his death.”

“What information? Tell me in detail!”

“Since Rajadhi Raja Singa had no children, they say there is now great confusion over who is to come to the throne.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 55 → scan 56 -->

“Sabash! Not bad at all! You seem to have kept yourselves well informed about everything happening in Sri Lanka!”

“Do we not both have to face the same enemy? That is why we have taken such care to learn those developments.”

“All right—what more do you wish to say?”

“Does not one wife of the dead king have a younger brother named Kannusamy, while another wife has a younger brother named Muthusamy?”

“Yes—they do.”

“Through that letter, Kattabomman asks you—through your friend Vellaiyathevan, and through this Sundaralingam, his friend—to consider which of these two, if he comes to the throne, would help us resist the British, and to make the way for that outcome.”

“Which of the two, in your estimation, is likely to wave the white fly-whisk for the whites?”

“We have heard that it would be good if Kannusamy assumed the kingship of Kandy, and that Muthusamy is one who could submit to the whites.”

“That is true!”

“Our wish is that you intervene immediately and ensure that Muthusamy, that slave of the British, does not come to the throne. That is why I have come.”

“A good idea! A good wish! But Pilimathalava, who has taken charge as first minister in Kandy, is a dangerous man!”

As Pandaraka Vanniyan said this in deep thought, the terrifying crack of gunshots burst out from a short distance away.

“Stay here! I'll be right back!” Pandaraka Vanniyan said and sprang onto his horse.
