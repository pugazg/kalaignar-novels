# Enmity and Virtue!

As resentment kept growing in his heart from moment to moment, time too moved on by days, by months—why, even by years. Pilimathalavai kept muttering that his life might suddenly end at any time, but before that he had to avenge Lord North's savage frenzy; only then would “Piyaseeli's soul” find peace.

Pilimathalavai was intent on revealing his open opposition to the English authorities only after he had prepared army divisions and weapons strong enough to resist the Kolumbu forces. Vikrama Raja Singan had no wish to concern himself with anything. Trusting Pilimathalavai and leaving the Kandy kingdom under his administration, he spent his days drinking deeply of the pleasures he desired in the women's quarters, until that itself became the purpose of his life. So whenever a note from Pilimathalavai arrived, the royal treasury was opened. The money Pilimathalavai required went to him with the greatest respect. No thought arose in Pilimathalavai's mind about plans for the growth of the Kandy kingdom, or about ways to meet the people's everyday needs—

<!-- source boundary: scan 372 → scan 373 -->

all the departments of that state were turned toward the single task of avenging the cruelty inflicted on Piyaseeli. Yet no one in any department knew the hidden reason why their activities were being redirected.

Preparations for a great war were the essence of Pilimathalavai's planned efforts. His attention was fixed on when the battlefield would be ready. Vikrama Raja Singan's dream was of something else entirely—in the bedchamber.

One evening, Pilimathalavai was discussing the expansion of Kandy's army with his commanders. They were moved as they heard the passion in his speech that English domination must not be allowed to take root in that land. But they did not know that his anger and fury had arisen from the cruelty done to his daughter. The commanders said that Kandy's forces alone were not sufficient to destroy the English completely and beat the drum of victory. As the seed of every effort, they said, it would be best to bring King Vairamuthu Pandaraka Vanniyan into alliance; then the banner of victory could certainly be made to fly.

“But we have made him our enemy. Will he come and conduct war with us?” asked Pilimathalavai.

The commanders replied that Pandarakan's principle was that, in order to defeat a common enemy, the bitterness and hostility among ourselves must be forgotten and action taken together; his sorrow was precisely that not all the governments on the island of Lanka agreed with that principle.

Rubbing his bald head, Pilimathalavai began to think. Then a warrior came and stood in a way that drew his attention. Pilimathalavai went close and asked softly, “What is it?” The warrior whispered something secret into his ear.

“Is that so?” Pilimathalavai said, opening his egg-shaped eyes wide. Turning to the commanders, he said, “We can continue in the morning.” They bowed and left.

Once they had gone, Pilimathalavai asked the warrior with some agitation, “Where is she now?”

“In your private room, my lord!” The warrior bent and straightened.

“All right. Go. I will see her,” Pilimathalavai said, and walked toward his private room.

The door was closed. He opened it and went in. In the dim light of the lamps her face became visible. Yes—Marthani sat there in a luxurious chair.

“Marthani! When did you arrive?”

“I came in the morning. I had to come without the king knowing, so I came here now.”

“Why? What could he possibly do even if he knew? He is my puppet now! Leave that aside—how did you become separated from Piyaseeli? Why did you part?”

“Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar put an end to the drama Piyaseeli and I staged in Mullaitheevu according to your plan. So we were forced apart. Where is Piyaseeli now? Has she come here?”

Overcome with emotion, Pilimathalavai stood without answering Marthani's question.

“What happened to Piyaseeli? Tell me!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 374 → scan 375 -->

Marthani appeared shaken.

“I will tell you. That English beast Lord North fed her to his lust and built her grave in his very bedchamber. He drank and tasted my darling until he turned her into a corpse.”

Marthani sat without moving. Tears ran from her eyes and rolled down her cheeks. Pilimathalavai paced the room for a while, trying to calm his grief and rage, when a suspicion occurred to him.

Marthani had always been one who shrank submissively before him like a tortoise. Yet now, while he stood, she leaned arrogantly back in a chair and spoke. What had caused this difference?

“It is all right that you are shedding tears for Piyaseeli. But I have been watching you since you came back from Mullaitheevu. Respect seems to have run away from you! Pride has attached itself to you! What is the reason for this change?”

The words came out soaked in anger. Marthani's only answer was a painful smile.

“What? I am asking you a question and you are laughing?” Pilimathalavai snapped.

Marthani moved aside the end of the garment that covered one of her legs. A foot was missing from the ankle downward.

“Marthani! What is this?” Pilimathalavai cried, embracing her.

“I should have died. It was Kuruvichchi who opened the road to my death—and then Kuruvichchi herself who closed that road and kept me alive.”

“I do not understand, Marthani. Tell me in detail!”

By then Marthani had stroked that bald head several times and kissed those egg-shaped eyes. Afraid that he might become angry because she had failed to complete the task assigned to her, she cast the net of her seductive skill.

“At one point Piyaseeli and I both fell into Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar's hands. The dagger she threw struck my heel and I fell. Because the dagger had been smeared with poison, the wound would not heal. Kuruvichchi herself, who had struck me down with that dagger, then treated me and cared for me like a sister. Days passed, months passed. The pain in my leg became unbearable. The doctor there said that the wound had begun to fester and that if it were left any longer, an entire leg might have to be removed and even my life would be in danger. Kuruvichchi and Pandarakan asked me what I wanted.”

“You told them to remove the foot?”

“I agreed only on one condition. I said that if they would not send me back to the Kandy kingdom, I would rather die there; if they promised to send me back, I would consent to have the foot cut off and live. Kuruvichchi took Pandarakan aside and spoke to him about something. Then both of them came to me and accepted my condition.”

“Cutting off the leg! Ayyo! You must have suffered terribly!”

“For this Pilimathalavai, uncrowned king of the Kandy kingdom, I must endure anything, must I not? But one thing! Do you know how attentively Kuruvichchi and Pandarakan cared for me while I was receiving treatment? Even though they knew I was their—

<!-- source boundary: scan 376 → scan 377 -->

enemy, when I think even now of how lovingly they treated me…”

“Enough! Enough! Do not think too much about it! You may grow fond of them. You may forget the enmity!”

“I do not know. As far as I am concerned, their virtue has certainly reduced my feeling of enmity.”

“Marthani! If enmity fades, our plans will be obstructed. Our victories will be delayed. However good our enemies may be, however many good deeds they may do for us, the madness of gratitude must never arise in our hearts because of it!”

Marthani remained silent in Pilimathalavai's embrace.

“I saw with my own eyes my daughter Piyaseeli's corpse—the body bitten and torn apart by that lust-crazed man! Tell me a way to avenge it!”

“You are asking me? Are there plans that fail to arise in this brain?”

“I am raising an army to take revenge. But I need a plan to deliberately draw the English into conflict. That is what I am thinking about.”

“I will give you the right plan for that.”

“You will. I know you will!” Pilimathalavai said, embracing her tightly.

At the sight of that scene, like a red-faced monkey embracing a green parrot, the lamps in the room seemed to laugh and dance in the gentle breeze.
