59

# From Today, Your Enemy!

“Everything in the Kandy kingdom is Pilimathalavai now. So he must be summoned to Kolumbu at once on the pretext of discussing an important political matter.”

An officer voiced this opinion at Lord North's council.

“Can you tell me the purpose of bringing him here?” North asked, raising his brows. The officer did not hesitate to answer.

“I suggest this in order to make Pilimathalavai furious with the Kandy king, Vikrama Raja Singan.”

“Vikrama Raja Singan is king only in name now. All authority is already in Pilimathalavai's hands. In that situation, why should he become angry with the Kandy king? How could he?”

“If we invent a story that it was Vikrama Raja Singan who deliberately planned to kill Piyaseeli by biting and tearing her in a frenzy of lust, Pilimathalavai will turn on the Kandy king for revenge. We use him first to bring down the king. Once the king's history is finished, we finish Pilimathalavai in the next stage. In the third stage we place the final full stop by making ourselves Kandy's only refuge. How is my idea?”

“The idea is good! But you are speaking as though you have forgotten me. In Kandy, a royal funeral procession has already been held for Piyaseeli on the claim that she died of burns. Piyaseeli herself told me that she and Pilimathalavai had deceived the Kandy king with just such a false story!”

“So what if she told you? The Kandy king discovered that Piyaseeli had left Kandy alive. He secretly sent spies to search for her. She fell into their hands. Vikrama Raja Singan decided that because she had deceived him by drowning him in sensual intoxication, he would punish her in that same intoxication. As planned, Piyaseeli was brought to the pleasure pavilion of a palace near Kandy. Vikrama Raja Singan arrived there. With honeyed words the king brought Piyaseeli under his control. He gave her more liquor than she could bear—and drank heavily himself. When Piyaseeli lay senseless, he tore her body apart. Half-dead, scarcely alive, Piyaseeli escaped from that pavilion and ran. On the way two British spies, embodiments of compassion, rescued her and brought her here to Lord North's mansion in Kolumbu. She told North everything that had happened and then died. If we paint a tragic picture like that, Pilimathalavai will flare up against Vikrama Raja Singan. He will stand aside and allow an English invasion of Kandy!”

“Bravo! A fine plan indeed! Any father who saw his own daughter in such a state would be shattered. He would set out for revenge. An excellent plan. But one thing! Why would our English spies bring Piyaseeli—

<!-- source boundary: scan 367 → scan 368 -->

here to the capital, Kolumbu, instead of taking her back to Kandy? Could that not make Pilimathalavai suspicious?”

“I have an answer for that too, my lord. Since she had been attacked by that madman in a frenzy mixed with lust and murder, they did not want to take her back into Kandy city where he still was. So they brought her to Kolumbu for immediate treatment.”

“Splendid! Splendid! They say it takes nine lies to hide one. You are the very crown jewel of liars!”

Lord North slapped the officer on the back and laughed loudly.

Then the coffin caught his eye. His laughter stopped at once.

Perhaps his conscience pricked him for having behaved like a beast toward a woman. He stood motionless, staring at the beautiful woman's coffin. The night's frenzy of pleasure flickered through his mind. Like a bank of fog, the scene held him still for a while. Then he turned to the officer and spoke in a trembling voice.

“Whatever else may be said, I should not have treated Piyaseeli so brutally.”

“If we sit grieving over what has happened, our ambition to conquer kingdoms will never be fulfilled. One day this woman would have grown old, wrinkled, bent double, coughing and wheezing, and died after suffering. Instead, she has been shattered now while still a statue of beauty. That is her good fortune! The blessing of dying in the intoxication of pleasure does not come easily to every woman, my lord. You have—

<!-- source boundary: scan 368 → scan 369 -->

done her a kindness. We must not lose the benefit she can still bring us.”

“Then we should send for Pilimathalavai at once, shouldn't we?”

“Yes, my lord. Tell him there is an urgent political matter to discuss.”

Lord North nodded. Soon a messenger from Kolumbu stood before Pilimathalavai in the Kandy palace.

“What sudden political matter is this?” Pilimathalavai asked. The messenger pursed his lips, bowed and said, “I know none of the details.” Pilimathalavai walked toward Vikrama Raja Singan's inner palace. Vikrama Raja Singan, who had made it his royal way of life to spend all his time with his wife, the Madurai princess, heard that Pilimathalavai was coming and ran all the way to the entrance of the women's quarters to receive him.

“An announcement for the king! There appears to be some urgent political issue in the capital, Kolumbu. A summons has come from Lord North asking me to come at once.”

“Oh! Do you need my permission for that?”

“I have not come to ask permission! Nor is there any need. I came only to inform you that I am going to Kolumbu, that is all.”

“Very well! Even this is enough. Thank you for the respect you show me. With the authority and position you hold in the Kandy kingdom, you could go even without informing me!”

“Take care! Let everything remain in good order here. I am leaving for Kolumbu.”

Just as Lord North had expected, Pilimathalavai reached Kolumbu.

<!-- source boundary: scan 369 → scan 370 -->

With the officer's help, Lord North told him the story they had prepared. Pilimathalavai did not appear to believe it. But when they opened the coffin in which Piyaseeli's body had been kept and showed her to him, he was shattered.

After all, no father could see his daughter in such a mangled state and remain patient. Pilimathalavai erupted like a volcano.

“All the lies you have told may fit together neatly. But I cannot believe them, because Vikrama Raja Singan lives in the Kandy palace almost like a prisoner under my control. That he could go to a palace pleasure pavilion without my permission—everything you say is imagination! A story!”

All the efforts North and the officer made to convince Pilimathalavai were wasted.

“My daughter was a certain kind of woman. I do not deny it. It is also true that I used her as a pawn in my game of statecraft. But that does not mean I can continue dealing with you after you murdered her with such cruelty. I will take revenge for revenge.”

Pilimathalavai overflowed with rage. He spat fury like a storm.

Lord North had expected one thing; what happened was something else. Shocked, he cried, “Believe me! Believe me!” in a pleading tone.

“I have believed enough! I have done enough for you because I believed you! After ruining the very life of the daughter of a man who trusted you, do you still pretend before me like friends?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 370 → scan 371 -->

At the very peak of his fury, Pilimathalavai strode out, gestured to the warriors who had come with him and were waiting in the corridor, and mounted his horse.

As the horses moved away, Pilimathalavai pointed toward the place where Lord North stood and growled, “From today I am your enemy! From now on my work is to oppose you!”

Lord North and the officer stood staring at one another's faces in astonishment.
