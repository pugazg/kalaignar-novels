67

# The Bloodstained Sword!

“Beautiful one! My beloved! A thunderbolt has struck the fortress of my mind and shattered it to dust; now I myself stand like a broken little wall. I had been waiting for the day when the white men would hold a ceremony to crown me—yet before my eyes Vikrama Raja Singan, Pandaraka Vanniyan and that little Kuruvichchi Naachchi are holding a victory celebration! Marthani! You must give me an idea to turn that celebration into an inauspicious one.”

With his face drained of colour, Pilimathalavai watched Marthani’s lips eagerly, waiting for her answer.

“I am a Sinhala woman—and you are a mine of experience, an outstanding administrator praised by everyone. Because of my friendship with your daughter Piyaseeli, I supported all her wrongs. Not only did I support them; I committed many further wrongs through those wrongs, and I regret them now. When I went with Piyaseeli to Pandaraka Vanniyan’s Mullaitheevu, I went with hatred at the very thought of Tamils. But only after seeing Pandaraka Vanniyan, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar and the Tamil people there closely did I understand that they were people of culture, determined that there should be no domination at all on this island of Lanka by foreigners such as the English—

<!-- source boundary: scan 415 → scan 416 -->

Think how deeply Pandaraka Vanniyan must care that the white men do not encircle the kingdom of Kandy, if he has brought all his forces here from Mullaitheevu. From now on, my mind will not consent to think even the slightest harm toward him or toward Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar, who is the queen of his heart!”

Pilimathalavai’s owl-like eyes opened grotesquely wide. A great disappointment overflowed in that gaze.

“Marthani! You proudly called yourself a Sinhala woman! What use is that? I dreamed of wearing Kandy’s crown and then lying upside down on the bed so that it touched your feet, embracing you in delight. When I am grieving because that dream has failed, seeing that you take no share in my sorrow makes me wonder whether you are a Sinhala woman at all!”

“What you say is amusing! Must a Sinhala woman necessarily be a smiling spider? Could you not think and reach a decision as I have?”

“Oh! You have already reached a decision—to betray the Sinhala people to the Tamils?”

“The Sinhala people and the Tamils both live on this island; both belong to this island. My wish is that these two peoples should receive equal rights here and live without hating one another. In such a wish there is no place for a word like betrayal. Please allow me to atone for the wrongs I have committed!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 416 → scan 417 -->

“I have been watching you ever since I came. You keep your distance when you speak. I cannot see that sidelong glance of yours that used to entice me. You have even covered the breasts that bewitched and drew me in completely with your sari, as clouds hide the sun. What is the reason? Are you perhaps going to become a Buddhist nun?”

“I have decided that I do not need the garb of a renunciant to scoop out and throw away the sewer filth that has risen high within me and walk along paths of virtue. My wish is to gain the purity and truthfulness required of a Buddhist nun. I have no faith in disguises.”

“How much you have changed, Marthani! There must surely be some immediate cause for this astonishing change.”

“I told you from the beginning. Pandaraka Vanniyan’s spotless friendship toward the Kandy king has made me hold him in great esteem. Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi do not think the purpose of life is merely to feed bodily desire; they live a life dedicated to an ideal. That ideal is that foreign domination must be driven from Lanka. Their words and deeds have affected my heart, and my own destructive actions of the past now feel as though they have made me stand on burning sand while a whip lashes me!”

“Bravo! Marthani, who once rolled in my lap, has suddenly become loyal to the Kandy king! She has become a devotee of Pandaraka Vanniyan!”

“No! I have become a ‘woman’ who has taken the womanhood I had cast aside and joined it back to my flesh, my life and the feelings of my heart.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 417 → scan 418 -->

“Then you will not tell me any way to stop the victory celebration in Kandy that wounds my heart, will you?”

“I say that celebration must not be stopped at all. How then can I give you a way to stop it?”

Even as their conversation continued, the sacred city of Kandy began to show a blooming face at the sights of the victory celebration.

Victory festoons adorned every street.

Rows of lamps shone in every house. Women and men alike wore new clothes, praised the king and danced joyfully in the streets. Soldiers rode horses along the royal road in procession, holding flags high in hands that also bore cruel swords. Amid those warriors, the Kandy king rode in a beautiful chariot with his queen. As though driving alongside that chariot, Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi came in another, their faces bright with smiles. In the Kandy palace, the great ceremonial hall where they were to sit and receive the praises and congratulations of the people had been splendidly decorated, captivating every eye. Seeing the heroes Vikrama Raja Singan and Pandarakan approaching in chariots, the people filled baskets with flowers, showered them like rain and danced in delight. In the ceremonial hall, upon a beautiful silver tray, a sword in a golden sheath gleamed on an ornate pedestal.

It had been arranged as a gift from the Kandy king to Pandarakan. After the procession ended amid a flood of enthusiasm, Vikrama Raja Singan and Pandarakan entered the ceremonial hall—

<!-- source boundary: scan 418 → scan 419 -->

Kuruvichchi and the Kandy queen followed behind them. The elders and learned men filling the hall rose to their feet and offered greetings and respect.

Pilimathalavai, who had not been seen anywhere in the victory procession until then, stood quietly beside the king’s seat in the hall. When the Kandy king saw him, he looked at Pandarakan in surprise. Pandarakan merely smiled and continued forward.

The Kandy king stopped before his seat, seated the queen beside him, and pointed to another nearby seat for Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi. Everyone sat in their places with hearts overflowing with joy. The Kandy king rose and began to speak.

“The foremost reason we have been granted the opportunity to take part in this auspicious celebration is Pandaraka Vanniyan himself.”

The hall shook with applause and cheers. Pandarakan bowed to those gathered there and straightened.

“An auspicious celebration! No, no! If I turn this into an inauspicious celebration, only then will my vow not remain unfulfilled,” Pilimathalavai’s lips muttered.

“First Minister Pilimathalavai! On behalf of our kingdom, I have planned to present a sword to Pandaraka Vanniyan—the supreme warrior, the fearless hero who blocked the English invasion and threw them into disorder. That sword must be presented to Pandarakan at this auspicious celebration. There it is, upon the pedestal—

<!-- source boundary: scan 419 → scan 420 -->

Take the sword and hand it to me with your own hands. I shall present that victory gift to Pandarakan.”

Before the Kandy king had finished speaking, Pilimathalavai went before him, bowed his head and stood there.

“So be it, Your Majesty!” he said.

Then Pilimathalavai removed the entire sword-belt and sheath hanging at his waist, drew out only the sword, and extended it humbly toward the king while bowing.

Every eye in the hall turned toward that sword. Blood soaked it and was still dripping down, drop by drop.

“Minister! What is this?” Vikrama Raja Singan cried in shock.

“King of Kandy! A woman plotted to mix ill omen into this auspicious celebration. That great sinner planned to finish off both the king and the queen. I finished her off and came here carrying the sword stained with her blood.”

“Who was she?”

“Marthani!”

The moment Pilimathalavai said it, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar sprang to her feet shouting, “Marthani? I cannot believe it!”
