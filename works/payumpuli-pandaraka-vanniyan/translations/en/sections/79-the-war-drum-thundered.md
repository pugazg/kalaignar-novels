64

# The War Drum Thundered!

“What has happened to your body at a time like this?” Pilimathalavai asked irritably as he came in, unaware that the man near him was Pandaraka Vanniyan. Pandarakan, in the physician’s disguise, answered his question. The acting suited him well—the trembling body and the weak, faltering words.

“The king is suffering from stomach pain. I have given him medicine. He will recover soon.”

“All right! All right! If your work is done, should you not leave? I have some important secrets to discuss with the king!”

“Forgive me! Forgive me! I am going now. Maharani! Be sure to give him the medicines I prescribed for two more doses. The king will recover!”

Bowing, shrinking and trembling, the physician took leave of Pilimathalavai and the queen, went as far as the door as though he were leaving, and then stood hidden in the crack beside it. In his urgency to speak with the Kandy king, Pilimathalavai paid no attention to the physician and sat beside the king.

“You know that the English detachments have come as far as Mahawilla Ganga and camped there, do you not?”

“I know. I received the news a short while ago!”

“And even after hearing that, you are lying here with stomach pain?”

“I was about to send someone to ask you to come at once. Before that, you yourself arrived!”

“What are we to do now? I was counting on military help from that Pandaraka Vanniyan. He said he would come and has deceived us beautifully!”

“Yes! Even I am astonished. I never expected my friend to abandon me midstream like this!”

“Your friendship is worth mud! I prepared to confront the English only because I expected him to come to Kandy’s aid somehow. Now I do not know what to do.”

“I have thought very carefully and reached a decision. If you are prepared to accept my decision, I will tell you.”

“There is a saying about standing the cow in the water and then bargaining over the price! Only if you tell me what thought and what decision you have made can I say whether I accept it or not!”

“I have already told my queen of the decision and obtained her consent.”

“How long will you keep your fist closed and ask whether I know what is inside? To forget that the enemy has reached Mahawilla Ganga is foolishness beyond foolishness!”

“Why should we regard them as enemies?”

“What else? Shall we regard the English and the Dutch soldiers who have come to help them as our friends and welcome them?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 397 → scan 398 -->

“There is nothing wrong in welcoming them!”

“What? I call them enemies! I burn to take revenge! To think of praising and welcoming them is sheer cowardice!”

“You are superior to me in intelligence, ability and experience. There is not the slightest doubt of that! If you knew the decision I have made, you would not be this angry.”

“Decision! Decision! What is this great decision—this astonishing decision that did not even arise in my own mind?”

“This is a decision that can arise only in the minds of those prepared to make any sacrifice for the country!”

“Then am I a man without the heart to sacrifice for the kingdom of Kandy?”

“Do not be hasty. If you listen calmly to what I say, it will be good for me, good for you and good for the people of Kandy.”

“Go on!”

“Somehow the white forces have arrived in great strength and are advancing on Kandy. We too have been deceived by trusting Pandaraka Vanniyan. In the present situation, if our forces clash with the enemy, we will suffer heavy losses and only embrace defeat. Not merely the soldiers—the people of Kandy too will die in large numbers.”

“So your decision is that we should surrender?”

“I understand that you ache to fight the English and take revenge on them! But there is one thing. If you know my full decision, you will be pleased.”

“Oh! Have you still not said it fully? Very well. I will listen patiently. What is your decision?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 398 → scan 399 -->

“We will allow the English army to enter Kandy freely, without obstruction! Not a single one of our warriors is to raise a sword against them! They will enter our palace. I will declare that I have renounced the crown and assure them that the kingdom of Kandy will submit to the English. I will ask them to hand the entire administration of Kandy, under their authority, to Pilimathalavai! The English will agree. They will seat you on the throne of Kandy, and I and my family will immediately leave for Tamilakam. That is my decision!”

On hearing this decision from Vikrama Raja Singan, Pilimathalavai could not believe his own ears.

Until now he had been the uncrowned king of Kandy. Now, without the spilling of even a drop of blood, he was to be crowned. As he thought of this, the fire of anger he had felt toward the English because of his daughter Piyaseeli gradually died down. Looking at the lines of joy appearing on Pilimathalavai’s face before him, Vikrama Raja Singan marvelled at the power of office.

Vikrama Raja Singan did not wish to rule as a slave of the English. So, as a stratagem, he had decided to renounce the crown. Yet, Pilimathalavai thought, the king had proved himself a fool by deciding to make him ruler of Kandy. Somehow, without hardship, the kingdom of Kandy was going to fall beneath his feet. And was not all this because of his daughter Piyaseeli, who had cared nothing for chastity? In his mind he thanked her.

The Kandy king looked at the queen with a curling smile. Her face showed no movement. Her only concern was that Pilimathalavai should not notice Pandarakan hidden beside the doorway.

“Then I shall order the frontier defences of Kandy to be relaxed! I will even ensure that our warriors are not seen moving through the streets!”

“That is right! Let our warriors drink liquor in their camps and sleep! Let the English army enter all the way to this palace like a dog walking into an open house. I myself will welcome them, hand the country over to you through them, take my leave and depart!”

Pilimathalavai’s immense joy became like the confluence of many seas. To become king of Kandy without sword or blood—was that an ordinary thing? He patted the king approvingly and left the room.

It was a good thing that Piyaseeli had been brutally killed! Otherwise he would never have become enraged with the English. The situation would never have developed to the point of an English invasion. Only because everything had happened unexpectedly was Pilimathalavai now getting the chance to become king of Kandy, even if Kandy became a subject country. Filled with this elation, Pilimathalavai departed. Pandaraka Vanniyan, who had been standing by the doorway, shut and bolted the door, stroked his beard and came laughing toward Vikrama Raja Singan.

“Well? Did I say everything as you told me? Was it all right?”

“Bravo! It was wonderful! Did you see how wildly Pilimathalavai’s hunger for office dances—

within him? Kandy may become a subject country, but so long as he gets to be its king, that is enough for him!”

Pandarakan ground his teeth as he poured out his anger at Pilimathalavai.

“He has made the people of Kandy believe that he is an opponent of English domination. Now he plans to place the blame for betraying Kandy upon me and take over its administration as a lackey of the Parangiyar!”

“That is what he thinks. Because you listened to my idea and acted accordingly, he too has believed it. But what is going to happen will be very different!”

Pandarakan laughed aloud.

“Let us see how your plan unfolds!”

Vikrama Raja Singan rose from the bed and paced about the room.

“All right. I will take my leave. I must send word to Kuruvichchi. Everything will happen as we planned. Do not worry. I will go.”

Pandaraka Vanniyan gripped the Kandy king’s shoulder, shook it warmly, and left, still in the physician’s disguise.

At Mahawilla Ganga, General Macdowall laughed fiercely and smashed a liquor bottle on the ground. The reason was the news he had received: there was no one in Kandy to resist his forces.

“Soldiers! Empty every bottle of liquor! Take up your weapons! There is no one in Kandy to stop us! Move at once!”

At the general’s command the soldiers sprang to their feet. Empty liquor bottles rolled across the ground. The war drum thundered. The army rose.
