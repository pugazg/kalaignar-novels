# Web of Conspiracy!

The marriage of Piyasali and Kannusami was celebrated in Kandy with great splendour. Muthusami was deeply disappointed when even Governor North of Colombo—who had sent Pilimathala an order to arrest Kannusami—attended the wedding and blessed the couple.

Pandaraka Vanniyan remained at the Kandy palace for a few days, wondering what results these strange games of statecraft would bring. News spread that Kannusami was going to be crowned king of Kandy and that the Governor in Colombo would not oppose it. Muthusami tried in every possible way to thwart the coronation by intrigue, but failed. Deciding that he should not even be in Kandy on the day Kannusami was crowned, he fled all the way to Colombo.

Governor North patted and consoled Muthusami as he fell at his feet and cried—the envoy of British domination offering comfort!

“Do not worry, Muthusami! I too have been watching how events are moving contrary to our intentions. But remember one thing. Do not make the mistake of weighing British brains as though they were ordinary! We will not rush as the Dutch and the Portuguese did. There may be anger in the mind, but we will not display it outside. The strength of our domination lies in approaching the enemy cautiously without underestimating him. Only then can victory be won. We are people who have gained many victories in precisely that way. That is why we are behaving as though we do not see the changes taking place in Kandy. It does not mean that we have washed our hands of Kandy!”

Governor North's words gave Muthusami a little courage. On North's advice he stayed in the Colombo palace as a helper to the British.

For Kannusami's coronation in Kandy, Governor North sent only a message of congratulations.

Fortunately, Pandaraka Vanniyan was pleased that he had been spared the trouble of seeing that white monkey's face once again.

But he knew nothing about the long letter Governor North of Colombo had sent Pilimathala. It was a mine of deceit, a weave of intrigue. Unaware of that background, Pandarakan spent several more days in the Kandy palace with his friend Kannusami.

On one side, anxiety over what might have happened to Kuruvichchi Naachchi; on another, worry about what his sisters—whom he had told to remain at the Mullaitheevu palace—might be thinking.

<!-- source boundary: scan 147 → scan 148 -->

And between those worries came another thought: perhaps it was best to remain in Kandy a few days longer to support Kannusami's rule.

Minister Pilimathala read and reread the long letter the Governor in Colombo had sent him. He would read it once on rising from bed in the morning. He would read it again before taking a little rest after his midday meal. At night, before going to sleep, he would read it once more.

“Create a quarrel between Kannusami and Pandaraka Vanniyan? Is that possible? Can I do it? If I can, the Governor in Colombo says he will give me the whole kingdom of Kandy and provide thousands of British troops to protect it. What sweet news! Not troops alone—the British Empire promises money in heaps and heaps. When such a pledge is given, even if God himself stands in my way, I must act beyond him! Pandarakan and Kannusami are like flesh and nail. How can they be separated? Does not the nail grow outward away from the flesh? In the same way I need only move Pandarakan a little away from Kannusami. But merely separating them will not be enough. I must create outright hostility. Only then will the two clash with one another. Once Kannusami and Pandarakan become irreconcilable enemies, I myself will take possession of Kandy with British help. The Governor has written only: ‘Let enmity arise.’ He has not shown even the slightest sign of how that enmity is to be created! If I ask the Governor himself for that idea too, will he not doubt my intelligence? I myself

will think! I will find a way to ignite a great feud between Pandarakan and Kannusami. But one thing: the seed of that feud must be planted before Pandarakan returns from Kandy!”

Many different schemes arose in Pilimathala's mind, each appearing before him as though pleading, “Choose me—choose me!” He rejected them all and finally selected one conspiracy as the most suitable. At first he himself felt a little ashamed even to think of that plan. Then he shook off the shame and rose with a victorious smile.

Knowing nothing of the web of conspiracy Pilimathala was preparing to sow hostility between the two men who were friends, Pandaraka Vanniyan was sleeping soundly in a beautiful chamber of the Kandy palace. He had spent a long time troubling his mind over Mullaitheevu and over the efforts of foreign enemies to plant the feet of their domination in Sri Lanka; at last he had fallen asleep, exhausted and unaware of what was happening near him.

A beautiful hand touched the lamp that burned brightly in the room and dimmed its light. It was a woman's hand. Then the fine gauze curtain gently moving around the bed was drawn aside. A woman came through the curtain and sat on Pandarakan's bed. In the faint light her eyes fluttered rapidly. Her lips seemed to compete with her eyes.

It was none other than Piyasili. She stared intently at his face. With her fingers she let fall the upper garment that had covered the beauty of her breasts, then gently touched Pandarakan's shoulder.
