“Tell him his story will go no further! Oh! Only if you return can you tell him what I said! How is my royal strategy? I have decided to break your Kurathan cunning with intrigue. Its first act is about to be staged now.”

Saying this, Kakkai Vanniyan gently tapped his cheek, laughed with a crowing sound, and ordered his six warriors:

“Hmm! Take Thanigaimala and tie him to the Kannagiyamman temple flagstaff. Not only his body—his mouth must be bound too. In a little while Nalla Naachchi will come here looking for me. She and I will complete our marriage before the Mother's shrine, and then, in our wedding finery, we will come before him and take his head as an offering.”

The warriors dragged Thanigaimala outside. The devotees lined up in front of the bushes were alarmed. Thanigaimala then raised his hand and made a signal. The signal meant that they were to remain quiet. So silence prevailed. Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors, hidden behind the bushes, shouted and leapt about in delight at their first victory. Their leader restrained their clamour.

Now came the sound of another horse! Not like before—it came at great speed and stopped at the temple entrance. Nalla Naachchiyar jumped down from the horse without even thinking of looking around her. She ran inside with only one urgent thought: she had to see her lover Pirmanandan—and see him alive. She ran so hard that she nearly stumbled and fell. Staggering, recovering herself and crying aloud, she rushed in. Before her sat Kakkai Vanniyan, deep in meditation.

“My dear! My dear! Why are you doing this? Please—

<!-- source boundary: scan 301 → scan 302 -->

look at me! Here I am—your Nalla Naachchiyar has come!” she cried, touching his thigh, shaking him, and beginning to sob.

“Who? Nalla Naachchi? My beloved parrot? My nectar? My very life? My treasure! Is it you? Is it truly you? Have you come?”

Kakkai Vanniyan had reached the very peak of his performance.

“It is I! Your own Nalla Naachchi! Thank goodness that Kurathi came—otherwise by now I would have lost my god!”

“My love! Though you knew this Pirmanandan's heart, though you understood that in that heart there is room for you alone, you sent me no definite word at all about our marriage. That is why I came to this decision.”

“Do you not know about my brother? How many troubles he has! How many problems! So I could not insist stubbornly with him. My sister too asked me to be patient for a few days. She assured me that we could obtain my brother's consent afterward. That is why I sent you no word. But is that a reason to make such a decision?”

“Nalla Naachchi! Even now I am not going to change my decision. Here, with Goddess Kannagi as witness, before her very presence, our Gandharva marriage must take place. You and I must bow at the Mother's feet as husband and wife. If you are going to torment me again by pointing to your brother as the reason, then here, in this very place, I will end my life.”

Having spoken with emotion, he looked at the Mother's image and cried out dramatically:

<!-- source boundary: scan 302 → scan 303 -->

“Mother! Kannagiyamma! This Pirmanandan, who loved Nalla Naachchi more than life itself, is about to fall dead at your lotus feet! Receive me!”

He took a dagger in his hand and raised it to stab himself in the chest. Nalla Naachchiyar, not realizing that it was an act, suddenly sprang upon him and snatched the dagger away.

“Let me die! Without you, what damned life is there for me in this world?” he wailed. Even tears streamed down his cheeks to support his performance.

Wiping those tears away with her beautiful fingers, Nalla Naachchiyar said,

“Have you still not understood that I feel the same way? I am ready this very moment! With Goddess Kannagi as witness, let us exchange rings.”

No sooner had she said it than he leapt up as though he would reach the canopy of the hall and lifted her into his arms.

“Shh! This is the Goddess's shrine!” she scolded him playfully, and he put her down.

The two exchanged rings there in the hall itself.

They worshipped Kannagi. They came happily out of the temple. Thanigaimala, tied to the flagstaff, watched the two of them approach the temple entrance hand in hand, rage boiling within him.

“Look there! A fool came to clash with me! He has taken his beating and lies there.”

So saying, Nalla Naachchi's lover Pirmanandan—Kakkai Vanniyan himself—pointed toward the flagstaff.
