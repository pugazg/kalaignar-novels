She sobbed and sobbed! She wailed! To console her, Thanigaimala drew her against his chest and, saying, “Do not cry! Be patient!” patted her on the back.

“Even at this moment, when I stand here having lost my husband, this feels so very soothing! Ayyo! Where did you learn this magic?”

Saying this, Marthani too embraced him tightly.

“Marthani! Whatever has happened has happened—your husband could not have gone very far from this region by now!”

“Then go right now, find him and bring him back!”

Pressing her cheek against Thanigai's cheek, Marthani lamented for her husband.

“Wait! I will cast a net across the whole Vavuniya forest, search for your husband, and bring him back here with me!”

Thanigaimala set out! Maragatham pulled him toward her, gave him a kiss, and sent him off.

Thanigaimala glared at her.

“This is the last kiss! Once my husband comes back, I will never come near you again!”

“Do not fail to find him on that account!”

Smiling, Thanigaimala went down the steps and came to the entrance. A green flag was flying at the front of the house. After looking at it, he sprang onto a horse.

The horse flew all across that region! He wandered about searching the whole day! There was no sign at all of the mute Jeyaseelan. Where could he have gone? Thanigaimala found no answer to the question he kept asking himself! Everything was a riddle! That bewilderment only deepened his confusion. What Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar had said kept striking and splashing against his heart like great raindrops in summer!

Unable to find Jeyaseelan anywhere, he finally urged the horse back toward home. He dismounted at the entrance to the house. Not seeing the green flag that had been at the front, he was a little surprised.

He entered the house. Thinking Marthani would be waiting eagerly for his return and would come running to him, he looked toward the upper floor.

The upstairs door was closed. Thinking he might rest for a while, he entered his room.

There was a woman there! She rose hurriedly and stood before him respectfully.

She looked like a Kurathi woman!

“You?”

“A Kurathi fortune-teller!”

“Your name?”

“Kala!”

“Kala? The Kala Marthani spoke about? That Kala?”

He asked with still greater surprise. When she nodded “yes,” she seemed like a heap of diamonds sparkling in the lamplight.
