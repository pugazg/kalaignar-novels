# What Mystery Was Hidden?

Thanigaimala ran from one floor to the next. Standing before him with her hair loose and dishevelled, Marthani wailed and wept. “My husband has gone away somewhere! He became angry with me and left me! Here—this is the letter he wrote and left behind!”

Thanigaimala took the letter and looked at it.

“To Marthani, with love! Chi! Chi! Wrong! Wrong! To address you—who would ask what price ‘love’ sells for—as ‘with love’ is madness of the purest kind. I loved you with my whole heart. I believed that you too held me dearer than life itself. A mute man who cannot speak! A man with a disability of some kind! And yet I used to swell with happiness again and again, thinking: Marthani bears holy love toward me, and lives believing that her husband is the god she sees with her own eyes. All these days I thanked God that, for me who works as a loyal spy of the kingdom of Kandy, you had become both a protective fortress and a loving wife. On the first night after our marriage, inside the bridal chamber, I looked only at your face and shed tears. Then you wiped those tears with your kanthal-flower fingers. I did not know then that they were not kanthal fingers at all, but the sharp fangs of a kattu-viriyan snake! I wrote and asked whether you truly desired a mute man like me. You grasped my feet and drenched them with your tears! Then I wrote another foolish question.

At the time it seemed to me a foolish question. But now I understand what a wise question it was. That question was: ‘You will never betray me at any time, will you?’ After reading it, the performance you put on—ah! Magnificent acting! ‘I will never betray you either in heart or in body. God created this life for you alone. What a shameful question you have asked me! Look at my eyes—do you see even a shadow of stain in them? My eyebrow—is it some bow that bends the moment it sees young men in their prime? My eye—is it an arrow? If my face is a lotus, this lotus will bloom only for the sun standing here before me! Even if I fell into a river or pond and were in danger of losing my life, and some man touched me and lifted me out, I would think I had become food defiled by another's saliva and would give up my life, my lord! This is a vow!’ You said all that and cried until your eyes swelled! Was all of that merely a play acted by a Neeli? I regarded you as greater than this whole world. With you beside me, I lived proudly thinking, ‘What do I have to worry about?’ How sacred is the husband-and-wife bond that arose between us—very well, let even that be cast aside. What of the vow that the two of us took for our king of Kandy, and, under his command, for his friend Pandaraka Vanniyan? Was it not to fulfil that vow, and to serve Pandaraka Vanniyan for that purpose, that we came to Mullaitheevu? But what have you done? Think for a moment! Would even God tolerate it? You do not leave even a stone planted in the street alone! You bow before it, stand lost in devotion as piety drips from you! You, who go about worshipping<!-- verified split-word boundary: scan 268 → scan 269 --> every deity in the town—are you now clinging to the shoulders of every man in the town? How did you have the heart to deceive this mute man? What wrong did I do to you? Have I ever cast my eyes upon any woman other than you? When I have lived having decided that you are everything to me, can you fail to understand that and commit such betrayal? What I saw today is true witness before my own eyes! How many more scenes of pleasure might there have been that I never saw? You must have been the heroine in all those scenes! Prey for lustful men! A dish laid out for a feast! You could simply have given your address on the street where women for sale live! Bees that swarm over flowers also swarm over filth, do they not? If a woman like you can marry a mute man like me, posing as though she has made an incomparable sacrifice, receive the townspeople's praise in daylight, and in darkness lie in the lap of who knows what men and let them bite her lips—what else can I do but throw beneath my feet and trample the respect I have held for womankind all these years? Thanigaimala—the sinner! The criminal! He behaved like pure gold. Yet he was the one who heaped live embers into my heart! What use is there in blaming him? If a man-enchantress with a body like yours deliberately goes and ensnares him—poor man, what can he do? He pounced! The moment you hurriedly prepared the food, left even your wedded husband hungry, and ran downstairs, a suspicion I had never before felt arose in me! I followed! I hid behind the pillars! After a little while the door was closed! Yes! Ayyo! A heaven of pleasure opened inside that room. That door was closed for that very purpose. Through the crack of the door—

I saw that dazzling, revolting sight! With the very mouth that praised me as Indra and Chandra while you embraced and kissed me, you pursed your lips and swallowed his lips—I saw that eyesore! How did your heart dare to do it? Ah! How many dramas have you staged to deceive me? Only now do I understand the plan you made: marry some man merely for the name of it, deceive the town, live as you please—live in pleasure, live in luxury—roll in the mire of sin—commit wickedness! Marthani! You are a great sinner! A demoness! For me to remain with you any longer is a danger to my very life! Even if there were no danger, it would be shameful! It could never be the act of a man with honour! I can carry out the command of my king, the king of Kandy, without your help! Under his orders I will give my labour to assist Pandaraka Vanniyan! Why, I will give even my life! Noble woman? Chaste wife? Enough of your company! Forget me! Quench your desire with Thanigaimala! If even that does not satisfy you, Pandaraka Vanniyan's army has plenty of rough warriors! Bring all of them under your sway! I do not wish to see your face again! I am going and will return! No! No! I am leaving! Thus, Jeyaseelan!”

“Did you read this letter? What am I to do? From the day I married him until this moment, I have never even lifted my eyes to look at another man. Some magnetic force from you pulled me toward you. Because of that I stumbled and fell. How many men have circled and circled around me! How many wealthy gentlemen stood ready to pour out money for me!
