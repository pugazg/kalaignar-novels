8

# Kaakkai Vanniyan!

Shocked, Sundaralingam looked at the grief-stricken elderly women and asked anxiously, “Do you know where Sangili Thatha might have been taken?” One of the women, who seemed to know a little more of the details, stepped forward and answered.

“Since the men who carried him away were British soldiers, they must have taken him to Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace.”

“Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace? Who does it belong to? Where is it?”

“That too is in a corner of this Vanni region. It is a separate kingdom. Kaakkai Vanniyan rules that little kingdom.”

“What? How astonishing! Another kingdom inside Pandaraka Vanniyan's kingdom?”

“Yes! That is how it is! Kaakkai Vanniyan has declared Pandaraka Vanniyan his enemy and has become a hireling of the British. British troops surround him for his protection. Emboldened by that protection, Kaakkai Vanniyan keeps causing Pandaraka Vanniyan many difficulties. Having brought him under their control, the British are using Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace as their base and devising plans to bring the Mullaitheevu region under their control somehow!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 65 → scan 66 -->

As the old woman explained these matters in detail, Sundaralingam stood with gooseflesh, realizing that despite her age, her attachment and affection for that soil shone through, along with her conviction that outsiders must not enslave it.

Hopeful that he could learn still more from her, he asked with great respect, “Mother, what is your name?”

“Thangachchi,” said the old woman. Even in that sorrowful atmosphere, Sundaralingam felt laughter rise within him. The old woman did not fail to notice it. “The name my parents gave me was Thanga Naachchi; it has been shortened like this,” she said. He wanted to learn more. Yet he decided that first he must send word through one of the warriors to Pandaraka Vanniyan and Kuruvichchi Naachchi that Sangili Thatha had been abducted. Accordingly, he sent one warrior hurrying to Pandaraka Vanniyan's mansion to inform them.

“Why does Kaakkai Vanniyan oppose Pandaraka Vanniyan?”

“Becoming a slave of the British is the first reason! Beyond that, there is another reason too!”

“What is that reason?”

“Kaakkai Vanniyan tried every possible way to marry Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar and failed in the end. She not only rejected his wish; she openly told Kaakkai Vanniyan that she loved Pandaraka Vanniyan. That inflamed his anger even more! From then on his whole manner changed. He already had the mind of a betrayer—as the saying goes, a monkey already, and then a sore on top of it—and Kaakkai

<!-- source boundary: scan 66 → scan 67 -->

Vanniyan has now become a mortal enemy of Pandaraka Vanniyan and Kuruvichchi Naachchi, and even takes supreme delight in proclaiming it.”

“Then could the carrying away of Sangili Thalapathi also have been arranged by Kaakkai Vanniyan?”

“I do not know whose arrangement it was! One thing is certain. The British soldiers who carried Sangili Thalapathi away would have taken him straight to Kaakkai Vanniyan's palace!”

“Then if we go to Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace, we can see him there, can't we?”

“You can see him! But you cannot rescue him!”

“Why?”

“In that palace, Kaakkai Vanniyan is the king! The entire guard is made up of white men! You cannot break through that protection and rescue him!”

“Are we so ordinary?”

“You are men who will risk your lives! Men of resolute hearts! There is no doubt of that. But my point is that this small force is not enough to defeat the British who guard the entire palace with weapons such as guns and cannon and bring him back!”

Thanga Naachchi's speech was filled with composure, experience and firmness.

“You speak with such clarity—I asked only your name, and you told me! What work did you do in your younger days? Was it perhaps palace service?”

“No! My work is not something that can be done only in youth. It is work of the mind, work that ripens and shines more and more with age! I am a teacher. The children of this village

<!-- source boundary: scan 67 → scan 68 -->

have studied with me! Even now many children study under me. Shall I tell you something with pride? Pandaraka Vanniyan, whose name is Kulasekaram Vairamuthu, and Kuruvichchi Naachchi, who today sits beside him as his equal bearing a sharp sword to strike down cruel enemies—both were my students! Along with that pride, I also bear one humiliation: Kaakkai Vanniyan too was my student! What can one do? In those student days, all of them stood as defenders of the honour of this soil. But with the change of time, in the hearts of a few like Kaakkai Vanniyan, the termite of selfishness raised its mound, and there the black cobra called treachery took up residence.”

“Mother! I feel I could go on listening to you. You speak so wonderfully. But we do not wish to delay. We have to go to Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace, do we not?”

“You have already sent word to the king about it—wouldn't it be better if troops came from there to support you?”

“Let those troops come when they come! In the meantime, we shall make our own attempt. Mother, with your blessing we are setting out for Kaarikkattu Moolai,” Sundaralingam said. Turning to the warriors who had come with him, he asked, “One of you go ahead and guide us on the road to Kaarikkattu Moolai!”

Then the old woman Thanga Naachchi looked at Sundaralingam and asked, “You learned every detail by asking me. But you have not said who you are, have you?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 68 → scan 69 -->

“Don't you know us? We are warriors coming from Pandaraka Vanniyan's mansion!” Sundaralingam replied, and Thanga Naachchi smiled gently.

“I can see that you are palace warriors. But the Tamil spoken by one of you sounds a little different! Hearing your Tamil, you seem as though you have come from Tamil Nadu!” she said, fixing her gaze on his face.

“Mother, you may notice a slight difference in Tamil pronunciation. But in feeling you will not find even the difference of a needle-point. When you speak truths without concealment before me, I should not hide the truth from you, should I? I come from Panchalankurichi. I am one of Veerapandiya Kattabomman's commanders. My name is Sundaralinga Kudumbar.”

“That is enough, younger brother! I need no more detail. Do not think you must tell me everything. I will not be offended. There will be so many secrets in the freedom struggles taking place here and there.”

Thus putting a full stop to the other details Sundaralingam was about to give, Thanga Naachchi said sorrowfully, “What can one do? The white men chose the time of the Vattrappalai Kannagi Amman festival to devise their schemes. Except for one or two people, everyone in the village has gone to the Vattrappalai Visaka festival. Those who came thinking this was the right time to spy used some pretext and carried Sangili Thalapathi away.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 69 → scan 70 -->

“Very well, Mother! We shall go now!” Sundaralingam said, urging his horse forward. Pandaraka Vanniyan's guards followed him a short distance, then hesitated beside some dense bushes. As though they wanted to tell Sundaralingam something important, they pulled their horses to a halt.

“It is not wise to go to Kaarikkattu Moolai Palace with reckless courage! The British soldiers there outnumber us many times over! We should think this through.”

When even the warriors who were supposed to accompany him hesitated, Sundaralingam too became uncertain.

“The warrior who went to inform the king will have reached the mansion by now! Let us see what decision the king makes!”

When all the warriors expressed the same view, Sundaralingam too had to yield to it.

Just as Thanga Naachchiyammai had guessed, the British soldiers carried Sangili Thalapathi to Kaakkai Vanniyan's palace at Kaarikkattu Moolai.

The British soldiers waited with Sangili Thalapathi in the council hall for Kaakkai Vanniyan. But he was reclining in delight with a woman in the women's quarters adjoining the hall. A wine cup in his hand; his head in the lap of that woman with intoxicating eyes. She was a hired woman summoned for that day's pleasure on the bed. For Kaakkai Vanniyan, who had made “live by any means” his rule of life, floating on waves of the smiles of beautiful women—and thinking that alone the purpose of having been born—was his heaven of luxury and pleasure.

<!-- source boundary: scan 70 → scan 71 -->

His complexion suited his name; his eyes drooped as though watching a cat, yet peered upward over the lids, seeming to drip venom at every moment except the instant of attack. His heavy body, his habit of speaking with his teeth bared, and his day-and-night hunger for the pleasures of the bed made it clear at a glance that he was a creature who had sold himself to his enemies and would betray his very country.

The British officer, tired after waiting a long time, struck the door of the women's quarters four or five times forcefully with the butt of his gun. Kaakkai Vanniyan opened the door and entered the council hall with the wine cup still in his hand.

The first figure that caught his eye was Sangili Thalapathi.

“Oh! So you've brought this lame old man?” Kaakkai Vanniyan asked, laughing loudly.

Even his masters, the British officers, found that laughter repellent. But they did not show it. “What should be done with this old man, Your Majesty?” the British officers asked. For Kaakkai Vanniyan, who knelt before them as though that one mark of respect were enough and as though honour, rights and freedom were otherwise unnecessary, the sight of Sangili Thalapathi sent anger surging through him.

“Hey, you lame fellow! You babbled that your granddaughter was some great beauty, a goddess descended from Indra's heaven, and that I was unworthy to marry her. Shall I show you now what my standing is, what my worth is, and what power I hold?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 71 → scan 72 -->

Sangili Thalapathi looked at Kaakkai Vanniyan and smiled, without a shade of fear or a trace of worry on his face.

“What is there for a lame man to laugh about! You are in my grip now! There is one way for you to save your life. Write a letter to your granddaughter Kuruvichchi Naachchi. Write: ‘Dear Kuruvichchi! Come at once and save me!’”

Sangili Thalapathi became like a tiger that had been provoked.

“What did you say, fool? My granddaughter's life is dearer to me than my own! Kuruvichchi Naachchi belongs only to Pandaraka Vanniyan! I am not so shameless as to make her a plaything on your cushioned bed, nor is my granddaughter out of her senses!”

“You will speak like that now! Once you're locked in the underground prison for two days and starved, you'll come around on your own and bow down! You will writhe and cry, ‘I will give you Kuruvichchi!’ I know! So throw this man into the underground prison!” Kaakkai Vanniyan shouted.

At a signal from the British officer, the soldiers removed Sangili Thalapathi from there and took him away to the underground prison.
