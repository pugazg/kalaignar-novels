49

# The Enemy Is Closing In!

Only now did Nalla Naachchiyar remember the lines of the poem her brother had written and shown her that day. Pandaraka Vanniyan had understood the truth: the young merchant his sister loved was not Pirmanandan, but the scoundrel and traitor Kakkai Vanniyan. That was why he had stood there shattered like a temple tower struck by lightning. I was the one who was deceived. My brother, who showers affection upon me like rain and loves me so deeply—when he asked who my lover was, I showed him that portrait. He left the place with his heart broken and ground to dust. Only now do I understand why! My dear brother's anguish—that his beloved sister, without realizing it, had become an accomplice to an act of treachery against the land and her people—had poured itself out as that poem of pain.

“You drew a picture and showed it to me, didn't you? Here, I too have drawn a picture! Read it! This is the picture of my life! I am leaving!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan had handed her that poem and left for Kandy. Even then, the poem had scorched her heart like melting wax. But her deep love for Pirmanandan had fitted a heavy lock upon her eyes and refused to let them open.

<!-- source boundary: scan 311 → scan 312 -->

Now the lines Pandarakan had written and left with her began to lament within her heart:

> The man who makes life itself a struggle  
> and lives,  
> rejoices when in pitch-darkness  
> he sees a flash of lightning.  
> If, entwined with that lightning,  
> a thunderbolt too falls upon his head,  
> and he survives even that without dying,  
> does he not live on, consuming fire!  
> Some who came beside him promising,  
> “I will give my life for you,” remain beside him  
> only to dig his grave!  
> Some who were born with him as his own kin  
> have themselves become a killing disease.  
> To stand against all this—  
> who will give me a heart that can bear anything?  
> Even if a crore of vultures tried  
> to tear a hole in the sky, I would say they could not!  
> Even if a crore of alien enemies came  
> to ruin the honour of the Tamils,  
> they would be defeated!

It seemed to Nalla Naachchi as though Pandaraka Vanniyan himself had entered her heart and was proclaiming those lines again and again, louder and louder!

“Alas! What a great traitor I have become! No woman could commit a sin like the one I have committed! Am I an Eelam Tamil woman? No, no, never! I have lost every qualification and every shred of honour to call myself that! My eyes, deceived into loving a degraded—

<!-- source boundary: scan 312 → scan 313 -->

man like you, who sells himself without shame for cash and money—shouldn't I gouge them out and make them a feast for the vultures? A sledgehammer is striking my heart! Let it keep striking! The treachery I have committed does not wound only my brother! It does not merely maim the honour of my family! Haven't I brought an indelible disgrace upon the sacred land of Lanka itself, upon the Eelam Tamil people!”

Nalla Naachchi shook off Kakkai Vanniyan's hands and stood before him, flowing like molten fire from a volcano.

“Why are you making such a grand fuss about Tamil people and all that nonsense? Am I not a Tamil? Am I a Sinhalese? Or a white man?” Kakkai Vanniyan asked with a mocking smile.

“You are a Tamil! By people, by language, by the blood running in your body, you are a Tamil! But a Tamil without shame or self-respect! A Tamil who performs worship at the feet of money! A Tamil who pawns his honour for office!” Nalla Naachchi poured out her fury. Yet he did not become angry. He would not. To lumps of flesh—to sacks made only for food—whose sole aim has become comfort, opportunity, a painless life and pleasures that never depart, even a heap of filth seems like a fragrant mound of flowers! Creatures that have discovered sweetness and taste in it—yes, pigs—do they feel ashamed when people turn away from them in disgust? In the same way, Kakkai Vanniyan ignored Nalla Naachchi's scorching words and once again moved toward her.

“‘Tamil! Tamil people! Tamil woman!’ you keep crowing! Answer what I ask you! After exchanging rings in the temple of Kannagiyamman, the goddess of chastity, and becoming husband and wife, is it Tamil culture to look at the husband whose hand you took and abuse him however you please as a villain and a traitor?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 313 → scan 314 -->

“It is by talking about culture like this that you make the whole community of women suffer! If a husband is steeped in evil conduct that destroys himself, it is the wife's duty to correct him! I do not care about you destroying yourself! You are destroying this entire land! You are destroying the very sense of being Tamil! A traitor to your people—a deceitful fox—a python gaping for office—a creature of luxury lower than a slave, letting its self-respect be prostituted to the degraded—am I your wife? Better that my life end than that I hear this cruel word! But this life will not be wasted without purpose. If I take your life—the life of one who performs throat-cutting deeds and hands his own people bound over to the enemy—and only then my life departs, then, if there is such a thing as heaven, that life will earn the fortune of ascending and sitting upon a warrior's throne there!”

“Nalla Naachchi! What are you babbling? Have you forgotten the love stories you and I spoke with joy beside waterfalls, beneath the shade of the banyan tree, by crystal streams, on the tops of rocks? Have you failed to remember the pledges we made to one another? Those delightful moments when we held hands and swore our vows—were they dreams? Fantasies?”

“Neither dreams nor fantasies! They are the history of a rash woman who stepped into fire without knowing it was not a clear pool of water! Her history cannot end without accomplishing something! It must not! Hmm! Draw your sword!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 314 → scan 315 -->

As she spoke, Nalla Naachchi drew her sword and raised it straight above his head. Realizing that words could no longer defeat her, Kakkai Vanniyan drew his own sword and blocked hers. The sword fight between them reached its height. Unable to withstand the swordplay of the warrior woman Nalla Naachchi, Kakkai struggled. Two or three times he fell to the ground and rose again. He stumbled. He floundered. Their two swords clashed as though streaks of lightning were flashing crosswise and lengthwise. At one point the hilts of the two swords locked together, and they stared into each other's faces. Four eyes remained fixed. In Kakkai's eyes was a kind of pleading; hers stood superior to his.

“Nalla Naachchi! If a lady kills her lord, hell is what awaits her!”

“Not my lord—not even the noble serpent resting upon the Lord's crowned head. What I have decided to bring down is the venomous snake writhing to betray the land!”

“Is there no reconsideration at all?”

“If there is such a thing as rebirth, then I will consider carefully. I will consider carefully how never again to be deceived by a treacherous fraud like you! Kakkai Vanniyan! It is too late now—I cannot grant you the alms of life!”

The swords, which had been locked together for a moment, tore through the air with a great sound and clashed again. Deciding that she must spare him no longer—that killing him for his wrongdoing was the only atonement—Nalla Naachchi leapt at Kakkai Vanniyan with her sword like a lioness springing upon a wild buffalo. Storm, cyclone, cataclysmic wind—none of them—

<!-- source boundary: scan 315 → scan 316 -->

could serve as a comparison for the fury with which Nalla Naachchi surged forward. Seeing it, Kakkai Vanniyan began to run away with his sword in hand. Nalla Naachchi would not let him go. She pursued him.

Before they had run very far, both saw warriors approaching on ten or fifteen horses. Delighted, thinking they must be Mullaitheevu warriors, Nalla Naachchi raised her sword to welcome them.

Kakkai Vanniyan was thrown into panic. Convinced there was no escape now, he stood completely still. Nalla Naachchi ran toward him with her sword raised. She now had fresh strength because she believed the Mullaitheevu warriors were coming.

Seeing no other way to escape the point of Nalla Naachchi's sword, Kakkai Vanniyan prostrated himself full-length before her and fell face-down on the ground, crying, “Mother! Save me!” Nalla Naachchiyar felt not a drop of pity. But she did not wish to stab him in the back while he lay face-down.

“You fraud who stages deceitful dramas! Get up! You must die! At the moment of death I grant you one concession. Die taking the sword in your chest! Let at least that one name redeem your life of treachery! Hmm, get up!”

Nalla Naachchiyar shouted furiously.

Kakkai Vanniyan, still lying face-down, slowly raised his head and looked. Suddenly a new light appeared in his eyes. He realized that the horsemen coming toward them were not from Mullaitheevu. They were his own warriors, beaten by the Mullaitheevu men in the fight at the Kannagiyamman temple and now fleeing toward him.

<!-- source boundary: scan 316 → scan 317 -->

Believing that Nalla Naachchi would not kill him so long as he remained face-down until the horses arrived, he lay there without moving.

As he had expected, his warriors reached the place. Kakkai Vanniyan picked up the sword he had dropped and sprang at Nalla Naachchi.

Realizing with a start that enemies had closed in around her, Nalla Naachchi was shocked.
