63

# The Disguised Physician!

“He has never so much as hinted to me until now about this weakness of his!” Kuruvichchi said in astonishment.

“Is this something a man can tell a woman? And besides, because of the vow the two of you have taken, Pandaraka Vanniyan would never have had any need to tell you! What? Am I not right?”

The physician stroked his beard and looked Kuruvichchi up and down.

“Physician! If Pandarakan is without manly power, this is not an illness that came upon him halfway through life. From what you say, it is clear that he was born this way! Only now has the truth come to light—that he made that vow with me merely to hide this incapacity and manage the situation!”

“You have said it exactly, amma! But there is one thing. What Pandaraka Vanniyan has done is terribly sinful! To conceal his own weakness, he has plotted to make a young flowering woman like you remain fruitless all her life. Such a scheme cannot be forgiven!”

“Physician! After what you have told me, all the affection, fondness and love I had for him have flown away!”

“Is that so?”

“Not only have they flown away—I have even begun to hate him!”

“Ayyo! Hate him? Oh dear, am I responsible for that?”

“Yes—from now on, you must take responsibility.”

“What are you saying, amma? I do not understand anything!”

“Are you not the miraculous physician who restores manly power to men who have lost it?”

“Yes!”

“Then surely you yourself can have no lack of manly power! You must be a very treasury of virility!”

“So what?”

“Today you have revealed the great secret that a man without manly power has used to deceive me all these days. That makes me respect you!”

“Respect?”

“Not merely respect—boundless affection too!”

“Affection? For me?”

“Not only affection—great fondness too!”

“Fondness? For this old man whose youth is past?”

“Not only boundless affection and great fondness—even an unchanging love has arisen!”

“What? What? What are you saying, amma?”

“What else? Pandarakan has treated with contempt the feelings flourishing in my young heart and planned to ruin my very life. Compared with him, you are many thousands of times better! Stronger! The moment I saw you, I surrendered my heart!”

“What terrifying praise is this! If Pandaraka—

<!-- source boundary: scan 392 → scan 393 -->

Vanniyan hears of this, will not his broad sword come flying at me?”

“Have I been deeded over to him as property?”

“Whatever it may be, you should not have changed your love in a single instant with such haste!”

“Physician! You too should not have come in such haste after sticking on that beard! Here—touch it and see. Near your ear, the beard has not even been pasted on properly!”

Pressing the physician’s beard against his cheek with her hand, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar burst into ringing laughter. Because the sound of her laughter could be heard outside, Pandaraka Vanniyan, who was in the physician’s disguise, covered her mouth.

“You thought you could deceive me, did you not? Now who has been deceived—you or I?”

“Neither of us can deceive the other! Nor will either of us be deceived!”

“Is your plan to go to the Kandy palace in this disguise?”

“Yes, Kuruvichchi! I have decided that I must meet the Kandy king only by going in disguise. Otherwise Pilimathalavai will devise some plot to prevent me and my friend, the Kandy king, from meeting at all! So, as I said before, you take command of the army and set out. I will send word from Kandy to the camp where you are staying. After that, our forces can begin to act!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan took leave of Kuruvichchi and set out. Seeing the handsome bearing of his majestic stride as he walked away, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar called loudly enough for him to hear:

<!-- source boundary: scan 393 → scan 394 -->

“The disguise will not betray you! But that warrior’s stride will!”

Hearing these words, spoken by Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar in overflowing delight, Pandaraka Vanniyan turned toward her with affection and smiled. At once he bent his back into a hunch and staggered forward, making her laugh.

He mounted the horse waiting ready at the palace entrance, waved to Kuruvichchi, and urged the horse onward. Standing at the entrance, that young woman watched in rapture as the man she loved—upright as a mountain, warrior of ideals, gold of principle, a great man who would not hesitate to lie even in death’s lap to defend the honour of the land—flew away on horseback in the stooped, shrunken guise of a physician.

His coming in disguise, his mischievous talk, her own initial shock, then her understanding the truth and trying to startle him in return—all of it played across Kuruvichchi’s inner eye. The waterfall of love made music like a woman wearing anklets and sent her heart spinning.

What fascination did she have for him? And was he any less than she in pouring affection upon her?

They had set aside the feelings that naturally rise in youth. Because their two hearts had been bound tightly by the rope of love to an ideal, that deep affection had spread out like a boundless sky.

Kuruvichchi Naachchi watched until the horse disappeared from sight. After a while, coming back to herself—

<!-- source boundary: scan 394 → scan 395 -->

she went inside the palace. At her command, the commanders made ready. Leaving only the soldiers needed to guard the Mullaitheevu fort and the frontier, she ordered all remaining forces to form ranks and march with her.

Meanwhile, in addition to the force that had set out under Major General Macdowall, another force under Colonel Barbut moved toward Kandy. The two English forces reached a place called ‘Mahawilla Ganga’, three miles from Kandy. When word suddenly reached Pilimathalavai that the English army had come so close, he was shaken. Agitated that Pandaraka Vanniyan’s military support had still not reached Kandy, he ran toward King Vikrama Raja Singan’s inner apartments. The door was closed. He himself hammered loudly upon it.

“No sense of time for his amorous games! The enemy is nearby, and at a time like this he is rolling about in bed with his virtuous wife!” Pilimathalavai muttered.

Then the door opened. Seeing that the queen herself had opened it, Pilimathalavai asked in a somewhat raised voice, “Where is the king?”

“He is unwell. A physician has come and is examining him,” the queen said, pointing toward the bed.

“Is that so?” Pilimathalavai asked as he looked toward the bed.

The king lay on his back, and the man in the physician’s disguise—Pandaraka Vanniyan—was examining him, pressing a hand upon his stomach.
