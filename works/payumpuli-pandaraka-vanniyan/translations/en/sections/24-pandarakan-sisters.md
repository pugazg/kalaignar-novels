# Pandarakan's Sisters!

After ordering that the spy from Kandy be brought in, Pandaraka Vanniyan went to the palace hall. There he saw two women who smiled at him and said, “Brother! Greetings!”

“Oh! Nallanaachchi and Oomaichchi! When did you both come?” Pandarakan asked, overflowing with affection.

“Just now!” replied Nallanaachchi.

“I came without knowing that elder sister was coming!” said Oomainaachchi.

Pandarakan's sister Nallanaachchi was administering the Trincomalee region under Pandarakan's rule. His other sister, Oomaichchi, was in charge of the region called Panangamam and assisted her brother in governing it.

Nallanaachchi was, true to her name, a woman of rare virtues—and no less gifted in beauty. She was slender, her complexion like sandalwood blended with vermilion. Though her charm and allure were enough to bring down those who saw her, she remained the lamp of a cultured household. Her appearance, bearing and manner made it clear that modesty and a deep capacity for thought came naturally to her. The other sister, Oomaichchi Naachchi, was mischievous. She too was beautiful. Every movement of hers carried the power to bewitch.

Yet she never strayed in the least from the domestic discipline and chastity prized by the Tamil community. She also had the courage to face anything without fear. Since Pandaraka Vanniyan loved both his sisters from the heart, he wanted to know what had brought them there so suddenly and unexpectedly. Oomaichchi Naachchi understood his curiosity and said, “I have come to talk about a marriage!”

“My marriage, of course! I had postponed it until my ideal was fulfilled. Now I have decided that I do not want to marry at all,” came the sharp reply.

“Brother! Just because I keep pressing you, have you become so angry all at once that you are saying you will never marry?” Oomaichchi Naachchi asked with a tinkling laugh.

“It is nothing like that! When a vine struggles to leap onto a branch that has been struck by lightning and is falling away, how can it be wrong to stop the vine from making that attempt? Poor Kuruvichchi Naachchi—at least let her live well!” said Pandarakan, his voice trembling.

“Brother! I did not come to talk about your marriage! We must talk about that too and reach a decision soon. But I rushed here now because of elder sister Nallanaachchi's marriage. And when I came for that, elder sister had already arrived before me!” Oomaichchi said, casting a teasing glance at Nallanaachchi.

“What is this, dear girl? Marriage? Has it been settled without even my knowing?” Pandaraka Vanniyan asked Nallanaachchi with a mocking smile.

<!-- source boundary: scan 124 → scan 125 -->

She blushed, lowered her head, and hid herself beside a pillar like a lovely moon concealed by a dark cloud.

“I'll tell you the whole story, brother!” said Oomaichchi.

“Oh! There is a story too? A love story, perhaps?” the king laughed.

Encouraged that her brother was listening without shock, Oomaichchi began enthusiastically.

“Brother! It is exactly the love story you think it is! But I have never seen the lover! Elder sister has never shown him to me either! Perhaps she is afraid. Since I am prettier than she is, what if he abandons her and falls in love with me?”

Oomaichchi Naachchi burst into her natural, ringing laughter.

Knowing his sister's nature, Pandaraka Vanniyan said, “All right! All right! Tell me what happened!” Just then a warrior entered with the spy from Kandy, introduced him to Pandarakan, and withdrew.

“Welcome! When did you arrive? Is my friend Kannusami well?” Pandarakan asked, seating the spy and sitting down himself.

“He is the one who sent me to you!”

“Anything important?”

“You know everything about the situation that has arisen in Kandy after the death of King Rajathi Rajasinga.”

“I knew the whole situation when the king died! Both men in the succession contest are my friends. Between Kannusami and Muthusami, who now has the better chance?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 125 → scan 126 -->

“As you first thought, Prince Kannusami had the stronger chance. But now it appears that, with the sudden intervention of British officers, Kandy's chief minister is weaving some sort of conspiracy!”

“And if that conspiracy succeeds?”

“Then the other prince, Muthusami, will be the one who can ascend the throne!”

“What? British intervention! And Chief Minister Pilimathala supporting it! A conspiracy! If my friend Muthusami is crowned through that scheme, Kandy will become ready to live on its knees before the British!”

“Yes! That is how completely the situation is turning upside down. Only if you leave for Kandy at once can Prince Kannusami be made king of Kandy. That is why Prince Kannusami sent me to you urgently!”

“Very well, spy! I shall leave tomorrow and come to Kandy. Tell Kannusami this, and tell him not to announce any decision before meeting me!”

After taking leave of Pandarakan, the spy from Kandy departed. Pandarakan's sisters, who had been waiting until then, returned to the hall.

“Well, Nallanaachchi! Who is this lover who is going to marry you?” Pandarakan asked cheerfully.

“Why ask elder sister? Ask me! I'll tell you! One day, when she had gone out on administrative business connected with Trincomalee, she met this lover. Some affection seems to have arisen in both their hearts. He is a great merchant. His name is Brahmanandan. He is said to be a young man. Even if he is no Manmatha in looks,

<!-- source boundary: scan 126 → scan 127 -->

somehow he has stolen elder sister's heart. After that they met two or three more times. The bridegroom has been in quite a hurry!”

“Here, Oomaichchi! He is going to become your elder sister's husband, is he not? Show him a little respect! You keep calling the bridegroom ‘he’ and ‘that fellow’!”

“That's simply how I speak! Never mind that, brother—listen to the matter. At their third meeting, when the bridegroom became impatient, elder sister stopped him and firmly said: marriage first!”

“Hmm! And what did this Brahmanandan say to that?”

“What would he say? As though a ripe fruit had slipped straight into milk, he said, ‘Very well! I am ready for marriage!’ and came forward to make the great sacrifice!”

“He is not making any sacrifice. Your elder sister is the one preparing to sacrifice. She holds administrative responsibility for Trincomalee; she is the sister of Pandaraka Vanniyan, king of Mullaitheevu. If she marries a merchant, that is the greater sacrifice!”

“Nallanaachchi elder sister! I have told the person who needed to hear what had to be said. You ran here ahead of me to tell brother and ask his permission—so ask him now!” Oomaichchi said, pulling her sister by the hands and placing her before their brother.

“What is this, Nallanaachchi? You have fallen in love with some unknown man whose place and background we do not even know, and suddenly you come asking for permission! What answer am I supposed to give?”

Oomaichchi Naachchi cut in.

“He is not some unknown nobody! Elder sister has drawn a portrait of her lover. A little hand-held portrait! Once you see it, you can decide whether the bridegroom is a suitable match!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 127 → scan 128 -->

“Where is it? Show me the portrait!” Pandarakan said, turning toward Nallanaachchi.

Overcome by shyness, Nallanaachchi lowered her head, her cheeks reddening. She took out a small portrait and handed it to him, then sprang away and hid herself behind the curtain in the hall.

Pandaraka Vanniyan looked at the portrait she had given him and started in shock.

He could not believe his own eyes.

The face he saw in that portrait was Kaakkai Vanniyan!
