## 3

# The Old Man Met on the Way!

Mullaitheevu is the name not only of a particular boat landing but of that whole region. When the boat that had brought Sundaralingam drew near one part of the Mullaitheevu coast, Bharathan looked at him and asked, “Where should we stop? At Mullaitheevu landing? Alampil landing? Or Silavathurai landing?”

With a smile, Sundaralingam said, “What is this, Bharatha? Why do you ask as though you don't know? We have to go to King Pandaraka Vanniyan's fort only after passing Vattrappalai. So take us to whichever boat landing is nearest for reaching Vattrappalai!”

At once Maruthu said, “Then Silavathurai is the nearer landing!”

“I've come trusting you fellows! Just take me somewhere suitable and put me ashore!” Sundaralingam said with a laugh.

“Is it enough merely to take you there and leave you? Don't we have to bring you back too?” Bharathan asked teasingly.

The three continued their lively talk as the boat reached the shore at Silavathurai landing.

After getting out of the boat, Sundaralingam looked at Maruthu and Bharathan and said, “You stay here! I'll be back before the night gets too far along.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 28 → scan 29 -->

“You're going alone—let one of us come with you!” said Maruthu.

“If one of you comes with me, then one of you has to remain alone here on the shore. So you two keep each other company,” Sundaralingam replied mischievously.

“There's nothing to fear on the seashore! You're the one who has to pass Vattrappalai and go to the palace. The path runs through dense forest; in that forest, except for tigers and lions, every other kind of animal is there. That's why it's better to go with a companion!” said Bharathan.

“Here is my companion!” Sundaralingam said, touching and showing the sword hanging at his waist, and took leave of them.

The morning sun's rays fell through the gaps between the tall forest trees, spreading beauty all along the path. Sundaralingam walked on, drinking in that natural beauty. Though it was forest country, small hamlets appeared here and there in little clusters. Even through those settlements, he had to trace and follow the winding route to Vattrappalai.

Sundaralingam was interested in learning how the houses in that region were built, and for that very reason he slowed his pace when passing through inhabited areas.

On long rectangular plots, houses with sloping roofs, houses with conical roofs and houses with rounded roofs appeared mixed together in those settlements. The roofs were thatched with coconut leaves, palmyra leaves and straw. The walls were mostly built of clay.

<!-- source boundary: scan 29 → scan 30 -->

Some houses had raised verandas. In front of the somewhat larger houses, there were pandals. Trees such as *kaaya*, *muthirai* and *vedukkunari*, described as strong, hard-timbered trees, had been used as the posts of those pandals.

Just from seeing the veranda areas and the fronts of the pandals, Sundaralingam understood that the people kept their houses very clean.

In front of the houses grew, close together, *pathiri*, *thodai* and *desikkai* trees, pomegranate trees, and also murungai, mango and jackfruit trees. A short distance in front of the houses stood the paddy-storage structures called *kombarai*. Cows and buffaloes grazed close to the houses.

One thing that attracted Sundaralingam was the sight of green-leaf festoons and banana plants tied at the front pandals of almost every house!

Sundaralingam became eager to know why almost every house wore such a festive appearance. But in the hamlets he had passed through, no one had come into his sight to answer his question. His curiosity therefore grew still more.

Clusters of settlements—the houses there in festive dress, yet not a single person conducting a festival to be seen: that was the great question that had arisen in his mind!
