# A Woman’s Atonement!

Just as Pilimathalavai had intended, the victory celebration ended as a celebration of sorrow.

Just as Pandaraka Vanniyan had sensed the poison inside Pilimathalavai’s bald head, spewing out through his bulging eyes, the Kandy king too had sensed it. So neither of them was surprised by Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar’s shock. They themselves had been struck by the same shock. The celebration stopped halfway. Pandarakan did not fail to notice Vikrama Raja Singan looking at him in grief-stricken bewilderment.

“It is all right! The celebration can be held whenever we wish. But only if we go at once can we understand the truth,” said Pandarakan, giving the Kandy king a reassuring pat on the shoulder. Saying, “Forgive me. Come with me,” the Kandy king left the ceremonial hall and walked quickly toward the inner part of the palace. Pandarakan, Kuruvichchi, the queen and the English followed him. Delighted that Marthani’s death had brought the auspicious celebration to an end, Pilimathalavai widened his owl-like eyes still further and followed them, rubbing his head as he walked.

Without knowing where Marthani’s corpse was,

<!-- source boundary: scan 421 → scan 422 -->

the Kandy king was walking through the palace corridors when he suddenly stopped and turned to Pilimathalavai.

“Where is Marthani?” he demanded, anger surging in his voice. Pilimathalavai understood the note of authority in the question.

“This courage has not come to him on its own! It has come because Pandaraka Vanniyan is nearby!” he muttered to himself.

“That wretch is lying dead in my room!”

Hearing Pilimathalavai’s reply, Vikrama Raja Singan asked as they walked, “In your room? Why did she come there? How did she come?”

“She used to be rather close with me! Trusting in that, she came to my room to tell me of the conspiracy she had devised. The moment I learned of that treacherous woman’s plan, my loyalty to the king overcame even me, and I finished her off.”

Turning on Pilimathalavai, Kuruvichchi said in a stern voice,

“Go and proclaim to people who do not know you that you are a man who speaks only the truth! They may perhaps believe you! We cannot believe this story you are spinning—that Marthani plotted to kill the king or the queen!”

“Why such haste? Why should I lie? Why would I have any need to kill a woman who was dear to me? Look, my room is only a little farther away! Once we get there, every truth will become clear!”

Rolling his bulging eyes and rubbing his bald

<!-- source boundary: scan 422 → scan 423 -->

head, Pilimathalavai gave a sinister grin. It was a truly terrifying grin.

Still talking, they all entered Pilimathalavai’s room.

“Where is Marthani?” Kuruvichchi asked in agitation.

Marthani had first come to Mullaitheevu intending harm and had later reformed. Kuruvichchi had not known her affectionately for very long, but she was moved when she remembered the tenderness Marthani had shown her even in that short time. Her beautiful eyes swept around the room. Marthani’s corpse was not there. But they saw patches of clotted blood all across the room. Seeing that Marthani’s body was gone, even Pilimathalavai was astonished. He was startled.

“What? Everything is a mystery! Where is Marthani’s body?”

Pilimathalavai grew irritated when Vikrama Raja Singan questioned him in a threatening tone. Why does he suddenly have so much courage and arrogance? he wondered, glaring at the king. Yet he himself was shocked: where had the corpse of Marthani, whom he had stabbed down only a short while earlier, disappeared to?

“Here! Right here, in this very place, I made that wretch a corpse with this sword!”

Pilimathalavai shouted the words aloud.

“Everything that happens in the Kandy palace is a mystery story! A woman supposedly plotted to kill the king and queen! She was supposedly dear to this man! He supposedly killed her! And the dead woman, instead of becoming a spirit, has supposedly gone bodily to heaven! Pilimathalavai, we are expected to believe all these stories, are we not?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 423 → scan 424 -->

Pandaraka Vanniyan asked the question not merely in mockery, but boiling like a volcano.

Pilimathalavai had planned to remain patient until Pandarakan had been sent back to Mullaitheevu and then successfully complete the final stage of his conspiracy in the kingdom of Kandy. So he did not lose his temper; he answered calmly.

“Believe that I acted out of loyalty to the king! Otherwise I would not have killed Marthani without even fearing that ‘stree-hatthi’ would befall me. Look—there is milk in these golden bowls! Marthani arranged to mix poison into it and give it to the king and queen. I stopped her. She refused. She told me she had struck a bargain with the English. She said the English had promised to make me king of Kandy and her its queen. She said that for this, the conspiracy had to be carried through. I told her I did not want the kingdom of Kandy, that until the moment I closed my eyes for the last time I would remain a loyal servant of Vikrama Raja Singan. But she said she would somehow give the poisoned milk to the king and queen and kill them. She said that even if I stopped her now, she would kill them someday. In the anger that came over me, without knowing what I was doing, I drew my sword and stabbed her in the chest.”

Refusing to believe him, Kuruvichchi cried, “All lies! Skilled drama! Brilliant acting!”

“You still will not believe me? Look how deadly the poison she mixed into this milk is!” he said, pouring the milk from the golden bowl onto the floor. At that moment, on a pedestal in one corner of the room

<!-- source boundary: scan 424 → scan 425 -->

a kitten that had been sitting there ran over and eagerly drank the milk. Even as it drank, it curled up, fell and died.

“Will you still not believe me? How could I allow an attempt to kill the king whom I value as my very life, using a poison powerful enough to destroy life merely by bringing it near the lips? How could I leave alive a treacherous woman who had conceived such a thing?”

The Kandy king’s face plainly showed that he was wondering whether there might be truth in Pilimathalavai’s words. But Pandaraka Vanniyan did not allow that doubt to linger. Turning to Pilimathalavai, he fired off questions: “Then where is the lifeless body of this Marthani whom you killed? Where have you hidden it? Why did you need to hide it?”

“I did not hide that wretch’s body!”

“Then where did it disappear to? Who hid it?”

Pandarakan’s voice rang out majestically.

“No one hid it! I hid myself!” came a woman’s voice from within the room. It was Marthani’s voice!

Before the stunned onlookers could turn, Marthani pushed aside a curtain and appeared, her clothes soaked through with blood.

“I am not dead yet! I want to remain alive for a few more moments! Everyone has come, just as I expected! I knew you would come, and so, holding on to my last breath, I waited behind this curtain with this dagger. For all my wrongs, I have already asked

<!-- source boundary: scan 425 → scan 426 -->

for forgiveness. I ask again now. Forgive me. This worthless man standing here wanted to turn the victory celebration into a celebration of sorrow. He cannot punish me merely because I speak disrespectfully of Pilimathalavai. He has already punished me. It is an outright lie when he says I dared to kill the Kandy king and queen! He is the one who asked me, ‘Girl, tell me a way to turn the auspicious celebration into an inauspicious one!’ I opposed his plan—and so he finished me off and fulfilled his own aim! Now my aim is about to be fulfilled! A Sinhala woman is now going to die as a Sinhala woman!”

Even as she spoke, Marthani moved faster than lightning. She snatched the sword from the Kandy king’s waist and, in the very next instant, drove it into Pilimathalavai’s chest. Not once—not twice—she stabbed until her fury subsided. Indeed, she kept stabbing until her own spirit subsided, and then collapsed to the floor.

Kuruvichchi ran to her, crying, “Marthani!” and caught her body. “Sister! Forgive me! A woman has atoned,” were Marthani’s final words, and she lay dead in Kuruvichchi’s lap.

As though deceit, conspiracy and the base instinct that sells self-respect to increase one’s own pleasure had all taken form and curled up there, Pilimathalavai’s corpse lay motionless in a flood of blood.
