# Everything Is for the Good!

Just as Thanigaimala wished, permission was granted for a hundred warriors to go with him. That evening he took them to a dense grove, seated them there, and explained what they were to do after coming with him. The warriors hid all the knives they carried inside their waistbands. Except for Thanigaimala, within a few moments all of them had transformed themselves into great devotees bound for the Kannagiyamman temple. Ten in one group, fifteen in another, five or six in yet another—they were to split up like this and go to the Kannagiyamman temple group by group. Once all the groups had reached the temple, Thanigaimala would arrive there on horseback. Kakkai Vanniyan would be there pretending to perform penance. But he certainly would not have come alone to the Vattrappalai Kannagi shrine. Somewhere near the temple he would have hidden his soldiers. Those men would surround Thanigaimala and somehow finish him off. But by making fools of them, driving them away, arresting Kakkai Vanniyan and—

dragging him back to Mullaitheevu, the task would be completed very easily. That was why a hundred warriors of Mullaitheevu were being sent in advance to the Paththiniyamman temple disguised as paragons of devotion.

Having laid out this plan, Thanigaimala began sending the warriors from the grove in separate groups.

“O maiden Kannagi!
O chaste wife who takes vengeance!
O lamp of Kovalan's lineage! Protect our family,
Mother!”

One such group went singing like this, carrying *kavadis* on their shoulders.

“O dear goddess—our
female deity Mariyamman!
A deity upon the earth—why,
is there none even in heaven, Mother!
O fiercely wrathful Mother—our
Kannagi Kali Mother!
We come asking for a boon, Mother,
you must grant it, Mother—just so!”

Singing in this way, another group set out toward Vattrappalai, leaping and frolicking, carrying *karagams* on their heads, with devotional flavour dripping from every step.

Thanigaimala was delighted to see his warriors carrying out the plan with even greater ingenuity than he had imagined. After all the groups had left, he would spur on his horse. Until then, he remained mounted beneath a large tree in the grove, watching.

Meanwhile, according to the plan devised together with Marthani, Piyasili in the guise of Kala reached the vicinity of Nalla Naachchiyar's mansion in Thirukonamalai. Now she looked even more convincing than before—a true-to-life fortune-telling woman. Bending, swaying and moving sinuously, Kala reached the mansion entrance and became agitated, insisting that she had to tell Nalla Naachchi a message mixed with urgency and danger—and that she had to tell it immediately. When the palace guards reported to Nalla Naachchiyar that such a woman was in a state of agitation at the entrance, she ordered them to bring Kala in at once. Kala entered, bowed with great respect, and stood before her.

“Who are you, woman?”

“A fortune-teller!” A Kurathi!

“Why have you come to me?”

Kala looked all around with her bee-like eyes. Then she answered softly:

“I must speak to you alone, Mother!”

On hearing that, Nalla Naachchiyar signalled to the warriors and her companions there and ordered them to move away.

“Kurathi! What did you say your name was?”

“My name is Kala! My full name is Kalavathi!”

“Did anyone call you here to tell fortunes?”

“I did not come to tell fortunes. I came running to stop a terrible deed.”

“Speak so I can understand!”

“That is what I came to do.”

Kala wiped her eyes with the end of her *mundhanai* and sobbed heavily. With each sob, her breasts rose and fell enough to attract even women.

<!-- source boundary: scan 294 → scan 295 -->

“Don't cry! You said it was urgent; you said it was dangerous! Why are you so hesitant to tell me?”

“All this trouble came because I went to tell your lover Pramanandan's fortune!”

The moment she heard the name Pramanandan, Nalla Naachchi was shocked. She withered with anxiety over what might have happened to him. Trembling, she asked, “What happened? What happened?”

“What remains to happen now? Something that must never happen is about to happen! I came running here somehow to stop it.”

Nalla Naachchiyar trembled from head to foot! Terrified by what dangerous news this Kurathi might tell her about her beloved, she seized Kala and shook her.

“Tell me! He is well, isn't he?”

“Up to this very moment he is well. But what may happen to him within a few *naazhigais*—only that Vattrappalai Kannagiyamman knows!”

“Ayyo! You are breaking my heart. Tell me what it is!”

“Mother! Your love for that Pramanandan is true, isn't it?”

“Can't you see how I am trembling?”

“I can! I can! But what use is trembling?”

“Who knows how much longer your beloved will remain in this world?”

“Ayyo! Don't torture me! Tell me the details!”

“He sent for me to read his fortune. I went.”

“Mm! Go on!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 295 → scan 296 -->

“Wait, Mother! Don't be impatient! ‘I will give you as much gold and treasure as you want. Look at the lines of my palm and tell me at once whether my wish will be fulfilled,’ he said! I replied, ‘Sir, great merchant-lord! Merely looking at your beauty is enough—that itself is my reward! I need no other gold or wealth.’ The moment I said this, he could not contain his anger! ‘Hey, Kurathi! Even if Urvasi and Rambai themselves descended from heaven, I would not touch any woman even in my thoughts except my Nalla Naachchi!’ he shouted loudly.”

Hearing this invention of Kala's, Nalla Naachchi felt as though she were flying through the sky on wings. Her heart lamented in anguish, wondering what danger had come upon such a godlike beloved of hers.

“When Pramanandan asked, ‘Will I attain the woman I love?’ I pursed my lips!”

“Why? Why?”

“His palm lines said so. I told him that truth. At once he fell to the ground, rolled about thrashing, and wept. ‘If my Nalla Naachchi will not be mine, what use is there in remaining in this world?’ he cried.”

“What happened after that?”

Poor thing—the warrior-woman who guarded Thirukonamalai could not bear her lover's suffering. Kala had now decided to make full use of those feelings!

“‘You will not marry Nalla Naachchi in this birth! But if, by some chance, that fate is to change, go to the Vattrappalai Kannagiyamman temple and remain in penance for three days! By Kannagiyamman's grace your love may be fulfilled,’ I told him!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 296 → scan 297 -->

“After listening to you, has my lover gone there to perform penance?”

“Not ordinary penance! Severe penance! Do you know what vow he made before leaving?”

“Tell me, tell me! What vow?”

“Three days of penance at the Kannagi temple! If you do not come and marry him within that time, his corpse will fall inside that very temple, he says!”

Without realizing it, Nalla Naachchiyar screamed aloud! “Bring me my horse!” she shouted.

Kala stood smiling at the crazed haste of Nalla Naachchiyar as she mounted her horse and flew away.

“Hey, Kurathi! What did you tell her? Where is Naachchiyar going?” asked those in the palace.

“Everything is for a good cause!”

Saying this, Kala bent, swayed, moved sinuously, and walked away from there.
