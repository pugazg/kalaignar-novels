74

# The Crow and the Sparrow!

“The news that the mighty Pandaraka Vanniyan—Kulasekaram Vairamuthu, with shoulders broad enough to lift a mountain—was defeated by me and fled is a golden page in the story of my life! My friend Edward! If Pandarakan’s body too had been lying among these corpses, I would have been deeply troubled. That leaping tiger has escaped for the moment only to become a tiger lying in wait to leap upon us again. Even though we possess so many modern weapons, cannon and shells, look how many of our soldiers lie dead here. However well-equipped an army may be, men who fight for pay cannot easily reap victory over those driven into battle by feeling and conviction. The battle fought here near Karsilai Madu has proved the truth of what I am saying!”

With sorrow on his face and, at the same time, the exhilaration of victory, Vanderi Berg held Edward’s shoulder and spoke in this way. Edward Madge was not without a reply.

“My friend! When one goes to seize another country, there is no reason for the ordinary soldier to possess the same zeal, eagerness and feeling

<!-- source boundary: scan 461 → scan 462 -->

as the commander! Those determined not to let enemies enslave their own land will value their lives as nothing and fight. They will kiss death gladly. But there can be no two opinions that soldiers in an army setting out to enslave others will not possess that feeling, enthusiasm or resolve. That is why hundreds of mercenaries must die in order to kill a handful of warriors who fight for a cause!”

“We both think alike, Edward! Shall we set about searching for Pandaraka Vanniyan? Or...?”

“In this situation we must not even think of such a plan! We have lost many soldiers in this battle. Pandarakan too has lost many of his men, but we must not treat lightly the few warriors who escaped with him. What we must do now is seize Mullaitheevu while Pandarakan is absent!”

“If so, what do you think Pandaraka Vanniyan’s plan will be?”

“With his strength reduced, he will not confront us face to face. From now on mountain caves will be his mansions! Dense forests will become the palaces where he lives! From there his camps will spring up throughout the forests!”

“Are we simply to watch while he grows stronger like that?”

“We too must wait for a while and increase our strength. First, let us go to the Mullaitheevu palace!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 462 → scan 463 -->

“Edward! I have one wish! Could we not fulfil it before leaving here for Mullaitheevu?”

As Vanderi Berg said this, a sense of shame, without his even realizing it, descended upon his head like a great burden and made him lower it.

“What wish? Is there any difficulty in fulfilling it?”

“None at all! I want to hold a stone-setting ceremony at this place. You must unveil the stone with your own hands!”

“A stone? What stone?”

“I want a stone erected here, engraved with the words: ‘At this very place near Karsilai Madu, Vanderi Berg defeated Pandaraka Vanniyan.’”

“I admire my friend’s extraordinary cleverness! We were both commanders who led the forces. But the memorial stone for the victory goes to Vanderi Berg—and another commander, Edward, must unveil it! Very well! A temporary victory over Pandarakan! Let all the glory of that victory go to Vanderi Berg alone!”

Vanderi Berg understood the mocking smile on Edward’s face. Yet desire knows no shame; at once he shook Edward’s hand in thanks and ordered the soldiers to prepare an inscribed stone to be set up there.

Memorial to Pandaraka Vanniyan at Karsilai Madu

At this very place Commander Vanderi Berg defeated Pandaraka Vanniyan.

Within a short while a stone bearing those words stood upright. Vanderi Berg gazed again and again at the words written upon it

<!-- source boundary: scan 463 → scan 464 -->

and swelled with pride. A silk cloth was draped over the stone. Cups of liquor were handed to the English soldiers who had survived and those who were wounded, and the cups were continually refilled. Such was the splendour of the stone-setting ceremony with which they celebrated their victory.

Once the drink had gone to Edward’s head, he went up to the stone and howled,

“Long live Vanderi Berg!”

As he pulled away the silk cloth, he tied it around his own neck and began dancing round and round the memorial stone. The other soldiers joined him, staggering and stumbling in drunken delight. Filled with the thought that the stone would sing his fame for all time, Vanderi Berg drank until his belly swelled and then, mistaking the stone for Edward, flung his arms around it.

Those who had lain around the stone on the ground in a stupor slowly rose one by one. Once Vanderi Berg and Edward had regained enough sobriety to understand each other, they roused the soldiers to enter Mullaitheevu.

As the English force moved through the dense parts of the forest, Vanderi Berg suddenly cried out in shock,

“Edward! There! There!”

and pointed to the ground. Edward stopped his horse and looked at the spot Vanderi Berg had indicated.

The path was thick with mud and slush. Hoofprints showed that several horses had passed that way.

<!-- source boundary: scan 464 → scan 465 -->

“Yes—Pandaraka Vanniyan must have gone this way with his warriors! This is certainly not the road to Mullaitheevu. So he has abandoned Mullaitheevu and is hiding somewhere else to establish a camp.”

Vanderi Berg accepted Edward’s explanation.

“My friend Edward! You must listen to what I have to say! All right?”

“I can listen only if you say it!”

“I will. I shall take one division of this force and follow the route Pandaraka Vanniyan has taken, and find out where he is hiding.”

“That makes sense too. I will enter the Mullaitheevu palace with the others!”

The two spoke privately for a short while about their plans. After Vanderi Berg turned onto another route to discover Pandaraka Vanniyan’s whereabouts, Edward led his force along the road to Mullaitheevu.

Edward was utterly astonished when he entered Mullaitheevu. No one opposed him or his forces. No one stopped them. Indeed, there was not even anyone to be seen before his eyes.

Seeing no resistance at all, Edward proudly urged his horse forward and entered the forecourt of the Mullaitheevu palace with his soldiers. Even then no one intervened. Edward could hardly contain his delight. He shouted to his men,

“Do you see? There is
not even a crow or a sparrow
here to oppose us!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 465 → scan 466 -->

Before the sound of his boast had died away, a warrior’s cry rang out:

“The crow died and vanished
long ago!
Here is the sparrow,
to oppose you!”

Edward Madge and his soldiers turned in shock toward the direction from which the voice had come.

Kuruvichchi, the woman warrior who had raised that battle cry, came forward and stood before them with her sword held high.
