# “Daughter, Your Cleverness!”

Preparations for the birthday celebration began throughout the Kandy palace with great splendour. Word had spread that Queen Piyasili, who had been confined within the women's quarters, would for that one day be free to come to the celebration hall. The handsome young men who had once hovered around her waited eagerly for the hour of the festivities, mouths watering at the thought that after a long interval they would have the chance to see her in person again.

Countless coloured festoons began to beautify every corner and recess of the palace within moments. Rows upon rows of oil lamps glowed along the edges of the walls, recalling fireflies shining among tree branches at night. Long garlands hung in strands from the tall stone pillars of that lofty palace, pillars too broad to clasp with one's arms. Young men and women sang Sinhala songs and danced along the palace corridors! In other sections of the same corridors, young men and women of the same age sang Tamil music and danced!

Amid all that sound, with spear-bearing warriors marching in formation on either side, Vikrama Rajasingan walked toward the festival hall. As he came, his eyes widened in astonishment.

Pilimathala came forward, bowed before him, and stood there. Shaking his hand, the king said,

“It feels as though an age has passed since I last saw the minister!”

Walking closely beside the king toward the seats, Pilimathala tried to answer in a soft voice.

“My heart broke after my daughter Piyasili was confined within the palace, Your Majesty! I could not bear even the embarrassment of appearing before your face! That is why, hoping for a little peace of mind, I travelled to several towns in our kingdom on affairs of state.”

“Oh! Is that so? Is the city of Colombo also a part of our kingdom?”

Pilimathala faltered! Yet the instant he stroked his bald head, the cleverness needed to manage the king seemed to flow from his brain.

“No, Your Majesty! I learned that Lord North in Colombo was devising a plan to pounce upon our Kandy!”

“That is old news!”

“If you hear the new news, even you will be stunned! I myself was so stunned that I went to Colombo!”

“New news?”

“Yes! Completely new! Lord North has planned to bring Kaakkai Vanniyan of Mullaitheevu and Pandaraka Vanniyan together, give both of them every assistance they require, and make Pandarakan his friend. He has already begun arranging it.”

“Even if God himself took incarnation and came, he could not make Pandarakan submit! He is a brave man who has resolved that he would rather let the grave of this soil bear him than bear the slavery of waving a ceremonial fan before the white—

<!-- source boundary: scan 202 → scan 203 -->

Parangiyars! Yes. My heart will not even permit me to imagine my friend doing what you describe.”

“Your philosophy is to think that everything you dislike is milk! Even so, should I not avoid failing in a minister's duty to state what is to come? That is why I employed a piece of statecraft and hurried suddenly to Colombo. I carried out exactly what had to be done to prevent even a drop of friendship from arising between Governor North and Pandaraka Vanniyan!”

Though the king did not believe Pilimathala's words, the formal relationship between king and minister made him turn an ear toward him out of convention.

“Minister! What device did you use to prevent any agreement at all from arising between my friend Pandarakan and Lord North?”

“Now that is the way to ask! That question is as sweet as pure honey to me! Instead of that, it would not do to invite trouble with betel and areca simply because of your boundless affection for Pandaraka Vanniyan and the ties you have with him, would it?”

“Enough advice! What is the essence of the achievement you accomplished in Colombo? Tell me! Briefly!”

“I will! I will say it briefly! Do people not summarize even the Ramayana in two or three sentences? Ravana carried away Rama's wife. Hanuman found her. They fought and caught one another. They brought him back—

<!-- source boundary: scan 203 → scan 204 -->

Even today in Tamil Nadu villages they jokingly summarize it like that. I will summarize my matter in the same way.”

“Do you have to circle around this much merely to be brief?”

“Forgive me, Your Majesty! It is nothing else! What I told Lord North in Colombo was this: the Kandy government is prepared to accept British authority and, through that, good relations! Therefore, with us as your allies, suppress and subdue Pandaraka Vanniyan...”

Pilimathala had barely closed his mouth after saying it when Vikrama Rajasingan sprang up in fury.

“What? What? Submit to British authority? And that is called good relations? Minister! Whom did you consult before saying such a thing to that Governor North—”

“Your Majesty! Do not rush! Saying ‘fire’ does not burn the mouth! You must understand that for the time being I told this lie in Colombo only to prevent friendship from forming between Lord North and Pandaraka Vanniyan.”

“Pilimathala! I respect you. But your actions have deeply wounded my heart. Do not do things that wound it still further. Even in jest, to say that some relationship might arise between us and the white men is an insult! A disgrace! A task that brings stain upon this soil!”

“Your Majesty! Whatever you may say, however much you condemn me—even if today you do not accept the stratagem I used in Colombo, in the future you will think back on it and praise me!”

“There is only one life! It will leave only once! I am a man who believes that life must leave with honour.

<!-- source boundary: scan 204 → scan 205 -->

So I will never agree to live as a slave to the British! Rather than remain their slave and preserve this position, it is the honour of warriors to stand against them and perish this very day! That is what is in my heart!”

Just as a trace of harshness began to show in the conversation between the two men, women on both sides showered flowers. Piyasili entered the festival hall, placing her silk-soft feet upon a spread of crimson carpet. The adornment she had made for her birthday plunged even the king—who hated her utterly in his heart—into enchantment.

Both shoulders were fully exposed. To make her bosom appear even more prominent, she wore, in the name of clothing, an extremely thin gauze. With a waist that seemed to bend—or perhaps to break—and with nine kinds of gems glittering from foot to head, she approached as though making arrows of her eyes to strike down Vikrama Rajasingan. She stretched her hands out before him. He took them and then wished her a happy birthday.

Without releasing his hands, she lifted them to her eyes and pressed them there, then kissed them.

Music performances, dance performances—many kinds of artistic presentations filled that festival hall.

Meanwhile, her eyes were searching for someone. Like his daughter, Pilimathala's bulging eyes too moved restlessly around the hall.

As Piyasili had expected, Vijayathunga entered carrying a bouquet. Before him, many others had already been giving her gifts. A little distance away, the king stood watching her intently.

<!-- source boundary: scan 205 → scan 206 -->

On one side was his confusion over Pilimathala's journey to Colombo. On the other was the sight of Piyasili standing there like an enchantress powerful enough to overcome that confusion and enslave him.

The moment Piyasili saw Vijayathunga, her face blossomed! The blossoming face at once twisted into another expression. She looked at her father. He too was looking at his daughter.

Vijayathunga placed the bouquet in Piyasili's hands. But despite the promise he had made her, he was not prepared to embrace and kiss her there. Fear had seized him. She, however, had no intention of letting him go. As she took the bouquet, she gently stroked his hand with her fingers. The intoxication of pleasure rose within him. He forgot himself. He embraced Piyasili. He kissed her.

Piyasili screamed like a demon: “Ah! Ayyo! What outrage is this?”

The head of Vijayathunga, who had been kissing her, was severed and rolled to the floor. The sword that had cut off his head was in Vikrama Rajasingan's hand. Blood dripped from the blade. Piyasili looked at her father and smiled. Satisfied that she had finished off her enemy through the king's own hand, Pilimathala winked at Piyasili as though saying, “Daughter! Your cleverness!”
