62

# Isn't He a Man!

With Marthani and Kuruvichchi Naachchi seated nearby, Pandaraka Vanniyan finished reading the letter from the Kandy king.

“Bravo! The work has come! The time has come for my friend and me to fight together and drive the white horde out of the island of Lanka!” he said with delight, running a hand over the sword gleaming at his waist.

“What matters is not merely that you and the Kandy king are joining to oppose the English. What is most important is that Pilimathalavai himself, who until now has been an indirect loyalist of the English, has now devised a strategy insisting that the English must be defeated,” said Marthani.

As though something had suddenly occurred to him, Pandaraka Vanniyan looked at Marthani.

“Yes! What happened to that Muthusami whom Kolumbu proclaimed as the king who would support Kandy? Where is he?”

Marthani answered with a mocking smile.

“Our king has written that one army under Major General Macdowall and another under a Colonel Barbut are marching toward Kandy, has he not? I hear that Muthusami is coming with the force led by Colonel Barbut. Pilimathalavai himself told me!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 384 → scan 385 -->

“Not bad! Pilimathalavai seems to govern his kingdom through women! What enormous secrets he has told you!”

“I am, after all, a woman who deals with him without hiding any of my own secrets!”

At such an indecent sentence from Marthani's mouth, Pandaraka Vanniyan's face changed. Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar turned away, walked toward the window of the room and pretended to look at the sky.

“What is it, Kuruvichchi? Why have you moved away and gone to stand there?” Pandaraka Vanniyan asked, mastering himself and going near her.

Seeing this, Marthani winked and said, “My presence may be an inconvenience. If you wish, I can go somewhere else.”

Kuruvichchi's anger boiled over.

“Marthani! Because I spared your life and sent you back home, you will say this—and more! The relationship between Pandarakan and me is a relationship of the heart, not a bodily relationship. Fools like you, who have come forward to conduct politics with your bodies, cannot understand our pure affection and sacred love!”

“Naachchiyar must not be angry. I forgot that you are young ascetics who regard the pleasures of domestic life as bitter neem fruit. Forgive me.”

Pandarakan interrupted.

“Do not be in such a hurry to turn us into ascetics! We too have the desire to lead a married life. We are not people who believe in rejecting it. Until English domination—

<!-- source boundary: scan 385 → scan 386 -->

is completely cast aside, until the trumpet of freedom sounds that no white man can enter Mullaitheevu, until there is no foreign occupation anywhere in Lanka—we have sworn there will be no bodily union between us. Yet the two of us love one another with all our hearts!”

Marthani smiled. After all, she was now the bearer of the Kandy king's letter, and that gave her a reckless boldness.

“Ayyo! This truly is a wonder of the world! A great warrior! A man whose beauty could defeat Manmathan! A man with mountain-like shoulders! A majestic male who can captivate maidens! And Naachchiyar—a graceful beauty, a woman of elegance praised by the four quarters! All the beauty in the world cannot equal the splendour of her body! Such a pair—poor things! To reject what they need and waste bodily pleasure because of an unnecessary vow—surely that is a wonder of the world!”

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar was itching to strike Marthani and no one else. That was why she trembled with anger.

“Marthani! Are all women in this world like you and Piyaseeli? Some demon-women treat chastity as a shop commodity and culture as something hostile to be discarded; they train their minds to change to one man tomorrow and another bed by the hour, and then raise their heads even in affairs of government. Do not calculate all women by the measure of such women!”

“You too stand beside this Payumpuli—the ruler of Mullaitheevu, the leader of the Tamils here—and attend to political affairs. Do you not shine as a treasure-house of chastity?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 386 → scan 387 -->

Before she could go further, Pandarakan called, “Marthani!” in a harsh voice and said to her, “Understand that a pebble and a glittering diamond are not the same!”

Marthani immediately touched his feet and bowed.

“Forgive me. I will never forget that the two of you saved me and sent me back to Kandy even though I had come on the enemy's side. It was because I did not forget that gratitude that I changed Pilimathalavai's hatred toward you and persuaded him to accept my suggestion that the Mullaitheevu forces should come to support the Kandy army.”

Pandarakan laughed and asked:

“You persuaded him, yes. But whoever you intend to persuade to accept your idea, you first agree to give yourself to him. Why is that?”

“That...that...is an illness that caught me because I was Piyaseeli's friend! But one thing: this is a powerful illness. No matter how great the warrior or how committed the idealist, it can bring him down! With one exception. That is you!”

Kuruvichchi now slapped Marthani hard across the cheek.

“Whom are you looking at when you speak such words? I have shown this much patience only because you brought the Kandy king's letter!”

Then, with the same anger, Kuruvichchi turned to Pandarakan and shouted, “Look closely at that letter. Is it really written by the Kandy king?”

“Yes, Kuruvichchi! The same suspicion occurred to me. The entire letter is written in Vikrama Raja Singan's own hand. So it is genuine.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 387 → scan 388 -->

Marthani then tried to speak to them with great humility and calm.

“I have become used to dealing with everyone without understanding propriety. That is why all sorts of words came out. The mistake arose because I did not think anything I said was wrong. I came here on good business; I should not have spoken of bad things. What can I do? It is a habit soaked into my blood. Please, both of you, forgive me.”

She fell to the floor, bowed to both of them and rose.

Thinking, “Some people are simply born this way,” Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi looked at one another.

“All right, all right. Get up! Go back at once and tell Vikrama Raja Singan and Pilimathalavai that I said my forces are ready to come to their aid, and that I myself will soon lead them there,” said Pandarakan.

Rubbing the cheek that had been struck, Marthani took leave and prepared to depart. Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi sent her off with two women as attendants. Then Kuruvichchi began discussing with Pandarakan how they should go to Kandy's aid.

“You said that you yourself would lead our army. What will you do if the English forces block you on the road?”

“If it is war, we must be prepared to face everything.”

“I cannot shake the fear that some scheme of Pilimathalavai may be hidden in this.”

“It is a clear fact that the Kandy king himself has sent the letter.”

“What if Pilimathalavai has imprisoned the Kandy king—

<!-- source boundary: scan 388 → scan 389 -->

and forced him to write a letter like this?”

“If we remain suspicious like this and do not send our army, the English force may enter Kandy before we get an answer to our doubts.”

“Then?”

“Listen. Our army will set out toward Kandy under your command and camp on the road near the city. Meanwhile I will take another route into Kandy, meet Vikrama Raja Singan without Pilimathalavai knowing, and learn the truth. After that I will send word to the place where you and the army are camped.”

“That is a good arrangement!”

“Kuruvichchi! Stay here. I will go speak with some of our commanders and come back.”

After Pandarakan left, Kuruvichchi sank into deep thought. The fact that he had told her to lead the army was very sweet news to her. A long time passed and Pandarakan did not return.

A warrior entered, bowed to Kuruvichchi and stood before her.

“What is it?” she asked.

“A physician has come to see the leader. He says the leader asked him to come,” the warrior replied.

An old man with a white beard followed him in. He was the physician the warrior had mentioned.

“Is the leader not here, amma?” By then the warrior had gone out.

“Why did he ask you to come?”

“Why does one call a physician? For treatment!”

“What is wrong with him? Why has he called you?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 389 → scan 390 -->

“What can I say? A towering build! The form of a lion! Leader of the whole country! Yet they say Pandarakan has no manly feeling. I have come to treat that.”

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar stared at the old physician in astonishment.

“That is why, amma, he does not come near you! He called me to treat it. Did you not know this truth until now?”

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar stared fiercely at the physician. Seeing how shaken she was, the physician said, “It is all right. Do not be afraid. I will cure the leader and make him a vigorous man!”
