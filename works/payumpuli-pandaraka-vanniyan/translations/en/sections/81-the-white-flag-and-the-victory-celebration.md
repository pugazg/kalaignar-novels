# The White Flag—and the Victory Celebration!

Macdowall had entered Kandy like a fierce tiger, only to flee like a felled goat and take refuge in a fort on the road toward Trincomalee. Pandaraka Vanniyan learned that his warriors were tightening their siege so that Macdowall could not come out—and at the same time another English force under a commander named Madge was advancing by way of Trincomalee. Pandaraka Vanniyan had been hurrying to save General Macdowall’s life from his own warriors, but meeting Commander Madge’s force first now became the more urgent problem.

Not long after Commander Madge set out from Trincomalee with his force, Pandaraka Vanniyan met him on the road. Pandarakan’s warriors had learned not only the tactics of escaping English rifle fire but also how to bring down those rifle-bearing soldiers by hurling spears and lances. The assault of that Parangi commander, Madge, could not stand before them. So he suddenly halted the fighting. His men looked toward him, waiting to hear his decision.

“Soldiers! If we are to defeat the Kandy army and Pandarakan’s army, there is only one way!” Madge said.

“What way? What way?” the English soldiers shouted.

“We must turn back to Trincomalee now. Otherwise every one of us will go to heaven. Which of the two shall we choose?”

Commander Madge was a somewhat amusing man. He had no desire to lose his life foolishly in a battle. He knew Pandaraka Vanniyan’s strength very well. He knew that Pandaraka Vanniyan had the power to stand and fight even without the strength of an army behind him. So, to the astonishment of Pandarakan and his warriors, Commander Madge turned tail with his force and fled toward Trincomalee.

Exulting in the victories won against the English in Kandy and near Trincomalee, Pandaraka Vanniyan again hurried toward the fort to rescue General Macdowall.

The Mullaitheevu warriors and the Kandy soldiers surrounding the fort where Macdowall had hidden moved aside and opened a path when they saw Pandaraka Vanniyan. He smiled at them all and raised a hand, ordering them to remain calm. The Mullaitheevu warriors, thrilled by the majesty with which their leader dismounted from his horse, crowded around him and shouted, “It is not good for you to go inside! We do not want you to enter alone!”

“Fear? Me? While all of you are here? While I am in your—

<!-- source boundary: scan 410 → scan 411 -->

hearts?” Pandarakan asked, shaking his broad shoulders as he entered the fort completely alone.

He passed through the cave-like entrance and went into the inner part of the fort. English soldiers approached from all four directions with rifles extended, aiming at his chest. Pandarakan had entered without even a man to guard him, yet he showed not the slightest agitation. He stood among them without losing his smile. Then General Macdowall came rushing toward him, rage boiling over, aiming a pistol.

“You have come and trapped yourself inside the lion’s own cave!” the white general said, laughing arrogantly. Pandarakan did not even look at him with contempt. Instead, that warrior Vanniyan laughed aloud.

“Foolish general! I have come now to save you from my own warriors. In other words, I have come to grant you your life.”

General Macdowall did not understand Pandarakan’s magnanimity. “You? Grant me my life?” he growled.

“Have you forgotten how you were caught in Kandy city as though in a trap and stood there helpless? Have you forgotten that you ran here thinking only of saving your head and hid in this fort? At least do not forget that I have come to save you because our code says one should not kill a man who is fleeing or hiding in fear for his life!”

“Oh! Are you teaching me the laws of war? Unable to run or hide, you have come and fallen into my hands. Now I want your life!”

Macdowall stretched the pistol toward Pandaraka Vanniyan’s forehead and was about to pull the trigger. In the next instant Pandarakan seized Macdowall’s wrist, pressed it hard and twisted it.

“You may take my life. But do you know how many warriors are waiting outside this fort to take yours—to pierce your body with holes like a sieve?” he asked calmly.

Even with guns aimed straight at him, this heroic man spoke with such composure—without agitation, fear or anger—that General Macdowall, seeing him at close quarters, stood astonished.

“Pandarakan! I know that the moment your corpse falls here, the warriors outside will scatter and run.”

“Macdowall! That is exactly where you have miscalculated. When a leader’s head falls, warriors may scatter; ranks may collapse; there may be no one left to hold them together. Such things may happen elsewhere. But my warriors are not like that. Even if they see their leader himself as a corpse, they will not leave the field while the last breath remains in their bodies. Do you imagine that after bringing me here alone and killing me, you and your men can leave this fort alive? Only a brainless man could make such a calculation. Come, let us see—shoot me down! Let your men discover whether your body is not torn to pieces in the very next instant. Poor fellow! I mocked you when I saw you run in fear after marching here with an army. Then I heard that your very life was in danger and hurried here in distress. I am not prepared to give any more explanations. It does not become a warrior—

<!-- source boundary: scan 412 → scan 413 -->

to do so. Here I am, alone. Kill me, and if you have the courage, escape from my warriors!”

General Macdowall was stunned by Pandarakan’s roar. He lowered the pistol in his hand and shook Pandarakan’s hand.

Meanwhile, the warriors surrounding the fort began shouting in anxiety over what had happened to their leader inside. As a long time passed without Pandarakan returning, their suspicion grew. The unease spreading across their faces gradually became a feeling that spurred them on, until one or two reached the point of trying to enter the fort. The guards there resisted them and died. In the same way, some of those guards killed one or two of Pandarakan’s warriors.

Just as the situation reached the point where all the warriors outside were about to storm the fort, General Macdowall came out first, holding a white flag. Pandaraka Vanniyan followed him, coming like a leaping tiger.

“Warriors! As a warning to the English, and as a declaration that their domination has no place on our soil, I am sending General Macdowall back to Kolumbu. I hope that after understanding the magnanimity with which we have forgiven, protected and released a commander who fled in defeat and hid himself, English domination will at least now leave this island. General Macdowall is now permitted to depart under a white flag and reach Kolumbu—

<!-- source boundary: scan 413 → scan 414 -->

safely. I ask all of you to make way for him.”

“Good!” Macdowall said, looking at Pandarakan, and left with his men. The warriors gathered outside the fort welcomed this with applause and joyfully praised their leader.

When Lord North saw Macdowall arrive in Kolumbu under a white flag, he flew into a rage. After Macdowall explained what had happened in detail, a council meeting was arranged under North’s leadership.

At that meeting, Lord North’s methods and the invasion itself were criticised.

Pilimathalavai had deceived them by claiming that he would support the establishment of the British Empire. Trusting him in the first place had therefore been a mistake. A still greater mistake had been giving Muthusami refuge in Kolumbu, believing that he possessed great strength, and finally suffering a major defeat because of him. The criticism continued in this fashion. At last the council ended the discussion by concluding that the most important reason for the defeat was that Pandaraka Vanniyan had come to the aid of the Kandy king and performed many feats of valour in battle.

At the very same time that the English imperialists in the capital, Kolumbu, were discussing the Kandy invasion, Vikrama Raja Singan was personally absorbed in arranging a victory celebration in Kandy. Pilimathalavai began thinking about how he could put his venomous mind to work during that celebration.
