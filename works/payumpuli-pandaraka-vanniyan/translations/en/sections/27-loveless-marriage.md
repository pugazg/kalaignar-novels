# Loveless Marriage!

Piyasil was a cluster of grapes! A young vine borne by Pilimathala! They say intoxication and delirium come only after drinking wine—but merely seeing this woman was enough for young men to lose their senses and fall. Old men would stand transfixed, saliva running from both corners of their mouths. Her waist was so slender one might think it would snap and fall if a hand touched it; her breasts were so full that one wondered whether that waist could bear such weight. Her large, beetle-dark eyes cast nets around young hearts and swept them in. A thousand men would become slaves to a single movement. Even the way she dressed could challenge a renunciate who had given up everything, defeat him in half a moment, and sound the drum of victory.

Pilimathala had such an enchantingly beautiful daughter. Unaware of the full extent of her father's dream of using that very daughter to make the crown of Kandy his own, Piyasili took advantage of the freedom he had given her to mix with Kannusami and kept circling around him. Many men stood in line around her, but she would brush against them without quite touching, touch them without quite yielding, and leave them swinging at the summit of a world of pleasure—while continuing to circle Kannusami as though she were already queen of his bed.

Kannusami too was a vigorous young man, the pampered son of the palace<!-- verified split-word boundary: scan 140 → scan 141 -->, raised with no one to question him. If heavenly nectar forced its way into his mouth, was he a block of wood who would refuse to taste it? At first he had hesitated: she is a woman who bewilders men—can there really be a relationship with her? But she would not let go. How she had learned so many arts at such a young age, he did not know; she taught him all of them.

Even while the king was alive, the minister's house was Kannusami's recreation hall, pleasure-house and altar of the god of love. In the women's quarters of the minister's mansion—in the hall, the verandah, the garden—by day, by night, at every hour: grape wine in one hand and Piyasili in the other. Their mouths would become the cups that held that wine. The more each drank from the other, the more honey flowed. Pilimathala would often pass by those revels as though he had seen nothing. When what he wanted was happening, why interfere?

Kannusami drank deeply from the nectar-cup she offered, but regarded her only as an object of pleasure. He longed to bite and devour that beauty no sculptor had carved. He knew that, just as many people enjoyed touching a smooth stone statue, some young men enjoyed touching Piyasili. The privilege he possessed was simply that he could enjoy touching her for longer. And that honeyed intimacy of Piyasili's—only after the time he was allowed to taste it did others receive their share. Yet she did not allow anyone to taste it fully, nor did she drive them away. She took pride in showing Kannusami how many men performed penance for the sake of her striking beauty. He regarded Piyasili merely as an ornamental figure upon a temple tower.

But that figure was planning to enter the temple's sanctum and seat herself as its principal idol, even as she lay kissing him.

“My darling! Look at me! All my beauty exists only to be offered to you! So many Parangi lords have come grinning and telling me that a single smile of mine is worth the whole world; with these silken fingers I touched and caressed them and sent them back by the very road they came. And my cousin who is meant for me—what a brute he is! You know from experience, darling, how much I like roughness. Even him, I only let kiss my waist so he would go away; I moved aside before becoming a feast for his burning desire. Why? For what? Think, my life! How many men yearn to seize and pull at this waist! Even if one or two of them sometimes succeed, is it not because I want you to taste this waist that I make your lap my bed? I swoon in your grip! Father knows of our relationship. Has he ever asked, ‘What strange thing is this?’ He has not. He will not! He and all our relatives have already decided we are a perfect pair. What remains now? Only marriage! Everything that is supposed to happen after marriage has already happened as rehearsal! Say yes—let us live as husband and wife!”

Kannusami now remembered how, saying all this, she had collapsed naked upon his body a few days earlier.

He concluded that Minister Pilimathala must have been speaking to his friend Pandarakan about Piyasili's marriage.

Pandaraka Vanniyan sensed that something had changed in Kannusami's state of mind. He turned to the minister and said, “Kannusami and I need to speak alone for a while.”

“By all means!” Pilimathala replied, signalling to everyone there and leading them away.

“What? Have you fallen into the net of love?” Pandarakan teased with a laugh.

“This love is not a union of hearts like the love between Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi! To tell the truth, this is not love at all. It is food for bodily hunger!”

As he said it, Kannusami hung his head before his friend.

“When even Kuruvichchi's love has been obstructed, love conducted for the sake of statecraft can only be a drama!”

“Yes, my friend! Pilimathala's daughter Piyasili—she is a goddess of beauty! Somehow she has enslaved me. And am I the only one? How many young men of royal families lie at her feet performing penance!”

“Oh! So the boon was granted only to you!”

“No. She picks out a few. Among them, I have the greater claim and the greater share of her time.”

“Do you know what your youthful excess has produced? A plan has now been made that you must marry Piyasili!”

“That alone is impossible. I have told her so plainly to her face!”

“Did she accept it?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 143 → scan 144 -->

“No! She says that if she cannot have me, she will not live. She says she will drink poison and die!”

“Why should you refuse to marry her?”

“She is a beautiful bird—but she eats worms!”

“For now, that very bird must be used as a dove of peace.”

“What are you saying? I do not understand any of this!”

“Governor North has ordered Pilimathala to arrest you, imprison you, and crown Muthusami. To get through this dangerous situation, Pilimathala has decided to make you his son-in-law.”

“If I become his son-in-law, is there no need to arrest me?”

“The Governor in Colombo may think that Kannusami and Kannusami's father-in-law Pilimathala could become closer friends of the British than Muthusami, might he not?”

“Marry Piyasili? Is she not a sweetmeat that every stray cat has sniffed?”

“And are you not the chief cat among those cats? Marry her first! Escape the danger! Then take the crown! After that, attend to the business of driving the other cats away!”

“Are you joking?”

“I am not. We must defeat strategy with strategy and preserve the rule of Kandy. After that there will be many more intrigues to face. I will certainly stand with you. For now, agree to this marriage!”

“What happens with Piyasili—is that something one can call marriage?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 144 → scan 145 -->

“You are the man who smeared dung on himself calling it sandalwood. So you are the one who must exclaim, ‘Ah! What a fragrance! What a fragrance!’ and enjoy it!”

“Is there no other way?”

“There is another way: hand the kingdom of Kandy over to Muthusami and open the door to the British!”

“No! No! If that is what it takes to keep British domination from setting foot here, forget Piyasali—I am ready to marry a woman more degraded than she is!”

“You knew who Piyasali was when you touched her, did you not? Agree to the marriage in the hope that she may reform from now on!”

Kannusami embraced Pandaraka Vanniyan in anguish and nodded his assent to that loveless marriage.
