# Midnight Drama!

In the light of that torch appeared a closely shaven bald head, owl-like eyes, a charming face that made one wonder whether Hanuman, who had once gone in search of Sita in the age of the Ramayana, had remained there after receiving the boon of immortality, and an unmanly gait. That figure leapt and growled and created an uproar in the room. Yes—it was Pilimathala!

Whenever someone introduced Pilimathala as the father who had begotten an alluring beauty like Piyasili, one could only suppose that the mother who had conceived Piyasili must have been extraordinarily beautiful.

In the torchlight it was unbearable to look into Pilimathala's eyes, which seemed to pour out in a waterfall the poison forever welling up inside his skull. Before he could enter the room, Pandaraka Vanniyan had already rolled Piyasili off the bed and onto the floor. He sprang to his feet in revulsion, as though his hand had touched a hairy caterpillar.

“Come, son-in-law! Look at this outrage!” Pilimathala shouted, grabbing the arm of Kannusami, who was following behind him, and dragging him forward. The son-in-law entered with his father-in-law, and what met his

eyes was the half-naked figure of the tender-bodied Piyasili rolling on the floor.

“Kannusami!” Pandaraka Vanniyan cried loudly, though his voice trembled with emotion.

Minister Pilimathala then displayed his acting talent to perfection.

“Do not insult my son-in-law by calling him Kannusami! Do not forget that my son-in-law is the king of Kandy! When the whole world knows that, with the blessings of the religious preceptors, Kannusami was given the name ‘Sri Vikrama Rajasingan’ and crowned, why do you alone show such arrogance? Call him Vikrama Rajasingan and fall at his feet!”

“Minister! Though the two of us may both be kings, to me he is still Kannusami, and to him I am still Vairamuthu! Such is the depth of our friendship!”

When Pandaraka Vanniyan said this, Pilimathala stroked his bald head. His bulging eyes swelled further and he began to laugh horribly, then poured out harsh words in a rapid stream.

“Friendship, he says! Friendship! Pah! Have you no shame? You abduct your friend's wife at midnight and call this betrayal friendship?”

“Minister, do not speak in haste! I was exhausted and asleep—she came here herself—she committed this outrage—”

“You sinner! You call false the very sight we have seen with our own eyes! She is my daughter. Even so, as her father I am not angry merely because you have ruined her. But you have ruined the wife of the friend who loves you as his own life and destroyed the honour of her chastity!”

Pilimathala lunged toward Pandarakan, then whirled fiercely toward his daughter Piyasili.

“You foolish girl! As your father, I could even forgive you for dallying with him. But simply because this traitor called you, how could you dare betray your husband? Vikrama Rajasingan is now the Maharaja of Kandy! This Pandaraka Vanniyan is merely like a palayakkarar—like a Kattabomman in Tamil Nadu, an enemy of the white man on the island of Sri Lanka! You, the queen of this country, came to dally with such an ordinary man? Dog! Demon! Should you not have kicked him with your foot and said, ‘Leave me alone! I am not merely a queen who rules a country—I am your friend's wife!’?”

Half her body covered by clothing and the remaining marble-like portions displayed without the slightest shame, Piyasili stood twisting and swaying.

“Father! Forgive me! Do not hurl barbs at me without knowing what happened! I did not come here of my own accord! Would I? Do you not know that I have shaped my life as a chaste wife who never crosses the threshold? My beloved Vikrama Rajasingan embraced me and

left, saying that royal affairs were rather heavy today and that he would return a little late.

“Unable to bear being separated from him even for a few naazhigai, I sought the help of a wine cup to ease my distress. I wandered in a daze along the palace corridor, letting my eyes roam in expectation of my king's return. Then two arms came around me from behind! Thinking it was the Maharaja, I yielded to the embrace. Only now do I realize that this treacherous betrayer, who knows nothing of the grammar of friendship, came in the darkness pretending to be my husband and dragged me here!”

After lamenting to her father, Piyasili went before Pandaraka Vanniyan.

“Hey, lust-maddened man! Tell the truth! Did you violate me while I was intoxicated? If even a mustard-seed-sized stain has fallen upon my exalted chastity, I will not live! Tell the truth!” she began to wail.

The drama deliberately staged by father and daughter was proceeding successfully, just as they had expected.

Pilimathala had used his own daughter to put into practice his plan: if he could create enmity between Kannusami and Pandaraka Vanniyan and separate them, could he not later sit as a slave-king of Kandy with British support? He stood rolling his eyes like drums sounding a victory proclamation.

“What is this, Maharaja? You are stunned into a pillar! The woman standing here deceived and robbed of her chastity is not merely my daughter Piyasili—she is Maharani Piyasili, wife of the king of Kandy! Does your patience still continue? Are you thinking, ‘He is my friend; let it go. Let us forget! Let us forgive!’? This is not land, Maharaja, to divide with borders and give each man a portion to rule! A woman! A woman—and a woman of royal lineage! Alas! Seeing you still standing so calmly makes me want to smash my bald head against this pillar and die!”


Wailing, Pilimathala moved to strike his head against a pillar. As though they had rehearsed even this beforehand, Piyasili sprang up at once and cried, “Father! No!” as she stopped him.

*Oh, father! How many poisonous dramas have gone into making the very nerves of Pilimathala's head! How skilfully father and daughter are acting! What an obscene scheme this man has devised—to turn me against Kannusami, place the kingdom of Kandy beneath foreign feet, and drink the water of office washed from those feet! What can one call the shamelessness of using his own daughter as the leading lady in this filthy drama?*

These thoughts pierced Pandaraka Vanniyan's heart like arrows.

Pilimathala, raging like a man possessed, shook Kannusami by the shoulders and thundered an oath.

“Will you take revenge upon this sinner who dared betray his friend and ravish his friend's wife? Or, as the father of the daughter who has suffered this disgrace, shall I cut off his head myself and wipe away the stain?”

Even as he asked, Pilimathala drew his sword and raised it toward Pandaraka Vanniyan.

Kannusami immediately stopped the sword and took it into his own hand.

“No! I shall teach the traitor a lesson myself!” he roared like a lion.
