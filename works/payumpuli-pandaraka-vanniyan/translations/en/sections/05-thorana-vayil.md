# Thorana Vayil

Wherever courage, wisdom and sacrifice radiate their light, I have the desire to honour them and fashion an epic of praise!

And especially when that courage, wisdom and sacrifice are possessions of the Tamil people, wherever they may be found, do I not have a deep concern that they must not be left unnoticed and lost to the cruelty of the flood of time?

Was it not as a result of that concern that I was able to give the Tamil people the great work _Romapuri Pandiyan_, based on the ancient history of the Tamils; then the great work _Thenpandi Singam_, about a later champion of Tamil honour; and after that, drawing upon stories from the _Purananuru_, the historical fiction _Ponnar-Sankar_, about those tigers of the battlefield!

As the next effort born of that same desire, I now set out to portray the heroic and sacrificial historical tale of the leaping tiger, Pandaraka Vanniyan.

Six years ago (in 1982), the Vavuniya District Development Committee in Sri Lanka had arranged a ceremony at a place called Karsilai Madu in Vavuniya to raise a statue to Pandaraka Vanniyan.

<!-- verified source continuation: scan 13 → scan 14 -->

The following is the message of greetings I sent for the commemorative volume published in connection with that ceremony:

“Just as Veerapandiya Kattabomman fought the white men in Tamil Nadu and sacrificed his life, so too, in Sri Lanka's Vanni country, the martyr and great warrior Pandara Vanniyan fought until his last breath under the slogan, ‘If there is freedom; if not, there is no life!’ and gave his life. I am immensely happy to learn that a committee including my dear friend, Member of Parliament T. Siva Sithambaram, has decided to raise a statue to him in Vavuniya and, in connection with it, celebrate a Muthamizh festival. Even if the present fails to give sacrifice its due honour, the future will certainly do so; this statue-unveiling ceremony is worthy of praise as a sign of that truth.”

From that day onward, I developed a desire to gather information about that great warrior from many people! Yet such information was not easy to obtain!

Professor Kalanidhi Vithiananthan, Vice-Chancellor of Yalppanam University, who sent greetings for the statue-unveiling ceremony, wrote:

“Pandara Vanniyan was the great warrior who fought foreigners until his last breath to protect the honour of Adangapattru, the Vanni country. He ruled with patriotism, keeping the welfare of the people in mind. British military commander Ondiribark erected a memorial stone to Pandara Vanniyan at Karsilai Madu in order to perpetuate his own fame. To proclaim Pandara Vanniyan's valour, the Vavuniya District Development Council is raising a statue to him in Vavuniya. It may be hoped that this statue will help confidence, patriotism and the voice of rights to rise not only among the people of the Vanni region,

<!-- verified source continuation: scan 14 → scan 15 -->

but among all Eelam Tamils whose confidence has been shaken and who have become dispirited. May this statue pave the way for people to desire good leaders, recognise them, and for good leaders to arise!”

He says that the British commander Ondiribark erected a memorial stone to Pandara Vanniyan at Karsilaimadu in order to perpetuate his own fame. What stone is that?

The memorial stone is the one shown here in the picture!

It is written in English on that stone that on 31 October 1803 Ondiribark defeated Pandara Vanniyan.

Was that freedom fighter truly defeated? Did his battle cry end with that? Or did that lion rise roaring once again? The serialized portrayal _Payum Puli Pandaraka Vanniyan_ is going to answer all these questions! A clear answer! An answer surging with valour!

When a statue was raised to that king who guarded honour, Mr. Amirthalingam, then Leader of the Opposition in the Sri Lankan Parliament, said in his message of greetings:

“Though the Tamil kings and Sinhala kings who ruled various parts of Sri Lanka lost their sovereignty through the British invasion,

<!-- verified source continuation: scan 15 → scan 16 -->

the last king of Vanni, the great warrior Pandara Vanniyan, fought with all his strength until the end to preserve the sovereignty of the people under his rule. An important cause of his fall was not only British occupation but also the base act of betrayal by the man called Kakkai, who proved like a disease born alongside one only to kill.”

That pure man, who had to encounter traitors, was not without good friends. Vikrama Raja Singan, also called Kannusamy, who ruled Kandy until A.D. 1815, was captured by the British, confined for sixteen years in Vellore prison in Tamil Nadu, and died in that very prison; he was Pandaraka Vanniyan's dearest friend.

There was also a maiden named Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar who, like a cool shade in which he could rest, softened a little the volcanic heart of that great Tamil sage, wounded by betrayal! That palace of love, built upon firmness of mind, was a palace of resolve! A palace of sacrifice!

Though the realms of Tamil kings and Sinhala kings in Sri Lanka fell one after another with the entry of the Portuguese, the Hollanders called Dutch, and the British, only the realm of Pandaraka Vanniyan and the realm of the king of Kandy remained capable of resisting the British, who had finally established their domination.

“Kolumbu, Kottai and Mattakkalappu,
and the undiminished kingdom of Yalppanam—
having seized them in a firm grip,
the people of the English land ruled them.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 16 → scan 17 -->

This folk song tells where British domination had spread in Sri Lanka.

It was amid that domination that Pandaraka Vanniyan governed the region bounded by the Yalppana Paravai Sea to the north, the Aruvi River to the south, Mannar District to the west and Thirukonamalai to the east.

He had to withstand both Dutch opposition and British opposition. Historical notes say that his sisters assisted him in governing and protecting the land—Nallanaachchi on the Thirukonamalai frontier, and Oomaichchi Naachchi in the Panangamam region on the Mannar frontier. When we see this, how fitting it seems that the Tamils living in that land worshipped Kannagi as their deity then and continue to worship her today!

<!-- source boundary: scan 17 → scan 18 -->

Cheran Senguttuvan, in Vanchi Muthur in Tamilakam, raised a statue to Kannagi, the goddess of chastity, bringing stone from the Himalayas after making Kanaka Visayar, whom he had defeated in battle, carry it upon their heads. The _Silappatikaram_ says that at that time Kayavaku, king of Eelam, introduced that worship of the chaste woman in Eelam. A story still current in that region says that Kannagi, whose anger was not cooled even after she burned Madurai, came and settled at a tenth place called “Paththappalai,” which, through change of usage, became today's “Vattrappalai.”

The warrior who bore the title Pandara Vanniyan, whose sisters and beloved Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar came in that Kannagi tradition, assumed responsibility for rule at a very young age and, by the age of thirty-four, established his fame at Kalachchaa!

His given name was Kulasekaram Vairamuthu; historical notes refer to him as Pandara Vanniyan, while the title of this serial story calls him Pandaraka Vanniyan.

The swordsman Vairamuthu was a descendant in the line of kings bearing the clan name Kulasekaram!

“Whatever form imperialism takes, when that imperialism attempts to seize the sovereignty of a people, the life of Pandara Vanniyan shows us clearly that those people will rise with vigour and fight for their rights. On this occasion I am proud to note that the people living as his heirs in the land where Pandara Vanniyan lived have expressed their political consciousness in Sri Lanka's political history as a people thirsting for freedom. The honour being given today belongs not only to the great warrior Pandara Vanniyan. What is being <!-- verified source join: scan 18 → scan 19 -->honoured today is his ideal and his yearning for freedom.”

Thus spoke M. Sivasithambaram, leader of the Tamil United Liberation Front, at the unveiling of Pandara Vanniyan's statue.

Even Sri Lankan Prime Minister Premadesa, in a message of greetings issued for that statue-unveiling ceremony, said:

“To honour those who deserve honour is a principle of moral conduct,”

and,

“I state firmly that the ceremony to raise a statue in honour of the great warrior Pandara Vanniyan is entirely the right thing to do.”

That he said so proclaims the fame of that warrior.

As for the figure of Kulasekara Vairamuthu, who took the battle sword as the emblem on his flag, leapt like a tiger and saw many battlefields:

Lofty bearing  
Broad chest  
Narrow waist  
Broad forehead  
Powerful shoulders  
Sharp gaze

From the next issue onward, we shall follow the footsteps of that fearless hero's life.

I offer my thanks to my friend S. Yoganathan, who gave me many notes for this story, held a government post equivalent to Collector in the Mullaitheevu and Vavuniya regions, and was also a writer; and also to Tamilkudimagan, Principal of Madurai Yadava College, and Kanthalakam Satchithanandam. With those thanks, I place before you the historical serial story of that Tamil lion.
