# The Path She Walked

With eyes like the tips of green grass beaded with dew, the people of that little village crowded the burial ground. They were engaged, with deep sorrow, in the work of burying Sangili Thalapathi, whose body had been brought from Kaakkai Vanniyan's palace. The warriors of Mullaitheevu stood in formation to accord the commander state honours. Having heard of the commander's death, men, women and children from nearby villages kept arriving in line after line. Pandaraka Vanniyan stood at one side watching the burial preparations, his face steeped in grief. A little distance from him stood Kuruvichchi Naachchi, looking only at the beloved face of the grandfather who, in a short while, would be lowered into the grave and covered by a blanket of earth. Her eyes seemed to shed drops of blood instead of tears.

Feeling a hand gently touch her shoulder, she turned. It was her teacher, Thanga Naachchi! With slightly trembling fingers, Thanga Naachchi wiped Kuruvichchi's tears. As though lifting the entire burden of grief pressing upon her head and placing it on Thanga Naachchiyar, Kuruvichchi embraced that

woman. Her beloved teacher stroked Kuruvichchi's head and pressed her cheek against Kuruvichchi's, consoling her. It gave Kuruvichchi a little comfort.

Sundaralingam, seeing with his own eyes how the people of Mullaitheevu in Sri Lanka stood overcome with feeling and honoured people of warrior stock who fought and died with a sense of honour for them, stood there entranced.

Held in the embrace of her teacher Thanga Naachchi, Kuruvichchi was flooded by vivid memories of her grandfather.

Little Kuruvichchi's mother dresses her beautifully, lifts her up and sends her off to school. Then Kuruvichchi's father, seated on the veranda, calls his daughter, kisses her cheek, blesses her and sends her on her way. Taking leave of her parents, little Kuruvichchi goes to school, finishes her lessons, plays and sings with her young friends, and returns home as usual when evening begins to fade. The moment she reaches the end of the street where her house stands, she feels something dreadful! The entire street is silent! One or two houses have burned down and still smoulder with smoke! Seeing this, Kuruvichchi thinks of her parents and, in anguish, runs toward the house crying, “Appa! Amma!” Her house has not burned to ashes, but the entrance facing the street lies completely shattered! She screams and runs inside. What does little Kuruvichchi see there? Her mother lies dishevelled in a flood of blood! Her father lies hacked apart, head separate from body! The light goes out of her eyes. Pitch darkness engulfs her!

<!-- source boundary: scan 111 → scan 112 -->

She cannot remain standing! Her head spins! She does not know what happened after that. After lying unconscious for two or three days, she becomes aware that she is in Sangili Thatha's lap.

Thatha consoles her. Foreigners, he tells her, are making a great effort to root their domination in Mullaitheevu. Whoever opposes them, they destroy. The soldiers sent by those hungry for domination, armed with the strength of their modern weapons, do not hesitate to commit any cruelty. When Thatha tells her that the deaths of her mother and father were part of that continuing violence, she can never forget how his eyes spat sparks.

That memory made the lips of her heart tremble and set her speaking to herself!

Mother—father—I was their only child! They sent me off in the morning! By evening they had become corpses! At that time I had no one but my grandfather! He was one of the commanders of Mullaitheevu, and circumstances did not allow him to stay beside me and look after me. He took me to Thanga Naachchiyammai and said, “Mother! You are now Kuruvichchi's mother! Father! God! Everything! Trusting you, I leave my granddaughter in your care!” and entrusted me to her. Even so, Thatha would come often to see me! As I grew older, he wanted me to excel in martial training too—sword training, spear training and the like. I was eager as well. Thatha himself gave me that training. One day news came that my grandfather had lost a leg on a battlefield! A turn came in life when I had to become the support of the grandfather who had been my support!

It was during that period that Pandarakan came to Thanga Naachchiyar's gurukulam to gain

<!-- source boundary: scan 112 → scan 113 -->

mastery in Tamil literature. Kaakkai Vanniyan came with him too! Though the surging love episodes in Sangam literature prompted Pandaraka Vairamuthu and me to glance at one another from the corners of our eyes, it was deeds of valour that brought our two hearts together! We thought of our union not as a love for sharing bodily pleasure, but as our joining ourselves together to perform some duty for our people and our land! When I told my grandfather that such a bond had arisen, at first he hesitated. “My child! Your mother and father became corpses before my eyes. At least you, the banner of this clan, should live long and live in peace—that is what I want! The man you love, that Vairamuthu Pandaraka Vanniyan, is a hero who roams about forever crying, ‘War! War!’ with shoulders itching for battle! How can you have a peaceful life with him, child? No—this is a dangerous desire!” he warned me.

“Is womanhood meant only to cling to manhood and run a household? Does it have no responsibility to fulfil the duties that men take upon themselves? That is why, Thatha, when I say I love him, I mean that I will join him to drive out the foreigners and overthrow their domination! There will be no marriage until that goal is achieved—he has promised to marry me after that!” When I told Thatha this, how happily he praised me!

“May this warrior spirit never fade, my child,” he blessed me. Yet I have caused that grandfather's heart to hurt. He must have embraced death, his heart burning, because he felt that my going to Kaakkai Vanniyan's palace to rescue him was a grave wrong. Aiyo! I set out to rescue Thatha myself only because I did not want my family problem<!-- verified split-word boundary: scan 113 → scan 114 --> to become a great war. When the issue could be resolved through negotiation without even the small fight I had expected, was accepting that not statecraft? I thought there was nothing wrong in sometimes using prudence even within our firm position of victory or heroic death! I never imagined that thought would strike at Thatha's very life! If I had not involved myself in trying to rescue him, he might at least have remained alive! Was I the one who destroyed even that? This is pain I cannot bear! I set out to rescue him because I feared the tortures he might be made to suffer in prison, did I not? Somehow, condemning my warrior spirit, my grandfather has brought his life to an end! Even now, as he lies here a corpse, that cast of valour remains upon his face! In a few more moments Mother Earth will gather this majestic form into her embrace!

Waves of memory rose, rolled and broke within Kuruvichchi's heart. A cry of “Long live Sangili Thalapathi!” struck the sky as his body was placed in the broad, deep grave. The warriors of Mullaitheevu saluted him. Pandaraka Vanniyan came near, touched Sangili Thalapathi's feet and brought his hands to his eyes, then gathered flowers and scattered them over his body. Thanga Naachchiyar supported Kuruvichchi as she came forward; Kuruvichchi too scattered flowers over her grandfather's body, kissed his feet with her eyes and rose.

When the burial was over, the villagers dispersed in silence. Sundaralingam looked sadly at Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi, who stood motionless.

“I am sorry that, in this atmosphere of grief, I am under the necessity of returning to my country.”

“That is all right, Sundaralingam! Tell King Veerapandiya Kattabomman and Vellaiyathevan that I will immediately establish contact with Kandy and fulfil their intention!”

“Naachchiyar! Once again, I offer my deepest condolences on the death of your grandfather, and take my leave!”

“Let enough of our warriors accompany Sundaralingam to the seashore and see him safely on his way!”

After saluting the two of them and Thanga Naachchiyar, Sundaralingam mounted his horse and departed. Warriors of Mullaitheevu followed him for protection.

Thanga Naachchiyar walked slowly over to Pandaraka Vanniyan and said,

“Vairamuthu! Kuruvichchi Naachchi, whom I raised and shaped like my own daughter, has suffered a great loss. Sangili Thalapathi was everything to this girl who lost her mother and father. From now on, you are everything to her. What more can I say from this place filled with sorrow! I am leaving!” She moved away. Even after some time, Pandaraka Vanniyan remained staring at Sangiliyar's grave. At last he prepared to mount his horse and leave. Seeing him about to depart, Kuruvichchi Naachchi too went toward her horse to mount it. Pandarakan's voice rang out, filled with harshness and anger.

“No! You are not to come with me!”

Kuruvichchi Naachchi stood stunned. Pandarakan spurred his horse fast and disappeared from there.
