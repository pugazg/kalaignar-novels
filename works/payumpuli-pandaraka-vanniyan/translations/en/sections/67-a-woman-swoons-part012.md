You might think that vine-like woman had taken two pointed heaps of salt piled up in a salt pan and fastened them to her chest—her waist bends like the middle of a snake's body! But she will not hiss like you! She is a young bird in the bloom of youth, drinking and delighting in erotic pleasure no matter who gives it to her! Do you know for what? You must be irritated, thinking, ‘What is this fellow doing, keeping me standing before him while praising some other woman from the crown of her head to the soles of her feet?’ But that woman worthy of all my praise and adoration—if I compare her with your beauty, you are the moon that stayed far away! She was the star that lay upon my thigh! Your beauty is a radiant garden in the sky! She was a lotus in a pond! When even my enjoyment of her delighted this bedchamber so much, do you know how it will leap and dance with joy if it gets to witness the love-play this lion lavishes upon this bounding deer?”

Nalla Naachchiyar, who had concluded that Kakkai Vanniyan seemed to have learned a special language solely for the bedchamber, glared at him and snapped, “Are you not ashamed to speak like this?”

“If you enter this room, you must leave shame out in the outer corridor—you must drive modesty away. Only after everything is over and you leave the room should that shame and modesty return to a woman, like—

—a stone thrown up into the sky coming back down! A man! A woman! In a place where no one else can show their face, why should shame show its face? Look here! Just as the sky slowly whitens before the morning rays appear—first a state like mist, then one like smoke losing its strength in the wind, after that brightness, then broad daylight, and then sunlight—you yourself are now going to experience each of those stages one by one. Just watch!” As he spoke, Kakkai Vanniyan gently tugged with his rough hand at the end of the sari that lay over Nalla Naachchiyar's breast.

With her hands bound, she did not want to stand naked before him—as a symbol of humiliation. There are some women in this world who ache with the thought, ‘When will a man strip away my clothes?’ Nalla Naachchiyar was not of that kind, and so she suddenly came to a decision. Where she poured away the fury in her heart, no one could know! Nor could anyone know how she turned her two burning eyes into flowers! Her lips, which had looked like pieces of fire from a furnace, somehow curled like curtains on a theatre stage to reveal the smile of her pearly teeth!

Seeing a ball of fire become a cluster of flowers floating on water, Kakkai Vanniyan silently praised and congratulated himself: Victory! Victory!

Now she spoke. There was no harshness in her voice. Instead, there was sweetness.

“Yes! If you truly—

—wanted to possess me, why did you have to lie that you were a merchant? You could have come directly to my brother, asked for my hand, married me, and then you and my brother would have become brothers-in-law. After that, not merely the British—even if this whole world tried, could anyone defeat the two of you? When there was a proper path for building a relationship, why did you uselessly nurture enmity instead?”

“Nallanaachchi! What are you saying?”

“How else can I tell you that my heart too has been yearning for you?”

“Is it really you speaking? I saw in you the fury of a hen driving away the hawk that comes to kill her chick! That is, you were a hen unwilling to lose the chick called chastity—and so you thundered like a summer storm! But now your voice sounds like a cuckoo that has come and perched inside a bangle-box and begun to sing! What is this change?”

“There is no change! I utterly disliked the path you followed to obtain me. That is why I hated you! I regarded you as an enemy!”

“And now?”

“There is no other way! I must live with the man to whom my heart has gone!”

“Truly?”

“Yes, my lord! It would be no great thing for me to reject you and marry another man. But would I not then become a woman who had lost the chastity I value more than my life?”

“So?”

“Rather than sacrifice that chastity at your feet against my will, would it not be better, in order to preserve it forever—

—for you to become my husband and me your wife, and for us to live that way all our lives?”

“Then your brother Pandarakan's enmity will fall upon us.”

“My loving brother will nurse his anger for ten days! Then he himself will forgive us! Even if he does not, it does not matter—I want no kingdom of my own! A place in the empire of your heart is enough for me!”

“But I am a man who has even rented out this heart to so many women!”

“I will not be one among them! I will not empty this heart and leave as they did! Dance with as many Urvashis and Rambhas as you wish! Sing with them! I will be to you as Radha was to Kannan, who danced with a thousand gopis.”

“Oh, my darling! I never knew that so many pearls of desire lay scattered in the depths of your heart!” Kakkai Vanniyan said as he moved to embrace her. She laughed and gave a playful little protest.

“Hmm! Your hands may embrace me, but my hands must remain tied so that I cannot even touch you?”

“Ah, I forgot that! Here—I shall remove not only the bonds on your hands, but everything hiding the form of my darling's body, and then I shall lift you and fall with you upon that golden, jewel-like bed waiting there!”

The intoxication of the liquor he had drunk, joined by the intoxication of lust, rose together to Kakkai Vanniyan's head. He untied the bonds that held Nalla Naachchi's hands. The instant he loosened them—

<!-- source boundary: scan 334 → scan 335 -->

—the woman who had been showing the grace of a deer suddenly became a tiger. She seized a burning torch from the room and, without missing her mark in the slightest, struck Kakkai Vanniyan in the face with it. He could not withstand the attack he had never expected. He fell against the bedchamber door; the force of his fall flung it open. The warriors outside sprang into the room in shock and surrounded Nalla Naachchiyar, who stood like a tongue of flame with the torch in her hand.

She used that torch alone to fend off every dagger, spear and sword that came at her. But how long could she keep defending herself that way?
