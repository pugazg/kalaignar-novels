# The Letter That Changed the Mind!

Although the victory celebration had ended as a celebration of sorrow, Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi found consolation in the fact that the kingdom of Kandy had not become enslaved to foreign imperialism, and they remained in Kandy with their forces for a long time. They stayed alert, expecting that Lord North would somehow launch a powerful attack on Kandy from Kolumbu. Pandarakan had instructed his younger sister Oomaichchi Naachchi, who had been administering the Panangamam region, to take on the responsibility for both the defence and administration of Mullaitheevu as well. Every day in Kandy was a special feast for him, for Kuruvichchi and for his soldiers—and this went on for years!

Because the Kandy king and Pandaraka Vanniyan believed that the English would not come toward Mullaitheevu first, but would try to gain a foothold there only after capturing Kandy, Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi devoted all their attention to defending Kandy.

During that period, a letter sent by Oomaichchi Naachchiyar from Panangamam reached Kuruvichchi.

Because the front of the letter bore the words, “For Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar alone to read in private!”, Kuruvichchi concealed the letter, went to a private room in the Kandy palace, bolted the door and opened it.

“My dear Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar—somehow my heart is bursting with the desire to address this letter to ‘my sister-in-law’! It looks as though the two of you are not going to fulfil that wish of mine soon! After being defeated in Kandy and fleeing in terror, the English have been lying low. It is said that these days they no longer revel and make merry in the city of Kolumbu. It is also said that the English officers have decided that there is no longer any question of launching an invasion—not only anywhere in Eelam, but in any part of Lanka. So we may trust that the band of white men camped in Kolumbu will not make even the slightest attempt to encircle our land. You two can therefore withdraw the vow you made. It can be said without the least doubt that your valiant pledge has been fulfilled. Can we not soon make arrangements for the marriage of you two? May I at least come to Mullaitheevu for the wedding? Or are you going to hold that too in the capital of your friend in Kandy? Do not blush and affectionately scold me, saying, ‘Go away, girl!’ And do not flare up at me in anger either! How could you have any anger toward your brother? Tell me soon the date fixed for the sweet and happy life of you both! With love, Oomaichchi Naachchiyar.”

Holding the letter in her hand, Kuruvichchi stared up at the sky through the lattice window. Every line of the letter made her think and plunged her into confusion.

Was what Oomaichchi Naachchiyar had written not true? The English, after suffering a great defeat in Kandy, appeared to be devoting themselves to their trade

and no longer seemed likely to show any interest in conquering territory. Should it not therefore be taken that the pledge made by Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi had succeeded? Two divine images in the temples of their hearts! Pandarakan in Kuruvichchi’s heart! Kuruvichchi in his! Like the images of god and goddess, each in the other’s heart! Such love, affection, respect and, beyond all these, devotion toward one another! Is love complete only when bodies rub against each other, embrace, rejoice and take pleasure? They have bound one another with pure love—is that not love?

It seemed to Kuruvichchi that the moon gliding across the sky was murmuring sentences like these—some ending in full stops, others engraved with question marks.

Drops of sweat budded on her broad forehead. Her golden lotus-face faltered, unable to decide whether to bloom or fold shut.

Having come to the conclusion that what Oomaichchi Naachchiyar had written was indeed right, she rose with a deep sigh. Her proudly swelling, high breasts did not fail to reveal just how deep that sigh was.

Why not give the letter to Pandarakan? Her heart, like an unseen voice, told her that she could.

She rose quickly, yet walked away only slowly. At this hour Pandarakan and Vikrama Raja Singan would be strolling in the palace garden. Should she give the letter to Pandarakan right in front of the king? No, no! That would not do! Let me go there! He will come over and ask me what it is. I can gently lead him away and then show him his sister’s letter.

It happened just as she had imagined. The two men were strolling through the garden, talking about something and laughing now and then. Pandarakan spotted Kuruvichchi standing there. “What is it, Kuruvichchi?” he asked eagerly. The Kandy king too came toward Kuruvichchi, walking beside Pandarakan.

“You are a bachelor! It makes no difference to you! But is the king like that? How long has the queen been waiting!” Kuruvichchi said, letting out a little giggle. Pandarakan was entranced to see the radiance spilling from that laugh compete with and defeat the silver light of the full moon, but he composed himself.

“Oh! The queen is waiting? I am going at once,” said Vikrama Raja Singan, quickly leaving the garden.

But his mind would not let him go. What Kuruvichchi had said about the queen waiting was probably a lie. She has driven me away because she wants to talk and enjoy herself with Pandarakan. Very well. I shall discover what game these two are playing and make them flee the garden in embarrassment. Without resisting the idea that had arisen in his mind, the king hid behind thick shrubs and creepers.

“What is it, Kuruvichchi? Why have you come here so suddenly?”

“Should I not have come?”

“Who said that? We have been in this palace for so many days. Not once have you come at night to see me, nor have I gone to see you!”

“I was not going to come tonight either! But a letter made me come!”

“A letter? Me? Did I write one to you?”

“Why are you getting flustered? Have you never written one? The letter I mean was not written by you!”

“Then who wrote it?”

“Oomaichchi Naachchiyar—your younger sister!”

“What has happened? Is there some trouble in Mullaitheevu?”

“The trouble is no ordinary matter! It is rather frightening!”

“Is that so? I wondered from the very beginning whether Oomaichchi Naachchiyar could manage responsibility for both Panangamam and Mullaitheevu! You were the one who refused to go and stayed here!”

“Even Nandan was granted the right to behold from afar in the street. Am I not entitled to that right?”

“What is this? The conversation has suddenly changed direction!”

“That is not my fault! It is the fault of this letter! Here—the letter written by your sister!”

“She has written it for your eyes alone!”

“Are your eyes not also present in mine?”

Pandarakan looked at Kuruvichchi in a certain way and then looked at the letter. His eyes ran over the writing as though noting that it was dated four or five days earlier. “It reached my hands only today,” Kuruvichchi replied, lowering her eyes in great shyness toward the big toes of her feet. The change of mind that Oomaichchi Naachchi’s letter had brought about in her was bound to come over Pandarakan too! Since there was no one else in the garden, he might even suddenly run to her and embrace her! Oh! And the moonlight was bright as day—Kuruvichchi’s heartbeats sounded so loudly that even her own ears could hear them.

<!-- source boundary: scan 431 → scan 432 -->

She could not raise her head and look at him. That made her feel even more shy.

How many battlefields had they discussed while looking directly at one another—how many plans of war had they devised! But now? Kuruvichchi stood with her head bowed, without lifting her gaze from the toe of her foot.

The letter came down so swiftly onto the tip of that toe that it hurt her there. The heavy rolled letter struck the toe and blood began to seep out. Paying no attention to it, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar raised her head and looked at Pandaraka Vanniyan in surprise.
