51

# Where Did That Kurathi Go?

“You said my friend Kannusami, the king, had sent you. That was all a fabricated story, wasn't it? Why are you silent? Speak! If you were genuine, why did you have any need to eavesdrop on our conversation? You ran because you were afraid the truth had come into the open, didn't you?”

Pandaraka Vanniyan kept piling question upon question on her.

Perhaps thinking she might escape by remaining silent, Marthani lay there with a bandage on the wound in her heel, blinking nervously.

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar could no longer contain her anger. She seized Marthani by the shoulder and shook her hard. “What drama are you staging? If you do not tell the truth now, the dagger that aimed at your heel will fly into your throat! Tell me—who are you and your mute husband Jeyaseelan? Were the two of you truly sent by the King of Kandy?”

Seeing Kuruvichchi's dagger raised toward her neck, Marthani was terrified. “Ayyo! Don't kill me!” she cried.

“Then answer! Were you really spies sent by the King of Kandy to assist the leader of Mullaitheevu?”

Kuruvichchi now brought the dagger close to Marthani's neck.

<!-- source boundary: scan 324 → scan 325 -->

“It is true! I swear, we were sent from Kandy!”

Hearing Marthani's answer, Kuruvichchi let out an angry laugh.

“Sent from Kandy—I accept that. But sent by whom? By the King of Kandy?”

“Yes!” Marthani's head nodded.

“Don't lie!” Kuruvichchi swiftly raised the dagger.

Before she could strike, Pandarakan caught Kuruvichchi's hand.

“It was Pilimathala who sent us!”

Realizing that there was no other way to save her life, Marthani told the truth. But Kuruvichchi did not stop.

“Then where is that mute Jeyaseelan who came with you? Is he truly mute?”

“Yes. He is mute. Even I don't know where he has gone.”

“Don't draw another curtain of lies! He is not your husband at all! I say even the name Jeyaseelan was invented for the treacherous drama you are staging.”

“No! No, not at all! Believe me! It is true that Pilimathala sent me and my husband here! My husband's name is Jeyaseelan! My husband's name is Jeyaseelan! God knows I swear this is true!”

“Do you know Pilimathala's daughter Piyaseeli?”

“I do! But she died!”

“Has the dead woman's spirit leapt from one body to another and begun roaming about in the form of Jeyaseelan?”

“Amma! I don't understand what you are saying at all.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 325 → scan 326 -->

“Of course you don't! Aren't you coquettes who made the false calculation that cats like you could destroy this tiger's den? You won't understand! Nor will Pilimathala, who prepared you and sent you here, understand that you are treading on the tail of a tiger that is wide awake! I will tell you more—listen! The spirit of the dead Piyaseeli entered Jeyaseelan's body! After that, the spirit entered the body of a Kurathi fortune-teller! Well? Am I right?”

“I don't understand anything! Spirits entering bodies? Kurathi or clods of earth? Everything is one great mystery!”

Marthani acted as though she had reached the very height of astonishment.

“You daring woman! Didn't you understand Thanigaimala's weakness and enchant him with your coquetry and glitter? Didn't you wail to Thanigaimala that your mute husband discovered it, could not bear the shame, and ran away? And did not that mute Jeyaseelan who was said to have run away later transform himself—come as a Kurathi—and embrace Thanigaimala?”

Marthani was stunned into silence. Overcome with wonder, Pandaraka Vanniyan looked at Kuruvichchi Naachchi and said, “Yes! You hid all these things without even telling me. How did you discover that treachery had come in their form? You say Piyaseeli—you say Jeyaseelan—you say Kurathi—none of it seems believable! Who told you all this?”

“I learned everything through that Kurathi!”

“Is that so?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 326 → scan 327 -->

“Yes! I could not recognize her when she was in a man's disguise. But when she came transformed into a woman who tells fortunes, I became certain that she was Piyaseeli!”

“I cannot believe it, Kuruvi!”

“I knew you would not believe me. That is why I put her in a cage.”

“Good heavens! What is this? You keep stacking one astonishing piece of news upon another!” Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar smiled and replied, “Men may be warriors. But women are not only warrior women—they are also discerning!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan looked at her mischievously.

“All right, all right! Where is that Kurathi?”

“Why ask for the Kurathi? Ask for Piyaseeli! Come with me. I have imprisoned her in a room in our palace. I dealt with her somewhat harshly and made her spit out all the truth!”

Explaining to Pandarakan, Kuruvichchi continued, “Do you still not believe me? Shall I tell you everything that Kurathi said? On a blank sheet bearing the King of Kandy's signature, Piyaseeli herself prepared a letter! That was the letter given to us! Everything happened according to Pilimathala's arrangement. Piyaseeli became Jeyaseelan! She made this Marthani act as her wife. The first success of their attempt was the incident in which they seduced our spy Thanigaimala! Because I had warned Thanigaimala, he was able not to be completely deceived and to break—

<!-- source boundary: scan 327 → scan 328 -->

their intrigue! Come with me! I will make that Kurathi Piyaseeli tell you every detail with her own mouth.”

Having said this, Kuruvichchi clapped her hands, summoned the guards, ordered them to make sure Marthani did not run away, and left with Pandarakan.

Four guards stood with spears before a room at the rear of the palace.

Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi arrived there. Kuruvichchi signalled to the guards to open the door. It opened. The two entered, with the guards following behind. Kuruvichchi was jolted with shock. The Kurathi she said she had locked up—yes, Piyaseeli—was nowhere to be seen. Only a torch was burning.

“Where is she?” Kuruvichchi shouted. The guards stared in alarm.

“You let yourselves be deceived and let her go!” the young woman cried in panic. At that moment a warrior ran there, panting. He stood before Pandarakan and bowed.

Before Pandarakan could ask, “What is it?” the warrior blurted out, “Your Majesty! Kakkai Vanniyan has killed Thanigaimala! They say he has also taken Nalla Naachchiyar to his fort! Wedding arrangements are under way there!”

Like the roar of a lion tearing through the silence of the forest, Pandarakan cried, “Ah! What?” and drew his sword.
