# Naalu Kaal Mandapaththil!

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar hurriedly dismounted from her horse and ran into the hall of the Kannagi Amman temple. There she saw King Pandaraka Vanniyan—Kulasekaram Vairamuthu—standing with his palms joined, worshipping the goddess Kannagi with his eyelids gently closed. She moved a little to one side and watched him without blinking.

Following the custom that even kings who entered the temple hall to worship Kannagi Amman should wear no upper garment and should tie even the *uthariyam* around the waist as a mark of respect, he stood there in that manner. Kuruvichchi Naachchi drank in with her eyes his broad, expansive chest and diamond-hard arms. Gratitude surged through her like a waterfall at the thought that it was Kannagi Devi who had granted her the boon of having this heroic young man as her beloved; with tears of joy overflowing, she bowed before Amman's image.

The people of the Vanni-Mullai region who had gathered for the Visaka festival watched in wonder, delight and blessing as the two lovers—in whom the joy of youth, the fullness of beauty and the clamour of life seemed to celebrate in every limb—worshipped the goddess of chastity inside the temple hall with such reverence and devotional rapture.

<!-- source boundary: scan 42 → scan 43 -->

The people, who had been disappointed the previous year after expecting the two to be married simply before Kannagi in that very temple hall, waited this year in the hope that perhaps the sweet event might finally take place.

After worshipping Amman, Kuruvichchi Naachchi next fell at Kulasekaram Vairamuthu's feet and paid homage. The king gently took her by both shoulders, raised her to her feet and placed the bunch of flowers in his hand in her hair. Taking kumkum from the tray held out before her, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar placed a tilak on his forehead. Then the two prostrated before Amman, worshipped, and rose.

Neither spoke until they had come out of the hall. Instead, their hearts seemed to sing together beyond their control. An elderly woman, unable to take her eyes off the young king walking majestically like a lion and the peacock-like woman beside him, looking like a statue carved from pure charm, said to a middle-aged woman beside her, “I feel as though even my gaze might cast the evil eye on them!” She used all her strength to split the lemon in her hand in two and, from a distance, warded off the evil eye.

“They still haven't married even today!” one man asked. “What if they haven't married? Even without a wedding, aren't they husband and wife already?” another said with a laugh. “Hey, speak softly! Someone might hear you!” warned a third.

<!-- source boundary: scan 43 → scan 44 -->

“Last year, love. This year, a Visaka festival for Kannagi Amman because their love has been confirmed. Next year, just wait and see—there will certainly be a wedding festival,” said one man like a fortune-teller.

Lost in thoughts that left them unable to notice either the people or their talk, the two reached their horses. On either side of the horses, more than twenty sturdy warriors sat waiting on horseback, carrying long spears.

Vairamuthu and Kuruvichchi Naachchi mounted their respective horses. Vairamuthu looked at her. She too struck him with the spear of her eyes. Attendants came running, helped him into his upper garment and respectfully handed him his sword. He untied the *uthariyam* that until then had been wrapped around his waist and draped it over his shoulder. He looked at her again. Waves of eagerness rose in Kuruvichchi Naachchi as she expected him to say something.

“Kuruvii! I hear you went chasing an elephant?”

The moment he called her “Kuruvii,” it felt as though the chill of mist had descended over her whole body. She let out a childlike little laugh.

The very next moment, the memory of what had happened a short while earlier seized her heart sharply, like a magnet pulling a piece of iron.

“In my eagerness to see you, I made a serious mistake!” she said.

“What mistake?” he asked with a faint smile, stroking with his finger the moustache that curved upward.

<!-- source boundary: scan 44 → scan 45 -->

“The elephant ran off in a frenzy, so I chased it to prevent danger to the people! At that time, I didn't know who he was, but a man was caught by the elephant. I saved him. But then the elephant charged at me to kill me—and by then that warrior attacked it with his sword. Before I could know how it ended, the temple bell rang. Anxious that you must have arrived and would be waiting, I hurried back here! I don't know what happened to him!”

As soon as she said this, he replied with a little agitation and at the same time mischievously, “Never mind! We can go to that very place now! Let Kuruvii show the way!”

Kuruvichchi Naachchi turned her horse toward that direction and urged it forward. He followed her. The horses of the escorting warriors formed ranks on either side.

Ten warriors were coming toward them with the arrested Sundaralingam. In their midst Sundaralingam strode with dignity like a tiger, without even the shadow of fear showing on his face.

“There! That's him! Our warriors are bringing him here!” said Kuruvichchi Naachchi.

“It doesn't look as though they are bringing him along! Doesn't it look as though they are bringing him under arrest?” said the king. He urged his horse toward the Naalu Kaal Mandapam by the roadside and stopped there in the shade.

She followed him and stopped with her horse in the shade of the mandapam. Looking at two of the escorting warriors, the king ordered, “Go at once and bring them here.” The warriors escorting Sundaralingam, seeing their king at the mandapam, hurried there as well.

<!-- source boundary: scan 45 → scan 46 -->

“First ask why they are bringing him under arrest!” Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar said close to Vairamuthu's ear. Perhaps she felt it would not be proper for her to ask the question herself while the king was present. Seeing Sundaralingam approaching unharmed, she felt relief within.

As soon as Sundaralingam and the warriors escorting him came near, the king leapt down from his horse, went forward and embraced Sundaralingam warmly. “You? Oh! Please forgive me that this has happened!” he said in a trembling voice full of affection. Sundaralingam gently freed himself from the king's embrace and managed, “It's all right!” Turning furious eyes on the warriors who had arrested him, Vairamuthu demanded, “Why did you arrest him?”

“Your Majesty! No one may hunt our forest elephants without the state's permission! But this man stabbed an elephant repeatedly with his own sword, tortured it and killed it.” Before the lead warrior could finish this answer, Kuruvichchi Naachchi said just loudly enough for the king to hear, “Otherwise that frenzied elephant would have trampled both him and me to death.”

Vairamuthu swept his gaze over all the warriors standing around him. His warriors, who could understand the meaning of that extraordinary look—formed as though from the coolness of the moon and the fire of the sun—as easily as one might grasp a commentary on *Aathichudi*, withdrew and stood at a distance.

“Once again, I ask your forgiveness for the mistake that has occurred,” Pandaraka Vanniyan began.

<!-- source boundary: scan 46 → scan 47 -->

Kuruvichchi Naachchi did not need much time to understand that the two men were already connected as friends. In the same way, Sundaralingam guessed that the warrior-woman beside the king must be Kuruvichchi Naachchi, and used his skill in conversation to confirm it. Looking at the king, who had apologized again, he replied, “Why all this between us? Your warriors did not know who I was, that's all!” Then, looking at Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar, he said, “On the way I met your grandfather, Sangili Thalapathi.” Hearing this, she could hardly contain her surprise. A smile fell from her like a flower dropping from a vine, and she looked at him and moved her lips: “Oh! Is that so?”

“Kuruvichchi! I haven't introduced you to who he is!” said the king. Cutting him short with a mischievous smile, she replied, “You should have done that first.”

“His name is Sundaralingam.”

“You told me that the moment you jumped down from your horse and embraced him!”

“Listen, I'll tell you the rest! You know that Vellaiyathevan, commander in the army of Panchalankurichi king Kattabomman, is my dearest friend. This Sundaralingam is that friend's friend! He too is the commander of a division! Vellaiyathevan, born in the Thevar clan, and this Sundaralinga Kudumbar, born in the Devendra clan, lack only the fact of having been born together; otherwise they are inseparable brothers! Like us, they are engaged in the struggle to defend their country...”

<!-- source boundary: scan 47 → scan 48 -->

Even as the king explained this, a new light spread across Kuruvichchi's face. She looked at Sundaralingam with affection, joined her hands and bowed to him. He returned the greeting.

“Did you suffer greatly in the storm during your journey?” Vairamuthu asked with concern.

“For those of us who are fighting the very storm of foreign domination, aren't storms like this ordinary?” Sundaralingam replied.

“I have heard that this Sundaralinga Kudumbar comes from the lineage of Vettum Perumal, who fought valiantly for the rights of the Pandya king! See, Kuruvichchi! The way he speaks seems to prove that it is true!” Pandaraka Vanniyan said, swelling with pride.

“I've forgotten the urgent work that brought me here! I have to return to Tamil Nadu immediately!”

“First let us go to the palace. You must stay there as my guest for at least one day.”

“Commander Vellaiyathevan asked me to discuss some important matters with you. He has also explained and written some notes about them in this letter.”

“Vellaiyathevan is my friend! In the same way, you too are my friend! So would it not be disrespectful to read his letter and discuss it with you like this in the middle of the road?”

“My view is that when the pressure of war is so severe—both in Tamil Nadu and here—there is no need for all those formalities! So let us sit in this mandapam itself and talk, because I must leave to return today.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 48 → scan 49 -->

“You are very stubborn! As you wish, let us sit here and talk!”

Agreeing, Pandaraka Vanniyan told Kuruvichchi Naachchi, “Take a few warriors and go keep watch on the road to Mulliya Valai! I have heard that a secret British force is roaming through our regions on spying work. So it would be good for you and a few warriors to remain alert on the Mulliya Valai road a short distance from here.”

She immediately set out from there, taking only a few of the warriors who stood ranked in the distance, and hurried toward the Mulliya Valai road.

“Will you give me the letter our friend Vellaiyathevan gave you?” Pandaraka Vanniyan asked, holding out his hand.

“Here it is!” Sundaralingam said, reaching for the letter tucked at his waist—and was startled. The reason was simple: the letter was gone!
