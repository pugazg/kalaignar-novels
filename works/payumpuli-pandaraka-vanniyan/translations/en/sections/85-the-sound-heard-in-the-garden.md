# The Sound Heard in the Garden!

“Your arrangement is magnificent! You have used my sister Oomaichchi Naachchiyar as an instrument and begun your drama through this letter!”

Kuruvichchi trembled when she saw that Pandarakan’s face, which should have looked cool in the moonlight, appeared like a tongue of fire. Seeing the blood seeping from her toe, Pandarakan said nothing more. He immediately tore off one end of his upper cloth and hurriedly wrapped it around the wound. Understanding that his face might be flame while his heart was sandal paste, Kuruvichchi said, “Forgive me!” Did she not know that the heart overflowing with valour also held just as much tenderness?

“Do not stand! Sit down!” He gripped her shoulder, pressed her gently down and seated her on a beautifully carved black-stone seat nearby.

“But you are standing!”

“Why? Must I sit beside you too? Have you come with the thaali already prepared as well?”

“Why must you flare up like this? It is a letter written by your younger sister! If I had not given it to you, would you not have been angry later?”

“I believe you when you say my younger sister wrote

it. But what should you have done the moment you read this letter? Should you not have torn it up and burned it yourself?”

“How could I have acted in haste without knowing what was in your mind?”

“After knowing me for so long, it is astonishing beyond belief that you still do not know what is in my mind!”

“Our ideal has been fulfilled to a great extent, so...”

“My sister thinks so! That is why she wrote the letter! Do you believe our ideal has been fulfilled? My innocent sister believes that the fattened white man camped in Kolumbu will never look our way again—that he will not come quarrelling with us, will not wag his tail on our soil, but will sit there with his mouth shut, minding only his trade! In that vast Tamilakam itself, the trader who came by ship today commands Tamil kings to pay tribute! English domination is spreading across the whole of Tamil Nadu! Compared with that, this island is no bigger than a mustard seed! Yet a mustard seed does not lose its pungency merely because it is small, and so we resist fiercely. It is a mistake to think the white man has become afraid! He is merely lying low! His armies may spring upon us at any moment. Do you understand what I am saying?”

“Your assessment may well be right. Or it may be a device to brush me aside!”

“Shame! You have put a price on love of country! Bodily hunger has caught you like a forest fire! Kuruvichchi! I never expected this change in you!”

“I too never expected that you would think so meanly of me. I live in a state where it is the thought of you that gives strength to my ideal, yet how quickly you have called me a woman driven by bodily hunger!”

“What else? Is it not the duty of a true wife to act in harmony with her husband in the responsible duty he has undertaken, and to give him cooperation without even the slightest obstruction so that his efforts may succeed?”

“You call yourself a husband! You call me a wife! I ask you to take on that status, while you refuse to grant me that status! Then where did this husband-and-wife relationship come from?”

Pandarakan was bewildered and stunned by Kuruvichchi’s question.

“A perfect question! A question like a lash across my friend’s back! Bravo, Kuruvichchi, bravo!”

Vikrama Raja Singan hurried toward them, laughing loudly.

“You are here?” Pandarakan’s face reddened in astonishment.

“You are angry already! Do not boil over any more!” said the Kandy king as he came near and embraced him. Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar was indeed a warrior woman, but because the matter concerned the very height of love, she stood with her head bowed.

“My friend! I have been listening to everything. I did not believe Kuruvichchi when she said the queen was looking for me. I hid because I thought that today I might at last get a chance to understand the two of you, who have always been incomprehensible riddles.”

“A king who rules the land should not have such a mind

at all!”

“You may even say a king should have no mind at all! Say it and be done!” I do not care about that! But one thing: you cannot deny that I have the right to worry that the domestic life of a dear friend who works day and night caring for my joys and sorrows should become a happy one!”

“Vikrama! Do you not know that Kuruvichchi and I are two in body, but one in life and in our noble ideal?”

“What if you were one in body too? Would becoming one in body make you two in life? Is being one in body something that happens all the time? Only once in a while, surely!”

Kuruvichchi could bear the embarrassment no longer. She turned away and looked at the moon in the sky.

“Oh! So all of you have been plotting together for a long time! You caught hold of my sister and made her write a letter, and now, clinging to that letter, you are putting a knife to my neck!”

“There is no question of your neck in this! The question is Kuruvichchi’s neck—the neck that longs for a thaali to be tied by your hand!”

“To Vikrama Raja Singan everything is a game! These temporary victories have unsettled you all! Who has prophesied that the white man will no longer try to dominate our land? Do not forget that the brain of a thousand foxes sits inside the head of one Englishman! Until we can write the final line declaring that those wretches wandering in a frenzy to seize countries have fled this island, none of your words seem clear to me. My love for Kuruvichchi is no less than my friend’s sympathy for her or my sister’s affection for her! I do not know how my Kuruvichchi forgot the vow we made—that until the complete freedom of this land is secured without flaw, the two of us would not enter into married

life!”

As Pandarakan said this with his voice trembling, Kuruvichchi ran to him, clasped his feet and pleaded tearfully, “Please forgive me.”

Pandarakan took her hands and comforted her.

“All right, all right! Get up! There is nothing here to forgive! It is the restlessness of youth!”

“And you alone are an aged old man! A tree without feelings! Is that it?” Vikrama Raja Singan retorted.

“Vikrama! What exactly do you want me to do now?”

“Pandaraka! Now you are coming onto my path! I want nothing else! I have one wish—to see the two of you in wedding attire!”

“And after that?”

“After that, do as you please! It is enough for me just to see you in wedding attire!”

“Very well! I shall fulfil my friend’s wish! But one thing: until our ideal is completely fulfilled, even after marriage we shall remain celibate!”

“That is enough for me!” Vikrama Raja Singan leapt in delight.

“I shall begin the wedding arrangements tomorrow itself,” he said.

“First send word to my sister,” Pandarakan said, letting a smile curl at the corner of his lips as he looked at Kuruvichchi.

All three sank into happiness. “Only now has my mind found peace,” Vikrama Raja Singan sighed. At that moment, a drum thundered through the Kandy palace. It was customary for that drum to sound at any hour whenever there was dangerous and urgent news.
