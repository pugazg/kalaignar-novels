# Panangamam in Enemy Hands!

The words that his younger sister had been killed made Pandarakan writhe as though millions of hornets had stung his heart at once. Tears poured down Kuruvichchi’s cheeks like a waterfall; she gripped a pillar to keep herself from collapsing. Like someone trying to cover a roaring volcano with his bare hands, Pandaraka Vanniyan held back the grief surging within him and, without letting it burst out fully, turned to the warrior who had brought the news and asked, “How was Panangamam attacked? Who did it?” The warrior replied:

“English forces are coming from three directions. One force is coming from Mannar. One force is coming from Thirukonamalai. It was the force that came from Yalppanam that had laid siege to Panangamam. Knowing that you were not in Mullaitheevu, the English soldiers planned to seize the Panangamam fort first—and they have succeeded!”

Hearing the warrior’s answer, Pandarakan demanded angrily, “Then who is leading the forces marching to surround Mullaitheevu?”

“A force from Mannar under Vanderi Berg—

and a force from Thirukonamalai under Edward Madge!”

“Oh! Vanderi Berg? That great tiger who mocked me, asking what Pandaraka Vanniyan could possibly do with the primitive weapons of spears and war-swords against the English cannon and gunpowder, yet collapsed when he could not withstand my sword? That very Vanderi Berg whose life I spared that day and drove out of Mullaitheevu—is he coming again to invade Mullaitheevu?”

Twisting his moustache proudly as he spoke, Pandarakan looked intently at Kuruvichchi. The tears flowing from her eyes had not stopped. He went to her, took the edge of his upper cloth and wiped away her tears.

“The battlefront is calling! There is no time to sit and lament! Get ready to leave!”

“First, should not the two of us go to Panangamam and see Oomaichchi Naachchiyar’s body...?”

Even as she said this, Kuruvichchi broke down and wept.

“If you keep weeping for your dead sister, we will lose Mullaitheevu to the English as well! Kuruvichchi, listen to me. You leave from here for Panangamam. Attend to your sister’s final rites and then come and join me at Mullaitheevu. I am leaving now, directly for Mullaitheevu with the forces.”

“Should you not also come to Panangamam to see your sister’s face one last time?”

“I am leading a war now! Every

<!-- source boundary: scan 449 → scan 450 -->

word I speak from this moment until this war ends is an order! Kuruvichchi! You are going to Panangamam with the forces! You will perform Oomaichchi Naachchi’s final rites! If the English army places an obstacle even in the way of that, you will break through it! You will come to Mullaitheevu in victory!”

The moment Pandarakan’s words came out as commands, Kuruvichchi wiped her eyes. In an instant she drove away the grief that had covered her heart and raised her head with the hardness of iron.

“Here! Take my sword with you! It will give you the feeling that I am coming with you!”

He drew his sword and gave it to her. Kuruvichchi kissed the sword and held it high in her hand.

Until then Vikrama Raja Singan had stood motionless, like one of the pillars there. He embraced Pandarakan with affection and said, “The forces of Kandy too will be sent to Mullaitheevu!”

“No! No, absolutely not! If the English are sending armies from three directions to conquer Mullaitheevu and Panangamam, they will not leave Kandy alone! Even if not immediately, danger will come to Kandy again before long. If I am alive then, I will come back to defend Kandy!”

Vikrama Raja Singan embraced Pandarakan tightly. Both men became masses of emotion.

Before dawn had fully broken, Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi took leave of the Kandy royal couple and departed with their forces.
