# Fox-Tail Tuft!

“Yes, yes—his body is like an anichcha flower! His father and mother are just the same—he is a child born true to them!” Marthani laughed, covering the situation.

“Oh! So the whole lineage seems to be a garden lineage! That is why the parents too are like flowers—and the child is a flower as well!” Thanigaimala teased.

As though thoroughly enjoying the joke, Jeyaseelan gracefully tilted his head, bent his arm, and lightly tapped Thanigai on the face.

“A flower-punch!” said Thanigai, winking. Afraid that he might begin to understand too much, Marthani immediately rebuked Jeyaseelan with her eyes.

Thanigai said he had arranged for both of them to stay in his own house and brought them there. When they reached the doorway, Jeyaseelan and Marthani looked the place up and down and stood stunned for a moment.

“Why are you so surprised? Don't worry! I am a lone bachelor. I will shut myself away in a room downstairs. You two can set up house upstairs. Whatever you need, just tell me at once. Even if it is a parijata flower, it will fly here to you!”

said Thanigaimala. Marthani looked at Jeyaseelan as though asking, “All right?” Jeyaseelan smiled and nodded in agreement.

Marthani, after all, knew Piyasili's hunger and taste!

So she turned to Thanigai and asked, “Aren't you married?”

“If that had happened, seven or eight little devils would have run to the doorway by now to welcome the two of you!”

“What is this? Calling children little devils? We have been doing penance because not even one has been born to us!”

Marthani played the part of a deeply worried woman perfectly.

“How old can the two of you be? Even if you begin four or five years from now, you can still count ten or fifteen!” Thanigai laughed.

“That won't happen, sir!”

“Why?”

“It is true that one of us is barren! So in this birth there is no child-bearing for us.”

Marthani's face seemed to grow still more troubled.

“Good thing, then! Even in your old-age romance there will be no interference from those monkeys called children!”

“Look at him! How casually he says it! The pain I suffer because not even the tiniest living thing has been placed in my womb—isn't that something only I know?” she said with a deep sigh, burying her face against Jeyaseelan's shoulder.

“I know a Yogeeswarar. I will bring him here one day. Or I will take you both and introduce you. He will give you some pills. If you eat them, a child will be born immediately!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 245 → scan 246 -->

Thanigaimala said this to the two of them with complete confidence. Jeyaseelan looked at Marthani, opening both eyelids wide like the wings of a bird about to take flight, as though asking, “Can such a thing really happen?” Marthani too gave a little hop, shook Thanigai's upper arm with her hand, and scattered astonishment: “What is this, sir? Giving a pill—and a child being born at once?”

“Marthani! Have you never heard the story of the Mahabharata? How were Kunti Devi's children born? Just as your husband is mute, Kunti Devi's husband was a man under a curse! Even before her marriage, while she was still an unmarried maiden, she ate a pill she had been given while thinking of the Sun. Immediately Karna was born through her cheek. Then, after her marriage, the Mahabharata says that she ate one pill thinking of Yama and Dharman was born, ate one thinking of Vayu Bhagavan and Bhimasenan was born, and ate one thinking of Indra and Arjunan was born!”

“Oh, go away! You are taking made-up stories and telling them all wrong! Eating a pill—and a child being born?”

Marthani said this and playfully struck Thanigai like a clap of thunder.

That is, Piyasili—using Marthani—was sounding out what kind of man he was. Thanigaimala shifted a little under that blow, but he did not move entirely away. Piyasili noticed that sharply

<!-- source boundary: scan 246 → scan 247 -->

and decided that with a little effort she could bring Thanigai under her control.

Even if winning him over had been only for carrying out their plans, Piyasili would not have taken such keen interest!

The very sight of his imposing, long-limbed build had already stirred her physical hunger. Again and again she looked at his lips and enjoyed them. To her they seemed like two elephant trunks joined one over the other and moving together. She stood yearning for the plundering pleasure of having her body bent and crushed by those trunk-like lips. Yet with great difficulty she restrained that feeling and carried on the mute-man performance skilfully.

Saying, “Come upstairs,” Thanigaimala led the two of them up the stairs. The upper floor of that house was arranged very simply.

“What is this—have you hung a fox's tail here as a tuft?” Marthani asked, touching the tuft with her fingers and admiring it.

“Spy work is fox-work, isn't it? That is why a fox-tail tuft hangs in a spy's house! Sometimes I struggle when no stratagem comes to me. Then I come here, lie down on this bed, keep staring at this fox-tail tuft without taking my eyes off it, and chant a mantra! Suddenly tricks for spy work begin to arise in my brain. At once I get up and set out to begin my work!”

“What mantra is that? We are spies too, aren't we? Can't you tell us?”

<!-- source boundary: scan 247 → scan 248 -->

“You two are spies too?”

“Why ask as though you don't know? Is the proverb ‘Only a snake knows a snake's legs’ false?”

“Whatever it is, I cannot reveal that mantra immediately! After we have known one another for a few days, perhaps I will tell you. For now you can rest here. There is the kitchen! You and your husband can cook whatever you need and eat.”

“And you?”

“I'll see how good your cooking is. If you can, throw a little downstairs too!”

“Why should I throw it? I'll bring it down and give it to you myself.”

“Many thanks! I am going downstairs. I do not want to stand here and disturb the two of you.”

Thanigaimala smiled gently at them and went down the stairs. Piyasili's eyes kept watching him as he left. Once she knew he had gone downstairs, Marthani bolted the upstairs door and came over to Jeyaseelan. By then Jeyaseelan was hurriedly stripping off all his clothes and throwing them into a corner of the room.

“Oh, heavens! How suffocating these clothes are! How suffocating!” Hearing Piyasili mutter like this, Marthani laughed and said—

“You are used to dressing with three-fourths of your body showing; of course this rough male clothing is a great burden to you!”

“But there is one thing, Marthani! However painful it may be to carry this male outfit, this very clothing gives me the pleasure of feeling as though a man is wrapped around me!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 248 → scan 249 -->

Unable to find any measure for Piyasili's hunger for men, Marthani took clothes from her bundle, held them out to Piyasili, who stood naked as she had been born, and said, “Here! Put these on!”

Piyasili took the clothes carelessly, threw them down, and embraced Marthani.

“All right, all right! Let me cook first!” said Marthani, freeing herself from Piyasili and going toward the kitchen. Piyasili picked up the discarded garment, slung it over her shoulder, and entered the kitchen too. The two stood side by side, talking as they attended to the cooking together. It was work Piyasili had absolutely no experience with—but she had to discuss some important plans with Marthani and put them into practice!

Downstairs in his room, Thanigaimala lay on his side on an ordinary broken bed, sunk in deep thought. All his thoughts were about Jeyaseelan and Marthani. Then he heard a sound as though someone had flicked the door with a finger. Thanigaimala turned his attention toward the door and sat up on the bed.
