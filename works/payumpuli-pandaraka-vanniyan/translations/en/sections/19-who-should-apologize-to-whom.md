# Who Should Apologize? To Whom?

“I will speak to Naachchiyar and see. If she nods in agreement, well and good! Otherwise, what I told you will happen! I am leaving!”

With that, Sundaralingam departed. Kaakkai Vanniyan had expected him to argue—to pour out irritation, saying, “Is it not enough that I have come and asked? Must Naachchiyar come too?”—and to leave after declaring that he was ready to face whatever happened. For a moment, therefore, Kaakkai was confused, and Sundaralingam's approach surprised him. Sundaralingam too had clearly come with the thought that, somehow, the issue should end peacefully for the present. Kaakkai therefore believed Kuruvichchi would accept his proposal and come to the palace.

At once, without his realizing it, his intoxication-reddened eyes withdrew beneath his eyelids like a tortoise retreating into its shell! Though Kaakkai Vanniyan shut his eyes within his lids, he could not shut in the desires that burst forth after opening the doors of his heart.

He imagined that Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar had already arrived. Without realizing it, he delighted in imagining himself rising, going forward, welcoming her and attending to her. He decided that the smile she sowed upon seeing him would be the first proclamation taking root as love for him

in her heart. Without standing before him in person, Kuruvichchi Naachchi appeared before his inner eye as beauty itself.

“How many days have I yearned—pined—to find a place in her heart! It refuses to happen; ah! What beauty! If there is such a thing as an enchanting rainbow, is she not that alone? I have gone round and round until I am worn away. This beauty refuses to sit on my lap! She hates me! Does she need to bring the strength of an army to conquer me? One sidelong glance from her would be enough for me to sign away my whole life as her slave! That long, high forehead! If it comes close to my face, it will be a Himalayan victory for me! The thick, curling hair that coils beautifully above her forehead—if I could stroke it and gently brush against her, ah! I cannot even measure the delight of it! Whenever I saw from a little distance in the gurukulam classroom the soft down growing like smoke and descending toward her ears, how I longed to touch it with my fingers! I do not know what magic lies in that mango-like body fashioned by Maayan, drawing men toward it! Will Sarveswaran really deny me a chance to settle the doubt whether there is even a waist within that body hidden by clothing? Though hidden from my sight—those thighs like plantain stems, that springing gait, appearing without appearing and piercing my very life—after seeing them only from afar again and again until I wilted, will there never come a day when I have the chance to touch and taste? That chance for pleasure—that sweet chance

<!-- source boundary: scan 96 → scan 97 -->

—may not come to me at once today, but let this day
—become the day that lays the foundation for that day! I believe it will! To make it happen, I must not hurry; I must remain calm! Sundaralingam will somehow persuade her and bring her here! That maiden—my jackfruit segment—will come here to rescue her grandfather! The moment she arrives, I must not look at her and ask whether we can mount Manmatha's chariot and go to a silent heaven! If I ask in haste, she will immediately understand why she was really invited! I must not even show that I have such a thought! She must think that I am merely cherishing our old gurukulam friendship! I must show that much skill! I need patience for that! Kuruvichchi Naachchi must be astonished and think, ‘Kaakkai Vanniyan is not as he was before; he has now become very cultured.’ I must treat her with more honour than she herself expects. I must tell her I will release her grandfather. I must go out of my way to press her to stay and dine as a guest. If an Arinamothu Chintham for a good opinion of me can be written in her heart today, then afterward I can continue my efforts to draw her to our side and succeed. Lord! If only she agrees to come here today, that is enough—she has unknowingly surrendered her heart to Pandaraka Vanniyan and is now suffering inwardly, struggling desperately to hide it! She fell in love with him without expecting that Pandara Vanniyan would swear, ‘No marriage until the goal is achieved; I will not touch you!’ Whatever else, she is a woman, is she not? Would she have no desires?

<!-- source boundary: scan 97 → scan 98 -->

Even sages living in an ashram, they say, made such a domestic arrangement within their ascetic life because they needed a lovely woman to embrace and enjoy—so how long can Kuruvichchi simply remain talking to Pandarakan and looking at him? If I show my skill slowly, little by little, without haste, Kuruvichchi will become iron drawn toward a magnet!”

Kaakkai's eyes now peeped out beyond his eyelids! Blood-red! Such was the intoxication from drink! And, on top of that, the torment of thoughts of a woman!

Thinking that, if Kuruvichchi did come to the palace, certain arrangements had to be made before she arrived, he drove the kitchen workers to hurry: a feast had to be prepared with many kinds of dishes! If Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar saw Sangili Thalapathi in the condition in which he was now lying in prison, she would be shocked and enraged and pour hatred upon him. So he immediately ordered every comfort to be piled into that prison cell. A cot—a mattress—and everything else needed! Even books!

His plan was that, at a sudden glance, it should look not like a prison at all but like the library of some great scholar! Only then, he firmly believed, would Kuruvichchi see it and develop respect for his righteous principles, and that respect, with time, would turn into enchantment.

After ordering that all the arrangements he had imagined be carried out in an instant, Kaakkai sat with the foreign officers who were his masters and emptied a few more cups of liquor!

As soon as a man came running and reported that every comfort had been arranged for Sangili Thalapathi in the prison cell, Kaakkai informed the officers and himself set out to inspect the cell.

Astonished that so many comforts had been provided all at once without the slightest warning, Sangili Thalapathi had picked up one of the books stacked there and was leafing through it. He still could not understand why he was suddenly being treated with such care and honour. Before any explanation could come to him even by way of conjecture, Kaakkai Vanniyan entered the cell.

“Sangiliyar! Are you well?” Kaakkai asked, casting his eyes once around the room. Sangili remained silent.

“Do not be upset that I behaved harshly. In front of the foreign officers I have to act that way! If I show our affection and attachment before them, they will begin to suspect me too! That is why I had to show a little more anger toward you. Otherwise I cannot remain a man trusted by the foreigners!”

Sangili Thalapathi pursed his lips and suppressed a smile!

“Whether someone praises me by saying, ‘Is there anyone like him?’ or belittles me by saying, ‘Him? Is he even a man?’—people like me have been useful to others seeking advantage through both methods not just today or yesterday, but from time immemorial! So I will not be distressed by any of it!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 99 → scan 100 -->

Sangiliyar's reply carried the firmness found in the hand of a warrior gripping the hilt of his sword as it rises. Kaakkai Vanniyan was shaken by the reply, but composing himself, he let out a foolish laugh and continued.

“The officers advised that you should be tormented in prison. Though I nodded in agreement to them, my heart would not allow it. That is why I have ordered that you be treated here with royal honours.”

Sangili Thalapathi cleared his throat slightly and looked steadily at Kaakkai Vanniyan. Realizing that even in heavy intoxication Kaakkai had not slipped from his path of speaking with oily deceit, he dropped a dry word: “Thank you!”

“Have you heard the news? Your granddaughter Kuruvichchi Naachchi is on her way here to rescue you!” Kaakkai said.

“Kuruvichchi?”

Sangiliyar could not believe it! “No! It cannot be! It simply cannot be!” he said with firm emphasis.

“She first set out for here intending to fight. On the way, there was a different thought! Because of that, a messenger came! On Kuruvichchi's behalf he asked that you be released. It has been indicated, indirectly, that if Kuruvichchi herself comes to the palace and makes the request in person, the matter will be considered. The officers have not the slightest liking for it. This arrangement has been made only because I insisted—most likely Kuruvichchi may accept my proposal and come to the palace to rescue you!”

“Never! Ours is a lineage that fights to

<!-- source boundary: scan 100 → scan 101 -->

win back what has been taken from us, not a lineage that stretches out its hands and begs for mercy!”

“It was British soldiers and officers who abducted you! I have taken responsibility for speaking to them and getting you released. If, to help that along, Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar comes to the palace—and asks the British officer for pardon—everything will be settled!”

“Pardon? For what? Why should pardon be asked?”

“Was killing a foreign soldier not an offence? Is that not why they have imprisoned you? Is there not a need to apologize for that?”

Sangiliyar's eyes became pieces of burning coal! Was his granddaughter, whom he had raised as a warrior woman, going to apologize for his sake? Apologize to enemies! And that too in a traitor's camp! He could not bear the thought.

“When is Kuruvichchi coming here?”

“She may come soon! As soon as she arrives, I will bring her here first. Should she not see that her grandfather lacks nothing in prison and take comfort from it? That is why!”

“Very well! Let it be so! I need one facility here immediately to pass the time!”

“Whatever you want, it is ready!”

“Nothing much! I am in the habit of writing poetry! Can I get the things needed for that?”

“At once! I will have them brought to you!”
“I am leaving!” Kaakkai Vanniyan said, and emerged from that underground prison.

Sangili Thalapathi was deeply confused! “My granddaughter—come here?” he asked himself, and let out a long sigh.

Before Kaakkai Vanniyan had crossed the entrance of the prison cell, a warrior ran toward him, stood bent with his hands clasped and said, “Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar has sent word that she agrees to come here to the palace and meet you.”

“Bravo!” Kaakkai exclaimed, struck the warrior hard on the back and strode away quickly.

The information the warrior had given Kaakkai fell upon Sangili Thalapathi's ears too.

Without realizing it, he cried out, “Ah!”
