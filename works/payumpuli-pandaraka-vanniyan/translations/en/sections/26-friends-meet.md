# Friends Meet!

Before Pandaraka Vanniyan reached Kandy, a messenger brought an urgent letter from Colombo to Pilimathala, who had been a trusted minister of Kandy's late king Raja Rajasinga. The letter had been sent by the British Governor, Frederick North. The moment Pilimathala read it, he was shaken. Governor Frederick North had ordered him to arrest Kannusami immediately and keep him in custody, and also to oversee all the arrangements for Muthusami's coronation.

Pilimathala was by no means a good man. Even so, he did not wish to submit to the British and become a puppet dancing as they made him dance. At the same time, following Raja Rajasinga's death, he had been suppressing within himself a mountain-sized desire to be crowned king of the kingdom of Kandy. He had thought that British help would be welcome if it served only to fulfil that ambition. Governor Frederick North's letter ran contrary to what he had expected, and it startled him.

After Raja Rajasinga, while no one had yet

been crowned king of Kandy, if Kannusami were arrested, the blame would fall upon Minister Pilimathala. If Pilimathala himself conducted Muthusami's coronation, then the British could bring Muthusami under their control and dispose of Pilimathala as well. After that, they could easily cast Muthusami aside too and bring the kingdom of Kandy under British domination. That was Governor Frederick North's plan.

Having understood that plan to some extent, Minister Pilimathala of Kandy thought about how he could avoid entangling himself in direct opposition to the British while still turning the administrative machinery of Kandy according to his own will.

If he arrested Kannusami, he would incur Governor North's anger—and if he arrested and imprisoned him, it would amount to going out of his way to seek Pandaraka Vanniyan's enmity.

Pilimathala also understood that helping make Muthusami ruler of Kandy in accordance with the Governor's wishes could harm his own future. Muthusami was a slave of the British, one who waited ready to carry out with his head whatever command they issued with their foot. If he were made king, Pilimathala's scheme—to provoke hostility between him and the British and, in the midst of that conflict, declare himself king of Kandy—would never come to fruition. On the other hand, if Kannusami were made king, Pilimathala calculated that he could nurture Kannusami's natural hatred of the British, create a battlefield between the two sides, and then, under cover of that conflict, become king of Kandy himself.

<!-- source boundary: scan 136 → scan 137 -->

He stood wringing his hands, seeing events begin to move in a direction utterly opposed to his statecraft.

Should he arrest Kannusami? Crown Muthusami? Build a tomb for all his future plans at once? While Pilimathala was struggling without knowing any way forward, news reached his ears that Pandaraka Vanniyan was approaching Kandy.

Pilimathala had a jealous heart that could not accept Pandaraka Vanniyan's valour, courage, steadfast heart, firmness in opposing the British, or the influence he held among the people. Even so, at that moment he needed Pandarakan's help.

He knew that Pandarakan was coming at Kannusami's invitation. Yet Pilimathala acted as though he too welcomed the visit and brought him to his own mansion.

Pandaraka Vanniyan's eyes wandered here and there, asking: Where is Kannusami?

“I understand whom Pandarakar is looking for! Your friend Kannusami, is it not?” Pilimathala asked playfully.

“Yes! I came at his invitation, did I not? What is the difficulty in Kandy's succession question?” asked Pandarakan.

“There is no difficulty! Just as you and I wish, Kannusami must be crowned king of Kandy. While I was making every arrangement for that, the British Governor North suddenly created a major complication!”

“Tell me in detail!”

“Arrest Kannusami! Crown Muthusami! Such an order has come from the Governor!”

<!-- source boundary: scan 137 → scan 138 -->

“Who is he to command us? The right to decide whose government should rule on our soil belongs to us—not to those tricksters who came from somewhere else, defeated the unsuspecting, and trumpet that no one can shake their imperial domination! They have no such right! None at all! There is no need whatsoever to bow to that order!”

“Pandarakar is a leaping tiger! A roaring lion! A peerless warrior who does not tremble at the sight of an enemy! I know that. But is not British military strength many times greater than the combined strength of our two countries? That is why decisions must be taken after careful thought.”

“What have you thought of? What decision have you reached?”

“A device has occurred to me by which I can tell Governor North that a situation has arisen in which Kannusami cannot be arrested.”

“What device?”

“I am going to send word to Governor North that my daughter and Kannusami have fallen in love and that matters have advanced to marriage—and that, while I am making those arrangements, I am at a loss as to how I could arrest Kannusami.”

“But North has to believe it.”

“If you set your mind to it, he certainly will.”

“If I set my mind to it? How?”

“Pandarakar must explain the situation carefully to his friend Kannusami and persuade him to agree to this marriage.”

“Me? Persuade Kannusami? I have already advised Kannusami to marry a Tamil woman.

<!-- source boundary: scan 138 → scan 139 -->

How can I now abandon that and tell him to marry your daughter—that is, a Sinhala woman?”

“Does this not seem the best way for Kannusami to escape danger and assume royal authority? The British have good confidence in me as minister of Kandy. If Kannusami becomes my son-in-law, might he not receive consideration for my sake?”

“Even if the two of us agree to this plan, your daughter must agree too.”

“My daughter lives for Kannusami! It is Kannusami who has not accepted her desire until now. Not a day passes without my daughter lamenting, ‘If I marry, I will marry Kannusami; otherwise I shall embrace death!’”

Pandaraka Vanniyan listened with a smile as Pilimathala spoke with melting emotion about love. Then he saw someone enter the palace hall and sprang up in delight.

“Oh! You have come too?”

Kannusami, walking in with a majestic royal stride, hurried forward, clasped Pandaraka Vanniyan's hands and embraced him.

“What is this? I hear your marriage has been fixed—and you never even told me!” Pandarakan asked teasingly with a wink.

“Whose? Mine? Oh! That Madurai princess you mentioned earlier? Her? Have you settled it?”

“The Madurai princess I spoke of was a Tamil woman, was she not? What I am hearing about now is a peacock of Sinhala land!”

Kannusami was utterly bewildered. He stood like a tree, staring at Pandaraka Vanniyan.
