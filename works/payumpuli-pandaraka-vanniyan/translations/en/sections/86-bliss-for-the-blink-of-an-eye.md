# Bliss, for the Blink of an Eye!

Eager to learn what news would follow the drumbeat, the three of them left the beautiful garden and walked toward the palace. Before they reached the palace corridor, warriors from Kandy and Mullaitheevu came running in agitation from every direction, weapons in their hands, and gathered there. Delighted to see the soldiers spring forth like swarming insects and invigorated by the sight of his king approaching, the man who had been beating the drum at the fort entrance ran over, bowed and stood before the three.

“What has happened?” Vikrama Raja Singan roared at him like a lion.

“It is said that enormous English armies have set out from Kolumbu. A plan has been made there to surround Kandy on all four sides and, at the same time, to send forces to encircle Mullaitheevu from all four directions. The English armies have already set out accordingly.”

As soon as the drummer delivered the news, Pandaraka Vanniyan flared up in anger. “What?” he cried.

“Warriors of Mullaitheevu, immediately form your battle formations and go to every sector to defend Kandy—

go!”

Vikrama Raja Singan did not accept Pandarakan’s order.

“My friend! If either one of our two countries falls into English hands, the other will then be destroyed and enslaved by them as well. So I and the warriors of my country will take the field to defend Kandy. Please leave at once for Mullaitheevu with your warriors! Before the English army nears Mullaitheevu, your men must reach its borders ready to resist them.”

Pandarakan knew that what Vikrama Raja Singan said was reasonable, yet for some reason his heart would not allow him to leave Kandy.

“Vikrama! Somehow we have been careless! We thought the foreigners who fled in defeat might return in vengeance and surround us, yet we failed to make the necessary defensive preparations. That was a grave mistake!”

“For the first time I see a shadow of anxiety on Pandaraka Vanniyan’s face.”

“It is not anxiety! You are asking me to withdraw from my duty to defend Kandy. That is why I hesitate a little.”

“There must be no hesitation! Listen to me! You and Kuruvichchi must leave here at dawn with the forces of Mullaitheevu!”

“Why dawn? If we are going to leave, should we not leave right now?”

“Nothing disastrous will happen before then! Did I not say that I wanted to see you and Kuruvichchi standing before my eyes as bride and groom—

must that wish not be fulfilled?”

“What is this, Vikrama? We have received news that enemies are coming to encircle both my country and yours! Is this the time for such games?”

“Come here. I need to speak a couple of words with you in private.”

With that, Vikrama Raja Singan led Pandaraka Vanniyan behind a silk curtain fluttering in the breeze.

“What secret is this, Vikrama?”

“Nothing much! Once war comes, either we or the enemy—one side or the other—must embrace death. The English know our strength completely, so this time they will not clash with us casually. It seems certain to me that either I or you, my life-friend, will fall in this war. At such a moment you must fulfil the wish that is piercing my heart.”

“Say it plainly! You want to see us in wedding attire. Is that all?”

“Not merely to see you in wedding attire. My queen has told me how many desires that Kuruvichchi has buried inside her heart and how deeply she has been suffering!”

“And so?”

“And so, nothing! Do not think I am babbling something inauspicious. Let this one night pass as a night of bliss with that noble woman who melts in devotion, thinking of you as her very god! Before you leave for the battlefront at dawn—

<!-- source boundary: scan 441 → scan 442 -->

let a dawn arise in Kuruvichchi’s life as well! Let there be one day that belongs to Kuruvichchi. Yes—even if it is only one single day of happiness, let it be so! If her heart finds comfort, then even death on the battlefield will seem utterly trivial to me afterward. My friend, please—for my sake, for the sake of your friend’s plea—make Kuruvichchi’s long-held dream come true!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan stood motionless, forgetting himself.

“No matter how much a woman of ideals she is, the force of youth has shaken her. That is why she poured out all the waves of her heart to the queen of Kandy. If, as the Kandy king says, either I or Kuruvichchi is killed in battle during this struggle—ah! How many desires would Kuruvichchi have to carry away with her!”

So much spoke within Pandarakan’s inner mind. He looked up at the Kandy king. Vikrama Raja Singan saw with delight that Pandarakan was looking at him with a smile overflowing on his face.

Pressing a hand on the king’s shoulder, Pandarakan said, “Very well! Let everything happen according to the king’s command!”

The next moment the king embraced him and, floating in a flood of joy, walked toward the inner palace.

“Queen! You too must have heard that war is coming toward our country and Mullaitheevu! Our forces are ready to defend Kandy! To defend Mullaitheevu—

<!-- source boundary: scan 442 → scan 443 -->

Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi will leave at dawn with their forces!

“Before that, you and I must immediately put into action the plan we had already discussed!”

“I am waiting for whatever command you give!”

“Do you remember that I said Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi should not merely exchange flowers, but should exchange pearl garlands?”

“How could I forget? I had those garlands prepared long ago and locked them safely in a casket!”

Saying this, the queen ran, brought a silver casket and placed it in the Kandy king’s hands.

Opening the casket, he took out the garlands, placed one in Pandarakan’s hands and one in Kuruvichchi’s, and said,

“Now exchange garlands with our loving blessings from the heart!”

Vikrama Raja Singan made the request overflowing with joy.

Eager to see what exactly was happening, Kuruvichchi stood like a statue, never raising her bowed head. In the yellow light of a lamp burning in the inner room, the faint smile peeping from her soft lips looked like a lamp of bliss rising between two hills.

“Hmm! Let it happen!” said the Kandy king in a commanding tone.

Pandarakan placed the pearl garland around Kuruvichchi’s neck. The queen beside her embraced Kuruvichchi and helped her place the pearl garland from her own hands around Pandarakan’s neck.

“The milk and fruit waiting for us are now all yours! Come, Queen, let us go!”

Saying this, the Kandy king closed the room door and started to leave. “Vikrama! What is this?” Pandarakan cried, catching the king’s hand and pulling him back.

Kuruvichchi too tried to slip out by hiding behind the queen.

But the two would not allow it. Vikrama pushed Pandarakan into the room, the queen pushed Kuruvichchi in, and they shut the door.

The Kandy king opened the door a little, looked at Pandarakan and said,

“I shall come at dawn and wake you,” then locked the door from the outside and went away.

Gone was the time when Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi had spoken freely and openly about countless battlefields and how to crush their enemies. Now they sat in that room like mute dolls, one in each corner, their faces hanging down.

What was this? Why were they like this? Who would speak first? Such was the struggle within both their hearts.

If the king had turned his own inner chamber into both the wedding stage and the bridal-night room for the two of them, how deep his friendship for Pandarakan must be! How pure his affection for Kuruvichchi!

The two sat thinking of the Kandy king’s sweet nature.

Very well—should not one of them speak first and break the atmosphere of silence? Kuruvichchi decided that she would be the one to begin.

“Enemies are marching toward Mullaitheevu! And here the two of us sit with our faces

<!-- source boundary: scan 444 → scan 445 -->

hanging down! What sort of spectacle is this?”

“This entire spectacle is your doing! You told the queen that your heart would burst from a love-earthquake. Why, you even told me that! Everything that has happened is the consequence of that. At a time when we should be devising strategy to defeat our enemies, here we sit awake trying to find an answer to the torment of separation!”

“I have already knelt and begged forgiveness for my mistake! It is true that the letter written by your sister Oomaichchi Naachchiyar caused my mind to waver. But the moment I heard your counsel, I overcame it at once! Even now I say this: according to the king’s wish we have put on wedding attire. That is enough for me! In the morning I am ready to put on the attire of war and face the battlefield!”

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar said this firmly and clearly, yet for some reason her eyes filled with tears.

Pandarakan noticed and walked quickly toward her.

“Kuruvi! In the morning you will put on the attire of war and face the field. True! What you will see in the morning is the battlefield. But now—come, let us see another field!”

He pulled her into a tight embrace. Together they fell onto the bed. For Kuruvichchi, who had suffered for so many days from watching him from afar and from standing close enough to see him while only deepening the longing in her heart, the bliss of Pandarakan’s embrace turned her heart into

<!-- source boundary: scan 445 → scan 446 -->

a cool grove and her sweet body into flame, filling her with delight.

But that bliss did not last even for the blink of an eye! The very next moment after they fell upon the bed in each other’s arms, there was a knock on the inner-chamber door. Both sprang upright in haste. They heard the sound of the lock being opened from outside.

The Kandy king entered as he opened the door. Startled at the sight of him, Pandarakan asked, “Vikrama! What is this? Why is there so much pain on your face?” The king stood silent. The warrior who had come with him spoke:

“It is said that the English army has killed your younger sister Oomaichchi Naachchiyar at Panangamam! The white men who have seized Panangamam are going to lay siege to Mullaitheevu!”
