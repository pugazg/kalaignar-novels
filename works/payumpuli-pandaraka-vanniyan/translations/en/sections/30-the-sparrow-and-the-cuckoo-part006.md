“...didn't say that!” What can I do? According to the pledge the two of us made, until our ideal is fulfilled there is to be no bodily union between us. But can either you or I stop our two hearts from embracing, fondling and delighting in one another, from blooming into smiles and showering each other with a rain of kisses? We cannot, Kuruvii, we cannot!”

Speaking in a voice choked with emotion, Pandarakan rested his head in Kuruvichchi's lap and kept gazing at her face. She stroked his legs, bent her face close to his, and devoured him with an unblinking gaze. Pandarakan's eyes became pools; Kuruvichchi's eyes became pouring waterfalls.

It was a bed without flowers strewn upon it. Yet two flowers lay there, drenched in the dew called tears. Feeling that the pleasure of love born while separated and turning sorrowful lament into song was something time itself could never conquer—greater even than the joy of joining in a tight embrace—the two floated through that dream-world upon a sea of tears.

“Kuruvii! I was the one who lost my temper, spoke harshly and left. If it is true that you have given yourself to me, should you not have run after me? If you had said just one word—‘I made a mistake without realizing it. Please do not make too much of it; forgive me!’—it would have felt as though four thousand sledgehammers were striking my heart again and again! Why did you become even angrier than I was, forget me, and never once look back?”

“You have not only thrown me into a flood of pain—you are hurling a spear at me as well! When I believe that everything to me

is you, you separated me from yourself, rejected me and left. What justice was there in that, Aththaan?”

With her cheek pressed to his, the ravishing Kuruvichchi asked that question. Unable to answer it, he seemed to decide that the only way to escape was simply to sweep her into his arms. He embraced her as though two bodies had become one.

Can dream-scenes not defeat even the tricks of a blindfolded magician? And so the bed became a bank of clouds. It carried the two of them through the blue sky as though swimming. Star-maidens winked knowingly and danced in a circle around them. Then those stars changed into vines and scattered flowers of many colours. Beneath that rain of blossoms, with the full moon as their umbrella, Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi walked across a silver mountain of snow with their arms around each other. They ran! They danced! They sang!

“Kuruvii!” he cried, running toward her. She too called him by the pet name “Paandu!” and raced toward him. But between the place where they were to meet yawned a vast mountain gorge. Paying no heed to the gap, each leapt toward the other. Locked tightly together, the two became one and plunged down into the dizzying abyss!

As the dream broke, Pandarakan muttered, “Kuruvichchi!” Turning over again, he clasped tightly the woman lying close against his back. Piyasili, thinking, *What a perfect moment—should I let it slip away?*, let the rest of her clothes slide off.

Only for an instant. Pandarakan awoke. He sprang up in alarm.

“Who is that?” he asked in shock. In the dim light, it took him a little while to regain his bearings.

“Piyasili! You? What is this?”

“Does it need an explanation? A bed—upon it, a handsome man—and beside him, a beauty whose charms remain undiminished!”

“Does this not disgust even you?”

“It is neither disgusting nor troublesome! Bees fly to flowers to feast happily on honey. Now the flower itself has come to feast upon this bee!”

“I have heard about you!”

“Your friend Kannusami must have told you plenty of stories about me!”

“Stories? They are stains upon womankind!”

“Are we not the ones who invent the meanings of ‘stain’ and ‘purity’? God created humankind to enjoy life fully! Are not pigeons, cuckoos, peacocks, tigers, deer and all the other wild creatures God's creations too? Do they go around telling pointless tales about chastity and disgrace?”

“Oh! You have cast off everything! Talking to you is like smearing oneself with sandalwood paste and jumping into a sewer!”

“In a little while, you will praise it as a flower market!”

Even as she said this, Piyasili ignored his attempts to stop her, sprang upon him and held him so tightly that he could not rise from the bed. Before he could wrench himself free and leap up, she knocked down the lamp that had been burning dimly there and extinguished it.

Just then came the sound of someone flinging open the bedroom door and entering. The newcomer held a flaming torch.
