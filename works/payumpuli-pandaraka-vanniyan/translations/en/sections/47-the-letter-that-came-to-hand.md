# The Letter That Came to Hand!

Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi descended the palace steps like the sun and the lovely moon walking side by side. In the front verandah they looked kindly at Jeyaseelan and Marthani, who bowed very humbly and then straightened. Gently stroking Marthani's head, Kuruvichchi asked warmly, “What is your name, girl?” “Marthani,” she replied, and introduced the man beside her: “This is my husband—his name is Jeyaseelan! He has been mute from birth!” Jeyaseelan saluted Kuruvichchi, then fell at Pandarakan's feet and buried his face against them. “Oh! What is all this? Get up, brother!” Pandarakan said, taking him by the shoulders and lifting him to his feet.

A sudden warmth flashed through the entire body of Piyasili, standing there as Jeyaseelan. She felt extraordinary pleasure when Pandarakan's hands touched her. She was the one who had cast the net—she was the one who had dared to attempt murder—and now she was the one left unsteady by his touch!

“Poor fellow, such a handsome young man! See how God made him mute from birth!” Pandaraka Vanniyan said with sympathy.

Marthani immediately interrupted. “Oh! Don't blame God! If he was in such a hurry to be born before God could give him the gift of speech—is God responsible for that?” she said with innocent impetuosity.

Hearing that, Kuruvichchi Naachchi let out a little “kaluk” laugh. As her lips parted with that laugh, revealing a glowing row of pearl-like teeth, Piyasili felt a little jealousy. She was standing there as Jeyaseelan, hiding with great difficulty both her grace and the shapely body that could draw young men toward her. That was why anger toward Kuruvichchi arose within her.

Kuruvichchi, with the heart of a warrior, had a face bright as lamplight. Seen from the front, it was beautiful; seen in profile, more beautiful still—such an extraordinary face! Was that not why Pandarakan had

given his heart to her and neglected Piyasili? The fire kindled by that thought was the source of her anger.

She had the sidelong glance that could bind young bulls and draw them in! She believed that if she swayed her waist, armies would topple at her feet! Was there any count of how many young men's hearts those two swelling charms had broken? Two hibiscus petals needing no painted red, brows shaped like the bow of desire that troubled lion-like warriors from afar—before such pride, even commanders seasoned in many battlefields would surrender to her embrace and

suffer! She would smile—and valiant men would thrill! She would touch—and they would fall worshipfully into her lap! They say the Akshaya vessel gives food without diminishing no matter how much is taken; she considered her own cheek to be such a vessel for herself!

And now that very woman stood before the one man who had refused the intoxicating gift of pleasure she herself had been eager to offer, hiding her thousands upon thousands of amorous arts—why, hiding even her own form—and standing as a mute husband!

She had forgotten the very aim for which she had come there! The incident in which she had even dared to attempt his murder had flown from her mind!

It was extremely difficult for Piyasili to act without showing him her true form! A deep sigh! She was careful that even Pandarakan and Kuruvichchi should not notice it. What if they saw her heaving chest and the Jeyaseelan disguise fell away? Fearing that, she slowly turned a little to one side. Jeyaseelan by disguise! Piyasili by the torment of desire! A rutting elephant does not spare tree or timber, whatever it sees! Among women too there are a few Piyasilis like that!

“Girl, have you come from Kandy?”

A question majestic in bearing, but filled with kindness. Marthani answered Pandarakan at once, “Yes, my lord!”

“I am no lord!” Pandarakan smiled.

“Forgive me. I forgot to call you king!”

“King, lord—leave all that aside! The one standing before you now is a human being! A human being fighting for the freedom of this soil!

Why have you come from Kandy looking for me? Is there some help I must give you?”

“We have come to help you.”

“Help me? What help can a man mute from birth and a woman together possibly give me?”

“We ourselves do not know what help we can give you! But our Maharaja Vikrama Rajasingan thinks so! He believes it!”

“Who? The king of Kandy?”

“Yes! Your friend Kannusami himself!”

“Look here, girl!”

“My name is Marthani!”

“All right! Foolish girl called Marthani! Only I may utter the name of my friend Kannusami. If anyone else says it, I will cut that tongue out and throw it away!”

“Oh God! Let only my husband remain mute! Don't make me mute too! Give me two slaps on the cheek for saying the king of Kandy's name!”

At once the mute Jeyaseelan intervened, gesturing with his hands and pleading that Pandarakan should not strike her cheek, but strike his cheek instead. An unchanging thirst that somehow Pandarakan's hand should touch his body! An unending desire!

“Not bad! You have so much affection for your wife!”

Pandarakan said, affectionately patting Jeyaseelan on the back. Piyasili, imprisoned inside Jeyaseelan, yearned for him to go on patting and stroking her like that!

“All right! All right! Was it the king of Kandy himself who sent the two of you?”

Pandarakan waited eagerly for the answer.

“We did not know whether you would believe us if we merely said so, so the king of Kandy gave us a letter bearing his own signature,” Marthani said, standing shyly.

“A letter? From the king of Kandy? Why didn't you say so all this time? Where is it? Give it here, girl, let me see!”

“It is on the horse! I'll bring it now!” Marthani ran across the verandah and outside.

Pandarakan sat on a long seat there, took Jeyaseelan's hand, and said, “You sit too, brother,” seating him beside himself.

Heat rose through the lust-driven Piyasili from the crown of her head to the soles of her feet. Thinking, “This male attire has brought me this stroke of fortune,” she sat beside Pandarakan, forgetting the world.

“Kuruvichchi! Have them bring something for these two to eat!”

“I'll go and bring it myself,” Kuruvichchi said as she left. Looking at the spy Thanigai, she gestured, “You stay here,” and went inside. Thanigai remained there alert, serving not only as a spy but also as Pandarakan's bodyguard.

Marthani, who had run outside, hurried back to Pandarakan with a letter in her hand.

Pandaraka Vanniyan took the letter and examined it closely. The royal seal of the palace of Kandy—yes, the king's seal—gleamed upon the envelope. He slipped in a finger, gently tore open the sealed letter, unfolded it, and looked. It was a long letter. At the end was Vikrama Rajasingan's signature. He

Marthani took both plates and handed one to Jeyaseelan.

Meanwhile, Pandaraka Vanniyan began to read the letter from the king of Kandy.
