# Pandarakan — Pagathur

“Warriors! You have come at the right time! You may have run here showing your backs to the Mullaitheevu warriors—but now you have a chance to display your courage! Do not let this fine opportunity slip away! Look at her standing there—a fawn with tiger claws! A rabbit with lion's teeth! I could not defeat her alone! Now she is a dove trapped in the middle with eagles surrounding her on all four sides! A little sparrow encircled by vultures! But one thing—a very important matter! Do not kill her! Smother her as one throws a basket over a hen, and take her to our fort! There, she and I shall have a grand wedding! Her brother Pandaraka Vanniyan must writhe when he hears the news! That she will die is certain—but before that I must enjoy her bit by bit until my desire is spent!”

Kakkai Vanniyan reared and danced like a beaten snake coming back to life. At his command, the warriors surrounded Nalla Naachchi and advanced with swords raised. The gaping wolves came at her with their swords aloft, underestimating the strength of a woman. Ready to teach them a fitting lesson, Nalla Naachchiyar began to advance, whirling her sword rapidly. Her sword fought with such speed that even lightning vanishing after tearing lines across the dark palm called the sky might have wondered what its own speed amounted to beside hers.

<!-- source boundary: scan 318 → scan 319 -->

Kakkai Vanniyan stood exhausted. Her courage threw him into panic. One or two heads of the warriors who came close to her rolled on the ground like balls. The arm of one sword-bearing man fell to the earth still gripping the sword. “Don't let her go! Seize her!” Kakkai Vanniyan kept shouting at the top of his voice. “If your skill is useless against one woman, I will have every one of you beheaded! Better to die by her own hand instead!” he yelled. “We are many; she is one. Somehow we can win!” Trusting in that, the warriors fought without retreating.

Nalla Naachchi sprang like a tigress, ripping through the bodies of the wolves, her sword revolving like a cyclone. Then all the enemy swords crashed upon hers at the same moment with such force that she lost her weapon and stood unarmed. Kakkai Vanniyan picked up the sword, broken into two pieces, and laughed wildly in triumph. By then Nalla Naachchiyar was trapped among his warriors and stood there wavering.

“No one's hand must touch her! She is my lover! My property! A goblet of nectar created by God for my pleasure! Bring my golden parrot away without letting her escape—but treat her with royal honour at the same time! Only today will this Kakkai Vanniyan's fort truly come alive!”

Following his order, his warriors formed a ring around Nalla Naachchi and marched her away from there.

<!-- source boundary: scan 319 → scan 320 -->

Until then only embers had surged in her eyes; now there was water too. Yet she conducted herself with the dignity of a warrior woman. With two sword-bearing men ahead of him and two behind as escort, Kakkai Vanniyan smiled and walked with a royal stride.

That beautiful dove was shut inside Kakkai's fort. Every gate was closed. Heavy guards were everywhere. Nalla Naachchi was in a bedroom paved with marble, both hands tied behind her back. There was no one else there. One torch alone seemed to weep for her, claiming to drive away the darkness. Her eyes swept around the room with an uncontrollable longing for some weapon—anything—to come within reach.

What use was it for something to be visible to the eye? Did it not have to come within reach of the hand? Her hands were bound. Let us see what is going to happen, she told herself, swearing that while this body remained alive she would never again allow the hand of any man to touch it. She paced the room.

At the same time, in the hall of the Mullaitheevu palace, Pandaraka Vanniyan paced restlessly. Looking at Marthani standing before him, he asked, “Is everything you say true?”

“Yes, Leader! Before God, I am speaking only the truth. Nalla Naachchiyar told Oomaichchi Naachchiyar, ‘If I marry, I will marry Kakkai Vanniyan; otherwise I will die.’ Oomaichchi Naachchiyar then told her, ‘Sister! Don't worry about anything! Go and see Kakkai Vanniyan right now! If news comes that the two of you have married, then brother Pandaraka Vanniyan—

<!-- source boundary: scan 320 → scan 321 -->

will have nothing left to say!’ It was your own sister Oomaichchi Naachchiyar, who rules in Panangamam, who gave her that courage! I hesitated over how I could tell you that such a thing was going to happen. So I told Thanigaimala, believing him to be your trusted spy. That sinner took me near the Vattrappalai Kannagiyamman temple and tried to rape me. My mute husband—the noble Jeyaseelan—tried to stop him and fought him. Jeyaseelan left Thanigaimala there and, afraid of you, ran off and hid somewhere! The King of Kandy sent us to assist you. I have lost my companion. Even that does not concern me now! Wherever my husband is, he will come looking for me. God will bring him and unite him with me. But what troubles me most is that, in a way I never expected, both your sisters have turned against you and gone to support your enemy!”

As soon as Marthani emphatically affirmed her statement, Pandaraka Vanniyan could barely keep his feet on the ground.

“Nalla Naachchiyar has been deceived! Another sister helped her! A traitor has challenged my very self-respect! My own sisters are on his side! Kuruvichchi! What do you think? What should I do now?”

“First, you must think calmly!”

Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar replied. Then, looking at Marthani, she said softly, “Marthani! Wait outside for a little while.” Marthani bowed her head, saluted both of them, and left the hall.

<!-- source boundary: scan 321 → scan 322 -->

Pandaraka Vanniyan watched Kuruvichchi Naachchiyar closely, waiting to hear what she would say.

“It is a very old saying, but I will say it anyway. Listen without dismissing it.”

“What?”

“What the eye sees may be false! What the ear hears may be false! Only thorough inquiry reveals the truth!”

“Explain clearly.”

“Do you believe Thanigaimala might have raped this Marthani?”

“I know him! He is somewhat weak where women are concerned.”

“That may be. But you must not reject what I say outright. It is an absolute lie that this Marthani is something he could obtain only through force! She is an actress in disguise! Chastity is merchandise to her! She is an obscene woman who treats her body like an inexhaustible vessel and ladles it out to everyone!”

“Would the King of Kandy have sent such a courtesan to me?”

“I do not accept that she came on the Kandy king's orders—and I do not even accept that the mute person called her husband is a man!”

“How can you say all this so categorically?”

“Because I knew you would not believe me at first, I hinted at my suspicions to Thanigaimala himself and warned him to be careful. Through Thanigaimala I learned that your sister was going to the Kannagi temple believing Kakkai Vanniyan to be the merchant Pirmanandan—and that there was a plan to send Thanigaimala alone to that temple and have Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors kill him.”

<!-- source boundary: scan 322 → scan 323 -->

“I knew that if you heard of this you would become angry with your sister Nalla Naachchi. That is why I recommended that you send a hundred warriors along with Thanigaimala. The ones who planned all these intrigues are this Marthani and Piyaseeli, who was disguised as the mute man!”

“What? Piyaseeli? But she died! Didn't you read the King of Kandy's letter?”

“That was not a letter written by the King of Kandy at all! It was a letter fabricated by these conspirators! The man who sent them was Pilimathala, the minister of Kandy!”

Pandaraka Vanniyan was stunned. Shocked, he turned toward Kuruvichchi as though he had stepped on fire.

“Why didn't you tell me all these things?”

“Because I did not want the noble bond between you and your sisters—the unfailing affection—to be destroyed. I tried to manage it myself as far as I could. But now the situation has gone beyond me.”

Even as Kuruvichchi was saying this, they heard the sound of someone running outside the hall. Both rushed toward the outer side and looked. Marthani was running away in panic. But Kuruvichchi Naachchi would not let her escape. She pulled the dagger from her waist, aimed at the heel of the fleeing Marthani, and hurled it with force. The next instant Marthani cried, “Amma!” and tumbled to the ground.
