# They Exchanged the Thalaiyaazhi

Inside the Vattrappalai Kannagiyamman temple, facing the pedestal in the front hall adjoining the *kaappu-kiragam*, Kakkai Vanniyan sat with his eyes closed and hands joined as though he were performing penance. His closed eyes kept peering out through the gaps between his lids to see whether anyone was coming, then lowering the lids again, staging a wretched drama of deceit. Just then, with song ringing out, devotees carrying *kavadis* and *karagams* crowded into the temple. Not realizing that they were soldiers, Kakkai Vanniyan very skilfully pretended to be a man deep in penance. Through the Kannagiyamman temple priest, those devotees offered their gifts to the goddess, worshipped with devout earnestness, and, saying to one another, “He seems to be some great holy man,” also bowed to Kakkai Vanniyan before moving out in line after line. Kakkai Vanniyan did not see them mischievously winking at one another as if to say, “He is our target,” and, “Thinking he is deceiving us, he is only ‘deceiving himself.’” He had by then squeezed his eyelids tightly shut and was muttering a mantra under his breath!

Those “devotees” who left the *kaappakkiragam* front hall split into groups of five or six at the temple entrance, sat down, opened their bundles of packed rice, and began to eat. Even then, their songs dripped richly with devotional flavour.

Hundreds of sharp eyes hidden among the dense bushes a little distance from the temple watched them sit in many different groups, eating and frolicking. Those eyes belonged to Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors. A man leading them sat behind the bushes on a large rock, holding a spear. Looking at his warriors, he said, “This seems to be nothing but a food-loving crowd! They have come with *kavadi* and *karagam* to offer homage to the goddess! Once they finish eating they will leave! Even so, let us remain alert!” The warriors nodded their heads in unison like a flock of sheep, agreeing with him.

While Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors remained among the bushes watching, they heard the sound of a horse approaching slowly. Not only those warriors but also the devotees seated around the temple looked eagerly at Thanigaimala as he dismounted. Just then the priest came out of the temple and stood there, somewhat startled. Thanigaimala looked at the priest and said, “A storm may blow here in a little while. It would be better for you to leave!” No sooner had he said it than the priest flew from the place.

Thanigaimala looked as though he were angrily glaring at his devotees sitting in groups at the temple entrance. “What is this crowd doing here? Get away from this place!” he shouted furiously. Admiring Thanigaimala's acting, the devotees gathered themselves up, rose, and went to sit in formation along the edge of the bushes where Kakkai Vanniyan's—

warriors were hiding.

“Who is this fellow? Why is he threatening us and driving us away like this? We came here to worship the Mother—why is he making such a commotion?”

The devotees spoke loudly as though they knew nothing, and their words reached the ears of Kakkai Vanniyan's warriors too. After all, hadn't the devotees spoken precisely so that those warriors' ears would hear them!

Thanigaimala removed his footwear, left it at the temple entrance, and went inside. Since everyone else had gone out, the *kaappukkiragam* front hall was quiet. Hearing the sound of someone approaching, Kakkai Vanniyan slowly opened his eyes and watched. He made certain that it was Thanigaimala.

“What is this? Is a crow performing dark penance before Goddess Kannagi?”

It was a question full of mockery.

Kakkai Vanniyan gave no answer.

“What? I am asking you—no answer?”

Thanigai gently tapped Kakkai on the shoulder!

At that moment six warriors suddenly leapt down from the upper canopy of the front hall. Even Thanigaimala's warriors had failed to notice that they had been hiding there, clinging to that canopy.

Those warriors who had leapt down threw an iron chain over Thanigaimala and bound him tightly.
